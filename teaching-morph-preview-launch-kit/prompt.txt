4. CENTRAL RENDER PROFILES AND STRICT PREVIEW BEHAVIOR

Resolve at least preview and final through one explicit render-policy/planning layer. The same scene data must select different execution strategies, asset detail, and effect budgets without changing teaching semantics, timing, narration, or intended endpoints.

For the preview morph itself, translate and scale the source toward the declared target placement. Keep the source as the sole visible representation until the transition's terminal scheduled sample. At that terminal sample, atomically replace it with the exact target state; afterward retain the target until the next authored event. Do not crossfade, show the target early, overlap both objects, insert a blank frame, or perform shape/path/mesh interpolation, morph-induced rotation, deformation, or material blending in preview. Ordinary source animation may continue only under the explicit live/snapshot sampling contract.

Define "last frame" against the actual canonical timeline/output schedule, not animation-callback count, eased progress, or the browser's delivered frames. For an inclusive interval [firstFrame, terminalFrame], use source for frame < terminalFrame and target for frame >= terminalFrame. Easing affects translation/scale only; overshooting or early-clamped easing must not trigger the swap. Adapt half-open internal ranges once at the shared boundary without accidentally adding time to clips.

Handle zero-duration and one-frame transitions, adjacent/chained morphs, trim/subrange rendering, skipped frames, pauses, reverse playback, direct seeking, loops, and different output rates. Each sampled timestamp has unambiguous ownership. Evaluation must be deterministic from scene data, canonical time, render profile, scenario and seed, with no dependence on previous callbacks or seeks.

Use declared anchors and stable logical bounds in explicit coordinate spaces, including nested transforms. Define fit/uniformity policies and guard zero-size or invalid bounds. Do not derive placement from changing screen-space or reduced-detail mesh bounds. The target must be cheaply ready for the boundary; bounded low-detail preparation is allowed.

Preview jobs must bypass full-morph correspondence construction, high-detail asset decoding/tessellation, expensive interpolation/shaders/simulation, and final-only effects wherever unnecessary. This includes mounting, imports, eager factories, prefetch, workers, and cache population. Hiding expensive output is insufficient. Instrument work by render job/profile so an intentional concurrent final export is not miscounted as preview work.
