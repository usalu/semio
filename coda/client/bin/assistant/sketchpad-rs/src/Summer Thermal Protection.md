





# 8 Requirements for Summer Thermal Protection

## 8.1 General

The verification of compliance with the requirements for summer thermal protection must be carried out either according to 8.4 or 8.5. The verification must be carried out at least for the occupied room in the building which, within the scope of application and with the planned sun protection measures, leads to the highest requirements for summer thermal protection, hereafter referred to as the critical room.

*NOTE:* Depending on room orientation, window areas, sun protection, etc., it may be necessary to verify multiple rooms.

The requirements do not apply to the product display area behind a shop window or similar installation, provided the product display area is structurally separated from the adjacent occupied room; otherwise, the product display area is considered part of the occupied room. Structural separation in this sense also includes completely closed shelving walls (at least on one side), closed lightweight partition walls, and similar partitions, but not curtains.

## 8.2 Summer Climate Regions

To account for regional differences in summer climate conditions, a distinction is made between summer climate regions A, B, and C for the territory of the Federal Republic of Germany regarding the requirements for summer thermal protection.

The assignment of the climate region to the individual location of a building is carried out according to Figure 1 (Map of summer climate regions).

If a clear assignment between the summer climate regions cannot be found using Figure 1, the following applies:

* between A and B, assign to B,
* between B and C, assign to C,
* between A and C, assign to C.

The regionalization of the map is based on the interaction of the influencing variables air temperature and solar radiation, and the resulting summer thermal behavior of a building.

## 8.3 Verification Process

### 8.3.1 General

Other technical regulations remain unaffected by the requirements formulated in 8.4 and 8.5.

### 8.3.2 Prerequisites for Waiving a Verification

a) If the window area fraction is below the limits specified in Table 7, verification may be waived.

**Table 7 - Permissible values of the floor-area-related window area fraction, below which a summer thermal protection verification can be waived**

| Inclination of the windows to the horizontal | Orientation of the windows^a         | Floor-area-related window area fraction$f_{W,g}$ %^b |
| :------------------------------------------- | :----------------------------------- | :----------------------------------------------------- |
| over$60^{\circ}$ to $90^{\circ}$         | Northwest through South to Northeast | 10                                                     |
|                                              | All other North orientations         | 15                                                     |
| from$0^{\circ}$ to $60^{\circ}$          | All orientations                     | 7                                                      |

*^a If multiple orientations with windows are present in the considered room, the smaller limit value for $f_{W,g}$ is decisive.*
*^b The window area fraction $f_{W,g}$ results from the ratio of the window area to the net floor area of the considered room or group of rooms. If multiple facades or e.g., bay windows are present for the considered room or group of rooms, $f_{W,g}$ is to be calculated from the sum of all window areas to the net floor area.*

b) For residential buildings as well as building parts for residential use,

* in which the critical room does not exceed a floor-area-related window area fraction of 35%, and
* whose windows in East, South, or West orientation (including those of a glass extension) are equipped with sun protection devices with a reduction factor $F_{C} \le 0,30$ for glass with $g > 0,40$ or $F_{C} \le 0,35$ for glass with $g \le 0,40$ (see Table 10 to Table 12), and
* in which the possibility for increased night ventilation exists,

verification may be waived. A glass extension is not used as the critical room.

### 8.3.3 Occupied Rooms or Room Areas in Connection with Structurally Separated Glass Extensions Not Intended as Occupied Rooms

**a) With ventilation only via the unheated glass extension:**

1) The verification for the considered occupied room is deemed fulfilled if the unheated glass extension has sun protection with a reduction factor $F_{C} \le 0,35$ and ventilation openings in the highest and lowest glass area, which together make up at least 10% of the glass area;
2) If 1) is not given, the verification must be carried out according to 8.5; the actual structural design, including the unheated glass extension, must be reproduced in the calculation;

**b) With ventilation not or not only via the unheated glass extension:**

1) The verification can be carried out using the method according to 8.4 as if the unheated glass extension were not present. In doing so, the transparent areas from the occupied room to the glass extension can be treated as transparent outer areas of the occupied room and fictitiously assigned the reduction factor that the transparent outer areas of the glass extension possess (averaged proportionally by area if necessary).
2) For verification according to 8.5, the actual structural design, including the unheated glass extension, must be reproduced in the calculation.

### 8.3.4 General Calculation Boundary Conditions

**a) Net Floor Area and Room Depth**
The net floor area $A_{G}$ is determined using the clear room dimensions. For very deep rooms, the room depth to be applied for the verification must be limited. The maximum room depth to be applied is determined as three times the clear room height. For rooms with opposite window facades, there is no limitation on the applied room depth if the facade distance is less than/equal to six times the clear room height. If the facade distance is greater than six times the clear room height, the verification must be carried out for the two respective facade-oriented room areas that result. When determining the effective thermal capacity, the enclosing components are only to be considered insofar as they determine the volume formed from the net floor area $A_{G}$ and clear room height.

**b) Window Frame Fraction and Window Area**
The simplified method using the solar entry indicator $S$ according to 8.4 was derived for windows with a frame fraction of 30%. As an approximation, this method can also be applied to buildings with windows that have a frame fraction other than 30%, as well as for skylights and roof lights such as roof domes, flat roof windows, and continuous roof lights.
To determine the window area $A_{W}$ to be applied, the clear structural opening dimension is used, i.e., the outer dimension of the window frame plus the installation joint or mounting joint. Plaster or any existing claddings are not to be considered.
For window elements with opaque parts (e.g., opaque panels, front-mounted boxes, mini top-mounted boxes, frame widenings), only the transparent sub-area of the windows is to be considered as the window area $A_{W}$ in the verification. Frames between the transparent area and opaque parts are fully attributed to the transparent sub-area of the windows in this case.
If the actual transparent area is known, based on this, the window area can be set as $A_{W} = A_{g}/0,7$ (windows and skylights) or $A_{W} = A_{L}/0,7$ (roof lights).

## 8.4 Solar Entry Indicator Method (Sonneneintragskennwerte)

### 8.4.1 General

The solar entry indicator method described here represents a simplified procedure based on the boundary conditions according to 8.5 for verifying summer thermal protection and was developed for typical room geometries. For special room geometries (e.g., event rooms, atriums), conducting simulation calculations according to 8.5 is recommended.

For the room or room area to be evaluated, the existing solar entry indicator (vorhanden) must be determined according to 8.4.2 and compared with the maximum allowable solar entry indicator (zulässig) determined according to 8.4.3.
The verification of compliance with the requirements for summer thermal protection is fulfilled if the existing solar entry indicator does not exceed the allowable solar entry indicator according to Equation (1):

$$
S_{vorh} \le S_{zul} \quad (1)
$$

*(Note: $S_{vorh}$ = existing/present, $S_{zul}$ = allowable/permissible)*

The verification cannot be performed using the simplified method described in this section if the rooms or room areas in question are connected to the following structural installations:

* Double skin facades; or
* Transparent thermal insulation systems (TWD).

### 8.4.2 Determination of the Existing Solar Entry Indicator

For the room or room area to be investigated regarding summer overheating, the existing solar entry indicator $S_{vorh}$ is to be calculated according to Equation (2):

$$
S_{vorh} = \frac{\sum_{j} A_{W,j} \cdot g_{tot,j}}{A_{G}} \quad (2)
$$

Where:

* $A_{W,j}$ is the window area of the j-th window, in $m^{2}$;
* $g_{tot,j}$ is the total solar energy transmittance of the glass including sun protection, calculated according to Equation (3) or according to DIN EN ISO 52022-1, DIN EN ISO 52022-3, or based on DIN EN 410 or guaranteed manufacturer specifications;
* $A_{G}$ is the net floor area of the room or room area in $m^{2}$.

The sum extends over all windows of the room or room area.
The total solar energy transmittance of the glass including sun protection $g_{tot}$ can be simplified and calculated according to Equation (3):

$$
g_{tot} = g \cdot F_{C} \quad (3)
$$

Where:

* $g$ is the total solar energy transmittance of the glass for perpendicular radiation incidence according to DIN EN 410;
* $F_{C}$ is the reduction factor for sun protection devices according to 8.6.

If structural shading needs to be considered for glass areas, $g_{tot}$ in Equation (3) can be modified using the reduction factor for shading by the environment, other buildings, or building parts $F_{S}$ according to DIN/TS 18599-2 (i.e., $g_{tot} = g \cdot F_{C} \cdot F_{S}$). The multiple consideration of a single influence (e.g., canopies) is excluded here.

### 8.4.3 Determination of the Allowable Solar Entry Indicator

The maximum allowable solar entry indicator $S_{zul}$ results from Equation (4):

$$
S_{zul} = \sum S_{x} \quad (4)
$$

Where $S_{x}$ is the proportional solar entry indicator according to Table 8.

## 8.5 Requirements and Boundary Conditions for Thermal Building Simulations

### 8.5.1 General

Particularly when the applicability of the simplified method described in 8.4 is excluded, a dynamic-thermal simulation calculation must be carried out to evaluate the thermal conditions.

For the evaluation of thermal comfort in indoor spaces, the reference values for operative indoor temperatures given in Table 9 are specified for the verification process. It must be proven that the overtemperature degree hours requirement value given in Table 9 is not exceeded in the critical room.

### 8.5.2 Calculation Boundary Conditions for Thermal Building Simulation Calculations

**a) Simulation Environment**
The program used for the verification must be named in the documentation.

**b) Usages / Usage Times**

* Residential buildings: daily, 00:00 to 24:00
* Non-residential buildings: Mon. to Fri., 07:00 to 18:00
* Central European Time (CET) is to be assumed for the whole year (no daylight saving time / CEST).

**c) Climate Data for Calculations**
Assigned Test Reference Years (TRY 2011) based on the climate region:

* Climate Region A: Normal year TRY-Zone 2
* Climate Region B: Normal year TRY-Zone 4
* Climate Region C: Normal year TRY-Zone 12

**d) Start of Simulation Calculations and Evaluation Period**
Calculations are to be run for a complete year, starting on January 1st on a Monday at 00:00. Public holidays and vacation periods are not to be considered.

**e) Internal Heat Gains**

* Residential buildings: $100 Wh/(m^{2}d)$
* Non-residential buildings: $144 Wh/(m^{2}d)$
  To be applied as constant heat gains during the usage time (treated as 100% convective heat gains).

**f) Target Room Temperature for Heating Purposes**
Ideal heating with purely convective heat input:

* Residential buildings: $\theta_{h,soll} = 20^{\circ}C$
* Non-residential buildings: $\theta_{h,soll} = 21^{\circ}C$

**g) Base Air Exchange Rate**

* **Residential buildings:** $n = 0,50 h^{-1}$ (assumed constant throughout the day, includes infiltration).
* **Non-residential buildings:**
  * During usage time (07:00 to 18:00): $n = 4 \frac{A_{G}}{V} [h^{-1}] \quad (5)$
  * Outside usage time (18:00 to 07:00): $n = 0,24 h^{-1} \quad (6)$

**h) Increased Daytime Air Exchange**
If the room air temperature exceeds $23^{\circ}C$ and is higher than the outside air temperature, the mean air exchange rate during the occupancy period (Non-residential: 07:00–18:00; Residential: 06:00–23:00) may be increased to $n = 3,0 h^{-1}$ in the next simulation step to prevent overheating.

**i) Night Air Exchange**
During the times 18:00–07:00 (Non-residential) and 23:00–06:00 (Residential):

* Base air exchange according to g), if night ventilation is not possible.
* $n = 2 h^{-1}$ (increased night ventilation) if window ventilation is possible.
* $n = 5 h^{-1}$ (high night ventilation) if cross-story ventilation possibilities exist (e.g., atrium).
* If a ventilation system is used, the air exchange rate may be set according to the system's design.
  *Condition for application:* $\theta_{i,Luft} > \theta_{i,h,soll}$ AND $\theta_{i,Luft} > \theta_{e} \quad (7)$

**j) Sun Protection**
Calculate $g_{tot}$ according to DIN EN ISO 52022-1/3 or Equation (3).
Control (if unknown): radiation-dependent control for N/NE/NW with a threshold of $200 W/m^{2}$ (Residential) or $150 W/m^{2}$ (Non-residential); for all other orientations $300 W/m^{2}$ (Residential) or $200 W/m^{2}$ (Non-residential).

**k) Surface Heat Transfer Resistances**
May be applied as constant according to DIN EN ISO 6946.

**l) Structural Shading**
May be considered (must be documented).

**m) Passive Cooling**
May be considered (must be documented).
