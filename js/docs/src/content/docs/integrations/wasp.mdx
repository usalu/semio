## ✨ Why Integration Matters

**Wasp** is a powerful engine for generating aggregations of modular parts through stochastic processes
It assembles modular Parts into large-scale, rule-based structures with speed and flexibility

**Semio** complements this by providing a seamless integration bridge into Wasp’s workflow  
More than that, Semio **extends Wasp’s functionality 🛠️** making complex aggregation setups easier to define, manage, and reuse

---

### 🔄 A Unified Workflow

The real strength of integration lies in the **unified Semio workflow**

- Start in **Semio 🧩** → prepare your Kit, Types, and Ports
- Move into **Wasp 🐝** → run stochastic aggregation
- Return with an **Aggregation → Design 🏗️** in Semio for further parametric refinement

Or begin in **Wasp 🐝** and bring the Aggregation into **Semio 🧩** to unlock extra layers of intelligence, metadata, various integrations and control

---

### 🔌 Port Families

One of Semio’s biggest contributions is the idea of **Port Families**  
Instead of manually writing long and complex graph grammars 📜 in Wasp, Port Families allow compatible Ports to automatically recognize each other  
This reduces setup time ⏱️ and makes the aggregation process more intuitive 🤝

---

### ⚙️ Connection Parameters

Semio extends Wasp with **Connection Parameters**  
Even after a Wasp Aggregation is generated, individual **Parts 🧩** can still be **transformed, rotated, or adjusted** through Semio’s parametric logic

---

## Conversion Methodologies

Semio and Wasp approach modular design differently, but together they form a **hybrid workflow 🔗**

### 🧩 Semio — Define the System

- Organize **Kits 🎒**
- Create reusable **Types 📦**
- Place them as **Pieces 🧩**
- Specify **Ports 🔌** for snapping logic
- Build structured **Designs 🏗️**
- Carry **metadata, variants 🎨, and flexibility** at every stage

👉 See the [**Think in Semio**](https://docs.semio-tech.com/think-in-semio/) page for more

---

### 🐝 Wasp — Execute the Assembly

- Define **Parts 📦** with fixed **Conns 🔌**
- Apply explicit **Graph Grammar Rules 🔗**
- Run **stochastic Aggregations 🎲**
- Explore many possible **assemblies quickly ⚡**

---

### ⚖️ Outputs Compared

- **Semio Design 🏗️** → captures **intent, modular logic, and metadata**
- **Wasp Aggregation 🐝** → delivers the **concrete result** of rule-based assembly

By linking the two, you get the **best of both worlds 🌍**

- **Semio** for design structure, logic, and flexibility
- **Wasp** for fast stochastic exploration and execution

👉 The following table places their **core terminologies side by side**, showing how each concept translates across the two platforms

## 🐝 Wasp ↔️ Semio Terminologies

| Wasp Term                  | Semio Term    | Meaning in Practice                                                   |
| -------------------------- | ------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| 📦 Part                    | 📦 Type       | The reusable distinct brick mold                                      |
| 🧩 Transformed Part        | 🧩 Piece      | A specific brick placed in the model with a fixed position and ID     |
| 🔌 Conn (Connection Point) | 🔌 Port       | The snap spots on each brick where other bricks can attach            |
| 🔗 Graph Grammar Rule      | 🔗 Connection | The rulebook that says which Ports can connect to which               |
| 🏗️ Aggregation (Aggr)      | 🏗️ Design     | The assembled structure — all Parts and Pieces joined under the rules |
| 🚫 _(no direct Wasp term)_ | 🎒 Kit        | A set of Types and Designs that belong together                       |

## 🔄 Wasp Integration Components

The Wasp integration in Semio creates a **two-way workflow** between the two systems

- **Semio 🧩** builds modular designs as **graph-based assemblies** with Types, Pieces, Ports, and Connections
- **Wasp 🐝** generates **aggregations of Parts** through rule-based stochastic placement

This integration allows you to **export a Semio Design into Wasp** for aggregation and then **re-import the result back into Semio** as a structured Design graph  
Semio provides clarity, rules, and modularity, while Wasp contributes flexible, generative assemblies

---

### 🧩 Types → Parts

Export **Semio Types** into **Wasp Parts**

- Semio’s Type contains geometry, Ports, and connection logic
- This component translates them into Wasp-compatible Parts and generates Rules and Groups needed for aggregation

**Inputs**

- `Ty` → **Type** (Type definition in Semio)
- `Ob` → **Object** (geometry associated with the Type)
- `Rt` → **Rotations** (degrees of freedom from Ports)

**Outputs**

- `Part` → **Part** (Wasp-ready Part definition)
- `Rules` → **Rules** (connection grammar for Parts)
- `Gr` → **Graph Grammar** (set of connection groups)

---

### 🚦 Filter Part

Filter out specific **Parts** before aggregation

- Refines which Parts are included in the Wasp aggregation process
- Useful to test assemblies with only certain modules or restrict unwanted components

**Inputs**

- `Part` → **Part** (available Wasp Part set)
- `Rules` → **Rules** (associated connection Rules)
- `i` → **Index** (filter condition or index)

**Outputs**

- `Part` → **Part** (filtered Part set)
- `Rules` → **Rules** (filtered Rules set)

---

### 🧩 Design → AGGR

Convert a **Semio Design** into a **Wasp Aggregation**

- Transfers a complete Semio assembly into Wasp for stochastic aggregation
- The output remains editable in Wasp, allowing testing of placement strategies, collision checks, or rule variations

**Inputs**

- `Dn` → **Design** (Semio Design graph)
- `Ty` → **Type** (Type definitions)
- `Part` → **Part** (Wasp-ready Parts)
- `Rt` → **Rules** (connection Rules)

**Outputs**

- `Aggr` → **Aggregation** (Wasp aggregation object)
- `Part Out` → **Part Output** (generated Parts after aggregation)
- `Part Out Flat` → **Part Output Flat** (flattened Part data)
- `Gr` → **Graph Grammar** (connection grammar used)

---

### 🐝 AGGR → Design

Convert a **Wasp Aggregation** back into a **Semio Design**

- Re-import a Wasp result into Semio
- The aggregated geometry becomes a structured Design graph, ready for transformations, connections, or rule-driven extensions

**Inputs**

- `Ty` → **Type** (Type definitions)
- `Aggr` → **Aggregation** (Wasp aggregation object)
- `Part` → **Part** (Parts from aggregation)
- `Part Out` → **Part Output** (resulting Parts)
- `Rt` → **Rules** (connection Rules)

**Outputs**

- `Dn` → **Design** (reconstructed Semio Design graph)

## Walkthrough Example — Metabolism Kit

For this tutorial we use the **Metabolism Kit**, based on the iconic Metabolist tower design  
The Kit contains multiple **Types 📦** that we will translate from **Semio 🧩** into **Wasp 🐝**, and then use Wasp’s **stochastic aggregation engine 🎲** to assemble into new configurations

---

### 🔹 Step 1 — Deconstructing the Kit in Semio

On the Semio side we begin with an existing **Kit 🎒**  
By deconstructing it we extract all of the core information needed for integration

- **Types 📦** → reusable molds
- **Designs 🏗️** → assembled structures
- **Ports 🔌** → connection points with degrees of freedom
- **Representations 🖼️** → geometry used for visualization or export

This prepares the data for translation into Wasp’s workflow

---

### 🔹 Step 2 — Translating Types into Wasp Parts

In Wasp, **Parts 📦** must include not only geometry and connection planes but also **3D connection logic**  
Semio’s **Ports 🔌** provide this by carrying information about **locking and rotational degrees of freedom**

**Inputs**

- `Ty` → **Types**
- `Ob` → **Objects** (3D geometry)
- `Rt` → **Rotation locks**

**Outputs**

- `Part` → **Wasp Parts**
- `Rules` → **Wasp connection rules**

This ensures Semio’s flexible Ports become static, well-defined Wasp Conns

![📤 Type to part](/Type-to-part.png)

---

### 🔹 Step 3 — Stochastic Aggregation in Wasp

With Parts and Rules generated, we can run a **stochastic aggregation 🎲** in Wasp

**Inputs**

- `Part` → **Wasp Parts**
- `Rules` → **Graph Grammar rules**

**Process**

- Wasp assembles the structure step by step following the grammar

**Output**

- `Aggr` → **Aggregation** representing a complete assembled design

💡 Use the **Filter Part 🚦** component in Wasp to control which Types are included in the aggregation and explore design variations

## ![📤 Wasp Aggregation](/Wasp-aggregation.png)

### 🔹 Step 4 — Bringing Aggregation Back into Semio

The final step is to re-import the **Wasp Aggregation 🐝** back into **Semio 🧩**

**Inputs**

- `Ty` → **Types**
- `Part` → **Wasp Parts**
- `Aggr` → **Aggregation**
- `Rt` → **Port rotations**

**Output**

- `Dn` → **Design** that mirrors the Wasp Aggregation inside Semio
