# Native Animate video and GIF encoding

The previous writer copied RGBA8 bytes into samples marked AVC1 and emitted empty SPS and PPS lists. Its test demuxed the track and verified the original RGBA bytes. ISO-BMFF demuxing succeeded because the container itself was readable.

Native Animate now sends frames through the framework H.264 encoder. Each partial starts with an IDR frame and carries the SPS and PPS needed for playback. Repeated frames retain their ticks through P-skip samples. The final MP4 concatenates compatible access units in order, preserving sync flags, dimensions and cadence.

GIFs use the original capture. A per-frame local palette prioritizes the configured owner and retains up to 40 exact colors from a deterministic sample of at most 32,768 downscaled pixels. Remaining colors use the same 216-color cube. Only indexed frames and their palettes are retained. The frame cache is bypassed for outputs that need the original capture, including GIFs and PNG sidecars.

The full picture and run-duration key names each published AVC fragment. Each render owns a private scratch directory and pins cache hits there until final assembly. Eviction and explicit cache flush remove published links while an in-flight movie keeps using its own links. Simultaneous producers publish complete files atomically. Filesystems without hard links use a private copy and same-volume rename. Modification times preserve recency across cache reopen.

## Verification

- PyAV decoded a separately compiled 16 × 16, 15 fps raster encoder smoke test with two distinct pictures and one repeated frame after each. The keyframes matched indices 0 and 2. On the dark owner the decoded RGB was `(6, 26, 31)`. On the light owner it was `(232, 230, 215)` against authored `(233, 230, 215)`.
- PyAV rejected a structurally readable MP4 containing raw RGBA in an AVC sample with an empty decoder configuration. The H.264 decoder reported invalid data when sent the packet.
- Pillow decoded a two-frame 640 × 2 GIF generated by compiling the production palette capture and stdio GIF89a and LZW encoder functions in a small native harness. It returned exact RGB for the dark owner `(6, 26, 31)` and chip `(17, 35, 40)`, and for the light owner `(233, 230, 215)` and chip `(218, 217, 204)`. The fixture's 800 × 3 original frames also verified GIF-only support and the normal 640-pixel downscale.
- The four native production cache unit tests passed in isolation. They cover hash stability, full-key reopening and eviction, persisted last-access order, and simultaneous atomic publication. The latter also verifies that flushing the cache preserves an active render directory.
- The refreshed native video package passed all seven writer and four cache regressions. The eviction case reopened the cache, pinned an existing AVC fragment, evicted its durable entry, and decoded both pictures from the assembled movie in order.
- The independent `animate-native-encoder.py` probe passed against the production writer and repository codec archives. PyAV 19.0.2 decoded four 16 × 16 H.264 frames at 15 fps with keyframes at indices 0 and 2 and expected owner and chip pixels within four channel units. Pillow 13.0.0 independently decoded four GIF frames and matched all owner and chip pixels exactly. The same probe produced the original malformed sample layout using stdio's MP4 encoder. PyAV decoded zero pictures from it. The probe's native harness independently reran all eleven writer and cache regressions.

The probe takes the frozen native Cargo archive directory and the pinned `rustc` executable. It writes its artifacts under the ticket's generated directory.
