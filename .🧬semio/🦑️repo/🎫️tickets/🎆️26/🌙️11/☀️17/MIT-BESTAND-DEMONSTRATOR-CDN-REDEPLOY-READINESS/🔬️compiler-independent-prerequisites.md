# Compiler capacity for independent release prerequisites

Two overlapping native release drivers each held six physical compiler leases on the 12-core host. Their repository-managed font and descriptor subprocesses then sought real compiler capacity in their own processes. The parent's process-local compiler scope cannot authorize a subsequent process to spend its leases, and the existing GNU Cargo jobserver distributes compiler work slots without lending the repository's physical capacity authority. Both outer scopes could retain every core while waiting on owner jobs.

The release pipeline detects its authored `repo` subprocesses. Its outer scopes share one restricted low-index lease range, sized to leave the standard compiler minimum available to independently admitted work. In the 12-core example the range contains nine physical cores. One ordinary release keeps its preferred six, and two can retain at most nine in aggregate. An independently admitted compiler child can acquire three core leases through its normal authority. Other capacity consumers keep their existing behavior. Multi-core release dependency lists and stage scheduling remain unchanged. During overlapping releases the reserved capacity can remain idle between independently admitted jobs.

On a single-core host, the driver's declared repository jobs settle under their own bounded child lifetimes before the driver attempts to claim the core. The plan keeps the relative prerequisite order and removes only edges for successfully settled jobs. The release plans authored at the repository root and Play use repository jobs throughout.

The focused subprocess regression exercised actual physical locks and a separate child operating under an issued resource control. Original allocation retained host-wide capacity across two lease owners and the child timed out after five seconds. With the reservation the child acquired the remaining three physical locks, and a normal lone parent still acquired six. An independent SQLite calculation verified aggregate lock counts, the prerequisite ordering and a language-neutral corpus covering hosts with one through 32 cores. The guarded 512 MiB replay passed two tests with 16 assertions. The larger multi-driver integration replay was deferred while real guest builds were finishing.
