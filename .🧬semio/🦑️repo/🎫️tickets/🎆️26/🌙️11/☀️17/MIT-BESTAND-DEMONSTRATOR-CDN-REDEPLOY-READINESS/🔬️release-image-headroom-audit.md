## Browser Memory Grant and Resident Planning

In the November 21 live snapshot, the ready-sidecar's run-many process 34282 waited for graph planning on its Unix socket. Its peer was descriptor 13 on resident host 39421. That host's graph-plan grant explicitly attached worker 34770, which held the graph image and the selected graph shard's exclusive leases. The ready-sidecar log was still recording file-inventory progress and the run-many process had no build child. The worker observation belonged entirely to the independent graph-plan grant. These are separate reservations within the same logical invocation.

The client's grant originally booked 512 MB for planning. At 168 seconds of graph-slice activity it upgraded to a 2,574 MB execution grant, with its process tree later measuring 66 MB. Its live grant records an upgrade and ramp but has neither an explicit upgrade request file nor a phase mark. In current production, the quiet-holder heartbeat can latch a single observation above the small reservation and queue a full execution upgrade. The explicit execution checkpoint runs after the verified resident plan and scheduler admission. The early upgrade used the heartbeat path. The original transient sample magnitude was outside the retained grant snapshot.

At one sample, 2,508 MB of the 2,574 MB grant was unrealized. The worker's observed memory was counted through the graph-plan attachment. The extra execution reservation was booked during a phase whose execution could only begin after planning. With 2,975 MB available and a 2,048 MB floor, the host could supply only 927 MB above its floor even without that unrealized reservation. Active swap also blocked the browser command at that sample. A later snapshot recorded the browser command admitted with a reduced 2,074 MB grant after the client's 976,935 ms ramp expired.

An isolated run used separate production build-holder and graph-holder processes plus a real child attached to the graph grant. A single injected 768 MB client sample reproduced the former upgrade from 512 MB to 8,193 MB while the graph worker remained live. The fix scopes automatic outgrowth upgrades to the ordinary quiet path. A service-planned caller retains its planning grant and records its resident observations until its explicit execution request. Planning-only peaks contribute to quiet observations rather than executed-run data. The regression passed both checkpoints after the fix. Twenty-two planned/follow-up tests and five ordinary quiet tests also passed. The live processes and memory policy were left in place.
