# Round 3 brief — anatomically and constructively correct models (2026-11-19)

`R` = `/Users/ueli/Documents/semio`, `S` = `R/🎓️teaching/🏛️architecture/🧍️staffage`, `T` = this ticket folder. Catalog asset: `S/🗂️catalog/<emoji category>/<slug>/🔣️.json` (member `asset`). Tools take `category/slug` (e.g. `animals/horse`).

## Why this round exists

The dev looked at the 3D staffage and found it incredibly sloppy: limbs and objects completely wrong proportioned, detail missing, skeletons wrong. He is right. Example: `animals/horse` reads as a wolf (short legs, dog head, no withers, no barrel) and `humans/standing-adult` is a box torso on tube legs with ball shoulders. All validators are green on both, because earlier rounds fitted the 3D to the 2D drawings and the rig, and those are wrong themselves. Green numbers prove nothing about quality. Your eyes and your knowledge of the real subject are the gate.

## Your job for ONE asset

Make the asset a perfect, handcrafted model of the real subject at every authored scale (1:50, 1:100, 1:200, 1:500) and in every list (`body` for the four tone views, `thermal` for the thermodynamic view, `structure` for forces and moments), end to end, including the roots it stands on.

1. **Reference first.** Before touching geometry write a reference table into your report: the real subject's key dimensions and proportions with sources (your knowledge, the asset's `provenance.references`, WebSearch for manufacturer data, anthropometric or veterinary anatomy tables). Humans: segment lengths and joint heights as shares of stature (hip joint about 0.52 H, knee 0.285 H, ankle 0.039 H, shoulder joint 0.818 H, elbow 0.63 H, wrist 0.485 H, head height about H/7.5, shoulder breadth about 0.245 H biacromial, hip breadth about 0.19 H; children have larger heads and shorter legs: a 6-year-old is about 6 heads tall), girths and depths of thorax, waist, pelvis, thigh, calf, upper arm, forearm. Animals: real skeletal landmarks (withers, point of shoulder, elbow, carpus, fetlock, hip, stifle, hock; scapula and humerus angles; skull length; neck length; barrel depth and width). Vehicles and machinery: wheelbase, track, overhangs, wheel diameter, roof height, tumblehome, real assemblies. Furniture, appliances, components, objects: real product dimensions, real construction (profiles, wall thicknesses, joints, fixings). Vegetation: real habit, trunk taper, branching order and angles, crown form.
2. **Audit against the reference**: the rig (`asset.rig.bones`: joint positions, bone lengths, hierarchy; a skeleton with joints in the wrong place is a root defect), `dimensions`, the 2D drawings of every scale and view, and the current solids. List every deviation with numbers in the report.
3. **Fix the roots.** Wrong rig joints, wrong dimensions, wrong 2D drawings are corrected in the asset file itself, together with everything that depends on them (poses, clips, anchors, supports, 2D shapes of all scales and views, thermal and structural data) so that the asset stays valid and animates correctly. Ruling E1 below says how 2D and 3D stay in agreement.
4. **Model by hand**, solid by solid:
   - `body`: real cross-sections everywhere. Lofts with anatomical or constructive sections, revolves, extrudes with real profiles. No box torsos, no tube limbs, no ball joints visible in the body, no segmented-cylinder barrels, no flat plates for organic or sheet-metal forms with curvature, no floating or interpenetrating parts, no gaps at joints (overlap segments so that every pose and clip stays closed). Living subjects: skull and jaw form, neck, ribcage and pelvis masses, muscle groups that shape the limb (deltoid, biceps/triceps, forearm taper, gluteal mass, quadriceps, hamstrings, calf, ankle, heel), hands with thumb and fingers at 1:50, feet or hooves or paws that are right; clothing as garments over the body with folds where the cloth hangs, not painted limbs. Things: every assembly the real object has, at the detail the scale can show (design.md: smallest feature 7.5 / 15 / 30 / 75 mm), with real radii, chamfers, wall thicknesses, wheel arches, glazing, handles, feet, fixings.
   - `thermal`: the thermal model in space as design.md says (every region, nested layer shells at 1:50, hidden regions as real internals, emitters as outlet patches on the surface (D2), areas honest (C29, C31)). It follows the new body exactly: no stale references, no shells poking out.
   - `structure`: the real load path (D3): the anatomical skeleton for living subjects (skull, spine with its curvature, ribcage, scapulae, pelvis, long bones with real proportions and joint positions, hands and feet simplified by scale), the real frame for things (chassis, frame tubes, box girders, carcass panels, legs, rails, columns, beams), joints at the real joints, supports at the support anchors, masses at the centres of mass. Never the body or a block.
   - Every coarser scale is authored anew for that scale (merged masses, simplified sections), still right in proportion and recognisable at a glance; never the finer list with parts deleted, never a blob.
   - `decisions` per scale: real bilingual sentences (en first, de second).
5. **Look.** After every substantial change and before finishing, render and open the PNGs with the Read tool: the sheet of every scale (`volumes-render.ts <asset> --scale N`) and, for 1:50 and 1:100, each list alone from at least four directions (`--list body --width 1600 --az 0|90|180|270|35 --el 0|20|60`; the same for `thermal` and `structure` at two directions). Use `r3-sheet.ts` instead when `T/r3-tools.md` says it exists (several directions and the rig in one image). Judge like a strict art director and an engineer: would a zoologist, an orthopaedist, a car designer, a carpenter accept this at first sight? Compare with what you know the subject looks like. Fix and look again. Do not stop at the first acceptable state; the bar is "perfect", grade A in every list at every scale.
6. **Check**: `$BUN volumes-check.ts <asset> --product` green at every scale, and the asset validates (`$BUN r3-validate.ts <asset>` when `T/r3-tools.md` says it exists; else say so in the report). Do not weaken rules or shared tools; a rule that is wrong for your asset goes to `T/author-requests.md` with measurements, a tool defect to `T/tool-requests.md`, and you continue.
7. **Splice** with `python3 volumes-insert.py volumes/<asset>.json "<catalog asset file>"` (it re-reads the asset and replaces only `volumes`). Root fixes outside `volumes` are written into the catalog file by your own small edit script (re-read the file right before writing, change only what you mean to change, keep member order and formatting style).
8. **Report** `T/r3-<category>-<slug>.md`: reference table with sources; audit findings; root fixes (old and new values); per scale and list what you modelled, solid counts, the final check line; what you saw in the final renders, direction by direction; anything still imperfect, stated plainly with the reason. Return only the report path plus five lines.

## Ruling E1 — one body, drawn and modelled

The handcrafted 3D model of a scale in `presentation.defaultPose` is the master of that scale's form. Where a 2D viewpoint (elevation, front, plan) contradicts the real subject or the corrected rig, the drawing is redrawn at the root so that it is the true orthographic view of that scale's body: per bone, with the same shape ids, roles, bone bindings, layer order and line hierarchy the drawing had, outlines clean and economical for the scale (no tessellation noise, no slivers; a drawing is drawn, not dumped). `T/r3-tools.md` says whether `r3-project2d.ts` exists to give you the projected outline per bone as a starting point; whatever the source, you open the 2D result and the overlay and judge it. Detail, texture and glazing strokes are redrawn where the form under them moved. Poses and clips must still read right after a rig change: render at least the default pose and one other pose or clip frame.
