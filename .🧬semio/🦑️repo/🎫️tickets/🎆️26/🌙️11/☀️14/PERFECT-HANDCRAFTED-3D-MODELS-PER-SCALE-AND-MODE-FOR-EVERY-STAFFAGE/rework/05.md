## First look (before any change), parent-carrying-toddler 1:50, pose `standing-carry`

Read at full size (`r3-sheet --only front|right`):

- The toddler sits on the tip of a forearm held straight ahead, 0.25 m in front of the chest and outside the parent's right shoulder line; nothing holds its back, the far arm hangs. Nobody carries 12 kg like that: the child sits on the hip or on the forearm against the chest, its trunk leans on the parent, the carrying hand closes around the far thigh or seat and the other hand supports the back or hangs free only for a hip carry.
- The toddler is a small adult: slim tubular trunk, long thin legs, no belly, a neck as long as an adult's. A 24-month child has a head of about a quarter of its stature, a barrel trunk with a round belly, short thick limbs with soft folds, almost no visible neck.
- The parent's thighs and shanks are tubes about 0.11 m wide with no calf, knee or ankle form; the trouser legs under the coat hem are narrower than the boots.
- The sleeves are boxes with a hard shoulder step; the hanging hand is a flat paddle with a thumb in the wrong plane.
- The parent's face is a mask (prism nose, slot eyes) on a narrow head; the neck vanishes in the scarf.
- Rig: one trunk bone from L5/S1 to C7, no clavicles/shoulder girdle, no lumbar/thoracic split, no finger bones on either hand; the toddler has no hand and no toe bones.
