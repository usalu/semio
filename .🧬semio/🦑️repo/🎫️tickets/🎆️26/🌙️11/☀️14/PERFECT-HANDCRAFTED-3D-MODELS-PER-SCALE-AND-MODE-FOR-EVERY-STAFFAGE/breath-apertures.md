# Physiological breath apertures

The 1:50 pigeon model has two nasal apertures in the cere. Each is 2 mm long, 1.1 mm wide and 0.64 mm thick. The previous feature check required the normal thickness to reach 3.8 mm. The sheep, pig and goat also use thin outlets where air crosses their facial tissue.

The feature rule now recognizes a thermal ellipsoid or an untapered extruded aperture when its longest face dimension matches the stated breath diameter within 1 µm and its normal extent is positive and smaller than its face width. The outlet carries both the emitter tag and its source ID. The corresponding breath emitter must belong to the tissue subject’s nasal or oral respiration channel and use an emitter anchor on the same bone. Every copy must lie within one declared diameter of a separately modeled surface of that tissue. This accommodates the paired lateral nares represented by one breathing source. The surface test uses the closest distance to the tissue mesh, including triangle faces and edges.

The language-neutral volume-rule fixture and feature scenario cover valid ellipsoid and extruded outlets and eleven controls. These cover ordinary ventilation, an unused breath declaration, missing or unknown tags, incorrect bone, a displaced patch, a non-physiological region, an undersized opening, a body drawing, a round bead and a longitudinally extruded marker. Three.js independently measured both aperture geometries. The original implementation failed the physical nasal fixture. A second red check moved the pigeon outlet one metre away while retaining all tags. The final targeted Vitest run passed all three selected tests, including the existing sheet and merged-tissue cases.

The current product feature rule was run across 14 animal and 28 human documents with no breath findings. The exact four model revisions reviewed were pigeon be30f63d26, goat c1117edbde, sheep b8dd8c1470 and pig d8c404a55b. Independent Three.js closest-point measurements on every one of their 44 authored nasal and oral outlet patches across the four scales placed the patch centre within one declared diameter of its tissue boundary.
