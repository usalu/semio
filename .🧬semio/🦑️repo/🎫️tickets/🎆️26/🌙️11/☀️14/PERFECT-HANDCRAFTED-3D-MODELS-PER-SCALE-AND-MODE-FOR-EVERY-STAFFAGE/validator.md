# Validator maintainer report

## 2026-11-22 — Rule changes authors must know

Decided once on 2026-11-22 (morning); none of these will be flipped back. Every change has a fixture and a test in `S/🧪️tests` (suites named per item) and is described in `S/README.md`, section "Volume". Re-run `r3-validate.ts <asset> --volumes` after reading.

1. **Thin sheets are declared: `sheet: true`** (new optional member of every solid, schema `S/🧬️schema`, twin of `slender`). `volumes-feature` lets a solid stay below the smallest feature of its scale as a sheet only when it is sheet-shaped (thickness = smallest extent; middle extent at least the feature and at least 20 times the thickness; for a planar extrusion the smallest width of its profile) AND declared: `sheet: true` on the solid, or a thermal layer `thicknessM` for its bone and material that equals its thickness (the long-standing gauge sheets, no flag needed). Use it for feather vanes, leaf blades, fabric, membranes, foils, sheet metal, true thin panels and plate-built vessel or shell walls; they keep their physical thickness at every scale. A declared sheet that is a lump or a strip (under 20:1), or `sheet` together with `slender`, is a `volumes-feature` finding. The geometric pass without declaration that was live between 01:23 and about 03:30 today is gone: an undeclared thin plate fails and the message says "declare `sheet: true`". Do not declare boards and panels of a coarse scale that should be merged into one mass at that scale; the flag is for parts that are truly thin in reality and still meant at that scale. Solids that passed only through the undeclared pass: `T/validator-sheet-impact.md` (21 assets, 111 solids). Hollow vessels need no flag: a `revolve` with a closed profile loop and an `extrude` with `holes` are judged by their overall extents, so the wall is drawn true (3.5 mm compressor shell). Tests: `🧊️volumes-connected` (sheet declarations), `🧊️volume-schema`.
2. **Long structure members keep their true section at every scale: declare `slender: true`.** A `member` solid of the `structure` list may be `slender` from 5 times as long as its largest cross extent and a length of two smallest features of the scale (15, 30, 60, 150 mm; it was four features for two hours, which wrongly shut out radius and ulna at 1:500). `🧪️tests/🧊️model-skeleton` pins femur, tibia, humerus, radius and ulna of the adults at one shaft section for every scale, so these are never thickened to the feature (elderly-with-cane, person-on-crutches and tour-guide did that at 1:500 and must go back to the true shaft with `slender: true`); without the declaration the true shaft fails `volumes-feature`. Everywhere else `slender` stays at 20:1 along the centre line. Compact solids below the feature (knob, bolt head, finger bone at 1:500) are merged or left out at that scale. Test: `🧊️volumes-connected` (long members: femur, radius, finger).
3. **`volumes-feature` judges every scale on its own** (live since 01:23): a coarse solid gains nothing from being identical to a 1:50 solid. A coarse list that is the 1:50 parts list fails with every part below 15 / 30 / 75 mm.
4. **`volumes-connected`** (live since 01:30; measured, `--volumes`): in the default pose the body is one object (a group that touches nothing must stand on the ground or at a `contact` or `support` anchor, and a part that a movable joint ties to another bone must touch it), and every structure group that is not only `mass` markers reaches a `support` solid, the ground or such an anchor (directly or through one body solid that stands there). Contact tolerance a fifth of the smallest feature (1.5 / 3 / 6 / 15 mm), exact triangle distances. No flag switches a solid off. Test: `🧊️volumes-connected`.
5. **Thermal outer area: model hollow parts hollow.** A solid of another region covers a face (so that the face counts as outer area) only when it stands between that face and the air: walking on along the normal, the first free space beyond it must be reached by the air. A neck, spigot, shaft or sensor that passes through a closed shell no longer makes the inside of that shell count (pump flanges, TRV sensor cap): remove the lumps that were put in to hide the inside and keep the true `areaM2`. Unchanged and final: a gap of at most a quarter feature (1.9 / 3.75 / 7.5 / 19 mm) is closed, an opening of half a feature or more is open; between the two the result depends on the voxel grid, so in the THERMAL list close a seam that is closed in reality to under a quarter feature. A real opening narrower than half a feature of a coarse scale (waste-bin lid notches, chalkboard running gap) is declared once as `thermal.minimumAirPassageM` (metres, the narrowest real air passage): the flood then resolves it at every scale and no gap has to be drawn wider than it is; when the asset is too large for that cell the new finding `airPassageCellM` says so. Tests: `🧊️volumes-flood` (hand values and an independent voxel flood).
6. **Silhouette of very small assets** (live since 01:24): the comparison raster has at least 256 cells along the long side of the object (it was a quarter feature whatever the size, 32 cells across a 60 mm valve); the limits stay in smallest features. The pen is not forgiven: a stroke is compared at its drawn width, and a stroke that ends early is a real difference; complete the stroke. Test: `🧊️volumes-connected` (raster).
7. **README corrected, kernel unchanged: loft `rx` / `ry`.** Quaternions are `[x, y, z, w]`. With the limb rotation `[0.5, 0.5, 0.5, 0.5]` `rx` lies along v and `ry` along w. Without `rotation` and a path exactly along u, `rx` lies along w and `ry` along v, but the slightest lean of the path towards v swaps them, so always state `rotation` on a path along u. Test: `🧊️model-frame` (loft axes).
8. **Tools.** `r3-sheet.ts --file <asset document json>` and `r3-2d.ts --file …` render a candidate (rig, poses, clips, volumes of the file itself) without touching the catalog (`T/r3-tools.md` sections 1 and 3a). `r3-project2d.ts` no longer drops or erodes sub-feature limbs at 1:500 (section "2026-11-22" of `T/r3-tools.md`; goat 1:500 elevation IoU 0.915 → 0.963). `r3-validate.ts` spends 94 % of its time in the print checks (`validateRanges`), not in `validateAsset`; one wasteful distance transform is windowed now (CPU −10 to −35 %, results identical).
9. **A coarse skeleton: one member solid may span several bones, `members: [bone, …]`** (new optional member of a `part: "member"` solid of the structure list, beside `member`; schema `S/🧬️schema`). The solid then is the member solid of every bone it names for `volumes-structure-coverage`, and `volumes-slender` judges it by its own length as before, so a spine of five 60 mm segments is one 300 mm rod at its true section with `slender: true` (from 5:1 and two features of length, item 2), a tail is one bar, and the skull member takes the mandible in. Two conditions, both checked: the named bones are distinct, exist and form with `member` one connected piece of the rig (each the parent or a child of another; finding `volume-member`), and in the default pose every named bone lies from joint to tip in the solid or within one smallest feature of the scale of its surface (7.5 / 15 / 30 / 75 mm; measurement `spanGapM:<solid>:<bone>`, 9 points along the bone), so a rod names only bones it runs along or bones the scale cannot show apart from it (a 16 mm collarbone beside the spine at 1:500), never a limb that sticks out. Do this instead of 75 mm rods for spine segments, clavicles, caudal vertebrae, mandible, metapodials and hip bones at 1:200 and 1:500; a bone that is long enough on its own (150 mm at 1:500) keeps its own `slender` member. The structural views colour and load the solid as its `member` bone, so name the bone that carries the run there. Test: `🧊️volumes-connected` (one member solid spans several bones), `🧊️volume-schema`.
10. **`volumes-connected` on the structure list never measures to a `mass` marker.** The gap of a finding and its "from …" solid are taken from members, joints and supports only; a member that merely passes a mass marker is no `near` finding any more, and an `unsupported` group names the member or joint it should have met. The `near` finding of the structure list now reads "has its own load path to a support solid, the ground or an anchor but passes within … of the members, joints or supports of the rest of the structure" (it said "stands on its own supports"): join the members at their joint or keep the two structures more than one feature apart. Nothing else changed, no asset becomes invalid by this. Test: `🧊️volumes-connected` (a mass marker is no load path).
11. **A hoop is a bent rod, not a sheet: declare `slender: true`.** A ring revolved from a closed profile loop off the axis (rib hoop, guard ring, lip, seam) is judged as a sheet by the radial width of its band, no longer by its diameter; `volumes-feature` therefore no longer tells you to declare `sheet: true` on a hoop with a thin band, and such a hoop with `sheet: true` fails the declaration. No declared sheet of the catalog is affected (checked: 0 of 152 assets); a flat washer or annular plate whose band is 20 times its thickness is still a sheet. A hoop keeps its true section at every scale with `slender: true`, which `volumes-slender` accepts along its centre line (circumference over section, 20:1), identically for every diameter. Test: `🧊️volumes-connected` (sheet extents, long members: rib hoops).
12. **`lateral-ambiguous` reports only what neither the rig nor a solid places.** A flat elevation shape on a bone that rests inclined (`abductionDeg` / `twistDeg` of the rest placement: a scapula 12° or 14° out) is the exact picture of a plate in the bone's plane and is accepted at rest; `volumes-silhouette` checks it against the body like any drawing. What is reported is the paper error a pose or clip adds: `min(extent of the shape, largest |w| of the body solids of the bone at that scale) · |w₁ − A·w₀| · 1000 / denominator` above 0.2 mm, where `|w₁ − A·w₀|` is `sin θ` for a bone resting in the paper (as before), 0 for a bone that only moves with its parents in the sagittal plane, and about 0.09 for a 12° scapula swung 25° about its own axis; a plane resting edge-on (a clavicle laid sideways) is always reported. What you do: keep the body solids of such a bone close to its plane (place the bone beside the body with `z`, not the solid with a `w` offset), or draw `solid` shapes; give a limb below an inclined bone its upright frame in the REST placement (`rig-rest.ts --solve`), not by pose offsets, which turn its front and plan drawings and are still reported. This replaces the sentence of `rig-lateral-rest.md` 1.6 item 6 that every flat elevation shape on a lateral bone is reported. Test: `✅️asset-validation` (`lateralAmbiguous`, numpy).
13. **Typified rows: `envelope: true` on a bone of the row.** A coarser scale may replace a row of many thin elements by fewer, thicker ones (balusters, pales, louvres, spokes). Give the row a bone of its own (fixed joint), model the typified elements at the coarse scale as solids with `envelope: true` (a `repeat` fits; `slender` may be added for true rods), say so in `decisions`, and draw the same elements in the 2D of that scale. `volumes-cross-scale` then compares the convex hull of the `envelope` solids of the bone with the convex hull of the finer solids of that bone and leaves both out of the volume band; without the flag the posts fail against the rods (spiral stair: p95 356 mm against 150). An envelope prism of a truss or mast behaves as before. Test: `🧊️volumes-rules` (`typified`, shapely).
14. **Outline strokes: the centre line is the body edge.** An unfilled closed outline around a part the solids fill (at least half of what it encloses, the solids taken at their true section) counts as filled up to its centre line, and a slender solid along it (rim wire, edge band) is now compared at its true width instead of the pen of the outline; before, the wire was widened to the pen and stood half a pen outside the drawing all round (65 mm pen at 1:500: 32 mm). Draw the outline centred on the true edge; strokes moved inside by half a pen may go back (`shopping-trolley` 1:500 plan: 0.898 centred before, 0.955 now, equal to the moved stroke). Unchanged and final: an open stroke and an outline around a void are geometry at pen width, on both sides (item 6). Test: `🧊️volumes-rules` (`follow.bodyEdge`, shapely).
15. **`volumes-views-disagree` also compares the place.** Two views of a scale must give the whole asset the same interval along the axis they share, both ends within max(one feature, 2 % of the extent), not only the same extent: a view drawn 50 mm aside (for example front and plan left behind after the root joint was moved; they are drawn in rest coordinates with the root at the origin) is a finding that names both intervals. The whole catalog passes today (152 assets, 558 scales, worst 0.9 of the limit). Per bone nothing fails: `info:positionDifferenceM:<bone>:<axis>` is information. Test: `🧊️volumes-rules` (`viewsPosition`, shapely).

## Connectivity and per-scale feature (2026-11-22, `validator-connectivity.md`)

Reported by authors 14, 09 (floating parts, open load paths) and 19 (coarse scales that carry the 1:50 parts list); measured first with `validator-connectivity-probe.ts`, table and per-asset findings in `validator-connectivity.md`.

- **`volumes-connected`** (new, measured, module `🔗️connected`, fourteenth rule id). Parts are the copies of the solids; two touch when their kernel triangles come within 0.2 smallest features (`connectedToleranceFeatures`, the seam tolerance of the area rules: 1.5 / 3 / 6 / 15 mm) or one lies inside the other. Exact triangle distances (vertex to face, edge to edge, edge through face) over a per-part cell grid, union-find over the pairs whose boxes come that close; no bounding box decides contact. Body, default pose: every group apart from the largest must rest on the ground plane or at a `contact` or `support` anchor, else `floating`; a resting group is a second object only when no movable rig joint ties its bones to bones whose solids lie in other groups (`detached`: wheel and axle, shoe and shank; `fixed` joints only place a bone, which is how the catalog rigs a lamp stand beside its table or a basket beside the gardener) and when it is more than one feature clear of the rest (`near`: `connectedApartFeatures`, a gap no drawing of the scale shows). Structure: every group that is not only `mass` markers must hold a `support` solid or rest on the ground or at such an anchor (45 catalog assets declare no `structure.supports` and stand on contact anchors), directly or through one body solid that itself stands there and that the part touches or lies in (a skeleton ends in the foot, the sole carries it to the ground: one solid, never the body as a whole, which would connect everything), else `unsupported`; in a list without any support solid (the sample figure: a skeleton idealised in the sagittal plane whose foot members end 40 mm above the ground) the largest group is the structure itself and every other group must join it; supported groups keep the clearance. Legitimate cases decided from the data: mass markers float (3067 in the catalog, at centres of mass); separate objects on the ground are legitimate (operating-table lamp stand, kneeling-gardener trug); emitters, plumes, flames and jets are thermal solids only (845 tagged, none in a body list), and the rule does not read the thermal list (a thermal list is surfaces, the bookshelf shelves rest on pins that are no thermal surface); `texture` and `slender` solids get no exception (a texture lies on its surface, a cord hangs from something); a held object must touch the hand. There is no flag, role or tag that switches a solid off. Gaps are measured exactly up to six features (`connectedGapCapFeatures`) and reported as "at least" beyond. Findings: one per list and reason with the worst four groups (first solid, further solids, gap, nearest solid, joint) and `data.solids`; measurements `looseGroups:<reason>` (limit 0), `info:groups`.
- **`volumes-feature`, per scale**: the allowance "a coarser solid may keep the exact dimensions of its 1:50 counterpart" is removed (`physicalSources` and its helpers are gone). It was the hole author 19 measured: of the solids below the feature of their scale that are neither `slender`, `texture` nor a thin role, most were identical to a 1:50 solid and passed for that reason alone (table in `validator-connectivity.md`). Every finding carries `data.solid`.
- **`volumes-feature`, sheets** (authors 08, 11; SUPERSEDED by item 1 at the top: the sheet must be declared): `isSheet` judges a thin sheet of any kind and any list by its two large extents, as `slender` is judged by its length: thickness below the limit, middle extent at least the limit and at least `sheetRatio` (20) times the thickness (for a planar extrusion the smallest width of its profile); where the thermal layers declare a gauge for bone and material the thickness must be that gauge (the earlier extrude-only rule, kept). A feather vane 24 x 1 mm, a 4 mm casing panel or a membrane pass at their true thickness; a lump or a strip does not. The earlier pinned expectation that a broad sheet without a thermal gauge fails is reversed on purpose (`🧪️tests/🧊️volumes-rules`, "retains the physical gauge …"). - **Thermal area, what reaches into a closed shell** (author 17; the edits described here were lost when the agent was stopped and were made again, item 5 at the top; the fixture case is now a 200 x 18 mm bar ending 2 mm above the floor, 0.52 m² against 0.5786): `outsideOf.reach` counted an inner face as `cover` (C29) when a solid of another region with any air-reached surface stood within half a feature in front of it, also inside a closed cavity (a neck, a spigot, a sensor that passes through the shell).
