# Perfect Handcrafted 3D Models per Scale and Mode — Design

Normative for every agent of this ticket. Change it only through the coordinator. It builds on `../../☀️10/ENGINE-NEUTRAL-3D-MODELS-FOR-EVERY-STAFFAGE/design.md` (frames, kernel, amendments A1–A6 stay valid) and replaces its single `asset.volume`.

`S` = `🎓️teaching/🏛️architecture/🧍️staffage`, `M` = `🧰️framework/🔨️modules/🧊️3d/🗿️model`, `T` = this ticket folder. Facts: `audit-volumes.md`, `audit-pipeline.md`, `audit-2d-scale-mode.md`.

## Principle

The 2D archive draws every asset individually for each preset scale (1:50, 1:100, 1:200, 1:500) and each view. The 3D archive does the same: one individually handcrafted model per scale and per mode, never a filtered copy of another scale. `maxDenominator` on solids is removed.

## Schema (`S/🧬️schema/🔣️.json`, typed twin, validator)

`Asset.volume` is replaced by the required `Asset.volumes`, keyed exactly like `representations` (`"50"`, `"100"`, `"200"`, `"500"`):

```
Volumes        { "50": ScaleVolume, "100": ScaleVolume, "200": ScaleVolume, "500": ScaleVolume }
ScaleVolume    = AuthoredVolume | UnavailableVolume
AuthoredVolume { status: "authored", denominator, body: VolumeSolid[] (min 1), thermal: ThermalSolid[] (min 1), structure: StructuralSolid[] (min 1), decisions: Text[] (min 1) }
UnavailableVolume { status: "unavailable", denominator }      exactly when representations[denominator] is unavailable; its reason lives there
VolumeSolid    = the six kinds as today (box, ellipsoid, capsule, revolve, extrude, loft; bone, material, role, tone, transform?, repeat?, weights?) without region/regions/maxDenominator
ThermalSolid   = VolumeSolid & { region: Slug, layer?: Slug }  |  { id, of: Slug (a body solid of the same scale), region: Slug, layer?: Slug }
StructuralSolid= VolumeSolid & { member: Slug (bone id of the member), part: "member" | "joint" | "support" | "mass" }  |  { id, of: Slug, member: Slug, part }
```

- **body** carries the tone views (dark-line, dark-solid, light-line, light-solid).
- **thermal** is the thermal model made spatial: every `thermal.regions[].id` of the asset has at least one solid at every authored scale; at 1:50 a region with more than one `layers` entry is modelled as nested shells, one per layer (`layer` = layer id, innermost first, real `thicknessM`); `hidden` regions (engine, exhaust, heater core…) are real solids inside the body; emitters are solids. A reference `{ of }` reuses a body solid where the thermal surface is that body surface — the geometry is then shared by construction.
- **structure** is the structural idealisation made spatial: every load-bearing bone of the rig is a member solid with its real load-bearing cross-section (frame tube, bone shaft, trunk, leg), joints are joint solids at the rig joints, supports are support solids at the `structure.supports` anchors, concentrated masses (`part: "mass"`) are compact solids at their centre of mass. Non-bearing cladding is absent.

### Level of detail per scale (what "individually authored" means)

Smallest modelled feature = the print profile's smallest printable feature at that denominator (0.15 mm on paper unless the asset's print profile says otherwise): 7.5 mm at 1:50, 15 mm at 1:100, 30 mm at 1:200, 75 mm at 1:500. A coarser scale is a new abstraction (merged masses, simplified sections, generalised outline), drawn against that scale's own 2D shapes — not the finer solids with parts deleted. Each scale's `decisions` say what was merged and why.

### Validator rules (catalog check; ids `volumes-*`)

1. `volumes-availability`: authored/unavailable mirrors `representations`.
2. `volumes-silhouette`: at every authored scale and every viewpoint drawn (elevation, front, plan) the body silhouette in `presentation.defaultPose` agrees with that scale's 2D filled shapes (IoU ≥ 0.92, Hausdorff ≤ 2 × smallest feature of the scale).
3. `volumes-bounds`: every authored scale's body bounding box agrees with `dimensions` (A6) within one smallest feature of the scale.
4. `volumes-feature`: no solid dimension below the scale's smallest feature (except declared thin sheets with `role` in a closed list).
5. `volumes-monotonic`: expanded solid count and tessellated triangle count do not grow towards coarser scales, and at least one of them strictly drops at every step unless the scale's `decisions` declare the model already minimal.
6. `volumes-cross-scale`: the body silhouette of scale k agrees with scale k−1 (IoU ≥ 0.85, Hausdorff ≤ 2 × smallest feature of the coarser scale); the volume of the union differs by at most 15 %.
7. `volumes-thermal-coverage`: every thermal region has a solid; every thermal solid names an existing region (and layer); the outer surface area of a region's solids agrees with `areaM2` within 15 % at 1:50 and 30 % at coarser scales; thermal solids lie inside the body bounding box grown by one feature.
8. `volumes-structure-coverage`: every bone that the structural evaluation loads has a member solid; member solids lie inside the body silhouette; supports sit on the ground plane or their anchor.
9. `volumes-bones`: every bone with drawn 2D shapes at a scale has a body solid at that scale; ids are unique per list; `of` references resolve inside the same scale.
10. Existing solid rules (sizes, loft, revolve, extrude) apply per list.

## Kernel (`M`, `semio.model/v1`)

- New top-level `scenes: Scene[] { id, title, nodes: Slug[], tags?: Slug[] }`: named sets of mesh-carrying nodes over one shared skeleton. Validation, tessellation, posing, edges, viewer honour the active scene.
- GLB: one glTF `scene` per model scene (core glTF, no extension), `scene` default = first. FBX: one scene per file (option `scene`).
- Everything keeps progress + cancellation and stays deterministic under bun and node (portable math).

## Staffage pipeline

- `modelOf(asset, { scale, view, environment? })` reads `volumes[scale]`: tone views → `body`; `thermal` → `thermal` solids, one material per region (and layer), coloured by the solved field; `forces`/`moments` → `structure` solids coloured by N / M per member, plus generated glyphs. `modelOfAsset(asset)` builds one document with a scene per authored scale × geometry (`s50-body`, `s50-thermal`, `s50-structure`, …).
- Exports: `<slug>.<pose>.glb` holds all authored scales and modes as scenes with the seven views as material variants; `<slug>.<clip>.s<scale>.<view>.fbx` per clip, scale and view; `model` JSON = the multi-scene document. The batch exporter exports every authored scale in one run and never deletes another scale's files; resume key = content digest of asset, schema and producing code.
- Axonometric depiction, React target and site: scale and view pick the matching model; downloads and viewer use the same quality and the same root-motion rule.

## Amendments (coordinator, after `audit-2d-scale-mode.md`)

- **B1 — members.** "Load-bearing bone" is exactly the set the 2D structural view colours: `eligibleBones` in `S/🔨️modules/🎨️depiction/🏗️structural-view` (a bone with `section`, or with `lengthM` ≥ 5 % of the drawn height). A bone with a `MemberSection` (`rect w h` / `circle d`) gets a member solid with that section; 2D `member` shapes (`from`, `to`, `section`) each get a member solid along that bar.
- **B2 — layers.** `ThermalLayer.thicknessM` is optional. A shell is modelled with its `thicknessM`; a layer without thickness is modelled at 1:50 with the thickness derived from its `resistanceM2KW` and the conductivity of its material when that is declared, else it is merged into the neighbouring shell and the scale's `decisions` say so. 295 regions have no layers and 469 have one: they are a single surface solid.
- **B3 — emitters and supports.** Each `thermal.emitters[]` entry is a thermal solid (nozzle/outlet of `diameterM` at its anchor, `region` = the emitter's own region when it has one, else tagged `emitter` on the region it leaves from). Each `structure.supports` anchor is a `part: "support"` solid shaped by its `SupportType` (contact pad, pin, roller, fixed block), sized by the scale's smallest feature.
- **B4 — hidden regions.** The 11 regions with `hidden` are real solids inside the body in `thermal`, never in `body`.
- **B5 — no copies.** Rule 11 `volumes-copied` (twin of the 2D `representation-copied`): an authored scale whose `body`, `structure` or real `thermal` solids are identical to, a subset of, or a uniform scaling of a finer scale's list is refused.

## Amendments (coordinator rulings on author and schema requests)

- **C1 — thermal area is the exposed surface.** `areaM2` of a region is compared with the *exposed* area of its outermost-layer solids: mesh faces that are (a) not buried inside another solid of the same scale (body, thermal or structure), (b) not lying in the contact plane of a `support`/`contact` anchor (ground, mounting wall), and (c) not declared section faces. New optional field on every solid: `sectionFaces: ("-u" | "+u" | "-v" | "+v" | "-w" | "+w")[]` — faces whose outward normal lies within 45° of that bone axis are section cuts through a continuing construction (a wall, slab or beam sample) or the hidden back of a one-sided patch; they count for no thermal area and are drawn as cut faces (hatch tone) in the viewer. Open solids (lofts without caps) stay valid for skins. Tolerances stay 15 % at 1:50 and 30 % coarser. Where an asset's `areaM2` is a plain mis-estimate against the honestly modelled object, the author corrects `areaM2` at the root in the asset and lists every changed value (old, new) in the report.
- **C2 — slender parts.** A solid may carry `slender: true` when its length is at least 20 times its largest cross extent (cord, wire, capillary, rod, hose, antenna). The validator verifies the ratio (`volumes-slender`). Slender solids are exempt from `volumes-feature`; at each scale they are as thick as that scale's 2D draws them (true size where the 2D uses an open stroke); they are excluded on both sides of `volumes-cross-scale`; a coarser scale may keep a slender part in `thermal` or `structure` without having it in `body`.
- **C3 — cross-scale with generalisation.** `volumes-cross-scale` is tolerant by one smallest feature of the coarser scale: silhouette mismatch pixels within that distance of the other silhouette's boundary do not count against the IoU (limit stays 0.85), and the union volume of the coarser body must lie between the volume of the finer body eroded and dilated by that feature (replaces the flat 15 %).
- **C4 — monotonic.** Triangle counts for `volumes-monotonic` are taken at one fixed tessellation quality (chord 2 mm, the same segments for every radius), not at the scale-adaptive quality.
- **C5 — silhouette Hausdorff.** Unchanged (2 features, maximum). Only filled 2D shapes take part; slender solids are compared against the stroke they follow, widened to the pen.
- **C6 — support anchors.** A `StructuralSolid` with `part: "support"` carries the required `anchor: Slug` (one of `structure.supports`); every support anchor has exactly one support solid per authored scale, and the solid is checked against that anchor (not the first anchor of its bone).
- **C7 — lattices and large objects.** Silhouette, Hausdorff and cross-scale measurements are resolution-independent: they are computed on vector outlines or on a raster whose cell is at most one quarter of the scale's smallest feature (tiled for large objects), never on a fixed pixel count. 2D open strokes (lattice bars, cables) take part in the silhouette comparison widened to their pen width at that scale, so a truss modelled bar by bar matches a truss drawn bar by bar; self-crossing 2D polygons are compared by their non-zero-winding area. A coarser scale that replaces a lattice by its envelope prism declares it in `decisions` and marks the prism solids `envelope: true`; cross-scale then compares the envelope with the convex hull per bone of the finer lattice.
- **C8 — foliage and view agreement.** (a) The 2D viewpoints of one scale must agree with each other before a 3D model can agree with all of them: where elevation, front and plan disagree on a shared extent of a bone by more than one smallest feature, that is a defect of the 2D drawing; the author corrects the less authoritative view at the root (elevation is authoritative for heights and depths along the viewing plane, plan for widths). (b) For solids of foliage masses (thermal region kind `foliage`, `fur`, `feather`, `hair`: irregular soft outlines) the silhouette Hausdorff limit is max(2 features, 2 % of the largest drawn extent of that mass); IoU ≥ 0.92 is unchanged. Trunks, branches, stems and every hard part keep the strict limit. Crowns are modelled as lobes (ellipsoids, lofts) that follow the drawn scallops, not as one ball.
- **C9 — silhouette and bounds limits recalibrated (coordinator's correction of rules 2 and 3).** The first limits (IoU 0.92, maximum Hausdorff 2 features, bounds 1 feature) were set without data; the previous gate that all 152 assets met was IoU 0.80 and bounds 8 %, and the 2D front and plan views are constructed drawings, not projections of one body. Normative now, at every authored scale, in `presentation.defaultPose`: IoU ≥ 0.90 in elevation and ≥ 0.88 in front and plan; the 95th percentile of the boundary distance (both directions) ≤ 2 smallest features in elevation and ≤ 4 in front and plan (soft masses per C8b: max of that and 2 % of the mass extent); the maximum distance is reported, not limited; bounds within max(one smallest feature, 2 % of the axis extent). An asset that cannot reach this because its 2D viewpoints contradict each other is repaired in 2D at the root (C8a).
- **C10 — texture parts.** Parts the 2D draws as `texture` or open `detail` strokes inside a filled outline (spokes, slats, seams, grilles) may be modelled as solids flagged `slender: true` or, for sheet-like texture, with the solid field `texture: true`; texture solids are excluded from the silhouette and cross-scale comparisons and from `volumes-feature`, and belong only to scales whose 2D draws that texture.
- **C11 — view disagreement is judged on the whole asset.** `volumes-views-disagree` fails only when the viewpoints of one scale disagree on an extent of the *whole asset* (height between elevation and front, length between elevation and plan, width between front and plan) by more than max(one smallest feature, 2 %). Per-bone disagreement is reported as information (it guides the author where the constructed front/plan drawings deviate from one body) and never fails. C8a is narrowed accordingly: authors repair 2D only for whole-asset extent contradictions and for plain drawing errors.
- **C12 — section views.** Where a 2D viewpoint is a section (a `ViewSection` with a plane), the 3D side of the silhouette comparison is the true section view: the cut faces of the body on the plane united with the projection of everything beyond the plane in viewing direction. Boundary distances are measured against that union.
- **C13 — smallest feature follows the drawing.** `volumes-feature` compares a solid's smallest extent with min(smallest feature of the scale, the thinnest filled 2D shape extent drawn for the same bone at that scale); a part the 2D draws as an open stroke is `slender` (C2). A solid is never forced thicker than its own drawing. A flat extruded sheet can retain its measured material gauge when that bone and material carry a unique thickness in the thermal layers at this scale, the extrusion matches that thickness and the narrowest in-plane span meets both the drawing feature and twenty times the stock thickness.
- **C14 — cross-scale is relative to the drawings.** The 3D models of adjacent scales need not agree better than their 2D drawings do: for each viewpoint the cross-scale IoU limit is min(0.85, IoU of the two scales' 2D filled shapes − 0.05), and the boundary-distance limit is max(2 coarser features, the 2D drawings' own 95th-percentile distance + 1 coarser feature). The check prints the 2D pair's numbers beside the 3D pair's.
- **C15 — thermal area by scale.** `areaM2` is one physical value per region, while coarser scales merge and generalise masses. The exposed-area comparison is therefore per region within 15 % at 1:50 and 30 % at 1:100; at 1:200 and 1:500 every region must still have a solid, and only the sum of the exposed areas over all regions is compared with the sum of `areaM2` (within 30 %). `areaM2` is corrected at the root only from the 1:50 model.
- **C16 — exchange area is not envelope area.** For regions whose `areaM2` is a physical exchange area larger or other than the drawn envelope — thermal `model` `leaf` (leaf area from the leaf-area index), and kinds `foliage`, `fur`, `feather`, `hair` — the exposed-area comparison does not apply and `areaM2` MUST NOT be changed to the envelope area; the region only needs solids (coverage). For every other region an author may correct `areaM2` at the root only when the new value lies within a factor 1.5 of the old one; a larger discrepancy is not applied but listed in `T/area-review.md` (asset, region, old, measured, what the model shows) for the coordinator. Corrections already applied beyond that factor, and any correction of a C16 region, are reverted.
- **C16 note.** The 46 corrections of exchange-area regions found by `area-audit.md` were restored to the committed values by `area-revert.py`. The 89 corrections beyond a factor 1.5 on other regions stay applied and are listed in `area-review.md` as pending review against the final 1:50 models; new ones are appended there unapplied.
- **C17 — enclosing outlines.** An unfilled closed 2D outline whose interior the 3D body fills at least half counts as a filled shape in the boundary-distance measurement (it already does in the IoU through the `encloses` test), so a drawn hoop around slats is not a boundary the 3D lacks.
- **C18 — physiological areas.** Regions of thermal model `body` (skin, clothing, hair of a living subject) carry physiological surface areas tied to the subject's `BodyPhysiology`; their `areaM2` MUST NOT be changed from a 3D model. The exposed-area comparison for them is 30 % at 1:50 and 1:100 (clothing drapes and overlaps); a larger deviation is a finding about the model (buried or overlapping solids), to be fixed in the solids or reported. The 73 round-1 corrections of body-model regions were restored to the committed values by `area-revert-named.py`.
- **C19 — `areaBasis`.** `ThermalRegion` gains the optional field `areaBasis: "exchange"`: the region's `areaM2` is a physical exchange area that the drawn envelope cannot show (leaf area, fur and feather coats, a heat-recovery honeycomb, fins, a coil). Such a region is exempt from the exposed-area comparison, needs coverage only, and its `areaM2` is never changed from a model. Absent means the area is the envelope surface. The validator requires `areaBasis: "exchange"` on regions of model `leaf` and kinds foliage/fur/feather/hair (rule `thermal-area-basis`), which replaces the implicit list of C16; the catalog regions get the field written explicitly. The HRV fan `core` (0.9 m² wetted honeycomb, set to 0.075 by an author) is restored and marked `exchange`.
- **C20 — layer shells keep their thickness.** A thermal solid that carries `layer` is as thick as that layer's `thicknessM` and is exempt from `volumes-feature` (a 0.5 mm steel skin stays 0.5 mm at 1:50); coarser scales merge layers (B2), they do not thicken them.
- **C21 — regions smaller than the scale can show.** The per-region exposed-area comparison is skipped at a scale where the region's `areaM2` is below the surface of a cube of one smallest feature of that scale (6 × feature²: 3.4 cm² at 1:50, 13.5 cm² at 1:100): such a region (an eye, a beak, a cere, a hoof at a coarse scale) is drawn at the size floor and still needs its solid, and it counts in the total of C15.
- **C22 — envelope solids and thermal area.** A thermal solid that is (or references) an `envelope: true` solid stands for a porous lattice: it gives its region coverage, but its hull surface is not the region's surface, so it is left out of the exposed-area sums (per region and total) at that scale.
- **C23 — section views declare the retained side.** `ViewSection` gains the optional `retained: "far" | "near"`: which half of the body the section view shows, seen from the viewpoint (`far`: the half behind the plane, the conventional section; `near`: the half in front of it). Absent means `far`. A hollow part in a section view is drawn as a filled closed shape with its bore as a hole (2D guide).
- **C24 — slender solids against strokes.** In the silhouette and cross-scale comparisons a `slender: true` solid is drawn like its stroke: its projected centre line is widened to the pen width of the 2D shape it follows at that scale (the body pen where no shape is named), on the 3D side as on the 2D side. A slender part therefore stays at its true size in the model at every scale and is never thickened to match a pen-widened stroke; its thermal area stays the true one.
- **C25 — what `sectionFaces` may mark.** Only (a) a cut through a construction that continues beyond the sample (wall, slab, beam, duct run), and (b) a face that lies against a surface the asset is mounted on or built into (a wall plate's back, a panel glued to its core, a glass pane's face inside its sealed unit). A face that is in free air — a vehicle underside, the back of a free-standing panel, the inside of an open shell — is exposed surface and is never marked, whatever the declared `areaM2` says; if the declared value counts only one side, that is a question for the area reviewer.
- **C26 — porous masses.** Solids of an exchange-area region (`areaBasis: "exchange"`: foliage, fur, feathers, hair, fins) are porous envelopes: (a) they do not bury the surfaces of other regions in the exposed-area test (bark under a crown, skin under fur stay exposed); (b) every 2D shape bound to such a region is a soft outline in the silhouette distance (C8b limit), wherever its boundary lies relative to the 3D; (c) in `volumes-cross-scale` their union volume is compared with the soft tolerance (the band is the C8b distance, not one feature).
- **C27 — segment shares of a physiological surface.** For a living subject the *total* of its body-model `areaM2` is physiological data (DuBois for humans, Meeh-type allometry for animals) and stays fixed. How that total is divided among segments (head, ears, trunk, legs, hooves, hands) may be corrected by the area reviewer — never by an author — to the shares the honestly modelled 1:50 figure shows, when a segment deviates beyond the 30 % of C18 and the model is sound: the reviewer rescales the segment values so that their sum is unchanged, documents old and new shares, and checks `metabolicShare` and `skinFraction` stay consistent. Sums at 1:200 and 1:500 (C15) are taken over all regions of the subject that are compared.
- **C28 — contact fraction.** The thermal state of a pose already declares, per region, the share of its surface that is in contact with something (`contact.fraction`: a saddle under a rider, feet on the ground, a seat under a sitter, a wall-mounted back). The exposed-area comparison therefore compares the measured exposed area with `areaM2 × (1 − contact.fraction)` of `presentation.defaultPose` (fraction 0 where the pose declares none). Contact burial by another solid of the asset and the contact plane of C1 are how the model shows that fraction; a region whose model buries a surface with no declared contact, or exposes one the pose declares covered, is a finding about the model or about the pose's fraction, to be listed for the area reviewer.
- **C29 — `areaM2` is the total outer area; exposure belongs to the pose (coordinator's correction of C1 and C28, after the area reviewer read the solver).** The thermal solver takes `areaM2` as the region's *total* outer surface and applies the pose's `exposedFraction` and `contact.fraction` on top of it; for operating regions `heatW / areaM2` is the flux density that sets the temperature. Making `areaM2` the exposed-only surface (C1) counts the covering twice. Normative now:
  1. `volumes-thermal-coverage` compares `areaM2` with the **outer area of the union of the region's own outermost-layer solids**: faces buried by solids of the *same* region and `sectionFaces` (C25) do not count; faces covered by solids of *other* regions, by a rider, by the ground or by a wall do count. Tolerances as before (C15, C18, C21; exchange regions exempt, C19).
  2. The exposed share measured on the model (what C1 measured, with C26) divided by that outer area is compared with `exposedFraction × (1 − contact.fraction)` of `presentation.defaultPose` and reported as `exposureDeviation` — information for the area reviewers, who own the pose fractions; it does not fail an asset.
  3. `areaM2` values that authors reduced to the exposed-only surface are re-decided by the area reviewers against the total outer area (hand calculation of the real object). For operating regions `heatW` is the physical datum and stays; the flux density follows the honest outer area.
  C28 is superseded by item 2.
- **C30 — wall thickness of hollow parts.** `volumes-feature` judges the overall extents of a part, not the wall of a hollow one: a cup, can, tube, casing or shell modelled as a hollow revolve, extrude with a hole or shell loft keeps its real wall thickness (0.2 mm aluminium, 2 mm sheet steel) at 1:50 and 1:100; at 1:200 and 1:500 hollow parts whose cavity is below the smallest feature are modelled solid. End walls and lids of such shells follow the same rule.
- **Process note.** An author ran `git checkout` on an untracked file (no effect). The project rule stands for every agent: no git command that modifies the repository or the working tree, whatever the target.
- **C24 refined.** The width a slender solid is drawn with in the comparisons is the width of the 2D shape it follows at that scale and viewpoint: for a stroke the shape's own `penMm` (else the pen of its role); for a filled strip the strip's own width across the solid's axis; where the viewpoint draws no shape for it, its true projected width. The 2D shape is found by bone and by nearest centre line. A slender solid may be curved or closed (a hoop, a ring, a bent hose): slenderness is path length over the largest cross extent, measured along its centre line, not over its bounding box.
- **C21 refined.** A region below 6 × feature² at a scale is left out of the area *total* of C15 at that scale as well (both its `areaM2` and its measured area): it is drawn at the size floor, so its solid is larger than the region by construction (a dog's nose, tongue and pads at 1:500 alone made the total 2.66 against 0.3). It still needs its solid.
- **D1 — glyph size in 3D.** In the 3D model (`modelOf`, exports, viewer) glyph solids — force and reaction arrows, moment arcs, plumes, the centre-of-mass mark — are sized in model space as min(paper-millimetre size × denominator, a share of the asset's largest body extent: 0.35 for arrows and plumes, 0.06 for the centre-of-mass mark), so the body fills the frame at every scale. 2D sheets and the axonometric drawing keep pure paper-millimetre sizes.
- **C29 as measured (thermal maintainer, verified on ten assets against trimesh: laptop, stepladder, cantilever-balcony, etics-brick-wall-12, park-bench, sorption-wheel, standing-adult, labrador-dog, city-car, apple-tree; every region within 1 % at 1:50).** (a) Only closed meshes bury, with outward normals; (b) two solids of the same region that meet within a fifth of a smallest feature (`SEAM_FEATURES`, 1.5 mm at 1:50) count as touching along a seam and leave no surface, so a disc in a ring is the same area whatever the tessellation of the two (before: +14 % to +23 % for 48 or 32 against 64 segments); a real gap above that keeps both faces; (c) a hidden region is its full mesh area, nothing buried; (d) `sectionFaces` is honoured on a `{ of }` thermal reference (the body solid's own is not inherited); (e) emitter and envelope solids never count at any scale.
- **C25 (c) — the interior face of a conditioned layered plate.** The area of a region of thermal model `conditioned` is the outward faces (outer face and exposed edges): the face of its layered solid that looks at the conditioned interior is a face against a surface the asset is mounted on or built into in the sense of C25 b, and is marked `sectionFaces` on the thermal solid or its `{ of }` reference. The validator has no special case for conditioned regions: without that mark both faces of the slab count and the area reads twice the value (etics-brick-wall-12 `wall-field` 2.08 against 1.0, cantilever-balcony `wall-face` 2.28 against 1.1, `thermal-break` 0.688 against 0.44). Area reviewers and authors mark the interior face; the area reviewers' rule R-C and the validator then agree.
- **D2 — emitters are outlets, not floating discs.** A thermal emitter solid (B3) is the outlet patch on the surface where the flow leaves — nostrils or mouth for breath, the exhaust tip, the vent grille, a patch of the back or crown for a body plume or transpiration — flush with or slightly proud of that surface, never a disc hovering at an anchor in the air. The plume itself is a glyph the view generates (D1), as in 2D. Anchors of kind `emitter` that lie in the air are projected onto the nearest body surface along the emitter's direction for placing the outlet.
- **D3 — structure is never the body.** At every authored scale the `structure` list is its own load path: members along bones with structural sections, joints, supports, masses. A `{ of }` reference to a body solid is allowed only where that body solid IS the structural member (a table leg, a tube frame, a beam, a wall); a structure list that references or reproduces the whole body, or one solid block for it, is a defect (visual reviewers found it at 1:500 across humans, animals, vehicles and machinery). The validator maintainer proposes the measurable rule.
- **C31 — outer means reachable from outside.** The outer area of C29 counts the faces of a region's solids that the surrounding air can reach: faces bounding a cavity that is closed off by solids of the asset at that scale (the inside of a sealed hollow section, an enclosed casing, a closed vehicle cabin or tank) are not outer and do not count — determined by the validator (flood from outside through the scale's solids), not by markers. An open shell, an open tube end or a duct mouth lets the air in, and its inner faces count unless they are `sectionFaces` (C25). Authors close what is closed in reality (cap the tube, glaze the cabin) and leave open what is open; reviewers' "outward faces only" values for enclosed casings and conditioned cabins follow from this without `sectionFaces` on interior faces.
- **C31 as measured (2026-11-20, thermal maintainer; product `🧊️volumes/🌊️flood` and `outsideOf` in `🧊️volumes/🧮️measures`, tests `🧪️tests/🧊️volumes-flood` against hand values and a trimesh/scipy voxel flood).** (a) The walls of the flood are the triangles of the *thermal* solids of the scale (the thermal list is the thermal model), closed or open, without the porous solids of C26, envelope solids (C22) and emitters; body and structure solids close nothing, so what closes a cavity in reality (a cap, a gasket, a pane, a filter) is a thermal solid of some region. (b) The flood cell is a quarter of the smallest feature: a gap of at most a quarter feature lets no air in (two solids that meet along a seam are tight), an opening of at least half a feature does (in between it depends on where the grid falls; do not model openings in that band). For assets so large that the grid would exceed its budget the cell grows; the cell used is the measurement `info:floodCellM` of the scale. (c) A face sample is outer when the air reaches the probe in front of it, or (C29) when a solid of another region covers it — the probe lies inside that solid, or its surface is the first one within half a feature in front of the face — and that solid itself has surface the air reaches. A solid enclosed in a closed cavity covers nothing: the inner face of a kettle against its sealed water, a casing panel against an enclosed compressor do not count; skin under a shirt, a seat under a sitter, a tube end inside the cap of another region do. (d) Faces buried by solids of the same region (C29 a, b) and `sectionFaces` (C25) are left out as before; a face of the outermost layer that lies against an inner layer of its own region is not outer (no marker needed). (e) A `hidden` region is not flooded: it counts its full mesh area. A region that is not hidden and lies wholly in a closed cavity measures 0 and fails; declare it `hidden`. (f) `info:exposedAreaM2` applies the same test on top of the burial by other solids and the contact planes. (g) No stand-ins: a sealed hollow part is modelled hollow with its real wall and its real closure, and `sectionFaces` is not put on faces that merely look into a closed cavity.
- **E2 — contiguous bands.** Each preset stands for a promised denominator band (`SCALE_BANDS` / `$defs.ScaleBands`: 1:50 → 1:20…75, 1:100 → 75…150, 1:200 → 150…350, 1:500 → 350…1000), so an asset drawn at every preset promises 1:20…1:1000. The `validRange`s of the authored representations must cover the union of the bands of the authored presets without a hole (product rule `valid-range-gap` in `validateAsset`, `uncoveredDenominators`; vectors `S/🧫️fixtures/🕳️valid-range-coverage`, shapely oracle `S/🧪️tests/🕳️valid-range-coverage`). The boundary between two authored neighbours may move; the finest start and the coarsest end never shrink; an explicit `unavailable` preset promises nothing. A `range-end-*` print finding is fixed by redrawing the shapes (and their 3D solids at that scale) to the profile minimum at the band end, never by narrowing. Report: `r5-range-gaps.md`.
