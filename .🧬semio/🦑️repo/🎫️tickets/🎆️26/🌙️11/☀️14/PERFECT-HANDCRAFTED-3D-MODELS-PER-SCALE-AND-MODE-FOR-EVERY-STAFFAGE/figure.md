# Figure kit (`figure-*`): anatomical human bodies at every scale — manual v1

For every author of a `humans/*` asset. Run from `T` with the repo bun: `source author-33-env.sh` (sets `$BUN`). `<asset>` = `humans/<slug>`. Output of the tools: `T/🗑️generated/figure/`. The kit sits on `kit-lib.ts` and the validator (`volumes-check.ts`); it re-implements no limit.

## What it is

| file | what |
|---|---|
| `figure-lib.ts` | anatomical loft templates per body part, person parameters, drawing cuts, station fitting, skin-under-garment, layer shells |
| `figure-build.ts` | `bun figure-build.ts <asset> [--scale N] [--config path] [--write] [--refit]`: body, thermal, structure, decisions of one or all four scales; `--refit` (scales 200 and 500) refits the human solids to the scale's drawings with `author-24-kitfit.ts` |
| `figure-prop.ts` | `bun figure-prop.ts <asset> --scale N --bone b --shapes id,id --id solid --write`: replaces one prop solid (pillow, bag, duvet skirt) by a loft cut from the drawings |
| `figure-closeup.ts` | render of a subset of solids (`--ids regex`); the auto-fit keeps the whole-body scale |
| `figure/<slug>.json` | the per-asset config (below); optional, defaults are an average adult man |
| `figure-fails.sh <asset> [scale]` | only the failing measurements of the product validator |
| `figure-areas.py <slug> [scales]` | exposed area per region against `areaM2`, signed, per scale |
| `figure-extent.ts <asset> --scale N` | which body solid sets each world-axis extreme (bounds findings) |
| `figure-probe.ts <asset> --scale N` | bone frames and the drawn shapes grouped by bone and region |

## Per-asset config `T/figure/<slug>.json`

```json
{ "sex": "male", "age": 38, "build": 1, "pregnant": 0, "stoop": 0, "statureM": 1.75,
  "gap": { "coat": 0.006 }, "skip": ["bag"], "keep": ["headphone-cup-l"], "shoeSoleM": 0.022 }
```

- `sex` `male|female` (DIN 33402-2 P50 ratios), `age` in years (children: relatively larger head, fuller trunk, thinner limbs; over 60: thinner limbs, kyphosis), `build` 0 lean / 1 average / 2 heavy (soft tissue gain per part), `pregnant` 0..1 (abdominal bump on trunk and pelvis), `stoop` (extra kyphosis), `statureM` (default: (thigh + shank) / 0.491 from the rig, Drillis-Contini).
- `gap`: air gap in metres between the innermost clothing layer and the skin per thermal region (default 4 mm for coats, jackets, tops, 2 mm other clothing, 0 for tight layers).
- `skip`: bones the builder leaves alone; `keep`: existing body solids of human bones to keep (worn props such as headphones are kept automatically through their non-body thermal region).
- Props stay with the author: every body solid whose bone is not a human bone (`bag`, `phone`, `pillow`, `duvet`, ...), every thermal solid of a non-body region and the breath emitters of the existing lists are carried over unchanged and their structure members too. Rebuild after you moved a prop and the references still resolve.

## What the builder makes (and where it follows the drawings)

1. **Evidence.** For the scale it reads that scale's own closed 2D shapes in the default pose and groups them by (bone, thermal region). Shapes without a region (1:500 silhouettes) join the single body region of their bone, or the skin region of head, neck and hand. A bone with only a plan shape (the plan ellipses `waist`, `trunk`, `shoulders` are top views, not slices) gets a template-only solid.
2. **Stations.** Per part a section sequence at the template landmarks plus a cluster of stations near both ends (so a slanted hem or a shoulder slope survives) is cut from the drawing: sagittal extent from the elevation, lateral extent from front (plan for lying bones), centre offsets from both; the template gives the superellipse exponent, the sections' centre line is the drawing's. Greedy station selection (`selectStations`, tolerance 0.18 / 0.25 / 0.4 / 0.4 feature at 1:50 / 100 / 200 / 500) keeps what the outline needs.
3. **Anatomy table** (`TABLE` in `figure-lib.ts`, half breadth, half depth, anterior centre offset, exponent by fraction along the part): head chin to vertex (jaw, cheekbone, brow, cranium egg shape), neck (trapezius flare), torso waist to shoulder line (rib-cage section, shoulder girdle), pelvis crotch to iliac crest (gluteal depth, trochanter breadth), thigh, shank (tibial plateau, posterior calf belly, ankle), foot (heel, arch, ball), toe, upper arm (deltoid), forearm (extensor mass, pronation twist of the section from elbow to wrist), hand (wrist, palm, knuckle line, finger mass). Sources named in the file header: DIN 33402-2:2005, ISO 7250-1:2017, Dreyfuss "The Measure of Man and Woman" (2002), Winter (2009) with Drillis-Contini (1966) segment lengths and de Leva (1996) masses; long-bone shaft diameters after Gray's Anatomy. They are rounded P50 values tabulated at stature 1.75 m (male) / 1.62 m (female) and scaled by the asset's own stature; they decide shape and size only where the drawing says nothing.
4. **Garments over anatomy (1:50).** Each (bone, clothing region) becomes a garment loft cut from the drawing; if the garment runs beyond the anatomical range of the bone (a coat skirt hanging from the torso bone, a hood above the neck) the part outside becomes an extension loft (`-lo`, `-hi`); drawn cuffs, collars, hems, hoods, brims become their own lofts. Under every garment sits the skin loft of the part: the template for this person, clamped inside the garment's inner surface (outer minus the real layer thicknesses minus the air gap). Bare regions (face, neck, hands) are the skin lofts themselves; ears, nose, eyes, thumb capsule and four finger capsules are separate solids at 1:50; shoes are an upper and a sole (the upper overlaps the sole by 3 mm so no coincident faces); hair is its own shell.
5. **Thermal.** Skin and hair regions reference their body solids (`{ of }`); at 1:50 a clothing region with layers is nested shells (outermost layer references the garment, inner layers are the garment inset by the real `thicknessM` of the layers outside it, `shellLoft` keeps tapered ends closed); coarser scales reference the outer surface only (B2). A body region without any drawn shape at a scale (hair, scarf at 1:500) gets a thermal solid from the head/neck template. Emitters and prop regions are kept.
6. **Structure.** Member per human bone: a tube along the skin centre line with the real shaft diameter of that bone (femur 28-32 mm, tibia, humerus, radius+ulna pair, spine, pelvic ring, skull ellipsoid), clamped to 0.7 of the local limb section; ball joints at the rig joints (1:50 and 1:100), mass ellipsoids at the centre of mass of bones with at least 10 % of the body mass (boxes at 1:200 and 1:500). Everything stays inside the skin.
7. **Level of detail.** 1:50 full anatomy as above; 1:100 simplified (mitten hands, shoe with sole, no ears/nose/eyes, no skin under garments, fewer stations); 1:200 four to six stations per part and no details; 1:500 the same cut with the 1:500 outline (oblique-bone hull, as author 33's tool did). Every scale is cut from that scale's own drawings, so `volumes-copied` holds.

## Workflow for a human asset

1. Write `figure/<slug>.json` (sex, age, build, pregnancy, gap).
2. `$BUN figure-build.ts humans/<slug> --write` (all scales, a few seconds), `./figure-fails.sh humans/<slug>`, `python3 figure-areas.py <slug>`.
3. Render sheets: `$BUN volumes-render.ts humans/<slug>` and open every `🗑️generated/volumes-tools/render/humans/<slug>.s<N>.png`; for a closer look `--scale N --list body --width 1600 --az 60 --el 10`.
4. Hand-correct what looks wrong in the source (`T/volumes/humans/<slug>.json`) with your own script; a later `figure-build --write` of the same scale overwrites it, so build first, correct after, or keep corrections as a post-step script.
5. Splice with `volumes-insert.py` only when the check is green or every remaining failure is explained with numbers.

## Known limits and next steps for the kit

- Joint caps (ellipsoids at knee, elbow, shoulder) and a trapezius slope on the torso top would remove the ring seams and the flat shoulders; the table has the numbers, the builder does not yet emit the solids.
- Plan-only shapes of upright bones (`waist`, `trunk`, `shoulders`) are top views and are not used as slices; a bone with only such a shape gets a template-only solid clamped to the union of everything drawn at that station.
- Thermal exposure of hands, shoes and clothing under coats is what the geometry gives; the C18 limits cannot be met by an honest model for these regions, so the validator stays red on them until the coordinator rules (physiological `areaM2` against exposed envelope area).
- `figure-closeup.ts` renders a subset of solids but the auto-fit keeps the whole-body scale, so it is of little use for zooming; there is no image library on this machine to crop.

## v2.1 Anthropometry: what was checked and how

Scripts: `figure-audit.py` (rig and BodyPhysiology of the 28 catalog humans) and `figure-audit.ts` (the 1:50 drawings of the same 28). Statements used as independent checks:

1. **Rig stature against the catalog's own heights.** S = 0.08 (ankle height) + shank + thigh + pelvis + torso + neck + head bone lengths of each rig; against `dimensions.heightM` of the 19 upright assets S / heightM = 1.006, spread 0.015 (min 0.983, max 1.037, the child and jogger on the high side). The kit now takes this S as the default `statureM` (v1 used (thigh + shank) / 0.491, which overstates the rigs' stature by 2.6 %: the rigs have (thigh + shank) / S = 0.478).
2. **Segment length ratios** (rig bone length / S, 28 catalog humans; the sleeper is lying but the rig is pose independent): thigh 0.2415 (sd 0.008) against Winter / Drillis-Contini 0.245 (-1.4 %); shank 0.2369 against 0.246 (-3.7 %); upper arm 0.1824 against 0.186 (-1.9 %); forearm 0.1471 against 0.146 (+0.8 %); hand 0.1041 against 0.108 (-3.6 %); torso 0.260, neck 0.0546, pelvis 0.0555, head bone 0.1235 (bone from the neck top, not chin to vertex: no table value to compare; the head loft table range is cut from each drawing). Verdict: the table's lengths are not used directly (the loft ranges come from each asset's own rig), the ratios they were derived from agree with the catalog's rigs within 4 %. The two children (child-on-kick-scooter, running-child) are the outliers in torso and limb ratios, as expected (large head and trunk); they are left to `age` in the config.
3. **Breadths and depths against the drawings** (`figure-audit.ts`: for every closed 1:50 shape of a human bone, the maximum half breadth and half depth over 21 sections, divided by the table's maximum at that asset's stature; mean and sd over assets): bare head 0.98 / 1.01 (sd 0.06 / 0.07, n = 28: verified); bare thigh 0.87 / 0.93 (n = 8); bare upper arm 0.78 / 0.90 (n = 4); bare shank 0.92 / 0.82 (n = 9, sd 0.3); clothed thigh 0.90 / 1.04 (n = 59), clothed shank 1.10 / 1.06 (n = 49), clothed upper arm 0.90 / 1.02 (n = 34), clothed torso 0.89 / 0.90 (n = 29: the catalog's coats and hoodies are 338 mm wide on average against a table shoulder breadth of about 390 mm, a drawing convention that leaves out the deltoids), bare neck 0.83 / 0.90 (n = 18; the table's 0.07 m trapezius flare is the maximum), hand 0.77 breadth and 1.5 depth (n = 44: the catalog draws hands as 64 x 70 mm mittens, the table has a flat palm 86 x 48 mm; the drawing overrules the table at every scale, so this changes nothing in the models). Clothing adds 6 to 15 mm per side to the anatomy ratios, which is why the clothed rows sit near 1.
4. **Surface area against DuBois.** The kit's template (male 1.75 m, mitten hands, head without hair, pelvis and thigh overlapping at the hip) sums to 1.909 m2 against DuBois (70 kg, 175 cm) 1.848 m2 (+3 %); segment shares head 5.3 %, neck 1.8 %, trunk 21.6 %, pelvis 11.1 %, arms 15.4 %, hands 3.6 %, thighs 21.5 %, shanks 12.8 %, feet 6.9 % (rule of nines: head and neck 9 %, trunk 36 %, arms 18 %, hands 5 %, legs 36 %): the legs are about 15 % high and the trunk 9 % low because thigh and pelvis overlap at the hip (both count the gluteal volume). The catalog itself: sum of the body-model skin and clothing `areaM2` / DuBois(mass, rig stature) = 1.04 (sd 0.14, n = 28, min 0.70 max 1.44); skin region of the head 4.7 % of DuBois (sd 1.2 %), hands 4.6 % (sd 0.9 %), legs 19.8 % (n = 4).

**Verified** (against the catalog and DuBois): the stature chain, the thigh, upper-arm, forearm lengths, the head breadth and depth, the total body surface within 3 %. **Plausible, within the sd of the catalog, not independently derived**: the clothed limb and trunk breadths and depths (they include clothing and the authors' conventions). **Remain recollection** (no independent statement in the repo or derivable from it): the female / male ratios (`FEMALE`), the `BUILD_GAIN` and age factors, the anterior centre offsets and the exponents of every row, the long-bone shaft diameters of `BONE_R` and the joint ball radii (`JOINT_R`; Gray's Anatomy as remembered), the foot rows (heel / arch / ball), the neck flare. Where a drawing exists none of these decides a size; they decide the shape of the part between the drawn extents and of every skin under a garment.

## v2.2 What the builder does now (at 1:50 and 1:100 unless stated)

- **Joint caps** (`CAP` in `figure-build.ts`): a rounded ball end at the proximal end of knee (shank), elbow (forearm), wrist (hand), ankle (bare foot), shoulder (upper arm, deltoid, 4 % larger than the section), hip (thigh, skin only: the garments of the hip and thigh overlap each other) and at the distal end of the neck (neck-head). The cap is an ellipsoid with the section of the part at the joint plane, so in a straight limb it lies inside the neighbouring lofts and fills the wedge that two lofts leave on the outer side of a bent joint; it is a body solid, not a thermal one (a cap that is part of a garment region would add its whole surface to that region's outer area, C29, although it is inside the garment). The coat or the sleeve is cut from the drawing and the cap is cut from the same stations, so the garment drapes over it; no ring seam is left at knees, elbows, wrists, ankles and shoulders in the 3D sheets.
- **Garments close** at cuffs, hems and collars: a garment loft ends with its own closing cap, the skin loft passes through, so what shows at a hem is a ring of the real garment thickness (layers plus air gap); cuffs, collars, hems, hoods (also at 1:200), brims are own lofts. A bone that the garment does not cover over its whole anatomical range gets a bare skin piece (`<bone>-skin-bare0`) in the skin region of its segment.
- **Hands at 1:50**: palm loft, four fingers of two capsules each (proximal and distal phalanx, tapered, slightly spread and curled towards the palm), thumb capsule from the drawn thumb; **1:100** mitten (one loft with thumb). The finger reach follows the longest drawn range of the hand in any view (`rangeUnion`), and in adoption the reach of the existing hand solid.
- **Feet**: the skin foot is the heel / arch / ball template clamped inside the shoe, the shoe is upper plus sole (upper overlaps the sole by 3 mm), the toe bone has its own skin loft inside the toe box; heel, arch and toe box are not separately visible in any render (inside the shoe), which is where they belong.
- **Head**: skin lofts jaw to vertex; the part above 50 % of the head height is a separate `head-scalp` solid (under the hair, no thermal binding: the declared `areaM2` of the face-and-neck regions does not contain the scalp, the hair region declares the hair-covered surface), the `face-skin` outer area is then +23 % against the declared value instead of +69 %; ears, nose and eyes as before; hair is a loft whose stations are finer (14, tolerance 0.2 feature) and a ponytail, braid or bun outline is its own curved loft along its principal axis (`ribbonStations`), which removes the loop mismatch of the standing-phone-user at 1:200 (p95 soft 67.5 mm against 60).
- **Bones without a closed drawn shape** (the jogger's upper arms: strokes only) get a template skin loft clamped to everything drawn, in the skin region of their segment.
- **Stature** default from the rig chain (v2.1).

## v2.5 Adoption of an authored human: `figure-build.ts <asset> --from-existing`

`bun figure-build.ts humans/<slug> --from-existing [--scale N] [--write]` (or `./figure-adopt.sh <slug> [scales]`) builds the kit's figure of the scale and merges it into the existing source: **kept** are the existing props (non-human bones), every body solid that a clothing or surface region references (the garments, shoes, their layer shells), unreferenced non-skin solids (buttons, hems), hair (when the source has hair regions) and all earlier decisions; **replaced** are the solids of skin regions per (bone, region) when the kit builds that bone (head, neck, hands, bare limbs, ears, nose, eyes, thumbs, fingers), the skin under garments, and all human structure (tube members, ball joints, mass ellipsoids); **added** are the joint caps over the kept garments and a decision line in both languages. A skin region whose bone the kit did not rebuild keeps its solids; `"keepRegions": ["leg-skin"]` in `figure/<slug>.json` keeps a region's existing anatomy on purpose; dangling thermal references are dropped and reported. Properties: every solid the adoption adds carries the tag `figure-kit`, so a second run drops the first run's anatomy and caps and builds them again (checked on jogger and skateboarder: identical counts and measurements); the existing thermal layer shells stay.

Proof (the asset's own previous source and the product validator with C29, which went live during the work; before = the source as found, after = adopted):

## v2.6 Known limits

- Shoulder slope of the coat and the shirt is the drawing's; the trapezius slope of the template is not emitted as a solid.
- Hip caps only for bare thighs; the hip junction of garments is the overlap of the pelvis and thigh lofts.
- Mouth and lips, toe separation, nails, the thenar eminence of the thumb are not modelled.
- Adoption keeps an existing garment as it is: a garment loft that follows its drawing less well than the kit's would is not improved, and a skin under it that the kit clamps to the kit's own garment stations can poke out of a thinner existing garment (none seen in the two proofs: the silhouette and burial rules are green).
