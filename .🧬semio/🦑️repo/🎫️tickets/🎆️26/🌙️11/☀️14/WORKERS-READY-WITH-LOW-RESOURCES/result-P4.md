Everything is prepared and tested against the live modules through an overlay; the burst is `T/🧰️scripts/P4/land.py` (T = `…/☀️13/FLEET-ONE-MACHINE-SHARED-HOT-CACHE-AND-REMOTE-EXECUTION/`). No `repo`, graph, build or cargo command was run, nothing was killed; the live read was only observed (`ps`, `footprint`, `sample`, ledger and generation files, read only).

## Verdicts

1. **The farm IS used; the "13 GB" is mostly resident pages, not physical footprint.** Parent `bun --eval` 46564 (started 16:59:42): resident 11.5 to 15.2 GB, physical footprint 4.2 to 5.4 GB at 17:11 to 17:14 (`footprint`: 4 459 MB dirty, 7 929 MB reclaimable), 5.6 to 7.7 GB at 17:18 to 17:20. A single `ps` at 17:10 can see "one child": the pool is created per wave, lives 35 to 60 s and is closed before the parent publishes (sampled every 2 s for 3 minutes: 6 to 11 child processes in 62 % of the 90 samples, 1 or 2 (the `repo-watch` child) in the rest; every pool had 5 to 8 workers, worker resident at most 1.57 GB).
2. **The parent is not bounded, and in the first wave after a (re)start it explodes.** Fresh read 59395 (started 17:23:39): footprint 0.8 to 1.4 GB through the 3.8 minutes of verification, then in the first farm wave (7 workers from 17:27:33) **1.1 GB at 17:27:49, 15 GB at 17:28:57, 26 GB at 17:29:44** (resident only 3 to 14 GB: the system compressed the rest), 5.2 GB at 17:30:10 when the wave was published; following waves 4.0 to 6.1 GB. Over all 135 footprint samples of the old and the new read: median 6.4 GB, maximum 26.6 GB, 2 % of the samples below 4 GB. The old read showed the same 17 to 26 GB between 17:16:56 and 17:17:54 (wave 128, 171 s instead of 60 s).
3. **Progress is real and durable, but a restart costs 5 to 9 minutes before the first wave.** Generations 122 to 140 were published between 17:08:47 and 17:42:41 (48 projects per wave, 44 to 305 s, median about 100 s); no wave is redone after a restart (owner index ranges 2-60, 62-165, 166-222, 225-279, … 440-487 in the order of the sorted stale set). The first wave after a restart took 7 min 53 s (16:47:08 to 16:55:01), 9 min 8 s (16:59:39 to 17:08:47) and 4 min 57 s (17:23:39 to 17:28:36, steered by the workers' priority list). Nine landings restarted the refresh today (the agent restarted again at 17:47:49).
4. **Grants hold.** Ledger at 17:14:27: `projects` (show, rebuild phase) reserved 14 889 MB, observed 5 015 to 5 219 MB, ramp 21 600 000 ms; `graph-farm` (exec) 5 jobs, 7 680 MB, holder = the show grant, admitted in the same millisecond as its request (the queue entry arrived 23 ms after the grant): the P2/V reservation lending works live, `exec` does not wait.
5. **Cause of the unbounded parent (in code, with numbers).** (a) `project-nodes/🟨️.mjs` `collectResident` compared `process.memoryUsage().rss` with a threshold that was raised to `1.5 * rss` after every collection: with 12 to 15 GB resident the collector fired once per process and then never again inside the waves. (b) The memory governor (`🧠️memory`, `createGraphParentMemoryGovernorV1`) admits a project by the host's available memory (`serviceMemoryHeadroomBytesV1`: total minus used minus 10 %), never by what the parent itself holds, and its learned peak per kind is almost nothing: `np/owner-peaks.json` of the live selection holds `language:cargo` [0, 0, 0, 25, 0, 0, 2, 0] MB, `language:typescript` [53, 0, 0, 0, 15, 15, 0, 15], `project` [2, 2, 2, 0, …], `root:language:cargo` [117, 74, 281] — so all 8 parent loops (`projectConcurrencyV1` = min(8, cores)) run at once, each doing the parent half of a project (assembly authority, record lookup, adoption) beside the workers. In a synthetic process with the same shape (8 loops, projects of about 200 MB live) the unbounded variant reached **8 concurrent owners, 3 021 MB managed, 4 908 MB footprint** (macOS `footprint`), i.e. the footprint is about 1.6 to 2.1 times the managed bytes (`heapUsed + external`). What is NOT proven: which structure holds the 4 to 5.6 GB floor between waves (no heap profile is possible; the wave row below now names the surviving managed bytes and the projects at the peak).
6. **Fix (prepared, tested, not landed):** the parent gets a declared growth budget and a collector that follows the managed bytes; the refresh logs one line per wave.

## What was built (all in `LIB/🏗️build/`, schema first, EN and DE)

- `🧠️memory`: policy keys `parentManagedMegabytes` 1536, `collectEveryProjects` 2, `collectAboveMegabytes` 768, `collectGrowthMegabytes` 384, `collectMinimumIntervalMilliseconds` 3000 (schema, `.d.mts`, fixture). `graphParentManagedBytesV1(env)` (declared budget, smaller when the grant leaves the parent less, never below the governor minimum). `createGraphParentMemoryGovernorV1` takes `budgetBytes`, `baselineBytes`, `ports`: headroom = min(host headroom, budget - (managed - baseline)), so the learned peaks become real and a parent that starts with a large resident state is not held to one project (growth, not absolute). `createParentCollectorV1`: after every 2nd finished project it reads the managed bytes (constant time) and runs one full collection above the trigger (768 MB, then 384 MB above what survived, never twice within 3 s); `finish()` collects at the end of the inference and answers peak, surviving bytes and collections.
- `🕸️graph/📥️inference/📦️project-nodes/🟨️.mjs`: uses both (the rss ratchet is gone), reports the managed bytes with the running projects to the meter at every tick and the collector at the end. `🏊️project-farm/🟨️.mjs`: `close()` reports workers, projects, adopted, failed, lost (jobs), declined to the meter, also when no pool ever started.
- New module `🕸️graph/📈️wave-row` (schema `Row`, `Texts`, `Policy`): `createGraphWaveMeterV1`, `renderGraphWaveRowV1` / `parseGraphWaveRowV1` (strict keys), `announceGraphWaveV1` (reads the footprint of the read process tree with `observeTreeMegabytesV1`, 5 s limit, null when unreadable, never fails a build), `renderGraphWaveTextV1`. `🕰️generations/🟨️.mjs` `build()` passes `{done,total}` with every published generation, `🔌️load` and `🕰️host/🟦️.ts` hand it on; the host writes `[graph-wave] {json}` to stderr right after `seams.published`, only when `SEMIO_GRAPH_WAVE_ROWS=1`.
- Server: `createGraphReadWatchV1` takes `onWave` (a row is progress and moves the newest generation); `inferGraphReadV1` starts the read with `SEMIO_GRAPH_WAVE_ROWS=1`, writes each row as one log line via `say` (`runGraph` passes `context.write`) and excludes rows from the failure detail. Example (EN, from the test): `graph-server: wave 128 published: 96 of 4700 projects, 87 s since the last, parent 4210 MB (managed 410 MB after the collection, peak 900 MB at hub-a, hub-b, 3 collections), farm: 45 of 48 projects adopted, 2 failed, 1 lost, 5 workers`; DE: `graph-server: Welle 128 veröffentlicht: …`.
