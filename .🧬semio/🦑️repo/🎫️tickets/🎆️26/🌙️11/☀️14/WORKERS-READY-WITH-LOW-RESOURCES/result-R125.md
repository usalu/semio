# R125: why the largest machine computes with one child, and the capacity accounting that fixes it (2026-11-17)

Scripts: `T/🧰️scripts/R125/` (T = `☀️13/FLEET-ONE-MACHINE-SHARED-HOT-CACHE-AND-REMOTE-EXECUTION`): `patch.py`, `patch2.py`, `patch-farm.py` (staged edits), `overlay.ts` + `run-staged.sh` + `stage-tests.mjs` (run any test against the staged files), `battery.sh`, `compare.py`, `land.mjs`, `restore.mjs`, `window.sh` (landing), `live.ts` (the governor's decision on the live host and ledger).

## 1. Where the numbers come from

- **14336 MB** is not authored anywhere: it is the learned size of the class `show:projects`. `estimates.json` holds 16 samples of the old read whose parent inferred inline (peaks 4563 to 14243 MB); `hostClassBoundV1` takes the decayed 90th percentile plus 25 percent, and the policy had no declared ceiling for `show` or `graph`. R122 moved all owner work into children (parent 2 to 3.5 GB) but the store still says 14 GB for days (half life 3 days).
- **"15414 available"** is the real reading: `parseDarwinCountersV1` takes free pages plus the larger of (inactive + speculative) and (file-backed + purgeable) pages. The live read itself, its children, and the other agents' processes are already out of that number (17198 MB at 10:36, 16560 MB at 10:42, of 32768 MB). The 27 GB of "memory_pressure" is the same machine with the inactive anonymous pages counted as free; the governor is right to be more careful than that.
- **Why 15414 turned into -2260 spare**: `decideHostCapacityV1` subtracts, besides the floor (4096 MB), the *unrealized* part of every ramping grant: reservation minus what its tree holds. The read's grant had reserved 14336 MB, held about 1900 MB and (a learned ramp of up to 6 hours: live grant `rampMilliseconds 21600000`) counted as ramping all that time: 12461 MB "reserved but not resident" were taken from the room. The live ledger at 10:36 before this change: headroom 641 MB, a farm ask for 9 children answered `admit` with 1 job (reason `lent`).
- Second limiter: with the host load at 40 to 60 (other agents' processes) every elastic farm ask was cut to its minimum job by the load rule, so the farm grew one child per ask.

## 2. What changed (landed 2026-11-17 11:30:00 to 11:32:27 UTC under `landing.lock`; a first attempt at 11:00 was restored because my compare table expected 5 tests in the new law file, it has 6)

1. A reservation is what a job needs: policy `show` and `graph` start at 3072 MB (their minimum) instead of 5120; new declared ceiling `read-parent` (`^(show|graph)(:|$)`, 4096 MB) so a learned peak of the old inline inference can never lift the read above four gigabytes; what the parent really holds beyond that is counted by its observed footprint (`max(reserved, observed)`).
2. A reservation is released to what the job uses: `keptHostMegabytesV1` (decision module): a ramping grant that held its slot 30 s while using under 25 percent of its reservation is idle; it is then charged `max(3072 MB, 150 percent of what it holds)` and never more than it reserved (policy `memory.rampIdleKeepMegabytes`, `rampIdleKeepBasisPoints`, both optional: without them the old rule). Used for the unrealized part, the committed memory, the lend of holders and the blockers. The live read (reserved 14336, held 1943) is charged 3072 + nothing more.
3. Not double counted: what a process already holds is out of `available` and charged by its observed footprint only; only the reserved-but-not-yet-resident rest is subtracted (this was already the formula; the fix is the size of the reservation and the idle release). Farm children of a run (`elastic`, `holder` live in the ledger) are no longer cut by the foreign load of the host: their width is cores minus one while the memory allows; the hard stops (pressure, swap) stay.
4. Farm children are booked at what children measure: `askWorkerMegabytesV1` (project farm): the declared megabytes until three children reported their footprint, then the 75th percentile plus 15 percent, within 768 MB and the declared size; used by every later ask of the farm.
5. The waiting line shows the arithmetic and the shortage (EN and DE): `hostWaitingLineV1`: `{spare} MB spare = {available} MB available - {floor} MB floor - {held} MB reserved but not yet resident - {taken} MB admitted ahead, short by {n} MB; load ...; behind <who holds it>` (new message `waitingShort`).
