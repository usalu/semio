# Targeted Graph Delta After a Missing Closure

The isolated causal harness places the same missing source-closure token in two of twelve owner fragments and changes no source facts when the owner memo is purged. The local and non-strict restored paths compute all twelve owners after quarantine. An ordinary `refresh` cannot reuse the base because `invalidate` writes `manifest: null` and `plan` takes the cold branch. Passing the parent through the existing `current` argument would let readers, coverage and merge code treat an archived or unhealthy manifest as the current seal.

A viable follow-up keeps the publication base separate from the fact and fragment reuse base.

1. Resolve the exact namespace and digest against the observed generation. A bounded streaming scan of its content-addressed fragments must show the full owning set. The expansion error's project array can cover only the chunk that failed. A reference from the global fragment, unclassified ownership, or unusable owner or fact metadata takes the existing full rebuild path. A verified refetch does not require quarantine.
2. Under the structural claim, write a compare-and-swapped tombstone with a schema-described repair intent. Its `manifest` stays null. The intent binds the missing leaves and affected owners to the original parent digest and sequence. Normal local reads and remote publication continue to refuse the tombstone. The archived parent is opened only for builders as a verified immutable patch source and remains retained while the tombstone references it.
3. Extend the engine's planning input with forced dirty owners and a repair base distinct from the expected pointer. Run ordinary frontier and fact verification against the immutable base and union its normal dirty owners with the verified affected set. A missing base, fact index, or frontier still plans cold. The generation number of the replacement and final pointer precondition come from the live tombstone. Normal delta fragment and streaming fact-index patching can then carry unaffected data by digest. The journal must include the tombstone identity and repair intent, and no pre-repair coverage or resumed result may satisfy the repair.
4. Qualify replacement and global closure references before journal restart or publication can seal a replacement. If a regenerated fragment still names an unavailable leaf, keep the tombstone or use the existing bounded fallback. Compare the final publish against the exact tombstone under live structural custody. On conflict, re-read the winner without merge or reinterpretation of the old archived parent. Claim cancellation must still be checked just before the swap.
5. Extend the two-owner fixture to require exactly two full derivations, identical untouched fragment digests and retained fact rows, and an absent current head while the tombstone is visible. Add missing-global, reference outside the failed expansion chunk, lost base, cancellation, peer publication and journal-resume cases.
