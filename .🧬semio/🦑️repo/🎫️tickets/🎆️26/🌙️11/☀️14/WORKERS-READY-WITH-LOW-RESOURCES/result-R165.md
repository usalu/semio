* Why a closure-only walk is NOT a pure walk restriction: `readJavascriptDependencySourceOwnersV1` needs the root scope `.`, derives each member's deepest owning scope among the LISTED scopes, and throws `Deepest Source owner must actually admit package` / `Exact declared Source owner edge required` if an owner scope is missing. The required set is the transitive reach over owner edges, which comes from walking each scope's members. A correct closure walk is a breadth-first walk over scopes (walk scope X, take the owner scopes of its members, walk those) with the declared scope names (cheap top-level listing) known up front. The producer then also uses the custody scopes for preservation sources, singletons and borrowed providers, so shrinking the custody changes what is preserved or retired. NOT built: needs the storage battery (`💾️storage/🧪️tests/*`), which needs the full workspace toolchain I could not stage. For the `repo-mcp` closure (inside `🧰️framework`) the expected walk is 29.6k of 98k directories (3.3x less), arithmetic from the index, not measured.
* The live store has NO installed-ledger evidence: all 6 `*.installed.json` receipts reference `…installed.ndjson` files that do not exist in `.cache/b/d/m/generation` (the other evidence kinds exist). With it missing, `verifyJavascriptDependencyInstalledReceiptV1` returns undefined and the gate answers stale `installed:<scope>` on this checkout. Cause not found (a sweep of evidence or a crashed seal); worth an owner's look. The workers' logs do show gate progress counters, so their ledgers exist.
* sync-gate wall: counters 7,328 / 7,360 / 7,392 at 18 s in g1-m01/m03/m08: one generation of about 7.4k directories takes 18 s, i.e. 2.4 ms per directory, because every directory was one `fs/promises` call through the thread pool.
