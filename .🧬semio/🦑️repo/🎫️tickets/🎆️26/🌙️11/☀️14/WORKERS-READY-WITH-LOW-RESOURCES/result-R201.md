Facts that explain the numbers:
- The 14 336 MB is not a constant of the 16 GB era. It is the learned class estimate: `estimates.json` class `show:projects` holds samples with peaks of 14 243, 12 808, 8 941, 8 370 MB (tree peaks of earlier reads on bigger machines or with the farm full) and the ramp rule counts the estimate in full for the 6 h `rampMilliseconds` of a rebuild grant. The class ceiling `read-parent` (4 096 MB) bounds a read only while the class has no measured peak.
- The three farm grants are measured by a stub: `requestBuildFarmCapacityV1` passes `observe: () => Promise.resolve(0)` ("0 MB observed" in the status line, since 6 354 s). That is correct by design (the memory of a child sits in the tree of the read; measuring it would count it twice in `unmanagedHostMegabytesV1`), and after their 60 s start ramp they cost the gate nothing. So the 26.6 GB is a nominal sum; the number that matters is ONE: `unrealized` of the `show` grant.
- Arithmetic of the status line (10:03): available 15 134 MB - floor 4 096 MB - unrealized (14 336 - 6 039 = 8 297 MB) = 2 741 MB spare (printed 3 172 at another sample). At 10:12 the same formula gives 4 801 and 5 586 MB spare. The farm (`farmHeldV1`) does NOT work from this book: it compares the tree footprint of the read with the hard share of the plan grant of its own process (`gate.grantBytes()`), so the 5 unstarted children of 09:14 were not held by the booking. They are all started now: the pools report `children busy 1 / 1 / 6 / 1`, `not started 0`, 8 to 14 jobs queued.
- Estimates that are never corrected by measurement: (a) the `show` reservation after the learned ramp; (b) the child grants' `perJobMegabytes` 1 536 vs 2.5 to 2.8 GB resident (about 0.6 to 1.1 GB footprint) of the children, which is harmless because they are charged 0 after the ramp and their memory is inside the `show` observation.

Who suffers: everything that asks the gate while the read runs (every `repo` command, the machine's own pool, the lab) sees `headroom` = available - floor - 8.3 GB and waits or is shrunk. The read itself is not the victim.

## 2. Fix by construction (staged and verified by model; LANDED 10:30:53 to 10:31:27 UTC, green: process dirs 12, library farm 6 / agents 2 / farm-width 7 / label 2, main 28, 0 fail; entry in landings.md)

Rule (policy keys `memory.settledAfterMilliseconds` 900 000 and `memory.settledKeepBasisPoints` 15 000, both optional, schema first): a grant that has run `settledAfterMilliseconds` since it was admitted or resumed and has been measured is charged `settledKeepBasisPoints` of what it holds (never under `rampIdleKeepMegabytes` 3 072, never more than it reserved), re-read at every 15 s heartbeat. In `keptHostMegabytesV1` (one added branch before the idle branch); a read observed at 4.3 GB is booked 6 452 MB, at 6.0 GB 9 059 MB; the estimate still counts in full for the first 15 minutes (above the learned time to peak 690 s; 600 s was tried first and the live law `🧮️farm-width` "busy one in full" at 9000 MB after 600 s turned the first landing try at 10:30:01 red, restored exactly) and for a grant never observed.
- Why this does not oversubscribe when footprints grow: the booking is never below the measurement (`committedOf` is max(kept, observed) while ramping), the physical side of the decision (`available - floor - unrealized`) is unchanged, so memory that is really used is never offered twice; the extra 50 percent is the derived headroom for growth between two heartbeats; above the reservation the grant stays at the old rule (observed counts).
- Law `host-capacity/🧪️tests/🧱️settled-read` (5 tests, real decision functions with this machine's numbers: 32 GB, 10 cores, 15 134 MB available, the live show grant and the three farm grants): (1) booked 14 336 without the rule (the live state), 6 452 for 4 301 MB observed with it, 14 336 inside the settle time, 14 336 unobserved, capped at the reservation; (2) spare 2 741 MB before, 8 018 MB after at 6 039 MB observed; (3) growth sweep 4 301 to 16 000 MB observed with available memory shrinking in step: booking covers the measurement, the room equals min(available side, budget side), and a request larger than the room is never admitted in full; (4) farm children cost min(reserved, 512 per job) in their start ramp; (5) the live `policy.json` carries both keys. RED before the code: `Closed host capacity Policy required: /memory/settledAfterMilliseconds is not allowed`; green after (5 pass, 33 expects). The independent check is plain arithmetic in the test (`available - floor - (kept - observed)`), the decision SQLite oracle of the main test does not model the idle or settled branches (the same as for the idle rule).
- Staged (scratch mirror with the real modules): the new law 5/5, lend 7, quiet 5, phases 13, followup 11, essential 6, upgrade 10, gate 31, unmanaged 4, partial-start 2, estimates 15, available 12: 0 fail. Live at base (before the change): library farm 6, agents 2, farm-width 7, label 2, main 28: 0 fail. `land.sh` rehearsed on the mirror: forced red restores exactly, a changed base writes nothing (exit 2), a missing test directory restores.
- What the NEXT command sees: every new `repo` process evaluates the gate with the code on disk, so the spare appears at once for all new commands (within one heartbeat of the read, 15 s). The running read, the pool roles and the resident sidecar keep the old function until they restart. The `show` grant of the running read is evaluated by whoever asks, so the effect is machine-wide as soon as the new asker is a new process.

- Therefore more children would not help; the lever is outside the build tooling (the desktop app polls git without pause; the slice lab of R197 shares the CPU). Owner of both: the person at the machine; not touched.

So it already "uses idle cores and competes fairly for busy ones"; at 0 % idle there is nothing to use.

## 4. Release of long owners to the fleet: what exists, what is missing

- The fleet already works the whole wave: pools publish 22 to 60 records per minute for this read's wave (`b42af294e180` at 10:12: claimed 316, published 274, 12 children, 28 per minute; `ver` = fleet, all origins pulling). The long Rust owners (2 170 s and more locally) are therefore also computed by pools, and their records are in the shared store.
- The missing half is on the reader: the running read has 0 fetched (old take poll, 90 inferred here); the parent claims each owner at its cursor and keeps it in a lane for the whole run (27 running lanes, 14 parked for a child). R194's published-names poll (landed 10:00) is the fetch half and is only in effect for the next read. Pools of other machines write the names lists already: 40 `pool-published-<wave12>.txt` files, 596 distinct names at 10:12 (waves 88bb 591, abd2 562, 97c7 547, 828d 277); the wave of the running read has only 1 name listed (its pool children started before the landing).
- NOT built (no time in the window): the symmetric release (an owner locally running longer than a derived bound is offered to the fleet and the first result wins, the loser is cancelled). Design for the next window, sites found: `project-nodes/🟨️.mjs` line 657-664 (`askUnits`, `ownerPool.ask(..., renew)`) already publishes asks for pending owners; what is missing is (a) `farm.run(job,{signal})` abort of the lane's job when `fleetPort` reports the record (the abort path exists in `🏊️pool` line 405), (b) the bound = N x the running median of finished owners, (c) the simulated-machines law (local job slow, remote pool idle, record appears in the shared store: local job aborted, the record adopted once, published once). With the published-names poll in the next read the fetch half makes most of this unnecessary: an owner the fleet has stored is taken before its lane starts.

## 6. After the landing (10:31 UTC) and recommendation

Measured live with the new code: `repo capacity status` 10:31:40 reports 13 497 MB available, SPARE 6 470 MB (was 1 607 MB at 09:44, 3 172 at 10:03) with the same grants; owners 118 of 4785 at 10:31 (28 owners in 19 minutes = 1.5 per minute, helped by lower load 31.6 after the lab/other work eased; 4 667 left = about 52 hours at that rate). The running read, the pool roles and the sidecar still run the old function; the effect so far is for every new `repo` process.

Recommendation: RESTART the read, but only after (a) the pool roles and the graph role run the 10:00 and 10:30 code and (b) nobody else is about to land (next windows 11:00, 11:30). Reasons: (1) the running read cannot use anything landed (old take poll: 0 fetched after 2.3 h; the lanes sit at the cursor and 267 of the last 690 parked lines wait for a child to VERIFY a stored record, i.e. the store already holds those owners); (2) the fleet keeps publishing (about 28 per minute on its wave, 596 names in the published lists already) so a read started now finds most owners present and takes the polled ones first; (3) the restart costs the priming step (20 to 24 min) and 10 minutes by design, both paid once. Expected effect of a restart is not measured; I have not run it.
The remaining known per-read levers, not built: cheap jobs (look and verify) are queued FIFO behind 25 to 45 minute Rust owners in each pool (`🏊️pool` `queue.push`, no priority; 1-child pools block two queued jobs for the whole run); a pool choice that counts a long-running child as blocked; the symmetric release of section 4.
