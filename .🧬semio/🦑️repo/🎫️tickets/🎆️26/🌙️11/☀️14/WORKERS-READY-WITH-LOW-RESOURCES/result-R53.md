## 2. What was built (wave 1, landed)

Files (all under `LIB/🏗️build/🕸️graph/🧮️derived-nodes/🧩️closure-kinds/`):
- `🧭️row-paths/🟨️.mjs`: `rowPathsV1` returns only `paths`, `dirs`, `outside`, `volatile`. A bare resolution is an **outside row**: no path list can bound what a package resolver reads (install trees, manifests, compiler configuration, links), so it is not guessed, it is **asked again** (re-executed through the host `hold`) once per start, in bulk for all parts that hold it. An owner row names its registry file (`📋️build-owners.json` of the row's own root) as a precise path. New `belowRootsV1` and the **subtree rule**: a row whose path lies below a directory the journal reports as added, removed or renamed is touched (see 4, this was a real missed change). The two global flags `configs` and `modules`, the `bare` ancestor index and the `config` part marks are gone.
- `📒️manifest/🟨️.mjs`: the inverted index is `paths` + `dirs` + `volatile` + `outside`; no `config`, `bareAny`, `bare`. `reachedKeysV1` finds index keys named by the journal or below a changed subtree root, for the affected-part bitset and for the vouch. Head version 4 (a version 3 head reads as an empty manifest, never trusted).
- `🟨️.mjs` (settler): part metas are version 2 (a stored version 1 meta is rebuilt from the part rows), summary trace shows `subtrees:`, the result carries `slow` (closures that were asked) and `farmed`.
- `🕸️graph/📥️inference/🧭️script-inputs/🟨️.mjs`: `partReachV1` (the per-pass reuse check of the walkers) uses the same row model, anchored patch.

Behaviour now: a dirty set marks exactly the parts whose rows name a changed path/probed directory/below a moved subtree (plus volatile and unwatched), the outside rows are re-executed (15 rows in the real store), every other closure is answered from the manifest, no row of it is checked.

## 3. The parallel walk (wave 2, landed)

`🏭️walk-farm/` (schema first: `🧬️schema/🔣️.json` = policy + the wire messages `Ready`, `Request`, `Answer`, `Failure`, `Recycle`; `🟨️.mjs` the parent; `👷️child/🟨️.mjs` the child). After 48 in-process derivations of a settle (a cold pass or the fallback), children are started (`warm`), sized `min(8, granted cpus - 1, floor((granted memory - reserve) / 2 GiB))`, below 2 there is none; the host load is deliberately not subtracted (the walk is syscall-bound, children hide latency). From then on every question is walked by a child (JSON lines over stdin/stdout, one question at a time per child, child ends on stdin close so none outlives the parent, recycles itself above 2 GiB). The pool width of the settler grows to the farm size, flushing per chunk of 256 stays. Any farm problem (child dies, start timeout, question failure, cancellation) makes the parent walk that question itself; 3 failures stop the farm for the run. `SEMIO_GRAPH_WALK_FARM=off` switches it off. Not used by the incremental path (derivations stay under 48).

Real parity check (`realfarm.mjs`, real deriver in a child against the in-process deriver, same tree): 4 real `walk` questions with 102 395, 24 876, 24 502 and 17 915 rows: fact equal and **all part rows equal**; the farm answered the three warm ones in 96, 43, 54 ms (in-process 823, 419, 378 ms), the first in a cold child in 15 s (in-process 6.6 s). 3 `derive` questions equal; questions that fail in-process (old root) fail identically in the child.

## Follow-up 1: the live read at 20 closures per minute (read 12801, eval child 12887)

Observed live (not killed): farm engaged with 8 children, children 0 to 1.6 % CPU (32 s CPU in 22 min), the PARENT at 90 % CPU, 6 to 8 GB footprint, later 13 GB RSS and 55 M objects (35 M strings), loop lag up to 21 s. So the farm was engaged and idle; the parent was the bottleneck. Cause (my bug, wave 2): every answer carried ALL rows of the closure (up to 100 000 rows, about 10 MB of JSON) although nearly all parts are shared by thousands of closures, and the line reader rescanned the growing buffer for each 64 KB chunk (quadratic). 7 392 closures times 10 MB parsed and interned on one thread. The in-process walkImportsV1 stacks in the starved samples are the first 48 derivations and the closures that waited for children that were still booting, not the farm path. Two of the 8 children never became ready within 42 minutes (37 MB resident); I could not reproduce that standalone (child ready in 0.6 to 1.5 s) and did not find the cause: the new farm kills a child that is not ready after 180 s, replaces it on demand and stops after 3 failed starts (the parent then walks itself).

Fix (wave 3, landed once under the lock, all targeted directories exit 0, standing preflight and heavy-questions green):
- Parts travel by id: the child names every part of a closure by `partIdOfV1` (digest of row keys, one shared function) and sends the rows only the first time per child; the parent registers a part id it already holds (`parts.known/use`) and refuses a mismatch (falls back to walking). Wire cost per answer drops from about 100 000 bytes to a few kilobytes (law: below 8 000 bytes per answer, 120 closures of 2 000 shared rows, 2 children).
- Linear line reader, messages `<marker><json>` so output printed by a module cannot corrupt the protocol, child stdout redirected to stderr, children and their pipes `unref`ed, farm closed at process exit.
- Batches: each child is fed up to 4 questions at once (pool width = children x 4), least loaded child first.
- Sizing: child budget 768 MiB (measured 200 to 430 MB resident), recycle at 1.5 GiB; the grant of 11 GB gives min(8, cpus - 1, memory) children.
- Throughput law (`🧪️tests/⏱️throughput`): 3 000 synthetic closures over shared parts: cold 1.17 ms per closure, warm (new settler over the stored manifest, journal naming 2 files) 0.03 ms per closure, bounds 25 and 15 ms.
- Worker (8 to 16 GB, few cores): the granted cpus minus one and memory decide; with fewer than 2 children's worth the farm stays off and the slice (about 78 projects, a few hundred closures) is walked in-process at about 0.1 s per closure cold, so a cold slice settle is tens of seconds to two minutes plus loading the part records it touches (about 1 s per 1 000 parts); the steady state is the warm bound above. Not measured on a worker.

Before: about 20 closures per minute (279 of 7 392 after 13 minutes, live) with the wave 2 protocol; the old pre-R53 code about 500 per minute. After: NOT MEASURED on the real read: read 12801 keeps its old modules and was still running when I landed (it had reached `flush 1 of 1` at 20:16Z with 13 GB RSS). ## Follow-up 2: shared closure entries off the parent thread

Question (a), does the wave re-derive what the settle walked: the settle walks derived QUESTIONS (`deriveClosureFactV1`), the wave needs closure ENTRIES (recipes, `ce` store). The in-process settle fills the process memo `closureEntriesV1` and the store as a side effect, so the wave reused them; with the walk farm the settle's walks happen in children, so that side effect is lost for the farm path: the wave re-derives them on the parent thread (this is a cost my farm added if the wave runs in the same process). (b) built: `offloadClosureRecipeV1` (walk-farm module): the first 4 recipes of a process are walked in-process, afterwards a shared farm (sized from the grant, closes after 120 s idle) walks them: request kind `recipe`, answer = value, rows, layers and the sidecar (walk facts and members that the process keeps per layer in weak maps and that the store sidecar is built from; they travel beside the layers and are re-attached in the parent). Hook: `produceClosureEntryV1` derives through `walkClosureRecipeV1` (script-inputs, anchored patch: `deriveClosureRecipeV1`, `deriveClosureRecipeForChildV1`, the call site). The parent parses the answer and `store.save`s it as before, the child never writes the store (its nested provider walks stay in the child, the farm is off inside a child). Any failure returns `undefined` and the parent walks itself. Law (`🏭️walk-farm/🧪️tests`, 7 tests): 40 recipes of 60 ms processor each, after warm-up the parent walks 0, its longest event-loop block is below 120 ms and the blocked time per entry below 10 ms (control: without the hook the same wave walked 40 on the parent thread, red).
Not proven: that a recipe walked by a real child equals the in-process one (value is persisted with `JSON.stringify` by the store, so it is JSON-safe by contract, rows/layers likewise); my real-deriver parity probe (`recipefarm.mjs`) did not finish under load 42 (its children never became ready, same unexplained start stall as above) and was stopped. Not measured: wave throughput on a real read.
