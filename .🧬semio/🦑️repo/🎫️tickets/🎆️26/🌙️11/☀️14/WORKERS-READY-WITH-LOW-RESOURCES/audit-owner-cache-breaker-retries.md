# Owner Lookups During an Open Cache Breaker

The replicated selected-slice receipts at 18:40–18:41 UTC recorded 1,498 unavailable and 72 transport outcomes in 2,024 looks on g1-m05, 3,478 unavailable and 35 transport outcomes in 3,779 looks on g1-m08, and 618 unavailable and 27 transport outcomes in 816 looks on g1-m12. Their separate normal-gate retry counters showed 131, 129 and 26 starts. Reported retry durations were aggregate across owner operations, so the receipts cannot identify a serial delay or attribute every retry to the open breaker.

The actual graph exchange returns a typed unavailable result before transport when the existing client breaker refuses work. The speculative owner pass previously retried that result three times, with pauses of 4, 8 and 16 seconds. The normal owner gate also used its existing timed retry twice when every lookup was already refused. That delays the ordinary local inference gate without a new HTTP request.

The speculative pass now treats a breaker refusal as locally undecided immediately while retaining the original unavailable fault counters. The normal gate receives the precise typed exchange reason and stops its per-owner retry on an already-open breaker. It preserves the bounded retry after an actual transport failure, including when that failure opens the breaker. The next failure can be a breaker refusal, which ends that owner's wait. The independent peer-adoption polls remain active and the normal gate still fetches again after the breaker expires. Genuine 404 responses continue through claim handling. The cache never authorizes content from a refused or cancelled lookup, and the existing verification, claim, publication and capacity controls remain in force.

A private authenticated transport fixture replayed five cases through the real project gate and the actual take pass. Before the change an already-open speculative look produced four attempts and three pauses, and the ordinary local gate returned without HTTP after two pauses. A real 503 caused two extra retries after the first breaker refusal. The final fixture observes one speculative attempt and no pause when initially open, one pause after the first real 503 and no repeat after the breaker refusal, and no pause before local inference when its initial request was refused. After expiry the genuine miss makes an HTTP request and reaches the claim route. A malformed response from a usable cache still retries. The neutral JSON trace and an independent SQLite summary agree. Existing integration also verifies a successfully adopted peer record after a real initial timeout and a peer arrival during polling.

The combined owner integration and fault fixture passed 13 tests and 227 assertions. The neighboring take, counters, upload and lease selection passed 42 tests while its 200-case Python oracle exceeded both the 5-second and 30-second runner deadlines. That oracle completed in 73.25 seconds in the isolated run with a 120-second test allowance. Its four-test file passed 424 assertions. The focused fixture passed again with the final SQLite left-join check, and scoped whitespace validation passed. Physical adoption of the changed code and a subsequent Ready milestone are separate from these fixture results.

The live worker receipts were read only. No cache breaker, owner claim, grant, process or graph pointer was changed in production by the tests.
