## PART A. Owner pool

### A1. What the cache really did (evidence)
`GET /status` of the production cache (signed read, 01:34 local, running since 19:07Z):
`ac`: puts 15 559, **conflicts 3 462, rejected 0**; `cas`: puts 75 602, **rejected 0**, conflicts 0; fleet `denied: {no-signature: 1}`; validation `complete`; evicted 5 650 entries. In `divergences.json` (cap 1 000 rows, newest 00:35Z) only **20** owner-record divergences fall after the pool rollout (23:46Z), 18 of them "refused origin g1-m01, kept g1-m03/m05". In the same window the pools counted ~283 failures (pool.json `failed` now: m01 20, m02 68, m03 9, m04 72, m07 76, m10 8, server 42).
Reading: every server-side refusal (400 invalid/missing blob, 413, 507, auth) increments `rejected`, which is 0; conflicts are 409s, of which the head-swap retries are normal and at most 20 are owner records. So **at least 90 % of the "fleet cache refused the entry" failures were never a refusal by the server**. They are the client returning `false` without a reason (`publishGraphEntryV1` / `client.putBlob` / `putResult`): an open breaker (any single request that timed out on this machine writes the `down` stamp and every publish of every child of the machine refuses for 15 s: `usable()` is checked first; timeouts: lookup/claim/head 1.5 s, result PUT 6 s, blob 20 s + size/2 MiB/s), a timed-out or aborted PUT (status never kept), a lost hold. What exactly fired on which machine cannot be read afterwards because the reason was thrown away: that is the defect fixed below. Correlation (not proof): the failure rate follows the number of children per machine (m04 28 %, m07 18 %, m02 16 % with 10 to 16 children; m10 1.6 % and m03 5 % with one).
The sampled `semio-s-artifact-*` share (82 %) is the base rate (3 481 of 4 774 owners are cargo owners) plus their longer inference; there is no content cause. Reproduction: the real owner `semio-s-artifact-procedural-2d-editor-commands` (manifest read from production) inferred in a private session against a private cache server configured like production (validation `complete`, 2.5 GiB, 512 MiB entry, 4 MiB result, 600 s grace; `proof/repro.mjs`): inference 10.7 s, **publish stored in 258 ms**. NOT reproduced: the 9-document shape (the fresh private store holds no closure entries, so the entry had 1 document), signed identity and provenance (private server uses tokens), load. So the content is not at fault; a loaded or lagging path to the cache is the remaining cause.
Latency from the server host to the cache (400 reads, 4 parallel): p50 2 ms, p99 40 ms, max 56 ms, none above 1.5 s. From the workers: not measured.
**Divergence (the 20).** What differs between two machines' records for one key was not found in the bytes (the refused blobs are evicted; heuristics by provenance did not find them). What is established: the stamps are zero (R49), the other row kinds (`exists`, `lstat`, `stamp`, `resolve`, `closure`) are neutral, and a record carries the sha256 of every file it read, so two machines that infer while the live tree is being edited (or a replica is one second behind) legitimately produce different valid records under one key: the taker checks the observations against its own tree. Both machines are macOS, same code digest, so the platform does not belong in the key. Decision: a divergence means **the key is stored**, it is no failure and no retry (counted `diverged`).

### A2. Fixes (all with tests, section A5)
1. **Reason end to end.** `B/🪪️graph-state/🛰️exchange/🧯️refusal` (schema first, 20 rows, EN/DE, Python oracle): code, `transient`, `deterministic`, `stored`. The client keeps status and the first 160 characters of the answer, or `no-answer` (timeout) / `unreachable` / `breaker-open`; `publishGraphEntryOutcomeV1` returns it; `publishGraphEntryV1` stays boolean for the other callers. The catch-all text is gone; `claim.why()` reads e.g. `the cache has no room left / der Flottenspeicher hat keinen Platz mehr [HTTP 507: Insufficient storage] (9 documents, 6662 KiB)`. `pool.json` gets `failures` by code, `kept`, `resent`, `diverged`, `shed`.
2. **Deterministic causes stop the others.** `limit-document`, `limit-entry`, `limit-documents`, 413, a malformed 400 are `deterministic`; `claim.deterministic()` is true, so the failure note (R49) is written and other machines leave the owner. Transient causes (timeouts, breaker, 507, 5xx, vanished blob, claim lost, head moved) never write a note.
3. **Bounded retry that keeps the work.** `Publish` policy: 4 attempts, back-off 2 s / 6 s / 15 s + up to 25 % jitter, never shorter than the rest of the machine's breaker (max 20 s); a lapsed hold is claimed again (`stored` by another: done). A record still not stored is written to `.cache/f/owner-pool-unsent/` (whole or not at all, verified on read, 24 records / 192 MiB / 30 min), and a child re-sends before every claim (every 20 s, two per look) and once at its end (30 s); the claim is asked again, so nothing is stored twice.
4. **"The cache did not answer"** (pointer): read again after 1 s / 3 s / 9 s (and after the breaker) before the machine reports it; the waves are kept as before.
5. **Why children end.** 11 of the 20 `a child ended (exit 0)` samples (all g1-m01) are **normal ends misreported**: when the first child of a wave exits 0 the wave becomes `finishing`; every later exit 0 fell into the error branch of `runner.onExit`. Exit 0 is now never an error (test red on the live runner: lastError "a child ended (exit 0)", green after). Other exits keep their meaning (75 recycled, 76 revision, 77 could not open, 78 drained, killed above the hard limit).
6. **Claim lost before the upload** (5): the new retry re-claims; the cause itself (loop blocked longer than the 300 s lease, or a server restart losing in-memory claims) was not separately measured.

### A3. What the 1 GiB is, and the bound
Measured on 12 production entries (record + closure documents, R59 packed form): 17 to 150 MB raw (1.8 to 14.4 MB brotli), record 13 to 536 KB, **99.5 % closure documents**: 9 to 65 documents of 2.5 to 7.0 MB raw each, the same blobs for every owner that names them. The three giants have 227 to 289 closure documents (`repo`: 1 524 MiB in 290 documents = 5.3 MiB each; `plugin-registry` 1 046 MiB in 227 = 4.6 MiB each): the same R59 documents, only 4 to 6 times more of them than the ordinary owner, because the owner consumes that many tool closures. R59's 9 to 34 MB is right for ordinary owners; its packed documents are not 4.6 MiB because of a missing build, as the earlier read assumed. Not measured: the content of the 290 documents of `repo` (not inferred here: memory).
Fix: `Publish.closureBudgetBytes` = 192 MiB (above every stored entry measured, max 156 MB of closures): closure documents are taken in the order of their keys until the budget is used and the first that does not fit ends the list, **without packing the rest**, so an owner never holds more than the budget plus one document (before: 1.5 GiB built, child at 5 GB). The record stays valid without the shed closures (a taker derives them); `shed` counts them in `pool.json`. Wire cost of a closure that exists is already zero (content addressed, `hasBlob`). Effect on the giants' takers (they re-derive the shed closures) is unmeasured.

## PART B. g1-m05, `dependencies`

### B1. Who holds the exclusive store-ownership lock: nobody
The lines `bun:dependency-storage remains active on dependencies:store-ownership:frontier:2080320 (exclusive, 3461 s)` are **progress records, not locks**: `runJavascriptDependencyStoreProducerV1` (`LIB/⚡️caching/🚀️bootstrap/📦️dependencies/💾️storage/🟦️.ts` line 749) calls `control.publish({resource:"dependencies:store-ownership:"+operation+":"+completedOperations, mode:"exclusive", elapsedMs: now - producer start})` every 64 steps of the ownership walk (`withPublishedBunWorkspaceOwnershipV1`, frontier replay in `🗂️workspaces/🟦️bun/🟨️.cjs`). The number in the name is a step counter that changes from line to line (frontier 2 075 648 -> 2 080 320, readText 1 887 744 -> 1 984 320 in the failed tail), `elapsedMs` is the time since the producer started (3 461 s), `mode` is the label of the activity vocabulary. There is no "waiting", "queued", "held by" or "blocked" line in either log (0 matches). So no earlier try's detached process, no pool child, no daemon and no stale lock file is the cause. The cross-process leases exist (registry with pid and start time, `🔒️leases/🧭️order/🗂️registry`) and show no wait.
What the stage does: a genuinely long walk. Measured on the running try 9 (00:40Z start): 477 056 steps in 62 s (7 700/s), then a second walk (counter restarts: 483 584 at 36 s of a new producer clock), 1 254 144 steps at 2 038 s (about 410/s on average); try 8 reached 2 080 320 at 3 461 s (600/s) with 1 560/s in its last two minutes. At 01:16Z (2 208 s) the counter stood still at 1 254 144 for 170 s (the mirror may lag). The rate falls by 5 to 20 times as the walk goes on; why (a re-derivation each time the replicated tree moves under the walk, per-step cost growing, Windows file system and virus scanner) is NOT shown from the logs. The 7 748-edge provider check before it took about 900 s in tries 1 to 6.
The stage cap is the cause of the failure: tries 7 and 8 were stopped at exactly 3 600 s while their counters were still moving.

### B2. Does a timeout kill the tree on Windows? Partly
`killTree` ran `taskkill /PID <wrapper> /T /F` (Windows) or `kill(-pid)` (unix). `/T` follows recorded parent ids from the wrapper; a child whose parent already exited is no longer reachable, and nothing recorded it. The adopted-stage check used `process.kill(pid,0)` on the recorded pid only (a reused pid, common on Windows, looks alive; and a timeout would then `taskkill` an innocent process tree). I found no evidence of an orphan on g1-m05 (cannot see the machine); this is a hole in the code, not a proven cause.

### B3. Fixes
1. **`🚦️ready/🌲️process-tree`** (schema first, EN/DE texts unchanged, pure + real glue): the stage supervisor samples the process table every 30 s (`ps` on unix, `Get-CimInstance Win32_Process` on Windows), records `{pid,startMs}` of every process of the tree in `.cache/f/ready/<stage>.tree.json`, and on timeout, cancel, vanished stage and before the next try stops exactly those still present **with the same start time**, leaf first, never itself or its ancestors, never pids 1 to 4, never a reused pid, also the orphans of exited parents. An adopted stage whose pid is another process now is treated as vanished.
2. **Soft cap.** The stage cap (`timeoutMilliseconds`) is a soft cap: past it the stage goes on while a counter in its log changes (`store-ownership:op:N`, `Checking declared package providers: N edges`, `"completedOperations":N`; heartbeats do not count) up to 4 x the cap (`dependencies` 4 h), and is stopped as `stage-timeout` after 10 minutes without a moving counter. The cap is not in the stage digest.
3. **Lease with pid and start time, holder named.** `createReadyLeaseV1` records `startMs`; `readyLeaseVerdictV1` (pure) frees a lease that expired, whose owner is gone, or whose pid belongs to another process now; `ready.json` says `(process {pid})` in both languages.
4. **The walk prints counts and resumes.** `LIB/⚡️caching/🚀️bootstrap/📦️dependencies/🕓️walk` (schema, Python oracle): a line `Ownership walk: N steps in S s, R per second since the start and r per second lately` (EN/DE) plus a `[DATA] Store ownership walk {...,"completedOperations":N}` record every 30 s, and a **checkpoint of the settled listings** (`writeJavascriptDependencyOwnershipIndexV1`, the same call that was made only at the very end) every 120 s once 20 000 steps are new; the next try seeds from it through the existing `seedBunWorkspaceOwnershipIndexV1` (stale-gate branch). Wired in `runJavascriptDependencyStoreProducerV1` `progress.advance`.
