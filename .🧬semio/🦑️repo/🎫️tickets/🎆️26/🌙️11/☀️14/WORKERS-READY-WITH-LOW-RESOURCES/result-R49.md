## 8. Failures of the live pool: cause, fix, failure hygiene, restart (18:20 to 18:43Z)

**Cause (proved by status messages after the cause-naming code reached g1-m08/m04/m05).** "the claim was lost before the upload" (g1-m08 `workspace`, g1-m04 `@semio-tech/framework-rs`): native cargo owners block the event loop of the puller for tens of seconds (R36 measured 29 to 130 s synchronous stretches), so the heartbeat (every 8 s) cannot renew the 30 s claim; the claim lapses, `publishGraphEntryV1` refuses to upload with a lost hold, the owner is claimed again by the next machine (that is why one owner failed on three origins). Second, rarer cause: "the inference left no record to export (inputs changed while it ran)" (g1-m05 `repo`): a file of the owner changed during the inference in the continuously edited tree, so the record is not persisted (racy/inconsistent); transient. In an isolated private session (`T/🧰️scripts/R49/repro-native.mjs`: the real session and unit of `semio-framework-geometry` from the live manifest, private cache, my runner code) the same owner is inferred and published in 67 s without error, so the content is not at fault.
**Fix.** Pool claims use a 300 s lease (`claimTtlMilliseconds`) and an expected duration of at least 240 s (lifetime cap 960 s), `tryGraphEntryV1`/`holdOf` take the lease from the caller; the failure message names the cause (`claim.why()`: nothing to export, unshareable spelling, claim lost, no upload right, refused with size). Failure hygiene: a thrown inference or an unshareable record publishes a bounded note (`owner-pool` entry `failure`, per record-state digest, record-code revision, cause, origin, time; valid 20 min, ignored when the revision differs); a puller that is granted a claim reads the note first and gives the claim back (`refused`), so the server's wave infers it. Transient failures (lost claim, nothing exported, refused upload) leave no note. Restart: the pool role has its own numbers in the sidecar schema (`Restart.pool`: look every 5 s, quiet 5 s, deferral at most 30 s, a finished owner may take up to 10 min), so a restart follows a change of the child's own closure within about 35 s instead of 90 s quiet plus 10 min maximum in a tree that is always edited (the old numbers were why the first pool children needed 10+ minutes). ## 9. The `other` divergence class: mutable heads by compare-and-swap (landed 18:46Z)

**What the documents are.** `served.br` (answer to one requester, "replaced by every newer answer", `🙋️wanted/🟦️.ts`), `costs.br` (one fleet-wide table, measured by whichever serving machine, `🙋️wanted/💰️cost/🟦️.ts`), `pointer.br` (my owner-pool pointer, published by any machine that runs a cold wave). All three are mutable "latest" heads written by several origins, but the cache treats every result as first-write-wins per origin, so each second origin got 409 and was counted as a divergence (95 in 18 minutes; kept entries `served.br`, `costs.br`, `pointer.br`, `n=1`). They are not facts of the tree and not per-origin observations: one latest value is wanted, so they became heads, not per-origin keys.
**Fix.** The writer reads the head and sends the digest of what it read in `x-semio-expect` (`none` for an empty head); the cache (`🖥️server/🟦️.ts`) replaces the entry only while the held bytes have that digest, whichever origin wrote them (no divergence row, counter `conflicts` for a stale writer), and `publishGraphEntryV1({head:true})` re-reads and retries up to four times (`swapHeadV1`), so the newest writer wins. A result without the header stays first-write-wins (owner records, units: unchanged). Call sites: `publishServedV1`, `publishOwnerCostsV1`, the pool pointer. Tests `🖥️server/🧪️tests/🧭️heads` (2): the swap across origins with the held digest, refused with a stale digest or `none` on a held head, never a divergence, a plain write still 409; two machines publishing the served answer of one requester both succeed and the newer one is read. ## 11. Pool process hygiene (defect found on g1-server 19:01Z: two pool processes, 15.6 GB)

**Cause.** (a) A restart left the old child alive: the child ignored SIGTERM until its event loop was free (its `SIGTERM` handler only sets an abort flag, and a native owner blocks the loop for tens of seconds), `superviseGraphChildV1` never sent SIGKILL, and an orphan guard in the child is a timer, which a blocked loop cannot run. (b) Up to 12 owners (cores minus one) ran in one process with no memory bound and bun does not give heap back. (c) The pool ignored what the machine itself was doing.
**What grows (measured, `scratch/R49/rec2.mjs`, four light owners in one process):** RSS 704 -> 1006 -> 1222 -> 1324 MB while the JS heap only goes 132 -> 240 MB and external 75 -> 116 MB, and a full `Bun.gc(true)` returns nothing: the growth is outside the JS heap (allocator-retained pages and native caches: TypeScript program/language service state, observation record stores). With `BUN_JSC_forceRAMSize=512MB` (what the graph child gets) the same four owners end at 747 MB (916 -> 747 between owners): the cap returns the memory. Which structure holds it is not proven. Native cargo owners grow far more (R36: 5 to 25 GB per owner stretch).
**Fix (landed, 17 of 18 landing directories green).**
1. Memory: the pool child is started with `graphChildEnvironmentV1()` (512 MB collector target, allocator purge); owners run at most 3 at once (`maximumConcurrent` 3); admission uses `min(host headroom, grant - rss)` with a grant of a fifth of the memory, 1.5 to 3 GB (`ownerPoolGrantBytesV1`, `SEMIO_OWNER_POOL_GRANT_MB`; an 8 GB machine gets 1.6 GB and keeps 6.4 GB); once the footprint passes the grant after work was done the puller takes nothing new, finishes the owners in flight, writes `lastError` "recycled ..." and exits with status 75, the supervisor starts a fresh process at once (memory returned to the OS).
2. No orphans: `superviseGraphChildV1` sends SIGTERM then SIGKILL after a grace (5 s, tested with a child that ignores SIGTERM: dead within the grace, `stop()` waits); the pool child installs no SIGTERM handler (the default action ends it even with a blocked loop) and exits at once when orphaned; a pid file `<root>/.cache/f/owner-pool.pid` (`🔒️guard`): a starting process that finds a live pool pid waits up to 60 s and then does not pull (tests: waits, refuses, takes over after the end, stale pid).
3. Priority: an owner is taken only while `admitRemoteExecutionActionV1` (the host's single admission decision, class "lent": after every request of the machine itself such as the `ready` setup child or the group server's wave) admits one more build job; otherwise `pool.json` says `yielding` (EN/DE text).
4. `pool.json` carries `rssMegabytes`, `grantMegabytes`, `siblings` and the reason.
