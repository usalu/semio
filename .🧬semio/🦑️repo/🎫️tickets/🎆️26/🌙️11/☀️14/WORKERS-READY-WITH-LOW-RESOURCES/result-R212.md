## A. The 8 GB peak of 3531 MB against 3276 MB (judge LANDED 16:30:12 to 16:30:14 UTC, "R212 landing A"; the target under 2048 MB is NOT reached and not provable without a lab run)
Where the 3531 MB are (from `timeline.jsonl` of run 4, sampled every 2 s, stage seconds; the machine had 4 to 5 processes, so one process holds almost all of it):
- 0 to 30 s: 121 to 1363 MB, the boot of the build kernel (every command of the repo boots to 1.1 to 1.3 GB in this unit: `repo-mcp` 247 to 1141, `workspace` 290 to 1152 in the same run).
- 30 to 60 s: to 1992 MB, the first ownership walk (derivation).
- 110 to 490 s: 2040 to 2670 MB, about 100 MB per scope (6 scopes), the bun install child adds 300 to 400 MB while it runs (5 processes in the samples at 738 s and 841 s).
- 509 s: 3008 MB (declared-provider checks, ready write), 844 s: 3409 MB (generation verify), 880 s: **3531 MB** at the start of the second ownership walk (`preservation-ownership`), then 3060 MB at the end.
So the peak is a high-water mark of one process that ran three walks and the ledgers one after the other, not the bun install of 12 scopes (children are 5th process, below 400 MB).

What the lab measures: `ps -o rss` summed over the machine's processes (`sliceLabProcessRowsV1`, `🧪️testing/🔬️slice-lab`), while the slice guard of the workers and the footprint gate judge the physical footprint (`summarizeReadTreeV1`: "footprints beat the resident size"). Resident size counts file pages of the runtime. Measured here with `/usr/bin/time -l`: `repo show projects` (kernel boot plus the dependency gate of 12 generations, 18 s) max resident 1,039 MB, **peak memory footprint 371 MB**; the ownership walk alone (98,719 paths) max resident 829 MB, footprint 735 MB (heap 192 MB after a full collection, so most of the walk's footprint is garbage and native memory of 310k `lstat` results awaiting collection). The real worker g1-m06 ran the stage in 299 s with a peak of 1988 MB (`ready-timings.json`); R135 measured 2921 MB.

The limit: `sliceLabLimitMegabytesV1` = floor(`treeShare` 0.4 x declared memory) = 3276 MB at 8192 MB (6553 at 16 GB), the same `treeShare` the plan guard uses; the stage booked 3328 MB ("admitted dependencies: 1 job(s), 3328 MB reserved"). The limit is a derived share of the host and not too tight for a correct stage by itself; the stage was judged by a bigger quantity than the guard uses.

Built (lab only): `sliceLabFootprintV1` + sampler: each sample sums the physical footprint of the machine's processes where `footprint` measures them (the same `readFootprintsV1` and parser as the guard, macOS), the resident size elsewhere; the verdict judges `peakFootprintMegabytes` and prints `peakResidentMegabytes` beside it (EN and DE text `peakResident`). A footprint over the limit still fails (law `the verdict judges the footprint, names the resident peak beside it, and a footprint over the limit still fails`). This changes what the next 8 GB lab run reports; it does not prove that the stage fits: the stage's real footprint is somewhere between the measured pieces (about 2.2 to 2.8 GB estimated from 3531 minus the resident-only part of the boot) and is NOT measured.

Memory levers measured, none landed:
- Forced full collection every 20,000 steps in the walk (`Bun.gc(true)` from the `advance` callback): footprint 719 MB to 477 MB (-34 %), resident 802 to 594 MB, derivation time not comparable under load (22 to 32 s). The existing `releaseIfGrownV1` does not fire (one release in a whole walk): it triggers on JS heap plus external memory growth of 256 MB, and the garbage here is native (`lstat` bigint results, directory records), so a release policy keyed to the footprint grant (`createFootprintGateV1`) would be the right place; binding that gate in the stage would also add its refusal at 85 percent of the grant (2830 MB of 3328), which the real stage (2921 MB at R135) would hit, so it is not done blind.
- The walk keeps a module-level `listingCache` for the process lifetime: 98,380 directories, 223,952 unprojected entries (60 MB of heap), and 141 MB of heap remain after a custody closed and the cache was cleared. A second custody in the same process raised the resident size from 697 to 987 MB. The walk's `plan` needs the collection roots among the entries, so storing only the projection (which hides them) is not equivalent: not changed.
- `Object.freeze` of every state and listing array creates two `SparseArrayValueMap` per path (197,100 objects for 98,719 paths in the heap statistics): a packed row store (typed arrays and an interned path trie, as R130 did for the graph) would remove them; it changes the observed-ownership shape that the generation receipts persist: needs its own round.
