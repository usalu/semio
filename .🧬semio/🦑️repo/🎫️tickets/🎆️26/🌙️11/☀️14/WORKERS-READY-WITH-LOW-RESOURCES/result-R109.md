# R109: why every pool went idle after each landing, and what moves the record revision now

Scripts: `T/🧰️scripts/R109/` (T = `☀️13/FLEET-ONE-MACHINE-SHARED-HOT-CACHE-AND-REMOTE-EXECUTION`): `stage.py` (anchored edits, backups), `land.py`, `window.sh` (lock, land, 17 test files in parallel, restore on red), `overlay.ts` (loader plugin that serves staged files for a pre-landing run), `battery.sh`.

## 1. What the "record revision" is made of
- The pool revision (`head.revision` of every wave, `ownerPoolRevisionV1`, the sidecar pool's `revision`, the `ready` line of a child) is `projectRecordKeyTextV1(null)` in `👁️observation/🟨️.mjs` = `JSON.stringify([OBSERVATION_REVISION, null, null])`.
- `OBSERVATION_REVISION` = `captureBoundedSourceRevisionV1(<that module's own physical source path>).sha256`: the sha256 of the BYTES of `👁️observation/🟨️.mjs`, nothing else (`📥️inputs/🔏️revision` hashes the one file it is given). The `8-hex` revisions in pool.json and in R104 are prefixes of that hash.
- So the revision moves on every edit of that one file by anybody (a comment, a new export, an unrelated fix), not on edits "of the build library" in general; observation is one of the most edited files. The same bytes were also the first element of the digest and of the fleet key text of every record read OUTSIDE a settlement round (`nestedProject` false), while records of owners inside a graph read were already filed under the schema stamp (`ownerRecordKeyTextV1`, `observeInferenceComputationV1` with `nestedProject`). Two keys for one rule set, one of them accidental.
- The deliberate contract is `🧬️schema/🔣️.json` `$defs.record.const.stamp` (now `semio.graph-record/2`), documented "Bump the stamp when the rows, the digest or the validation of a record change for the same inputs". It is already what `recordStampV1()`, the nested record key, `inferenceRecordIdentityV1` and `declareFormatStampV1("graph.record")` use.

## 2. Fix at the source (staged)
`projectRecordKeyTextV1(key)`, the record digest and the fleet key text now all use `recordStampV1()`; `OBSERVATION_REVISION` stays only for the stale-module guard (`requireCurrentObservationV1`) and the process-local producer/publication images. Result: `projectRecordKeyTextV1(null)` is `["semio.graph-record/2",null,null]`; an ordinary landing (including an edit of the observation module that keeps the stamp) moves no pool revision, no record digest and no key. Bumping the stamp is the one deliberate way to move it.

Test (red first, `👁️observation/🧪️tests/🆔️record-revision`): loads the real module in a child process under a Bun loader plugin that tells it its physical source is a copy (plain, with an unrelated edit and a new export, with a bumped stamp in the copied schema). Red on the live tree: revision `["0c26371a…",null,null]` instead of the stamp. Green staged: plain = edited = `["semio.graph-record/2",null,null]`, bumped = `["semio.graph-record/2-next",null,null]`, the sha256 of the module appears nowhere in the text.

## 3. Why no wave appeared for many minutes (question 2)
- A running read keeps the revision it started with (`OBSERVATION_REVISION` is read once at module load; a pinned implementation is never reloaded). R104's `announce` publishes once at the start of the owner resolution, `learn`/`settle` republish only when the open list shrinks 10 % (and then under the same, old revision). A running read never re-announces under a newer revision: it cannot, it does not run that code.
- A pool of the new revision declines every wave of the old one (`reason no-current-wave`, observed live: `g1-m07/pool.json` at 02:00 lists 8 waves, all of another revision). Its only ways to get a wave: a read that STARTS under its revision announces (the take pass of such a read reaches the announce within the setup time), or the server pool's `starved` hook starts `repo show projects`, which queues for the graph builder behind the very read it is replacing. A worker pool has no `starved` hook at all.
- Fix 2.1 removes the cause: with the revision tied to the stamp, waves published before a landing stay acceptable after it, and the running reads stay the right publishers.
- Added keep-alive (staged, `ownerPoolKeepAliveV1`, policy `republishKeepAliveMilliseconds` 600000, publisher `alive()` from `learn` and `miss`): a long read puts its unchanged wave on the pointer again every 10 minutes with a fresh `createdMs`. Why: a wave is listed only 3 h (`manifestMaximumAgeMilliseconds`) from creation, and R104 saw a pointer entry vanish ("reads `undefined`"). Test `🧪️tests/🔔️keep-alive` (rule, and a fake-publish run with a clock: nothing inside the interval, same wave again after it from the reporter and from a running owner, attempt restarts the interval, nothing after `finish`).

## 4. Pool derives its own wave: NOT done, and why
I built the derivation (a `🧭️derive` module that walks the project list with `workspaceBuildFilesV1`, calls `discoverBuildProjectsV1` with an `announceOnly` hook at the point where the reader creates its publisher input, and answers head/session/units; a runner `derive` hook adopting the wave locally). The experiment on the real tree stopped at `discoverBuildProjectsV1`: `receiveScriptProcessInvocation` throws "Original parent Script process invocation required". Only a process born by the `repo` launcher (or the test birth ladder, owner `repository:genuine-test`) carries `SEMIO_SCRIPT_PROCESS_INVOCATION`; the sidecar and its children do not, and discovery is also only reachable after a read holds the graph builder. Faking a birth from the sidecar would bypass the routing admission, and a `repo show projects` with an early end would queue behind the builder (same as `starved`) and, ended early, must not publish a graph. I did not ship a derive hook with no safe production wiring. The route that fits the architecture: a catalogue operation (e.g. `repo graph announce`) that runs the discovery under its own invocation WITHOUT the builder lease and writes the three wave files; the pool then spawns `repo` for it and adopts the result. The runner hook and the test (pool with only an old-revision wave adopts a derived one and claims within the interval) are small once that operation exists.

## 5. Question 3: are records of the older reader still useful?
Owner records of a read are filed under the stamp key (nested) and carry rows/closures validated by content, so a reader that started before a landing and a pool or reader after it read each other's records whenever the stamp is unchanged; before the fix the pool waves of that reader were declined, the records themselves were not. After the fix the top-level key agrees too. A stamp bump makes all of them misses by design.
