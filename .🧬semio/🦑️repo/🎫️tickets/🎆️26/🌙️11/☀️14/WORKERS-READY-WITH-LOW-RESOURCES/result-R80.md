## 1. What I could and could not reproduce

A look of the worker (`look` in `emojiProjectJsonNodes`, `PN/🟨️.mjs:378`) is NOT only the record take: it runs the whole `inferProject` with the take-away compute, i.e. `observeProjectInferenceV1` (record, 10 to 30 ms) AND, after it, `resolveClosureRefsV1` (late mode: `closureApiV1.resolve` over every closure recipe of the record), `onOwnerObservations` (row log, `derivedFacts`) and `result.projects[name]`. The earlier one-pass harness (`produceProjectRecordV1`) only covers the first part, which is why it showed 10 to 30 ms.

A faithful take of the real store is not runnable outside a graph writer: the store `.cache/b` is a native image whose reads need an issued reader store (`Unissued Native observation image refused` / `Original bounded graph writer custody required`), a private root cannot be built under 500 MB (R76). So the harness reads the live store with plain `fs` (read-only: raw record `np/<key>.json` and the closure store `ce/{e,l,v}`), builds the real environment from the real wave manifest of the fleet pool (4 613 units, fetched read-only with the machine's signed client), and runs the REAL `resolveClosureRefsV1`, the real layer verdicts and the real caches in one closure pass with 4 looks in flight. Only owners whose record AND all closure entries are stored are used (this machine: 40 of the first 200 are fully stored; 2 279 recipes, 795 stored; the server's tree churns, so 1/3 of stored entries are stale here and get derived, which the workers' do not: numbers below separate the two).

## 2. Where the time goes (measured, real stored layers of this machine)

`layer-bench.ts`, 600 real layers of `ce/l` (avg 720 KB of JSON, 2 500 rows each), per layer: read 2.8 ms, brotli 0.7, sha256 0.5, JSON.parse 2.0, row sharing 2.4, row hold check 14.7 (total about 23 ms with the unmemoized check; 12.5 ms per layer on the workers, with the row memo of `projectObservationHoldsV1`). `harness` run (60 eligible owners): 856 distinct layers checked in 9.1 s = 10.6 ms per layer, 6 354 verdict hits, 1.5 owners/s at 4 looks.

`overlap.ts`, 1 500 real layers: 3 928 956 rows but only 134 131 distinct rows (3.4 %) and 82 719 distinct paths; kinds: lstat 1.6 M, analysis 0.47 M, resolve 0.62 M, exists 0.52 M, fileExists 0.37 M, field 0.29 M. So the cost is not a stat per file: it is that every distinct layer (52 841 on this store) is read, decompressed, parsed and walked row by row (the row memo already exists, but is looked up per row after `rebaseRowV1` and `JSON.stringify(row.fact)`, about 5 us per row even for a memoized row). Floor of the current design: 52 841 distinct layers x 12.5 ms = 11 min single thread, not 95 min; 95 min comes from the verdict being per round and from 12 looks serialising on one thread.

## 4. Design (not built, in the order of benefit)

1. Layers as row-id arrays over one persisted row dictionary (schema first, closure store version 4): the 3.4 % distinct rows are written once to an append-only dictionary (`ce/rows.pack` + `.idx`, offset-indexed, 134 k to 400 k rows), a layer becomes a `Uint32Array` of row ids named by the sha256 of that array (4 B per row, 10 KB instead of 720 KB, no brotli, no JSON). A pass verifies each DISTINCT ROW once (134 k x 6 us = 0.8 s) into a bitset; the verdict of a layer is the AND over its id array (2 500 lookups, about 5 us). 52 841 layers then cost 3 to 5 s instead of 11 min, with no per-owner state: the bitset is the only pass-lived structure (16 KB per 134 k rows) and is dropped with the round. Rows are materialised lazily and streamed only when a consumer needs rows (the row log append).
2. Take = presence under key + header digest: accept a record whose key matches (R68/R73 key) after the entry digests (`record.digest`, sha over rows + layer digests + value digest, already stored) and the bitset verdict; no layer is parsed. The one batched presence pass already exists (`ownerRecordsPresentV1`, one `readdir` census); extend it to list `ce/e` once.
3. Bulk fleet presence: after the census, one batched presence+fetch for the missing owners (the existing `takePassV1` asks owner by owner, `fetched: 0` on workers because present owners are looked at first), then infer only the remainder.
4. Memory: `result.projects` and `derivedFacts` of a cold part must be streamed to the on-disk part per owner (project JSON is 20 KB, but the expanded lists and `derivedFacts` supports are not needed after `rowLog.append`); round verdict maps byte-capped.
5. Parallelism only after 1: with a 5 us layer verdict the single thread is no longer the limit; worker threads would add 150 MB of heap per thread and a 30 to 45 s module boot per thread here (measured boot of the library: 45 s under load 30), so I would not use them.

Landing cost: items 1 and 2 touch `💾️entries`, `script-inputs`, the observation module and the closure schema, i.e. every tool closure: one landing restarts every worker (R63 section 8), so it must be proven by a real-process law first (fail before, pass after): the harness above is the "before"; the "after" law needs the new store format and a converter-free fresh closure store (greenfield, no migration: the old store is simply collected).

## 5. Proven / not proven

Proven: the take is dominated by the closure layers (about 80 %, 10.6 to 12.5 ms per distinct layer); only 3.4 % of the layer rows are distinct; layer verification, closure resolution and the resolved project do not grow the heap; the counters the workers report do not explain the 13 to 15 MB per owner; the harness (`take-harness.ts`) reproduces the cost side on real stored records read-only (1.3 to 1.5 owners/s at 4 looks, 40 to 60 owners).
Not proven: the retainer of the 13 to 15 MB per owner; any target of the task (50 owners/s, 3 min, 0.2 MB per owner, 2 GB); that the id-array layer design reaches them (estimate: layer verdicts 3 to 5 s for the whole store, the rest of the take then bounded by the record read of 10 to 30 ms x 4 774 = 0.8 to 2.4 min single thread, so the 3 minute target is plausible, 50 owners/s only with the records opened in parallel).
Landed: nothing. After-landing watch: not applicable.

## The retainer (named by the report, then measured on the real store)
`closure:entries.sidecar`: the loaded closure entry cache (`inference:closure-entries`) charged an entry about 4.4 KB (512 + 32 per row + 16 per value item) but each loaded entry also holds `digests` (274 layers on average, 155 KB of JSON per entry on disk) and the SIDECAR (`walk facts` and `walk members` per layer). The sidecar lists were unpacked into a fresh array per entry and layer although only 2 938 to 4 559 distinct walk strings exist: real store, 300 entries: 68 711 distinct member arrays, 5.75 M references, 233 KB of heap per entry (14 entries per owner = 3.4 MB per owner), and under a take of 3 766 entries 4 GB on a worker. The cache cap (96 MiB) never bit, 3 766 entries were charged 16 MB. The Set per layer digest in the script table (`walkMembersByDigestV1`, 40 bytes per member) and the re-adding of every member of 274 layers at every view of an entry (330 k Set adds per resolution) added to it.
Fix: (2) one shared walk-facts instance and one shared members array per layer digest, byte-capped in the store (`closureSidecarCacheBytes` 96 MiB, schema EN/DE), digests interned, entry charge includes the digests (`closureEntryDigestBytes` 48); (2b) the digest table holds that same array (no Set), a list is merged once (WeakSet), `someMemberV1` without spread; (2d/2e) members of one digest are ONE array per store extended in place (my first union copied a wider array per variant: 30 M references, caught by the real-store harness `load-entries.ts` before it ran on workers long).
Measured on the real store of this machine, 300 entries loaded and held: before 233 KB per entry (68 711 arrays), after the in-place union 203 KB per entry with 13 170 arrays = number of distinct digests (4.1 M references, 33 MB; this part now scales with distinct layers, not with entries or owners).
