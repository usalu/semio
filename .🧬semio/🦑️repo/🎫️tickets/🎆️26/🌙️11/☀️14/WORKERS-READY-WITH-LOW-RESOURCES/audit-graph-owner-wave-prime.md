# Shared Closure Preparation in Graph Production

The generation engine invoked its host preparation hook before reading the current pointer, deriving a plan or extending its structural claim. That hook waited for the required tool-closure preflight, then detached a complete shared-closure prime into the serial prime-child lane. When late owner inference entered the farm path, it queued its own complete prime on the same lane and awaited it before owner work. The second child could load a durable result from the first, but it still opened a new deriver, visited the recipes and verified cached observation rows and stored layers. If the facts moved, the new child could derive the recipe again.

Previous coordinator runs measured 241 and 291 seconds for the shared-prime phase. One report found all 49 recipes already present. Those receipts did not measure the two launches separately. The source trace establishes their order and the private regression observed one extra pre-owner launch per successful preparation. It does not establish a physical wall-time saving for the full graph.

The host now awaits the existing tool-closure preflight alone. It checks cancellation before and after that preflight, including when the preflight returns a previously completed validation. The existing late-owner phase runs shared-closure preparation with its own wave signal, through the capped child when it has a farm and inline through its existing fallback. The detached launcher and its declaration were removed. This gives up speculative warming during graph planning. At the former launch point the builder has yet to choose the pointer and work slice, the final claim can still refuse the work, and the cold inference cancellation scope has yet to be created. Starting only after those checks means a later wave always performs its own normal validation. Capacity requests, graph custody, exact fragment and closure qualification, and publication fences keep their existing controls.

The first fixture run failed before routing because it lacked a mock invocation control. The corrected baseline ran the actual generation host and reproduced an extra launch for one successful preparation and two launches across two waves. The fixed routing passed a cold preparation, cancellation followed by a new attempt, and consecutive preparations. An independent SQLite query compared outcomes and counted zero detached launches. Two intermediate aggregate runs exposed Bun mock isolation problems. One reached the real child and timed out. Another leaked a replaced prime binding into the child-lane suite. The final fixture runs the mocks in a private Bun process. The combined host and capped-child selection passed six tests with 27 assertions. The graph loader and carried-closure oracles passed 114 tests with 378 assertions, and the adaptive-wave and targeted-repair oracles passed 24 tests with 255 assertions. Scoped diff checks passed.

At 18:14 UTC, the coordinator's 32,768 MiB host reported 5,006 MiB of admission headroom above its 4,096 MiB floor. Its older whole read PID 24613 was still first in the memory queue for 14,336 MiB after about seven hours. The running graph-plan and its builder were older slice work. Fleet published no current generation. The old waiter never reached the changed preparation route. This audit supplies no new whole seal, adoption receipt or physical Ready completion.
