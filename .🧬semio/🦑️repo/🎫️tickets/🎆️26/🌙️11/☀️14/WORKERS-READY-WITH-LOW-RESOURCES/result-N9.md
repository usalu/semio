## 2. The mechanism (schema first, EN and DE texts)
- Protocol (`🔖️stamps/🧬️schema/🔣️.json`, admission code, independent Python oracle `🐍️.py`): new key `derived` (ids of inferrers whose effective stamp is derived), two new inferrers `input-precision` and `package-managers` (scope project, sources = the code files above). Effective stamp = `semio.graph-inferrer.<id>/1+<32 hex>` where the hex is the digest of `{answers of every corpus case, canonical digest of the declared schemas, digest of the direct import lists of the entry files}`.
- Corpus (`🔖️stamps/🧮️conformance/`): manifest `🔣️.json` (entries, schemas, fixtures, runner per provider), runners `🏃️runners/🟨️.mjs`, core `🟨️.mjs`, schema with texts. **Reuses the existing language-agnostic fixtures** (no copy): the precision fixtures `🎯️precision/🧪️tests/🔣️.json`, `🔣️glob-dialects`, `🛤️router` and the manager fixture `🔌️manager-files`, which already carry frozen expectations and independent oracles (Python, minimatch, tsc). The test reproduces those frozen expectations from the runner outputs (10 derivations, router rows and refusals, minimatch verdicts), so the corpus is not a new unchecked claim.
- Size: **input-precision 122 cases, package-managers 18 cases**; one evaluation 20 ms and 2 ms. The closure scan (transitive relative imports: 184 and 54 files) and the content hashing cost about 50 to 70 ms; a warm settle (local record) 50 to 70 ms, a cold settle 46 ms. The bootstrap of the live plugin including the settle: 1.1 s.
- Coverage law (test): the kinds are derived from the provider's own schema and tables, not from a coverage tool: the 11 `Read` kinds of the precision schema (`definitions.Read.oneOf`, first required key) and the managers of `🔣️catalog.json` plus the ecosystems of `🔣️policy.json`; every one must be exercised by a case (`coverageViolationsV1`), and a missing kind is reported as `read:newest` etc. A provider that gains a kind without a fixture fails that test.
- Where it is computed and kept (item 3): once per closure state. Key = sha256 of the content of the transitive closure of the entries, the schemas, the fixtures and the corpus code (`closureKeyV1`); record `<user cache>/semio/inference-conformance/<id>/<key>.json` (macOS `Library/Caches`, Windows `LOCALAPPDATA`, Linux `XDG_CACHE_HOME` or `~/.cache`; `conformanceDirectoryV1`). The record is verified locally on every read (well formed, belongs to its key, its stamp is the one its parts make). Fleet: `🪪️graph-state/🔖️conformance` (`conformanceFleetPortV1`) publishes and fetches the record as an entry of the fleet cache keyed by inferrer, closure key and platform; a fetched record is used only after the same local check; tested against a real local cache server (second machine takes it without evaluating; another key, another platform, offline answer nothing).
- Settled at the end of `libraryBootstrap` (`settleConformanceStampsV1`, never throws), so every process (command, resident service, farm worker) has its stamps before it infers; `projectStampPathsV1` lists a derived inferrer only while it has a stamp.

## 3. What a corpus cannot catch, and the safety nets
- A behaviour change on an input shape absent from the corpus (a branch of `deriveInputsV1` no case reaches, a new JSONC or glob construct, a platform dependent answer on Linux or Windows), a behaviour change in a transitive dependency the corpus does not exercise (the closure is hashed to decide when to evaluate again, but only answers are compared), and a new field of a manager descriptor that inference starts to consume without a case for it.
- Nets: (1) the schema digest and the direct import lists count, so a structural change always moves the stamp; (2) the coverage law above; (3) the audit: `runConformanceAuditV1({projects, infer, stored, signal, progress})` infers the sample completely again and compares the canonical digest with the stored record; `sampleNamesV1(names, count, seed)` picks a deterministic sample (new seed, new sample); `auditReportTextV1(report, "en"|"de")` and the `AuditReport` schema give the report; progress and cancellation honoured between projects; a failing inference is a `failed` entry, not an exception. **N8 can call it from the background refresh** (they supply `infer` and `stored`); a difference means the corpus missed something: add a case.
