## 1. Why "0 machine(s) of the fleet delivered" (traced, live state read 2026-11-18 00:00 UTC, read-only)

Chain of a selected read (`repo run workspace:setup`, 590 owners, an admission read) on a 16 GB worker, from the code:

1. `project-nodes/🟨️.mjs` line 540: `let ownerPool; if(!options?.ownerAdmission){ ownerPool = createOwnerPoolPublisherV1(...) }`. A selected read always has `options.ownerAdmission`, so **`ownerPool` is `undefined`**: the read never announces a wave, never says what it needs, never calls `miss`.
2. R143 wall 30 opened the fleet PORT (look, claim, publish in the reader process) for admission reads and left the pool off ("the farm, look queue, owner pool and warm pass stay off for them"); law `🤝️selected-fleet` pins exactly that (`let ownerPool;if(!options?.ownerAdmission){`).
3. The reader infers what fits in its own process (589 of 590 in the m01 log), the one heavy owner (`@semio-tech/norm-js`, about 4842 MB, alone cap of a 16 GB reader 4096 MB) goes to `never`. After the loop `inferAll` ends with `waitHeavy()` (line 604-617): `if(takeScope===undefined||ownerPool===undefined) throw refuseHeavy(...)`: with `ownerPool` undefined it **refuses on the spot**, with `producers = Object.keys(fleetPort.producers()).length` = the machines whose records THIS read fetched. `0` here does not say "no machine is alive": it says "none of this read's records came from the fleet", which is true because nobody produces admission records.
4. Even if a reader announced, **a pool child could not produce the record**: children build the job with `ownerJobV1` (`selectedTargets` undefined, `selectedInputs = []`, no admission) and publish under the key `projectRecordKeyV1(head, root, json, undefined, [])`; the admission reader looks up `projectRecordKeyV1(head, root, json(generated dependencies applied), selectedTargets, selectedInputs)`. Different key text, different claim digest: a record a child stores is never found by a selected reader.
5. The waves that exist (`.cache/f/owner-pool-waves/*`, 6 waves of 4738 to 4775 owners, all published by g1-server `ueli-mac:28979|42303`, newest 23:55:33 UTC) are waves of FULL reads. They contain `@semio-tech/norm-js` with **no `megabytes`** (0 of 4774 units carry a cost: the publisher's own cost table `.cache/f/graph-costs/owner-costs.3.json` has no row for norm-js, and the table is per machine). A unit of unknown cost is "heavy" for nobody (`ownerPoolHeavyV1`), so no puller takes it first: it waits at a random place of a 4774-owner list that the fleet works at about 25 owners per minute in all (`pool.json` of the nine machines: claimed 2 to 355, `ownersPerMinute` 0 to 7.4).
6. Every worker pool is alive and on current code (all origins `pool.json` updated within the last minute, state `pulling`, 5 to 13 children each, except g1-m02 `idle`/`nothing-fits`, g1-m06 pointing at an old wave 2026-11-16, g1-m04 at an old wave 11-16, g1-m09 without any `pool.json`). The reasons they are slow are their own work (`yielding: 0 of 10 cores idle`, `nothing-fits: 0 MB above the 2048 MB floor`).

So the fleet has capacity but the chain is cut at three places: no announce (1), no admission-capable child (4), no cost on the unit (5); and the reader refuses before it could wait (3).

## 2. What was built (staged and tested in scratch before the window; patches `🧰️scripts/R164/patches.py`, staging `stage.py`, battery `battery.py`, landing `window.py`)

New member `🌐️owner-pool/🤝️fleet` (schema, `🟦️.ts`, 6 tests, imported by nothing before the landing): the fleet view. `ownerPoolReadFleetV1(store)` reads every `<store>/v1/origins/*/pool.json`; `ownerPoolProducersV1(machines, need, now)` = alive (status younger than 60 s, pool not off/too small) and child grant >= need, the most free grant first; `ownerPoolTurnMillisecondsV1` = rank x 6 s (largest free machine at once, the next after its turn, a machine that cannot hold the owner has no turn); `ownerPoolNoProducerTextV1` = EN/DE refusal text naming the sizes of the fleet ("1 x 32 GB (one owner up to 9830 MB), ...").

Landing patch (anchored, 9 files, restore tested byte-identical on a scratch mirror):
1. **A selected read announces and is served.** `project-nodes/🟨️.mjs`: `let ownerPool;if(!options?.ownerAdmission){` -> unconditional; every unit of the wave carries `admission {targets, inputs, generated}` (schema `Unit.admission`, wave id includes it, so two admissions of the same owners are two waves). New shared functions `ownerAdmissionSelectionV1`, `ownerAdmissionCompilesV1` (the reader's own expressions, moved) and `ownerAdmissionOfV1`; `ownerJobV1` builds the admission job from them (selectedTargets, selectedInputs, native/pipeline of the selected targets, generated dependencies), `produceProjectRecordV1` and the pool driver key the record by `job.selectedTargets` (null for a full read: unchanged) and infer with `admission` true. Reader and puller build the job from the same functions, so the record key is the same by construction.
2. **The need travels.** Publisher method `ask(units, forced)` publishes a wave of exactly the owners the reader cannot fit, each with `megabytes` = its known footprint (or the reader's own solo limit + 1), apart from the big wave (own id, own keep-alive). `waitHeavy` calls it before waiting and with every renew.
3. **Largest free machine first.** Runner writes `totalMegabytes` and `grantMegabytes` (hard child limit, 30 percent of the machine, at least 3072) to `pool.json` (schema `Status`); the child passes `turn(unit)` to `pullOwnerPoolV1`: a heavy unit (>= 2048 MB) is not claimed (nor walked into by the ordinary walk) before the puller's turn has passed since it first saw it.
4. **A named, bounded wait, no refusal while a producer lives.** `waitHeavy`: refuses at once only when the fleet is known and NO alive machine has a grant for the need (refusal names the fleet sizes, EN and DE, appended to the owner list); otherwise it waits with the existing bounds (30 min without a record arriving, 6 h in all) and the "silent" end (5 min) is held off while any fitting producer's status moves.

## 4. Why one machine reading and eight fetching does not happen for selected reads (analysis, not built)
- `loadBuildGraphOnceV1` sends every selection other than `all` (default `SEMIO_BUILD_GRAPH_SLICE_READ` unset) to `loadBuildGraphUnpinnedV1(..., baseline:true)`: the attempt loop with `scope = buildGraphScopeKeyV1(selection, registry)` that stages `selected/<scope>.json` and its authority in the machine's own graph store. The only users of `remoteGenerationExchangeV1`/`publishGenerationV1`/`restoreGenerationV1` are the generation host (`🕸️graph/🕰️host`) and its wave upload, i.e. the path of `all` and of the opt-in `generation` slice read. A scope key exists in the exchange (`generationExchangeKeyV1(..., scope)`, R137), but nothing calls it for a selected attempt, so a machine that sealed a selected graph never says so and no other machine looks.
- What every machine therefore repeats: the start phase (admission closure, 150 to 390 s on a loaded 16 GB machine, R143 wall 35) and the take pass over the 590 owner records (cheap when the records are in the fleet cache). The owner records ARE shared (R143 wall 30); with this landing the heavy ones can now be produced by a machine that fits them.
- Design for the missing piece (not built here, needs a real two-machine run to be proven): after `loadBuildGraphAttemptV1` seals, upload `selected/<scope>.json` + authority receipt (with `seal.owners` digests, R156) as one content-addressed document under a head keyed by (identity, platform, scope, origin); before an attempt, fetch the best head (`rankGenerationHeadsV1`), accept per owner exactly the owners whose digests hold against the local tree, and run the normal attempt only for the rest (records of those come from the fleet). That removes the start phase for eight of nine machines but not the per-owner acceptance.
