# Graph Slice Capacity Custody

The resident slice holds a host reservation while its native worker and its descendants are active. The admission signal used to remove that booking as soon as it was cancelled, ahead of the resident cleanup path. A private file-backed host gate proved that a competing 20,000 MiB build entered while the cancelled 20,000 MiB slice still held its termination latch on the 32,768 MiB fixture host. SQLite independently confirmed that the two reservations could not coexist. The signal now cancels only the admission wait. An issued reservation is released explicitly after worker cleanup.

Two startup cases hit the same ownership boundary. A cancellation during asynchronous process-limit creation still started a worker before the repair. A synchronous worker constructor refusal kept both untransferred allocations. The resident caller rechecks the signal before launching, keeps both allocations under its own custody until worker creation succeeds, and disposes of them if launch never takes ownership. Native worker cleanup still owns the release after a successful launch. The original error and exact cancellation reason are preserved.

All three red cases reproduced independently. The 10-file host-capacity and slice suite passed 65 tests and 433 assertions on the final run. Two existing graph-cut cases hit Bun's default five-second test timeout on the first combined run. The unchanged assertions passed on the rerun with a 60-second test-runner timeout.
