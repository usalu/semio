## Verdicts

1. **When a generation is uploaded: only at the end of a complete read, never after a wave (PROVEN in code).** `loadGenerationGraphV1` (`GEN/🔌️load/🟨️.mjs`) calls `publishRemoteGenerationV1` at three places: after a shared read that found the generation current (`ifAbsent`: only when the cache holds none), after attaching to a peer's builder (`ifAbsent`), and once after the builder loop ended (`if(published)`). The per-wave hook `host.published` only prints the wave line and yields. The server's read never ends (4 000+ projects, restarted by every landing), so it uploads almost never: its service log (`host-89ee733bc283.log`, since 11:50 yesterday) has exactly two uploads, generation 97 and 104 (`5104 documents (47 MB) in 32 s`, `(46 MB) in 25 s`). The production cache says the same: the provenance of the server's head key shows 12 writes between 08:45 and 11:23:48 UTC yesterday and none since (about 14 h).
2. **Which head the workers take, and why 60 (PROVEN from the stored documents).** Heads are per origin (`generationExchangeKeyV1(identity, platform, origin)`); the reader took the head with the newest `publishedMs` (`remoteNote`), i.e. the newest UPLOAD time of any origin. Heads in the production cache at 01:29 UTC (`T/🧰️scripts/P13/lab/heads.py`, read-only): m06 generation 73 (5 100 owners, uploaded 00:55:43, 33 min old), m03 generation 67 (00:48:59, 40 min), nothing of `g1-server` (its file is gone from `v1/main/ac/17/`; the collector drops action results whose blobs left the content store, the likely cause, NOT proven), and the old `semio`-instance head 72 (06:10 yesterday, 19 h). m03 rewrote its head at 11:24, 19:18 and 00:48, m06 at 00:55: the newest head was always a WORKER's. The number in `generation 60 … restored` is not the server's counter: restore renumbers to `max(local, producer) + 1` and the producer's number is that machine's own counter (m03 59, m06 73, server 104/206): numbers of different origins cannot be compared. A worker that uploaded after any completed read therefore shadowed the server.
3. **Cost of a restore and of an upload (measured).** Restore (worker logs, 8 samples): 5 103 to 5 104 documents, 48 MB, downloaded in 6 to 17 s; every restore downloads all documents (not changed by this package). A delta upload of one 48-owner wave (generation 210 against 209, read-only measurement program on the live store): 50 documents, 17.3 MB read (manifest, fact index 10.8 MB, frontier, 48 fragments 0.18 MB), 2.3 s CPU, peak footprint 1.4 GB, against 5 104 documents / 46 MB / 25 to 32 s for the full upload; most of the delta is the fact index, which changes in every wave. The documents are content addressed, the cache is asked once which of the 5 104 it misses (`content.missing`), and only those are written.
4. **The finding of P12 needs one correction.** "The fleet generation is ten hours old" was true while the worker heads were 6 to 19 h old; since 00:48 two workers completed reads and uploaded. The structural cause stays: the freshness of the fleet's head depends on who happens to finish a read.

## What was built (tests first, schema first, EN and DE; all green on the overlay)

- **Head ranking by state, not by upload time.** Note schema (`GEN/🌐️remote-exchange/🧬️schema/🔣️.json`) gains `origin`, `stateMs` (when the producer last changed its generation store: the time of the tree state, never the time of the upload), `implementation` (digest of the code that built it) and optional `stale` (owners the producer knew stale, 0 after a complete read, absent = unmeasured); Policy gains `unmeasuredStale` 500, `staleOwnersPerMinute` 1, `ownersPerChangedPath` 0.25, `shadowMarginOwners` 50, `servingTotalMegabytes` 24000 (equals the graph server's `serveTotalMegabytes`, tested), `rankCandidates` 3. `generationHeadScoreV1` = owners the reader would find stale = `stale` (all owners when the reader's code differs, `unmeasuredStale` when unknown) + drift since the head's state (changed paths x 0.25 when the reader measured them, else age in minutes x 1); `rankGenerationHeadsV1` orders by score, then newest state, then origin name. A reader with a snapshot port (`cursors.distance`, wired in `🕰️host`: set difference between the head's snapshot and the reader's manifest, the cursor S/X introduced) downloads the snapshots of the best 3 heads and uses the measured distance; any miss falls back to the estimate. `ahead()` stays a probe (no download).
- **No shadowing.** `publishGenerationV1` skips with `remote-better` (renamed from `remote-newer`) when the best head of ANOTHER origin leaves a reader as few stale owners or fewer; a machine under the serving size (`os.totalmem` below 24 000 MB, or `serving:false`) must beat it by the margin of 50 owners. "current" now compares `stateMs` (an old state of another origin with equal signature no longer counts as current). A reader that holds a generation of its own code keeps it against a newer head built by other code (`keepsLocalV1`), and the load now passes its `implementation` to restore/ahead and `stale:0` to every upload of a complete read.
- **The upload follows the waves.** New member `GEN/📡️wave-upload/` (schema with Policy, State and bilingual Texts; module; tests). `createGenerationUploaderV1`: `wave(row)` notes the stale count of a published wave (`total - done`), `tick()` (never awaited) starts at most ONE upload at a time of the newest sealed generation when it is not the one looked at last and 3 minutes passed since the last look (so it is debounced and always the newest), a failure backs off 2 minutes and never reaches the caller, a generation the cache refused is looked at again after 10 minutes, the state (`wave-upload.json` next to the steering files) survives a restart, `stop()` aborts the upload in flight. `localGenerationUploadSourceV1` reads the pointer of the local store and publishes with a fresh exchange per attempt (a failed exchange disables itself for its lifetime).
- **Where it runs: the graph server's agent, not the read.** `🌐️remote-execution/🛰️sidecar/🕸️graph/🟦️.ts`: `inferGraphReadV1` hands every wave row to `onWave`; `runGraph` creates the uploader only on a serving machine (>= 24 000 MB), ticks it every server round (5 s), allows it only under normal memory pressure with >= 4 096 MB available, gives the server's snapshots to heads (`snapshotFor` from the journal cursor store), and stops it in `finally`. So the upload costs the read nothing: another process, the delta only (verdict 3), bounded by the documents the wave changed.
- **One line more in the worker's `restored` line** (both languages, schema and mjs kept equal): `…restored from the remote cache, 5104 documents (48 MB) downloaded in 9 s, from a state 4 min old with 38 projects stale` / `…, von einem Stand von vor 4 Min. mit 38 veralteten Projekten`.
- Also fixed on the way: a dead cache with several origins read as "absent" (no `down` flag, a timeout per load); now it throws when every head read fails.

## 3. What a worker will see after this lands

`graph: generation N (5101 projects) restored from the remote cache, 5104 documents (48 MB) downloaded in 6 to 17 s, from a state A min old with S projects stale`, then `refreshing K projects`.
- N = the server's local generation + 1 (about 211 and rising, one number per wave); the number is no longer comparable with the old 60.
- A: the debounce (3 min) + the wave interval (recent waves took 53 to 295 s) + the upload (5 to 30 s) = 3 to 9 minutes, never hours; A is printed.
- S, K: S is what the server's read still has open at that wave, K = S + edits of the last minutes (about 1 owner per minute measured over a day). In steady state (server converged between landings) that is tens, as expected. **Right after the landing it is NOT tens**: the three scripts change files that are in the closure of every project, so the implementation digest changes, all 4 100 projects are stale for every machine (the server's own wave lines say `441 of 4123`), and every head (old code: counted as all owners stale) is worse than the server's wave heads. The ranking then picks the server's wave generation (stale 3 600 falling by 48 to 960 per wave), and only after about 10 to 15 waves (40 to 60 minutes) are S and K in the tens. A worker restarted inside that window sees thousands, as before.
