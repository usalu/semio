  - **Not fixed, larger:** 601 `file` rows (whole-content sha) are held by 1 000 or more projects, about 20 of them by **all 4 739**: `build/📥️inputs/🎯️precision/*`, `⚡️caching/🚀️bootstrap/📦️dependencies/🔌️managers/**` (catalog, policy, npm, bun, cargo, go, uv, dotnet), `🏃️process/🔌️providers/📋️project.json`, and 4 300 projects hold the `Cargo.toml` of each framework crate (`🧰️scripts/N6/filerows.ts 1000`). These are the inference code's own imports read as content; they belong under the declared inference stamp (one row), not under a per-project content row. Not built.
- **What a cargo target consumes from the tool files** (answer to U's prerequisite): of the four providers a cargo target's owner holds rows of `script-provider-ports` 1 359, `command-imports` 1 359, `command-references` 1 359 (one per file of the cargo script's walk) and **no** `module-imports` row (that one is only read by script-backed generic targets, `closurePackagesV1`, for the external packages). What depends on the content of a tool script: the roster of its import closure (imports), the ports it defines, the URL and runtime references. The inferred shape of a cargo target (inputs list of the closure) depends on the roster only. The task hash legitimately depends on content and stays untouched (it is computed from the named input group at run time).
- **Why the owners were stale at all:** a project's walk is already named by one derived fact (`{"walk":1,…}`, its support held by a pseudo owner), but `resolve()` also gave the owner the same raw rows (`entry.rows`, inlined small layers): for `hub-directory-rs` 28 000 of its 29 664 rows (4 077 of 4 091 analysis rows) are also support of the two derived facts it holds. A changed import row therefore staled the owner without asking the derived fact whether the roster changed. Reproduced in a fixture (a script-backed target: 86 shared rows) and fixed: `resolve()` now leaves out of the owner's rows every row a held walk fact supports (not `program` facts, whose raw tsconfig row is deliberate: its test says so). Rows stay equal to the old result minus the support (test), also through the row log used by the generation build (new `exclude` on the builder).
- **Low-capacity read:** policy, request, serve and wait are built and tested against a real cache server on a free port; **not wired into `repo show` / the generation build**, so there is no end-to-end number. Measured inputs: one wave of 48 projects holds 7.1 GB in the parent (U: 7 121 and 7 172 MB), verification 1.0 to 2.1 GB. With the policy a machine admitted 4 096 MB infers 18 projects per wave and at most 72 before it asks the fleet; 6 553 MB: 41 per wave; 7 200 MB or more: the full 48 (192). A 16 GB worker with 4.1 to 6.5 GB and 4 700 stale projects therefore asks the fleet for all 4 700 (test) instead of starting 97 waves.
- Why `waited for another machine` is 0: the wait fires only when a second machine asks for the same project key while the first holds the claim (`decideGraphEntryV1`; the project-records test proves it with two ports). On the server nobody else was running a graph read, and `seeded` 0 because the server's own records exist locally. It is not broken; it never had a concurrent second reader. The fleet will not take projects until a second machine reads.
