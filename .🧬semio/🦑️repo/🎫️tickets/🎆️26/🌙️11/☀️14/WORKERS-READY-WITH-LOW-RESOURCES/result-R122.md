## 2. The rule, made absolute

The parent of a read never infers an owner, never walks closures, never runs TypeScript resolution, never derives a closure entry and never resolves the closure references of an owner while a farm exists. Every such job is a farm job; when no child can take it, the job waits in the queue and says why (one bilingual line per owner and minute, `queuedJobTextV1`).

Mechanism (schema first: `👁️observation/🚧️read-parent/🧬️schema/🔣️.json`):
- Guard `👁️observation/🚧️read-parent/🟨️.mjs`: `markReadParentV1(alive)` marks the process while the farm exists; `assertNotReadParentV1(kind, what)` throws `graph.read-parent-computes` (EN and DE) in a marked parent. Called in `inferProjectBodyV1` (infer), `deriveClosureFactV1` (walk), `resolutionConfigsV1` (resolve), the derive of `produceClosureEntryV1` (derive) and `resolveClosureRefsV1` (refs). The read marks its parent right before its lanes start and unmarks in its `finally`; `farm.exists()` is false once the farm is off, closing, aborted or has no usable and no pending pool, so a dead farm still lets the parent finish (loudly) instead of hanging.
- Farm (`project-farm/🟨️.mjs`): `immediate` starts the children at the creation of the farm (path 1); `prepare` waits without a time limit for the first child while the farm exists (paths 4 and 5), retries a failed, lost or empty job on another child up to 3 times (empty: 6 times, 2.5 s apart, for the racy window) and then throws `graph.farm-job-failed` naming the owner and the reason (path 6); `observe` (verdicts and references) waits for a child without a limit and answers undefined only when the farm is gone (paths 7 and 11); new `exists()`.
- Observation (`verdictFinal`, `prepareAttempts`): a `stale`, `other` or `missing` verdict of a child is final, the reader settles and verifies nothing (path 7); the farm is asked up to 3 times to prepare a record before the reader would compute it (path 8). Both are options of `observeProjectInferenceV1` and `observeInference`; without a farm nothing changes.
- Project nodes: the child verdict is asked for every lookup of an owner with a farm (take or not), the child resolves the closure references of every owner (`askOwnerRefsV1`, second ask when the first verdict gave none; a source that does not parse is answered as data and the parent withholds the owner from it without a derivation; a failure is named and asked again, then refused) (path 9); the shared closure prime runs in the capped prime child (`primeSharedClosuresInChildV1`) (path 10); the prepare job and the observe job carry the verdict directory of the read.
- First derivation of a shared closure: `claimDerivationV1` in script-inputs: the first process of a read that needs a missing or stale entry takes a `claim-<digest>` file in the verdict directory of the read and derives it; the other children poll the store for 100 ms ticks (up to 120 s) and reuse the stored entry; the claim is released when the derivation ends, so a later stale entry is claimed anew.

Laws (red before, green after; `🧪️tests` of each member): `🚧️read-parent` (guard, 2), project-nodes `🚧️read-parent` (every walk/derive/resolve/infer/refs function refuses in a marked parent; references asked of a child; source asserts, 4), project-farm `🚧️no-inline` (5: immediate start, silent pool queues with the reason, three failures refuse, observe waits unbounded, observe refuses after three failures), observation `🚧️final-verdict` (3). Existing laws that encoded the old rules were rewritten: farm main (3 tests: a pool that never starts now queues, adopted owners answer true), `hub` (a second failure now refuses), `first-child`, `captured`, `wave-tally` (a lost job now refuses after 3 tries; the meter reports `lost: 3`).

Lanes: `planGraphFarmV1` already takes the smaller of owners/6, cores minus the reserve (9 on this 10-core Mac) and the memory plan (`farmChildFootprintMegabytes`: typical 1720, cap 2500, alone 4200 MB, tree share 40 percent). With `immediate` the 9 children are asked for at the start of the read. The real limit on this host is the grant, not the plan: see the open points.

## 4. Not verified, open points

- No real read ran with this code. The live read (pid 5001, parent 4624) runs the old code and must be restarted by the coordinator; the farm of a restarted read now starts its children at the creation of the farm and every owner waits for them.
- Measured: nothing on a real read. The harness proves the rule (a marked parent refuses every walk, derive, resolve, infer and refs function; a silent pool queues with the reason; three failures refuse), not the throughput. The expected effect: parent CPU for closure work goes to about 0, the event loop of the parent is no longer held by walks (the 18 s lag came from them), throughput is that of the children.
- The host grant remains the limit: on the live host the farm got ONE worker because the read itself reserves 14 336 MB as class `projects` (200 percent of its learned size, 5.3 GB resident in reality) and the host had -467 to -1456 MB spare above its floor. With the parent no longer computing, that reservation should shrink: next lever (host-capacity `show`/`graph` class sizing), not touched here. Until then a restarted read has one child and every owner queues for it, with the queue lines naming the reason; the progress watch ends a read that makes no progress at all.
- Assembly authority of native and release owners (`projectAssemblyAuthorityV1`, 130 s over 23 owners in the live read) still runs in the parent: R121 step 2 measured that replaying its rows in the parent costs more than computing them. It hashes files; it walks no closure and runs no TypeScript resolution.
- A job that no child can do ends the read with `graph.farm-job-failed` (owner and reason, EN and DE) instead of being inferred in the parent; a source modified within the racy window is retried 5 times 2.5 s apart before that.
- When the farm is gone for good (identity refused, host granted none, every pool disabled, cache declined the leaves) the parent infers as before and says so (`exists()` is false then); the rule is "while a farm exists".
