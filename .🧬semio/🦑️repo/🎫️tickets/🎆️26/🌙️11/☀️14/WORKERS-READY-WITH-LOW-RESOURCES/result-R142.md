## 2. Causes, with confidence

(d) **Proven by the code: the no-progress watch could not fire in the tail.** The sign of `createProgressWatchV1` contained `looks.count`, raised in `finally` of every `look()`; the poll `takePollV1` (rounds every 2 to 20 s while owners are missing, batches of 48) and the wait for heavy owners (`awaitWantedV1` calls `take` -> `look` for every name every 2 s) raise it without any owner arriving. So the sign changed for ever, `graph.no-progress` never fired, and with it the R135 back-off (`stage-stall-exhausted`) never started; the progress line `taken N of M` kept printing. Also: the status line has no reason, `reportParkedOwnersV1` only names owners that run with 0 rows.

(c) **Proven: heavy owners were neither reported nor failed on time.** `never` owners (recorded cost above the solo limit of this machine, e.g. wfc-bitmap, flow-flow-rs) are only waited for; the only end was `awaitWantedV1` (5 min silence, 30 min stall, 6 h) and it throws `owner-too-heavy` only from inside `waitHeavy`; a read that is not yet in `waitHeavy` (loops still parked in the governor) never reached it.

(a) **Likely, not proven: pool and read starve each other.** `ownerPoolSizingV1` yields to a waiting request of the machine's own work only while `yieldAfter (5 s) <= waited < yieldUntil (600 s)`; after 10 minutes the pool deliberately "comes back" (fixture case "a request older than 10 min was not held up by the children: the pool comes back"). A solo owner of the read that waits longer than that for 4 GB or more then competes with 7 to 10 pool children of ~0.9 to 1.1 GB each, and each pool child that ends is replaced. Evidence for the state: m02/m10 `nothing-fits` with 2457 MB "held for the machine's own work" and 7 to 10 children; m01/m03 `pressure`. Not seen: a pool status with reason `waiting`. I did not change the pool: the 10-minute rule is an explicit, tested decision, the read's wait is not necessarily a gate waiter in the pool's view, and a wrong change would stop production on eight machines. Proposal for a lab proof first: a tail marker written by the read when its only runnable owners are solo ones (`.cache/f/owner-pool-tail`, owner, megabytes, age) that the pool honours with target 0 until the owner has run or the marker is older than a bound; to be validated in the slice lab with `--memory=8192 --cores=4`.

(b) **Not established.** Lease take-over exists for the inference path (`graphHeldVerdictV1`: `compute` for a holder that renews for the lease time, `lapsed`), but `never` owners are never computed by their reader, so a claim held by another machine's pool for such an owner is only waited for. The store has no claim table; the m02 pool shows `held: 1`, the m06 pool `held 43`, `running 0` (units 9075): that machine holds 43 owners it does not run. Needs the cache server's claim listing to decide.
