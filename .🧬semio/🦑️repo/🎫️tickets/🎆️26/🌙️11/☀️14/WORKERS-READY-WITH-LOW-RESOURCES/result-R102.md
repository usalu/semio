# R102: why 3 % of a wave is open, what sets a worker's inference lanes, and where one owner spends its minutes

Scripts: `T/🧰️scripts/R102/` (T = `☀️13/FLEET-ONE-MACHINE-SHARED-HOT-CACHE-AND-REMOTE-EXECUTION`): `land.py` (anchored edits, `--apply`), `restore.py`, `window.sh`, `runall.sh`, `overlay.ts`. New tests: `owner-pool/🧪️tests/🌍️spread`, `project-nodes/🧪️tests/🛣️lanes`.

## (a) Why only about 3 % of a wave is claimable
Nothing gates the other 97 %. Read in code: `pullOwnerPoolV1` claims an owner by the digest of its own record state only; there is no dependency order between owners, no window and no wait on a reader (a wave's units are published by `createOwnerPoolPublisherV1.units()` for every owner the reader has not taken). R101 measured 328 of 330 random claims answering `stored`: 97 % of the owners already have a record in the fleet cache, they are done, not blocked. The cost of that is in the puller, three things:
1. It ended its pull far too early. `doneJumps` 3 with `gallopRun` 4 means 12 stored answers in a row end the pull ("the wave is done"). With 3 % open, 0.97^12 = 69 % of the pulls end after 12 claims while about a thousand owners are open; the supervisor looks again only after `reopenMilliseconds` (1 to 10 min). Simulation (old code, one puller, 2000 owners, 2 % open): published 1 of 40.
2. It examined at most 2 owners per round trip (window 4, but `slots + prefetch - running` = 2 in a one-slot child), each 0.24 to 1.5 s: finding one open owner among stored ones took 0.5 to 1.5 min.
3. Heavy owners (megabytes >= 2048) were taken first in LIST order by every puller, so N machines met on the same two or three heavy owners and the losers got `held`. Simulation (8 pullers, 3 % open, 6 heavy): after the first round only 4 distinct owners ran.

## Change (a)
- `ownerPoolRendezvousV1(puller, name)`: score in [0,1) from the digest of both; heavy owners that fit the puller's grant (`fits`, so a machine whose plan limit cannot hold an owner never claims it) are taken in the order of this score. The `puller` id is already unique per child (`host:pid:tick:time`), so N children walk N orders. The light walk keeps its seeded random start and contiguous neighbours (neighbours share imports; the fixture of the walk equals the Python oracle and is unchanged).
- `doneJumps` is replaced by `doneStoredMinimum` 192 (stored answers in a row; 0.97^192 = 0.3 % to miss a 3 % open set) and a run of stored answers of `2 x claimWindow` widens one round to `claimWindowMaximum` 16 claims (only while nothing is ready, so no hoarding).
- Not done: "largest free memory first" across machines needs the other machines' grants (a weighted rendezvous over published `pool.json` free memory); today a machine only skips what does not fit.

## (b) What sets the lanes of a worker's own read
`limit` in `project-nodes/🟨️.mjs` is `min(projectConcurrencyV1(options), jobs)`, and `projectConcurrencyV1` is `min(min(8, cores), SEMIO_GRAPH_CUT_CONCURRENCY)`. Nobody passes `projectConcurrency`; the cut ladder sets the variable (`slice-pool`): a 6.5 GB plan starts at level 1 (4), and each "plan stage grew to 9127 MB, more than the 6553 MB" restart goes one level up, halving to 1 at level 3. The workers' `slice-memory.json` (`limit 1`, `activeCount 1`, `warm.lanes 1`) is that. The governor (R97 admission) already admits owners by measured footprint, so the cut was a second, blind cap on top of it.
Change: `inferenceLanesV1({cores, cut, parentMegabytes, ownerMegabytes, explicit})` = min(cores <= 8, max(cut, floor(parent growth budget / p75 of the owners' known megabytes, default 800))), at least 1; explicit still wins. `projectConcurrencyV1` stays (cut tests unchanged).

## The slow step of one owner on a worker
`ready-log-workspace.txt` lines "owners N of M; running (...)" are gone (the logs were rewritten by restarts; only `Reading the project graph slice` lines remain). Evidence from `slice-memory.json` of g1-m02 at 2075 s: `cpuSeconds user 1643 + system 251` in 2075 s of uptime, i.e. the parent is saturated on ONE core while the machine is idle; `looks.count 90`, average 22 s, slowest 216 s; phases `lookup.settle` 2.16 million ms over 165 lookups (13 s each, `settledReads` 6176 entries, 205 MB), `lookup.gate` 1.03 million ms, `observe` 1.27 million ms for 13 finished owners (about 97 s each); active owner `procedural-generation2d-rs` 497 s. So the slow step is not the owner's own inference (7 to 8 s on the server in a separate child): it is the look/settle work of the take pass (stale closure entries derived again, 21 217 of them on m01) running on the same event loop as the single inference lane, which starves it. R101 landing 2 (verdict by the provider instead of the cache) targets the settle cost; the extra lanes do not add event loops (the farm children still do the CPU work), so this is only proven once a worker runs the new code.

## Not verified
