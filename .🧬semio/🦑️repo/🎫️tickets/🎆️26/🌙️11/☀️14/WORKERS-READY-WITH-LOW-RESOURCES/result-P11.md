## Verdicts

1. **The first wave after a start is long because the read offers every fresh priority project in it, not because it verifies.** Measured on the start at 23:08:50 (read 10787, 3 requests held, priority list of about 1 500 names): wave 195 at 23:25:16, **986 s**, farm "11 of 503 projects": 503 = the 48 projects of the wave plus **455 fresh priority projects of the requesters** that `offerFreshPriorityV1` (called by `discoverPart` of EVERY wave, `🤝️prefetch`) looks up and infers before the wave's own loop so that their records reach the fleet cache. The set of already offered names (`offeredFreshV1`) lives only in the process, so a restarted read offers all of them again, and it never knows that the wave before it looked up the very same projects itself.
2. **Proof that the offer pass is the difference.** Farm count (projects sent to `prepare`) minus the size of the wave, wave by wave:

   | wave | read kind | wave size | farm "of M" | offered = M − size | seconds since the last | compute minus (setup + projects + tail) |
   |---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
   | 183 to 189 (7 waves) | refresh, no request held | 48 | 48 | 0 | 280, 82, 53, 259, 263, 136, 121 (mean 171) | not printed yet |
   | 190 | request, first wave | 48 | 207 | 159 | 1 066 | not printed |
   | 191 | request, first wave | 48 | 95 | 47 | 302 | 124 s (160 − 36) |
   | 192 | request | 48 | 96 | 48 = wave 191 | 396 (of which 272 s pause, below) | 49 s (104 − 55) |
   | 193 | request | 120 | 168 | 48 = wave 192 | 118 | 30 s (81 − 51) |
   | 194 | request | 120 | 240 | 120 = wave 193 | 231 | 110 s (183 − 73) |
   | 195 | request, first wave | 48 | 503 | 455 | 986 | **365 s** (665 − 300) |

   Without a priority list (refresh reads) the farm count equals the wave size in 7 of 7 waves and nothing is offered. With one, every wave offers exactly the projects of the wave before it (192, 193, 194: 48, 48, 120 = the sizes of 191, 192, 193 and 193 offers what 192 did, nothing else), and the first wave offers everything fresh. The seconds follow: about 0.6 to 1.0 s per offered project (192: 1.0, 193: 0.6, 194: 0.9, 195: 0.8 s). The samples agree: the main thread of the pre-part (23:15:56 to 23:19:20, 39 samples of 4 s) has the signature of project lookups and inference (native other 44 %, JSON stringify 15 %, JIT 14 %, collection 9 %, brotli 7 %, sha256 7 %, Map/Set 4 %, utf8 decode 4 %), the same as the part's own project loop (23:19:30 to 23:22:50: other 35, JIT 15, stringify 9, sha 8, brotli 7, file system 7, collection 5, Map/Set 5, readFile 5 %), and NOT the signature of the verification passes P9 sampled in the 22:09 read (JIT 1 %, JSON 35 %, utf8 decode 26 %).

   Memory (physical resident size of the read, polled every 10 s from 7:04 after the start): 12.06 GB flat (reader, plan, prime; the first poll already saw it; the point where it got there is not measured), then **12.1 to 17.4 GB within 40 s at the end of the offer pass** (23:18:46 to 23:19:27), 16.5 to 17.4 GB during the part, 13.0 GB parent after the wave, managed 4.7 GB after the collection, peak 15.3 GB, 57 collections.    Reads without a priority list start in 150 to 290 s; reads with one start in 314 to 1 066 s, in the order of the number offered. 5. **A second finding: the pause between waves is not in the stages.** `since the last` contains the yield of the background read (`yieldBackgroundGraphV1`, up to 20 minutes while another command of this machine waits): wave 192 took 124 s of work and 272 s of pause; after wave 194 (22:56:01) the read sat at 0.2 to 2 % CPU for the 4 minutes I watched (22:57 to 23:01; 3 s `sample`: 2 096 of 2 118 samples in `kevent64`, workers idle) and published nothing until the process was replaced at 23:08 (12 minutes). Seen on the machine: nothing in `build-agent.log` names the pause. The new wave line prints it (`yield`).
6. **What the read still does that cannot be attributed from outside:** the 207 s before `buildOnce` (and the 744 s of the 22:09 read) are the reader attempt (membership stat pass, plan, verification), the prefetch of stale projects, the wait for the builder custody, the start of the process, and, when the tree moved meanwhile, a second plan (`planUnchanged` is false while 50 machines edit). Which of them it is can only be read from the next start: the wave line now prints `start` (seconds from process start to the first build), `start.reader`, `start.prefetch`, `membership`, `plan.index`, `plan.facts`, `plan.settle`. I did not guess: no change was made to verification.

## Expected numbers (model from the table above; the live confirmation is the next wave lines)

- First wave of a start with a long priority list (today 986 s): the 365 s outside the part shrink to the cutoff plus at most 48 offers (about 40 s): **about 660 s**; the start after that (names persisted, wave looked up) offers nothing new: about 600 s. Reads without a priority list: unchanged (150 to 290 s).
- Waves of a request read after the first: 30 to 110 s less per wave (waves 192 to 194: 231 s → about 120 s; 124 s of work → about 75 s), because the wave before is no longer offered.
- A refresh in which every project is a hit: **not a few minutes.** Per wave a fixed 30 to 40 s (prime 4, patch 16, setup after B1 about 1, pack 1, cutoff and release collections 5 to 10) plus 0.3 to 0.6 s per project (waves 191 to 194: 0.6, 0.4, 0.4, 0.6 s): 4 500 projects are about 30 to 45 minutes. The per-project hit cost (a stored record is brotli-decoded, hashed, parsed, its rows interned into the row log and written into the index again) is the remaining term and needs the support key above plus a carry-over of unchanged owners; it is not touched here.
