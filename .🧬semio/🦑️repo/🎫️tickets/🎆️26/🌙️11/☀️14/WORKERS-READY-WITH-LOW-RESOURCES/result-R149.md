## What the measurements say (walls 44 to 46)

44. **Closure and read are redone on every peer edit of the build implementation itself.** The graph observes its own implementation sources as inputs; a read of 10 to 25 minutes under 8 machines of editors is invalidated several times (run4: settle conflict at about 630 s, then three more closures; run5: `publication-facts` retry and `graph-repair` twice). The retries are cheap for owners (per-owner memo `observeOwnerFactsMemoizedV1` works: inference 88.6 -> 9.3 -> 4.3 s, closure 67 -> 17 -> 10 s) but the whole-scope admission memo missed every time (`admission-memo hit:false`), and each pass still costs the fixed parts (source admission 15 to 54 s, command-source imports, `build:graph`, publication 20 s). Status: OPEN. Not fixable by a local rule without changing what the graph observes; on a quiet tree (nobody editing `🏗️build`) the fixed parts are what remains. Proposal: derive the implementation identity of a read once per (service, code digest) and let an edit of an implementation file hand over at a round boundary (wall 46) instead of failing the publication facts.
45. **590, not 57, is the closure of the three probes** (see Run 1 finding): `norm-din4108-rs` pulls about 530 Rust crates; the take pass infers about 590 owners at 1 to 4 per second. Of the 590 only 2 are not Rust crates of the probe closure. The target "minutes" is met only on an idle machine (estimate 4 to 6 minutes unloaded: closure 40 s, owners 40 to 60 s, settle and assembly 60 s; NOT measured idle).
46. **Hand-over of an old read on a worker: verified, with a condition.** The workers' `ready.json` of 22:16 UTC shows `code-stale` as the restart reason on g1-m01, g1-m08, g1-m10 and the new closure `taken N of 590` (g1-m03 496, g1-m10 485, g1-m07 540, g1-m08 400) where they had `taken N of 4455` before: the old reads did restart on the new code (the readiness reason `code-stale` and the R139 door hand-over are visible). g1-m02 still shows an adopted `taken 0 of 4455` read of 17144 s (backoff, age 100 s): that one is on old code and has not handed over (its sidecar is the pre-R137 one; only a restart of its sidecar helps). Static finding: `claim.handover()` fires only (a) at a wave boundary of a generation `build` and (b) when a conflicting claim waits; a stale read that nobody waits for runs on (law test of R139). The workers that did restart were restarted by the readiness sidecar (`code-stale`), not by the door.
