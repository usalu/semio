## 1. g1-m01: why a read that says "11134 of 1800" is never stopped

Evidence (`origins/g1-m01/ready.json`, `ready-log-workspace.txt`, `slice-memory.json`, 08:38 to 09:05 UTC): the stage `workspace` (attempt 68, "adopted") prints `Reading the project graph slice (11802 s): waiting for the fleet to infer @semio-tech/s-dev-composition (beyond the expected 1800 s) / ... 11265 of 1800.` every 2 s. Three rules of the supervisor each see "progress" in that line:
1. The stall tracker (`readyStallTrackerV1`) normalises seconds (`11802 s`), but a counter `N of M` stays in the line (by design, `1957 of 2927` is progress), so the step's own seconds counter is a new line every 2 s.
2. The processor-time liveness (`readyLivenessV1`) renews the stall window whenever the tree used 500 ms in 5 s: the read burns CPU (`owners.cpuSeconds` user 2428 s of 11,800 s) re-importing the same fetched record that it rejects.
3. R181's `code-stale` only fires for a changed declared constant (A2), and none changed.
So none of the rules could ever fire: the stage cap (~4 h) was the only end.

### The rule (landed)
`readyStepWatchV1` in `🌲️process-tree/🟦️.ts` (pure), used by `runReadyStageV1`:
- It reads the newest **step line** (`stepLinePatterns`, EN and DE: the progress line whose phase is `<English> / <German>` and ends `<seconds run> of <bound seconds>.`; the note `beyond the expected N s` is cut so the step keeps its identity when it crosses its bound).
- Overdue seconds = `scaled(bound, speed factor) + stallStopMilliseconds` (the silence window that already ends a silent stage here, itself scaled by the speed factor): `readyStepOverdueSecondsV1`. Derivation: a declared bound is written for the reference machine and lengthened by the speed factor like every limit of the readiness path (`scaledMillisecondsV1`); a step is overdue when it has been past what this machine is expected to need for as long as a stage may be silent. Both parts follow the speed factor; no number of its own. For a 1800 s step on a reference machine: 2400 s; for factor 2.83: 6786 s.
- "No other progress" = no line that is new after normalisation and is not a step line (a `taken N of M` counter, an owner finished, any message), and no other step. The step's own seconds counter, the repeated `[repo] still working` heartbeat and the **processor time of the tree are not progress for this rule**. Overdue = step past the limit AND quiet for `stallStopMilliseconds`.
- Never in the `executing` phase (R176). The stop is the same as the stall stop (tree SIGTERM, SIGKILL after the grace), kill reason `overdue`, typed refusal `stage-step-overdue` and next-try reason `step-overdue` (EN/DE: stage, step, seconds run, declared bound, limit, quiet seconds, pause, count of 5), counted in the same consecutive-stall budget (5, then `stage-stall-exhausted` with back-off, R135) and restarted on current code.
- The quiet clock starts at the supervisor's first look at the stage. A restarted supervisor that adopts a stuck stage therefore ends it after one silence window (600 s at factor 1), not at once.

### Laws (`🚦️ready/🧪️tests/🪜️step-overdue`, 17 tests; red on the old code: the exports are missing, the file does not even load)
- parse in both languages, inside and beyond the bound; owners lines and heartbeats are no step.
- **producer/consumer law**: for every declared step of `🧠️memory/📋️slice-report` (all 11 steps) the real `describeSliceReadStepV1` output, rendered with the real resident template `host-slice-detail` in EN and DE, inside and beyond the bound, is read by the supervisor with the right bound and seconds. A new step, a changed template or a changed phase format that the supervisor cannot read turns this law red.
- limit formula and scaling (1800 -> 2400; factor 2.83 -> `ceil((ceil(1800000*2.83)+ceil(600000*2.83))/1000)`).
- the four requested laws, as pure watch laws and as real stage runs (fake `repo`): overdue step -> stopped with the step named (EN and DE variants, within 30 s of the test clock); step within its bound -> not stopped; executing phase -> never stopped (the tasks only burn processor time); a step beyond its bound that advances its counter while a `taken N of 590` line moves -> not stopped. Plus: processor time alone does not save an overdue step (the m01 shape); another step is progress; the quiet window counts from the last other progress.
- loop level: the stop is the typed refusal and reason in both languages, not a failure; the log line names step, seconds, bound, limit.

### R181's declared-constant rule and R184
Decision: the two declared constants (`selected-exchange` head key `revision`, observation record `stamp`) are bumped when **a result changes that another process accepts or reads**: the head key a peer accepts from a sealed read, the persisted record format. They are **not** bumped for a change of how a read *waits*, what it counts as *progress*, or what it *says*: R184's wait bound and progress sign changed no result byte, and a bump would hand over every healthy read of the fleet and make peers refuse each other's sealed reads. The consequence is the old problem R184 hit: a read on old code with a liveness defect is not handed over by a constant. That class is covered from outside, and only from outside: the step rule above ends **every** wait that declares its bound and does not end itself, whatever its cause. The docstring of `readySemanticsV1` now states this rule. The law that would have caught R184 is the producer/consumer law plus the step laws: a wait that is a declared step is covered by construction; a wait that is not a declared step is the existing static law of R177 (every used step is declared, every declared step is used). A law that detects "a liveness fix landed without a bump" in code is not possible (no machine-checkable difference between a liveness change and a result change); I did not invent one.

What "exit status -" means: `ReadyRunResultV1.exit` is `null` and the refusal text printed `exit ?? "-"`. `null` arises on three paths in `runReadyStageV1`: (a) the exit file exists and holds `exit: null` (the wrapper's child ended by a signal; since R186 the file also holds the signal and the tail gets a signal line), (b) the wrapper pid is not alive and no exit file exists (the wrapper itself was ended without a chance to write: SIGKILL, memory pressure, reboot), (c) the sampler's process table says the wrapper pid has another start time ("reused"). Attempts 56b and 57 have no signal line, so they were (b) or (c); attempt 56a is (a) via the stall rule. **Which of (b) and (c) cannot be decided from the store**: the supervisor's host log and `kills.log` of g1-m02 are not in the store, and the supervisor wrote no line for those paths.

What the evidence does show:
1. No kill of the runner's roster is involved: the 08:25:38 containment file lists the registry (561 entries, `not-ours` 309) and **no** "stopped process left behind" line; the roster-kill lines are in the 07:24:04 file only (two `bun` processes of the earlier try, SIGTERM from runner 68204), i.e. before R190 reached this supervisor. The wrapper is not in a roster of an earlier try while its own try runs.
2. The memory governor is not involved: peak memory of the tree is 106 MB (attempt 57); the governor gates only before a start (`memory-low`).
3. The stall rule is not involved: outcome `stage-failed`, not `stalled`; the stalled attempt (56a) carries the signal line.
4. g1-m02 is the machine under memory pressure: `pool.json` 09:05 "waiting: the machine is under memory pressure or swaps (45 MB/s out), no child runs"; the replica daemon's `view` tier took 224,411 ms in its last pass (`attest.json` latency), `publish p95` 68 s; the slice worker of attempt 57 (`slice-memory.json`, 08:34:31) was 512 s old, **phase "start", 65 MB resident, no step begun** (m07's first step started 96 s after the start on a loaded machine), i.e. the read was starved, not working.
5. Code defect found that fits (c) and a swapping machine: `readyReadTableV1` took `now()` at the **end** of the `ps -A` call (`child.on("close", ... readyParseUnixTableV1(out, now()))`). `ps` states the elapsed time of each process as of its own start, so a table that takes longer than the tolerance (15 s) made every process look that much younger; `readyIdentityV1` then says "reused" for the wrapper and the supervisor ended the stage with `exit null`, no signal, **without stopping its tree** (the vanished path settles the tree, this one did not). A `ps` of 15+ s is plausible under 45 MB/s swap-out (the table timeout is 20 s). This is a proven defect of the code, not a proven cause of attempts 56b and 57.

### Fixes (landed, laws red first)
- `readyReadTableV1`: start times counted from the moment the table was asked for (the new `run` parameter makes the law injectable). Law: a table that takes 30 s (injected clock) leaves the wrapper `same`; a start 40 s away is `reused`; a failing or null table is empty; plus an independent oracle: the real table's start time of the test process is within 3 s of `Date.now() - process.uptime()`.
- A "reused" verdict must hold in two reads in a row, then the tree is settled like a vanished process (before: ended with no stop, no line).
- The refusal no longer prints `-`: `stage-failed` renders `{ending}`: `exit status N` / `no exit status, ended by signal SIGKILL` / `no exit status and no signal: the stage process vanished, the last lines of its log say how` (EN and DE, `readyEndingV1`); the failure record carries `signal`; the supervisor line "failed (code, ...)" uses the same words. Already verified live: g1-m02's `ready.json` now says "Stage workspace failed (exit status 1); retrying with back-off." (supervisor 92088, restarted 09:02:36 on the landing).
- The ending of a vanished stage process is self-describing (tail, failure mirror, in EN and DE): "the stage process N is gone: it left no exit file and no signal was recorded, so its wrapper was ended without a chance to write the end (a SIGKILL ..., memory pressure, a restart of the machine); it was last seen alive X s ago and this supervisor sent no signal / had sent a stop signal to its tree". The "reused" path writes recorded start, both seen starts, the tolerance and how long the last table read took.
- Laws: vanished wrapper (the test kills the wrapper with SIGKILL: failure and refusal say it, signal null); signal ending (`kill -KILL $$` in the stage: `ended by signal SIGKILL` / `durch das Signal SIGKILL beendet`); the ending words for every combination.

### What to look for on g1-m02 (not decidable from here)
