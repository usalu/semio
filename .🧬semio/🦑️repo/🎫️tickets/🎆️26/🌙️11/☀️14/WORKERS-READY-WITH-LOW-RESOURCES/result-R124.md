## 2. What does not fit, with numbers (g1-server, host view read at 09:41:30 UTC with `host.ts`)

- total 32768 MB, available 15851 MB, pressure normal, load 43.45 on 10 cores (other agents' processes).
- floor (`floorShareBasisPoints` 12.5 percent of the total, clamped 1536 to 4096 MB): **4096 MB**.
- grants of the gate: nine `graph-farm` grants (1 core and 1536 MB reserved each, `observedMegabytes 0`, never measured) and the `projects` grant (2 cores, **14336 MB reserved**, 7826 MB observed). Untouched reservations: 6510 MB of the read plus 9 x 1536 x 25 percent (`farmUnrealizedShareBasisPoints`) = 3456 MB = **9966 MB**.
- spare = 15851 - 4096 - 9966 = **1789 MB**; one more child is priced at `childMegabytes` 1064 MB (p75 of the sizes children reported after an owner, plus 15 percent): `byMemory = children + floor(1789 / 1064)` = 1 + 1 = 2. With the price 1237 MB (the value of 09:05) it is 1 + 1, with 1800 MB it is 1: the arithmetic admits one child and the pool says `nothing-fits`. The memory the read really holds (7826 MB for the plan, the farm workers' resident sizes) is already out of the 15851 MB available: the 9966 MB subtracted on top are the read's reservation on paper.
- cores: the grants book 9 + 2 = 11 cores of 10 (idle farm workers book a core as well), `booked = max(0, 10 - 11 - 0) = 0`, so the core rule falls back to the minimum share (a quarter of the cores, 3); the measured idle cores (`os.cpus()` between two looks) are 0 at load 43, `byLoad = max(3, children + 0)`. The server is saturated by other processes, so even with a perfect memory rule the pool could add little there.
- at 10:03 (after the first landing) the farm of the read had a waiting request (`graph-farm`, batch, about 1 minute old) although 19038 MB were available and only 1085 of the 14336 MB of the `projects` grant were touched: the gate holds the read's memory on paper and refuses its own next worker; the pool was held to one child as well. Both idle on paper, memory physically free.

What "yielding" yields to: `reason: yielding` is only the label of a target below the cores where memory is not the smallest of the three limits (`byMemory > min(byCores, byLoad)`); R101 gave the pool a quarter of the cores and removed the hard load gate, R113 made starts slow (`startSpacingPolls` 8 looks = 16 s, `childMinimumAgePolls` 90 looks = 180 s before an idle child may be drained or another is started, one child loading at a time, `startWarmupPolls` 90). What it yields to is (a) the cores that the machine's own grants book, idle or not, (b) their untouched reservations, and (c) in the `waiting` branch every request of the machine's own work that has waited 5 s: then all children are drained and killed after 3 s.

Per-child estimate against which budget: `childMegabytes` is a price per additional child against `available - floor - untouched reservations`; children recycle after one owner at the recycle limit (6 percent of the memory clamped to 1200 to 3000 MB, plus 600 MB growth since ready), they are killed at 30 percent (hard limit, 3072 MB minimum).

### 16 GB worker whose own read runs (computed, not measured: no worker host view exists)
Total 16384, floor 2048 MB, read plan 6553 MB (40 percent) of which perhaps 3000 MB touched: unrealized about 3500 MB; a typical available of 7000 to 9000 MB: spare = 1450 to 3450 MB; at a price of 1237 to 1800 MB the arithmetic adds one or two children to the ones that run (steady state two to three children). The pool's children and the read's farm do not compete for the same owners (the read's gate and the pool claim the same entry state `tryGraphEntryV1`, the loser sees `held`), they compete for memory and cores only.

### Where the pools really lost time (not capacity)
On the workers the target was 5 to 8 children (children 5 to 8, memory not the limit at 09:34) and still 0.2 to 0.6 owners per minute: **93.8 percent of the claim attempts of g1-m01 answered `stored`** (4757 of 5074, 87 to 94 percent on the others, 76 percent on m08), 0.7 to 1.9 percent were granted. A claim costs about 2 s on a loaded machine (verification of the stored note), a child ends a wave after 192 `stored` in a row (`doneStoredMinimum`) and the wave rests 60 s: 10 `finished` exits with 0 owners in 30 minutes on m01. The census at 09:43 (rerun of `R116/census.ts`, 300 judged) says 538 of 4775 owners (11.3 percent) are rejected on the server's tree (853 of 4619 at 06:40), mostly `record row: readdirNames` (473 of 538); the claim of a worker says `stored` for them: the verdict of the claiming machine and the census differ, and nothing in the pool can make an owner open that its claim judges fresh. After the restart (10:07, 5 minutes) the pools made 900 to 1100 claim attempts each: 2 to 6 granted, 0 to 2 published. Open owners are rare for the pool; this is not capacity.

## 3. What changed (landing 1, 10:00:39 to 10:02:49 UTC)
1. One budget (`ownerPoolSizingV1`): the memory the machine's own work really holds is already out of `available`; its untouched reservations are held back only up to `ownHoldShareBasisPoints` (15 percent of the total memory); while a request of it waits the whole reservation is held (landing 2 refines: what the waiting request asks for). Cores that the own work books but does not use are the pool's: with measured idle cores the cores rule is `idle` (the load rule), not `cores - booked`. Without a read the pool gets all of `available - floor`.
2. Child price measured: `ownerPoolChildMegabytesV1(samples, live)` is the p75 over the finished owners' sizes and the resident sizes of the children that run now (an idle or loading child is a real child), plus the margin; the default 800 MB only while nobody reported. One child always runs unless the pressure is critical or the machine swaps (pinned by a test over available = floor + 1 and reservations up to 50000 MB).
3. The text says the number: `reasonText` (EN and DE) states what the next child needs, what is free above the floor, what the own work holds, how many children run (`nothing-fits`), the idle and booked cores (`yielding`), the seconds to the next start (new reason `starting`, from `ownerPoolStartDelayV1`), the seconds waited (`waiting`) or the swap rate (`pressure`). Live on g1-server at 10:05: "waiting: the next child needs 1493 MB but only 0 MB are free above the 4096 MB floor (15258 MB are held for the machine's own work, 2 children run)"; on g1-m01 "needs 2171 MB but only 1213 MB are free above the 2048 MB floor (0 MB are held for the machine's own work, 3 children run)": a 16 GB worker without a read is bound by memory at three children of about 1.8 GB.

Proof (red then green): new `🎛️one-budget` 7 tests (red: `ownerPoolSizingV1` missing), `🧮️lifecycle` 4 to 5 (red: `ownerPoolStartDelayV1` missing, green includes a loop that equates delay 0 with `ownerPoolMayStartV1`), fixtures and the independent Python oracle (`🐍️oracle.py`: sizing, limit numbers, live samples; 6 new cases; one old case changed on purpose: the reservation hold is capped), main test 30 (target, limit, child estimate equal to the oracle). Battery of 13 files green after the landing, none worse than before; both standing preflights (2, 10).

## 4. Landing 2 (10:30:02 to 10:32:23 UTC): the judge of the pool
Root cause of "stored 94 percent": `freshVerdictV1` (the verdict with which the claim of a pool judges a stored record, `staleNoteOf` in `📦️project-records/🟦️.ts`) compared only `file` and `analysis` digests and `closure` rows. Rows of any other kind (the `readdirNames` of a project directory, `exists`, `directories`, `typescriptRealpath`) were never judged, although a taker rejects a record on them (`record row: readdirNames` is 473 of the 538 rejected owners of the census 09:43). The claim answered `stored`, no pool ever refreshed those owners, every reader inferred them itself, the pool had nothing open. Now `freshVerdictV1` takes `rowHolds` and the claim passes `observationRowFreshV1` (the taker's question) for every other row kind; a row it cannot judge counts as fresh.
Also in landing 2: the pool holds back what its waiting requests ask for (`waitingMegabytes`, from the gate's waiters) instead of the whole untouched reservation (the server at 10:03 had 19038 MB available, a farm request waiting for 1536 MB and the pool at one child), and with measured idle cores a waiting own request keeps only a quarter of the cores free.
Proof: `🌱️fresh` verdict cases (3 new, Python oracle, 4 to 5 tests incl. a real directory: a new file makes the record stale, an edit of a sibling does not), `🤝️pool-reader` (2 to 3: a record whose listing changed must not be `stored` for the pool; red before: "the pool must not call stored what a taker rejects on a listing"), pool sizing fixtures (2 new cases) and `🎛️one-budget` 7 to 8. Battery of 23 files green under the lock (none worse than the baseline; the main pool test and `📦️project-records/🧪️tests/🟦️.ts` each have one failure before and after).
