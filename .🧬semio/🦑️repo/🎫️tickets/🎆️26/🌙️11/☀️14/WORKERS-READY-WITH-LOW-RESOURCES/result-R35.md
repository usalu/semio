## 1. What holds the memory (evidence of the LIVE reads, observe only)

What `vmmap` says for pid 86616 at 7.7 GB (file `vm1.txt`): writable regions 13.7 GB, **swapped out 7.6 GB (56 %), resident 115 MB (1 %)**. The memory is one region type, `App-Specific Tag 1` (the bun heap: JavaScriptCore plus mimalloc), 91 regions, mostly 128 MB each, 8.4 GB virtual, 7.6 GB swapped, 109 MB resident. Everything else is small: system MALLOC zones 45 MB, mapped files 34.5 MB, JIT 11 MB, SQLite page cache 0.9 MB, stacks 0.4 MB, no IOKit. So it is **not** off-heap native buffers, not mapped files and not the node store's SQLite caches; it is memory the bun runtime itself allocates, and 30 s later 4 GB of it can be gone again (3944 MB) or +4 GB in 60 s (37994: 4058 to 6027 to 7868 MB in 80 s, about 50 MB/s).

Parent against managed heap (server log, before this package): wave 220 tree 16099 MB against managed 5228 MB after the collection (3.1 times), wave 234 18349 against 5285 (peak 8387, 58 collections), wave 223 (fresh start) 4515 against 2266. The tree includes 7 to 8 farm workers of about 1 GB, so the parent alone is about 2 times its managed heap: allocator pages the collector does not return.

The persisted structures the closure phase loads are NOT big enough to explain it (measured from the store `.cache/b/g/m/derived-nodes/n`, 79 415 records, 249 MB brotli): `closure-part` 28 208 records, 191.6 MB JSON, 1 193 808 rows; loaded as JS objects with the settler's row interning they cost **365 MB footprint, 352 MB after a collection and 153 MB after `Bun.shrink`** (script `scratchpad/R35/parts.mjs`); `closure-meta` 121 MB JSON; `script-walk` 6 812 records 300 MB JSON; sharded `closure-manifest` 2 502 records; the fact index is 13.7 MB packed, at most 512 MiB expanded, cached at most 128 MiB (`🕰️generations/🧬️schema/🔣️.json:37,39`). Together they are under 1 GB as objects, not 8 to 31 GB.

Attribution by code (UNPROVEN: bun has no symbols and no heap snapshot can be taken from a live process, so the sample shows the main thread 100 % busy in JS, 2990 of 2990 samples, nothing more): what grows is state that lives for the whole settle PASS of 8 129 closures and is never released between chunks or waves:
1. the validation round `pass.round` that every derivation shares (`🧩️closure-kinds/🟨️.mjs` `pass`, `deriveClosureFactV1` `pass.round ??= new Map()` in `📥️inference/🧭️script-inputs/🟨️.mjs:787`) and the per-round maps keyed by it in `👁️observation/🟨️.mjs:243-244` (`memoRowChecksV1`), `:383` (`nestedChecks`), `:681` (`roundDirectories`): they hold one entry per distinct fact probed (about 1.2 million rows) until the pass ends;
2. the script-input cache `environment.scripts` of the deriver (`📦️project-nodes/🟨️.mjs:151`, created once per process in `🕰️host/🟦️.ts` `deriveAgain`): unbounded `files`, `closures`, `walkNodes`, `reachState.*` maps (`🧭️script-inputs/🟨️.mjs:59,484,599`);
3. the `held` map of the settle (`closure-kinds:runOnce`) keeps a `partRowsV1` support for every closure until the end;
4. no `Bun.shrink`/settle on the serving path: `settleGraphMemoryV1` and `reportRetainedGraphMemoryV1` (R2/R9) are quiet without a plan limit or `SEMIO_GRAPH_PHASES=1`, so **no per-wave cache line exists in any live log** (`build-agent.log` has none; the server keeps only "last output of the read"); the collector `runtimeCollectGarbageV1` never returned pages to the system.
5. the physical footprint is invisible to every decision: the governor charges managed bytes only (`🧠️memory/🟨️.mjs` `graphMemoryPressureBytesV1`), the service headroom and `capacityOfGrantV1` used `process.memoryUsage().rss`, which FALLS to 100 MB while the pages are swapped out (86616: rss 113 MB at footprint 3944 MB) and RISES with freed pages the footprint does not count (37994: rss 5850 MB at footprint 1449 MB). ## 2. What was built (landed)

- New module `🧠️memory/🦶️footprint/` (schema first: `🧬️schema/🔣️.json`; code `🟨️.mjs`, types `🟨️.d.mts`, tests `🧪️tests/🟦️.ts`, 19 tests). `readProcessFootprintBytesV1` (macOS `footprint`, Linux `smaps_rollup` Pss+SwapPss, others resident set; a failed reading is `undefined`, never the resident set), `createFootprintMeterV1` (cached, one background reading per 5 s, single flight, never blocks), `decideFootprintV1`/`footprintHeadroomBytesV1` (run below 60 % of the grant, settle from 60 %, wait from 85 %; numbers in the schema), `createFootprintGateV1` (`admit(signal)`: from 60 % collect+shrink at most once per 30 s; from 85 % after that the registered relievers drop what a pass can re-read; if that does not help, wait in 1 s steps at most 20 s per call, then no further wait for 120 s, always cancellable, so it can never deadlock a read), `releaseRuntimeMemoryV1` (collect, `Bun.shrink`, collect), `settleWaveMemoryV1` (the wave line), the process gate `useFootprintGateV1`/`footprintGateV1`, `grantBytesOfV1`.
- `🧩️closure-kinds/🟨️.mjs`: `useCapacityGrantV1` installs the gate with the grant of the process (the single bind site of `📜️script.ts:123`); the settle's `pool` calls `gate.admit(signal)` before every derivation; a reliever drops `pass.round`, the part verdicts and the interned rows (only at the hard limit); `Bun.shrink` after every 256 closures and every chunk and at the end (was a bare collection).
- `🧠️memory/🟨️.mjs`: `serviceMemoryHeadroomBytesV1` and `🧮️derived-nodes/🟨️.mjs` `capacityOfGrantV1` read the physical footprint of the gate instead of the resident set; `createMemoryGovernorV1` has a `hold` port and the parent governor's default holds every further owner while the footprint is above 85 % of the grant (the first owner always runs: no deadlock).
- `🕰️host/🟦️.ts` `published`: after every published wave `settleWaveMemoryV1` releases the inference memory and the string interner, collects, shrinks and writes ONE line per language to the read's stderr: `graph: wave N settled from X MB to Y MB (managed M MB, grant G MB)` / `Graph: Welle N beruhigt von X MB auf Y MB (verwaltet M MB, Zusage G MB)`. Nothing here can fail a read (all in try/catch, readings time out after 15 s).

## 5. Not verified / for the others

- The 31 GB peak cause is attributed by code, not proven by a heap snapshot (stripped bun). The relievers drop `pass.round` only at 85 % of the grant: if the real growth is the script-input cache `environment.scripts` (point 2) or `held` (point 3), they do not release it; the next heap evidence decides. Next candidates, not done: bound `environment.scripts` maps by bytes, stream `held` to the store per chunk, a per-chunk fresh `pass.round`.
- The farm children are not gated (they do not bind a capacity grant) and the farm IPC has no back-pressure: no backlog was observed (children idle waiting for work), so none was added; the parent now holds further owners (`hold`) when it is above 85 %.
- The grant is a reservation of the scheduler; the gate compares the physical footprint with it. A read whose steady state really needs more than 85 % of its grant waits at most 20 s in 140 s (14 %) and settles at most once per 30 s: that costs time, the cap is the point.
- Linux and Windows readers are tested with fake ports only (`smaps_rollup` parsing is the existing parser); `tsc` was not run; the percentages and the 30 s/120 s are schema values to tune once a real wave line exists.
- `reportRetainedGraphMemoryV1` is still quiet on a serving machine; the new wave line is not (it is the log R2/R9 meant to be there).
- Process deviation: once I ran `pkill -f mon4.sh` to stop my own duplicate monitor loop (it matched only the scripts I had started; no other process was touched). The monitors ended by themselves otherwise; none is left running except possibly one detached `mon4.sh` loop of mine that ends 24 min after 10:19 local.
- The open blow-up needs the heartbeat/trace of the next read (phase name) and then a heap census (`graph: heap census at … MB: managed …, external …, … objects; most numerous: …` is written to the read's stderr and the trace file once the footprint passes 60 % of the grant): that names the structure (Array/Map/String counts) of the 20 to 30 GB. Candidates, unproven: the per-owner payload built by the parent for the first wave of 265 owners (265 owners are admitted by the governor of the parent against managed bytes only; the `hold` port only holds a further owner while the footprint is above the hard limit, and the first owner is always admitted), the in-process inference of owners while 8 workers idle.
- The R33 overrun stop (2 times the grant after 5 min of silence) works but each restarted read repeats the same first wave, so it ends the thrash without making progress; the cap of the parent in that wave is the next package.
