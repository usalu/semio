# Warm Farm Lane Cancellation

The whole-graph owner pass already stops speculative warm work when the take pass releases required inference, including its 30-second timer. The live successor showed repeated warm progress at 7 of 4,631 alongside queued required inference after more than 1,610 seconds. That observation and the source agree that outstanding speculative work can overlap required work well after the intended release. They do not establish that speculation was the sole cause of slow progress.

The warm wave previously passed its signal to the lane loop only. The loop tested it before starting an owner. The farm wait and submitted job listened only to the whole wave. Cancelling a warm lane therefore left its active child and any submitted queue entries running. The pool already supported cancelling one submitted job. It removes that job's queued entry or retires only the child currently executing it. Each child handles one job at a time. Putting the warm signal on the pool or hub would have closed required work too.

The warm wave now forwards its signal to readiness waits and job submission. The abort wakes those waits, keeps the whole-graph signal live and stops counting interrupted owners as processed. A pool drops queued siblings sharing the cancelled signal before resuming dispatch and wakes the queue after removing a cancelled head. Its active cancellation retires only the worker currently owned by that job, then the regular slot lifecycle replaces it after exit when further work needs it. Required observation and inference use the whole-wave lifecycle. The settled warm rows already on disk remain usable. The existing worker writes a durable row only after settlement and ignores a torn final line on read.

A neutral fixture drove the real warm wave, project farm and pool with controlled IPC workers. A SQLite oracle checked warm dispatch, worker termination, required observe/infer and durable rows. The baseline hung past the 1.5-second regression deadline. The fixed test settled both an active warm job ahead of required work and a warm job queued behind active required observation. A second test used two worker slots and a memory-gated queue to prove that dropping a queued warm head admitted a smaller required inference while the other required owner remained active.

A combined suite passed 26 tests and 237 assertions across warm lanes, project farm worker runs, retirement, and staged-adoption decline. A targeted follow-up passed four tests and 69 assertions covering both cancellation regressions, existing real-worker cancellation and the bilingual decline reason fixture. The ordinary unit tests exercised real child replacement and durability. No live producer or queue state was changed.
