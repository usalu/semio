# Graph read capacity wait clock

On 2026-11-21 UTC the graph sidecar detached reader 19453 at 11:09:09.404 and reattached it at 11:09:10.795. At 11:09:47.445 the watchdog recorded `stalled` and raw silence of 2,379,955 ms for that reader. The exact 4096→14336 MiB rebuild request was still waiting for memory, with about 6.2 GiB available in the last recorded observation. Earlier observations at 10:55 and 11:05 had verified the same wait. The unbundled sidecar and its subprocess resolved the expected workspace sources. The terminal receipt preserves raw silence and counters. It does not preserve the effective silence or the two clock samples needed to attribute that original stop to the exact race reproduced below.

The sidecar previously derived the stored progress timestamp by subtracting elapsed silence from a separately sampled wall clock. A clock tick between samples fabricated a different timestamp. The active tick discarded the legitimate wait credit when this apparent timestamp differed from the checkpoint, and an asynchronous proof callback could reject a valid sample for the same reason. Within the ordinary probe cadence the next tick could then stop the reader on raw silence alone. The follower now reads the watchdog’s scalar progress timestamp directly and uses one clock sample per tick for verdict, raw quiet and effective quiet. The asynchronous callback compares the same exact timestamp. The existing proof validity, sample cadence, restart checkpoint format, loss detection, absolute limit and raw memory policy stay in force.

The neutral timing vector and SQLite timeline oracle demonstrate that the raw clock crosses the policy threshold while the correctly credited clock remains below it. The private native fixture detaches and reattaches the same owned reader, revalidates the live wait and deliberately advances the clock between calls. Before the fix the fixture observed a stop record. Afterward it confirmed the reader stays alive under the exact proof. Loss, absolute deadline and raw memory cases also passed. The capacity fixture allows an observed proof callback to settle before detaching. The orphan fixture waits for both the published terminal receipt and actual runner exit, which are distinct events.

The final native cohort ran 58 tests and 560 assertions across graph follower, graph server, read watchdog and host-capacity gate, with all tests passing. Only private fixture processes were started or stopped during validation.
