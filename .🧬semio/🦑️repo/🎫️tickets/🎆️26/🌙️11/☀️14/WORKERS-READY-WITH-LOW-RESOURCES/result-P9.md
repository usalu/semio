   Conclusions that the samples carry: (a) the parent is CPU bound on ONE thread; the waits are the governor's (`wait` median 3 to 37 s per project in the trace), not input/output (0.9 % real I/O in the loop); (b) the loop is serialisation work (brotli + sha256 + JSON + utf8 decode + Map/Set = 57 % of the thread) and almost no project logic (JS 17 %); (c) collections cost 13 to 21 % of the thread overall, with episodes of 100 % for 15 s (22:26:00 to :10, a forced `Bun.gc(true)` of a 12 GB heap: the same call took 0.34 s at 2.7 GB and 8.4 s at 11 GB heap in my own measurement); (d) the first minutes of a part are a file system storm (`openat` + `getdirentries` 47 % of samples) over all projects, not only the 48 of the wave; (e) the lookup itself is cheap: 760 stored records (brotli + header parse) decode in 187 ms, median 526 observation rows, 6 closure recipes, p90 1 479 rows, max 118 354 (`workspace`); the trace shows `pre` 1 to 100 ms; a hit at the lookup is milliseconds, as the brief wants. Measured on the real store: a closure layer of 150 801 rows decompresses in 28 ms, parses in 35 ms and costs 429 ms to intern into a fresh row log (row key + stringify + write), and a new row log is made for every wave; the whole fact index (74 MB raw, 11.5 MB packed) packs in 0.8 s at the quality the build uses (quality 5), recompressing it at the default quality would take 83 s: the pack is not the bottleneck.

   **Where the seconds go, in the order of size** (evidence level in brackets): (1) work of the whole graph that every wave repeats whatever the wave holds: file system walk and parse of all manifests and project files before the loop, the first touch of the shared closure layers (11 s until the first project of wave 189 was done: stored layers read, verified, interned into the new row log) and the fact index patch with its forced full collection after the loop, together 33 to 156 s per wave [measured as the gaps of the table; the split inside them is read from samples and code, not from a profile]; (2) collections of a 4 to 12 GB heap [sampled, 13 to 21 %]; (3) the per-project share 0.43 s in a quiet wave [31 s, first project after 11 s, 47 more in 20 s]. The retained heap (3 to 5.9 GB after a full collection, P8) multiplies (2); what holds it is still unnamed.

2. **What was removed or built (tests first, 21 plus 4 plus 3 new tests, all green on the overlay; neighbours equal to the live tree)**
   - **Waves that grow while nothing is inferred** (`🕰️generations`): `nextOwnerWaveV1` (exported, schema Policy keys `ownerWaveMaximum` 960, `ownerWaveGrowth` 4, `ownerWaveBudgetMilliseconds` 300 000, EN/DE descriptions). A wave that reports `inferred: 0` makes the next wave `min(960, 4 x size, size x 300 s / elapsed)`, at least the base 48; a wave that inferred up to a quarter of its projects keeps its size (shrinks only when over the budget); more than a quarter, or no report, returns to the base wave. The base and the maximum follow `SEMIO_SLICE_WAVE_BASIS_POINTS`, so a 16 GB worker never grows beyond half. The count comes from the loop itself: a project is counted once when its lookup missed (the farm preparation or the inference body is called only on a miss; proven by a test against the real observation module: a hit calls neither, a changed key calls both). It travels `project-nodes -> discovery (onInferred) -> part -> shapeComputeResultV1 -> buildOnce`. Existing wave tests are unchanged and prove that waves without the report stay exactly 48.
   - **The stages of a wave in the wave line** (`📈️wave-row`, optional key `phases`, schema, parse, meter, EN `, phases: prime 0 s, plan 2 s, compute 107 s, compute.setup 31 s, compute.projects 61 s, compute.tail 15 s, fragments 1 s, patch 22 s, pack 1 s, publish 2 s`, DE `, Phasen: …`): `buildOnce` marks prime, plan, compute, fragments, patch, pack, publish; the part adds `compute.setup` (everything before the project loop: manifests, catalog, farm), `compute.projects` (the loop), `compute.tail` (tests, policy, edges, metadata). **The next wave line after the landing is the profile this report could not take**: it names, per wave, the stage that costs the seconds, from the running read, without a restart or a profiler.
   - Changed existing tests (their expectations named the exact text I had to change): `📈️wave-progress` (the info of a wave now also carries `phases`), `🚪️away` (the farm preparation now counts the miss first).
   - **Not built, with the reason**: the persistent reuse of the shared closure layers across waves (a row log and a support patch per read instead of per wave) and the skipping of the re-upsert of derived supports whose layer digest did not change (design: carry a support key per pseudo owner in the manifest, compare before `derivedUpsertsV1`, test with the existing Python oracle of the generation laws). Both attack fixed cost (1) directly, but without a function-level profile of that cost and with the fleet running on this code, a change of the generation format is not something to land blind. The stage line above gives the number that decides it. `closureRecipeKeyV1` memo, row-log text cache, collector duty limit: no evidence of size in the samples, not touched.

3. **Expected refresh time** (model: waves x fixed cost + projects x per-project share; fixed 50 to 110 s and per-project 0.43 s in a quiet read, from the table): now 103 waves x 120 to 173 s = **3.4 to 4.9 hours**; with the landed policy and a budget of 300 s per wave roughly 10 to 11 waves of about 450 projects = **45 to 55 minutes** in a quiet read, up to about 2 hours when the per-project share is the 1.6 s of a busy read (waves 187 and 188). The target of a few minutes is NOT reached: it needs the fixed cost under about 10 s and the per-project share under 50 ms, which is the work of the two items not built. A refresh after a typical build-code landing re-infers the truly affected projects (farm) and the rest as hits; the first wave of every restarted read still costs 744 s of verification before it starts (three passes, measured above; restarts happened a dozen times today).

4. **Safety of the larger waves**: the server stops a read after 25 min without a wave (`inferStallMilliseconds`); a wave is bounded by the 300 s budget at the speed of the previous one and grows at most 4 times, a farm wave of 960 misses would take about 19 min (57 s per 48 on six workers), a parent that infers alone at several seconds per project would not finish 960 in 25 min, but a wave after a clean one is at most 4 x the clean one and misses are served first (workers' priority list), so the hit tail is where waves grow. A stalled read restarts from the published waves and starts again with 48.
