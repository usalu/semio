## What was built

1. `B/🪪️graph-state/🙋️wanted/` (N6's module, extended; schema first in `🧬️schema/🔣️.json`):
   - `📖️read/🟦️.ts` `fleetReadV1`: (a) a machine admitted at least `serveHeadroomMegabytes` (9 400 MB) infers itself, always (it is a serving machine; without this the 32 GB server would ask the fleet for its own 5 000 projects); (b) `planStaleReadV1` with the projects the fleet already served (the answer to an earlier request of the same machine, so a retried round does not ask again); (c) `local` stays silent and touches no fleet; (d) `fleet`: publishes the request, waits (bound 15 min, poll 2 s, progress once per change or every 10 s, cancellation by the invocation signal), stops waiting as soon as what remains fits this machine (`settled`); (e) if more remains than it can infer, or the fleet cannot be reached and the rest does not fit: says so in EN or DE with what it waited for (`wanted`, `arrived`, `seconds`, `missing`, `local`) and throws `RepoErrorV1("timeout","graph-wanted.expired")`, exit status 75 declared as `Refusal` in the schema. Never a hang.
   - `🟦️.ts`: `servedStateV1`, `publishServedV1`, `readServedV1` (the serving machine's answer: the project names it finished, the generation, schema `Served`), `wantedTextV1`, `settled` in `awaitWantedV1`, `graphKey` optional in `serveWantedV1`.
   - `🛠️server/🟦️.ts` `runWantedServerV1`: every 15 s serve the live requests of the other machines (one `repo show projects` under the fleet claims and the capacity gate, then the answer); when nothing was served, refresh the fleet generation in the background after the worktree was quiet 3 min (`state.wal` mtime), with a maximum age of 10 min against constant edits, only with `normal` pressure and 9 400 MB free, and only for the machine that wins the fleet claim of the interval (eight capable machines must not all verify the tree every few minutes). A failing read answers nobody and is held off for the quiet time.
2. `B/🕸️graph/🤝️prefetch/🟦️.ts` `fleetReadStaleGenerationV1`: admitted memory = half of the host as the capacity gate sees it (`SEMIO_HOST_CAPACITY_TOTAL_MEGABYTES` reaches it through `hostMemoryMegabytesV1`), lowered to the grant of a slice-pool load when it has one (`SEMIO_GRAPH_MEMORY_MB`/grant share); NOT the current command grant (a quiet forecast grant of 1.5 GB says nothing about what the machine can give). `SEMIO_GRAPH_FLEET_READ=off` disables, `SEMIO_GRAPH_FLEET_WAIT_MILLISECONDS=<n>` shortens the bound (proofs), every non-refusal fault of the fleet step ends in "graph: the fleet-first read failed (…), reading locally".
3. `B/🌐️remote-execution/🛰️sidecar/🕸️graph/🟦️.ts` `runGraph` (private role module, so its restarts follow its own closure), `superviseGraphServerV1` (restart after a failure, `SEMIO_GRAPH_SERVER=off`), role `graph` in the sidecar dispatch.

## What a 16 GB worker will see in its next bootstrap round

With N7's booking (2 048 to 6 553 MB, admitted at once) the slice worker starts; after the shared pass it finds the stale projects. If they are more than 72 to 164 (what 4.1 to 6.5 GB can infer in four waves), it prints (EN or DE by the language of the invocation):
`graph: N stale projects, 0 taken from the fleet, this machine can infer 72 (18 per wave); asking the fleet for N`, then `graph: waiting for the fleet: x of N projects arrived` once per change, and continues when a machine with headroom answered; the next builder attempt restores the newest fleet generation (X's path) which carries the served projects. If no machine answers within 15 minutes it prints the `graph: nothing to wait for any longer: …` line and the command ends with status 75 (retry later) instead of waiting for memory for ever. A worker whose fleet cache is unreachable and whose stale set does not fit gets the same refusal at once, with `graph: the fleet cache cannot be reached …`. Until the graph-server of at least one machine with 9.4 GB free runs the new code (all agents restart within about a minute after landing 2), nobody serves: the first round after landing can therefore still end in the refusal.
