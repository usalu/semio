## A. The refusal that names no owner and says "0 MB"

### A1. Why the text is empty (reproduced in 10 lines, `scratchpad/R158/t1.ts`)

`R145` added `producers` to the values of the take loop's heavy refusal (`refuseHeavy`, `🕸️graph/📥️inference/📦️project-nodes/🟨️.mjs`) but the catalog text `graph.owner-too-heavy` has no `{producers}` placeholder. `repoErrorV1` renders both languages at the throw site and refuses a value without a placeholder, so building the refusal THREW `LanguageTextError: unknown-placeholder:graph.owner-too-heavy:producers` (reproduced: `refuseBuildV1("unserved","graph.owner-too-heavy",{named,limit,producers})` throws). That error's message contains the substring `graph.owner-too-heavy`; the host's `heavyRefusalOfV1` matched on that substring (`/graph\.owner-too-heavy/`), found no values and no JSON and returned `{named:"", limit:"0"}` (its defaults): "…yet:  need(s) more memory … (0 MB)". Every heavy refusal of the take loop since R145's landing (20:46 UTC) was rendered this way; no unit test built the real refusal (all used hand-made values).

### A2. Which owner and why it was refused on 16 GB

The log of g1-m07 does not carry the name (that is the bug). The owners phase had done 589 of 590 and the last one was in the `never` set (`neverHere`, line 417): R145's rule judged the RECORDED managed cost of an owner times the footprint factor against the alone cap of a child (4200 MB on 16 GB). Recorded costs are not measurements of one owner in a capped child: the cost table of this checkout (`.cache/f/graph-costs/owner-costs.3.json`, 378 owners) holds wave estimates and lax-heap peaks of 3000 to 6220 managed MB for owners that cost far less alone in a child (R145: `s-dev-composition` 858 MB footprint with the 512 MB heap declaration, 2517 MB managed with a lax heap; the table of this machine has `repo-search-go` 6220, `framework-3d-js` 4154, `framework-canvas-icons-rs` 4135 MB, none stopped). So any owner with a noisy recorded cost above 2100 managed MB was refused up front on a 16 GB machine without a single child run. Which of the 590 owners it was on m07 cannot be read from the store (the host log of that machine is not in it); candidates by cost table are the ones above plus `s-dev-composition`/`repo-lib`.

## B. The three reads of the `workspace` stage

### B1. What the three reads are (evidence: `scratchpad/R154/lab1/m1-ready-workspace.log` and `m1-host-*.log`)

It is NOT one `run-many` planning three times. The client log shows the same request three times: `Loading the project graph … / Planning every target of the workspace.` at lines 77, 308, 411, each preceded by `The graph service holds this request and is working on it (462 s)` and `Starting the repo graph service for this host`. The host log shows why the service was gone: `2026-11-17T22:27:51Z shutdown memory` immediately BEFORE `graph: plan answered, managed 1519 MB, resident 2909 MB`, and `22:32:26Z shutdown memory` before `plan answered … resident 3194 MB`. The service retires itself when its resident size passes `maximumResidentBytes` (2.5 GiB) by 25 percent (`sweep` in `🏛️service/🌐️host/🟨️.mjs`): the guard `!governed` looked only at active slice workers, so a service that had just finished a read (the worker is gone, the answer is being built and sent with a 2.9 to 3.2 GB resident graph) was drained and cut after 5 s: the answer was lost and the client started a new service and a full new read (each 200 s of source admission, the admission memo missed because the tree moved). Two completed reads were thrown away, the third was cut by the lab limit: "no verdict in 1015 s".

Fix (same window): `governed` also holds while `governor.inFlight>0` or any connection has a request: a service in the middle of a plan is never retired for memory; it retires at the next sweep after the last request. Law: `🏡️resident/🧪️tests/🌐️host` "a service over its resident bound is never retired while a plan is in flight" (a read of 6.2 s, longer than the 5 s drain cut, with `maximumResidentBytes:1`): red before (the request is cut), green after, and the existing "exceeding the resident bound ... finally retires" law stays green.

### B2. "Planning every target of the workspace" and the closed-plan wall (seen on g1-m08)

The text is the host's progress line for the roster request `*:*`. `invocationPlanRequestV1` sends `*:*` for every selection that is not `tasks` (so for `run-many --projects=<literal> --targets=build`), and the planner expanded `*:*` to EVERY task of the slice graph (`targets=[...tasks.keys()]`): all targets of the closure of three projects (259 projects) instead of their `build` tasks. On g1-m08 the read finished after about 1580 s (`owners done 590 of 590`) and then the plan was refused: `Closed scoped plan required: /scope/targets must contain at most 4096 item(s) (6276 tasks, 2504 edges, 259 projects, 4443 scope targets)`. That is the next wall after the read; it also made "up to date" impossible (6276 tasks incl. tests and lint).

Fix (same window, second script): `rosterTaskIdsV1(selection,tasks)` in the planner port: for an owner selection with literal projects and targets the roster is those projects times those targets that exist (what they need follows from the plan walk; a project without the target contributes nothing), for every other selection every task as before. The host text becomes "Planning the selected targets and what they need." / "Plane die gewählten Ziele und was sie brauchen.". Law `🏡️resident/🧪️tests/🎯️roster-scope` (3): closure of one selected project, exclude, missing target, `all` and targetless owner selections keep every task, a slice of 7500 tasks with three selected projects gives a scoped plan of 4 tasks inside the closed limit; red before (2 of 3).

### B3. One owner pass per run, zero on the second (NOT done)

The law "one stage run performs exactly one owner pass when nothing changes, a second run with nothing changed performs zero" needs a run of the real host (two reads of the same selection against the same tree); the evidence says the multiplication of reads came from B1 (the answer lost), not from three planning requests, so the one-read property is expected after B1 on a quiet tree. The selected read (`SEMIO_BUILD_GRAPH_SLICE_READ` default) is `loadBuildGraphUnpinnedV1` with `baseline:true` (`loadBuildGraphAttemptV1`, its own `selected/` snapshot, no generation publish/restore: R137 §2.2); the second-run-in-seconds property would need the selected read to publish and restore its sealed generation by scope (R137's key already has `scope`); that is R136's slice assembly and was not touched here.
