## Method (all three)

One parametric 3D design per scale feeds the rig, the four volumes and the three 2D views: every filled 2D shape is the orthographic projection of the body solid it draws, computed from that solid (revolve: radial envelope of its profile, capsule: hull of its end circles). Large revolves are drawn as the polygon of the reference tessellation of the silhouette validators (chord 20 mm, 24–64 segments, the −w reach `cos(π/N)` for odd N), because a true circle differs by up to 19 mm from the 32-gon the validator meshes and the 1:50 Hausdorff limit is 15 mm. Coarse scales are new designs fitted (`fit_ring`, `fit_lateral`) to the plan box of the same tier/plate at 1:50, so every scale has the same extent (bbox within 0–2 mm at all scales) and no scale is a copy (`volumes-copied` clean; emitter outlets sized per scale). Default pose `calm` = rest, so the measured silhouettes are exact.

Common physics: ρ 1.25, power-law profile (0.22), `wind_force` of NW-V1 (cd on the silhouette band, reconfiguration above 8 m/s, EN 1991-1-4); calm 2, fresh breeze 8, gale 22 m/s. The stem offsets of the wind poses are the slopes of a tapered cantilever (green E of the species) on a rotational root plate (authored-estimate), whorl/plate and spray/limb offsets authored on top. Clips: `wind-sway` (9 smooth keys, looping, first = last, phase lag by bone depth), `gust` (8 s, load tracks 0.05 → 2.75 × breeze, 70 % of the gale lean), `diurnal-transpiration` (24 s loop, sunlit-fraction tracks for every region, emitter tracks). Joint limits are set to 1.25 × the largest offset any pose or key reaches. Masses: wooden solids of the 1:50 body × green density + needle and twig estimates per tier/plate (below-ground roots excluded). Thermal: bark passive (areas = lateral areas of the 1:50 solids), needles `leaf` (exchange area, C3), two transpiration emitters (sun/shade) with ≥ 5 anchors, every pose binds every region and emitter. Structure: fixed `base-support`, support disc, stem members (revolves at 56/58/60/62 % of the stem radius per scale), slender members along every spray/limb bone.

## norway-spruce (Picea abies)

- 30.0 m × 8.48 m × 7.41 m, 3580 kg; dbh 0.60 m, clear stem to ≈ 2.3 m; 51 bones (root plate, 4 stem segments + leader, 15 whorl bones, 30 sagittal spray bones).
- Crown: 15 tiers; tier = drooping skirt of revolution on its whorl bone (slightly elliptic and off-centre), west/east sprays on spray bones with upturned tips, north/south sprays and four diagonal hanging sprays (the comb) on the whorl bone. Needles 140 m² sun (from 14 m) + 200 m² shade, gs 2.2/1.5 mm/s.
- Poses: calm, windy (1.3 kN, 17 kN m, tip 0.21 m), storm (5.5 kN, 71 kN m, tip 0.87 m), snow (25 kg/m² on the open plan area of every tier, sprays bent 6–14°).
- Scales: 1:500 stem + crown in two revolves (sun/shade) with the stepped envelope of the 1:50 crown (3 solids); 1:200 stem + 15 fitted skirts (20); 1:100 skirts, w/e sprays, two hanging sprays per tier, comb strokes (77); 1:50 everything plus bark and plan needle strokes (162).

## european-larch (Larix decidua)

- 30.0 m × 9.22 m × 7.77 m, 4077 kg; dbh 0.62 m, clear stem to ≈ 4.5 m; 39 bones; 11 thin, widely spaced storeys with the stem visible between them, upswept branch ends, long pendulous shoots; irregular radii, offsets and ellipticity per storey.
- Needles 110 + 70 m², gs 4.0/2.8 mm/s (deciduous, highest of the set). Poses: calm, windy, storm, autumn (golden needles, transpiration 15 %; the bare winter crown cannot be drawn with the same shapes, stated in notes and pose text).
- Scales: 1:500 crown envelope never thinner than the stem (3 solids); 1:200 16; 1:100 59; 1:50 126.

## atlas-cedar (Cedrus atlantica)

- 25.0 m × 16.40 m × 14.99 m, 9070 kg; dbh 0.84 m; 27 bones: stem (root plate, 3 segments, leader), 11 sagittal limb bones (their capsules run out of the rig plane to the plates), 11 vertical plate bones.
- Plates: flat domed lenses of revolution with 1–2 tufts (1:50), 1 tuft (1:100), fitted single plates (1:200, 1:500). Limb bark is its own region. Needles 190 + 140 m², gs 1.8/1.2 mm/s; hot dry reference environment (28 °C, 35 %, 850 W/m²) and a deep midday depression in the diurnal clip.
- Poses: calm, windy (0.7 kN), storm (3.0 kN, 40 kN m), snow (20 kg/m² on every plate, 36.6 kN, limbs bend 2–3°, plates tip outwards).
- 1:500 keeps the eleven plates and limbs (24 solids) instead of one crown: the tiers are the identity of the species (stated in `decisions`); suitability passes (min feature 0.27 mm).

## Remaining doubts

- The drawings are stylised: tiers and plates are solids of revolution, so their lower edges are straight in side view (the hanging sprays break them up at 1:50 and 1:100); the result reads as the species but is more schematic than the hand-drawn polyhedra of the older trees.
- Stem segments meet in a visible seam line where the stem shows (larch between storeys, cedar at 6.5 m), as noted for the older vegetation.
- Leaf conductances, root rotational stiffnesses, needle masses and the Atlas cedar wood values are authored estimates (no Wood Handbook entry for Cedrus); Norway spruce and European larch use Wood Handbook proxies.
- The larch autumn pose keeps summer geometry (shapes cannot be toggled).
