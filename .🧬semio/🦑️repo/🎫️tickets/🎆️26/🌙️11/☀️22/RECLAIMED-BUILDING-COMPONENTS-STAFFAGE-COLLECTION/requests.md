
## reclaimed-steel-column — structure solver: one rigid body for parts that stand on the ground on their own

Measurement: `solveStructure` solves the reactions of the whole asset as one rigid body (`reactionSolution`, 6x6 on all active supports). Storage aids that stay on the ground (two 160 x 160 x 1000 mm bearers, 12.0 kg each) either add their weight to the column supports (bearers with mass and only the column-on-bearer contacts active: column diagram off by 236 N of 2 495 N, 9 %) or split the column reaction with their own ground contacts (contacts at the same x share the vertical force equally: the column sees half its reaction). Workaround used: bearers and sling are massless bones, asset mass = column only (254.4 kg), stated in `structure.assumptions`. Proposal: solve each rig subtree that is not tied by a movable joint and has its own active support as its own body (or let a contact anchor declare which bodies it separates), so storage aids can carry their real mass.

## reclaimed-paving-slab-pallet — volumes-thermal-coverage: copies of one `repeat` never seam

Measurement: the 1:100 stock packs were first one 500 x 50 x 500 mm slab box with `repeat` count 8 (step 50 mm, faces exactly touching). `info:outerAreaM2:slab-stock` read 9.600 m² (every face of all 16 copies, `info:cavityAreaM2` 7.0 m²) against 2.600 m² for the same slabs as 16 separate box solids, and the face of the neighbouring region against that pack was dropped too (`slab-field` 1.100 against 1.600 m²). Separate solids of one region that touch count as a seam (C29 b); copies of one `repeat` do not. Workaround used: individual boxes at 1:100. Proposal: let the seam rule of C29 b treat the copies of a `repeat` like separate solids, so stacked goods can use `repeat` as the brief asks.

## reclaimed-paving-slab-pallet — structure solver: reinstalled pieces share one rigid body with the pallet

Same root as the steel-column request above: the six field slabs lying in the field are rig descendants of the pallet, so the 2.5 kN wheel load is shared by the nine pallet feet too (equal-stiffness rigid-body solution) and member forces appear at the field slab carriers. Stated in `structure.assumptions`; same proposal (separate bodies per subtree with its own active supports).

## reclaimed-parquet-crate — structure solver: one rigid body for parts that rest separately

Same root as the requests above. Lifted pose: the 10.8 kg bearers stay on the ground but their weight goes into the two fork-hook reactions (3.6 % over the true fork load); reinstalled poses: the relaid 27-block field (8.98 kg) and the crate on its bearers (292.9 kg) rest separately, but the equal-stiffness rigid-body solution shares the crate weight with the four field bedding contacts. Stated in `structure.assumptions`; `structure.supports` is empty and every pose names its own contacts (bearers, fork hooks, field). Proposal: solve each group of bones under its own active supports separately.

## reclaimed-rafter-bundle — structure solver: one rigid body, measured on rafter, stack and slings

Same root cause as the steel-column, paving-slab and parquet entries above, measured here. `solveStructure` treats a pose as one rigid body on supports of equal stiffness, so a pose with two bodies that are supported separately shares their reactions between all supports. Reinstalled pose (rafter on wall plate pin and ridge purlin roller, the remaining stack of 2.64 kN on two bearer contacts): solver 1.292 / 1.136 / 1.286 / 1.134 kN against the hand values 1.347 / 0.862 / 1.320 / 1.320 kN; the rafter then shows a false end moment of 844 Nm at its free foot and a peak of 898 Nm instead of 498 Nm. With all vertical loads the rigid reaction field is a plane a + b x + c z, so four independent vertical supports can only be matched by chance. Workaround used here (no shared code touched): the determinate yard reactions under the bearers are applied as known `carried` loads (`yard-under-bearer-a/b`) in the reinstalled poses and in `lifted` (bearers left on the ground), and the roof or the hook supports alone stay contacts; every pose then reproduces the hand values exactly (residual below 0.001 N). Internal supports of a part that lies on another (the reinstalled rafter on its stickers) are frictionless contacts; that is exact in the stored pose. Proposal: let `solveStructure` split the evaluated state into the connected groups of bones whose subtree carries its own contact or support set (or accept a pose-level `bodies` declaration) and solve each group on its own supports.

## reclaimed-timber-roof-truss — structure solver: a truss is a closed lattice, the rig is a tree

`solveStructure` cuts member diagrams through the rig tree, so the closed triangles of a truss cannot be represented: the top chords and webs hang from the bottom chord at their lower joints and their diagrams are cantilever cuts, not axial forces. Measured on the reinstalled W-truss under roof covering and full snow (9.00 m span, 2.20 m rise, 17.25 kN total): reactions 8.63 / 8.63 kN are exact (hand value 8.63), but the moment view shows 17.5 kN·m in each top chord and 70 N·m in the long webs, where a pin-jointed Fink truss carries about -19.6 kN compression in the top chords at the heels (8.63 kN / sin 26.05°) and +17.6 kN tension in the bottom chord, with only the local panel moment of about 1 kN·m (1.42 kN/m over 2.5 m panels). Stored poses that share stillage weight are avoided here by giving the stillage no mass (it stays on the ground in every pose and carries nothing). Proposal: an optional `rig.closures: [{ bone, at, to, toAt }]` (a pin that ties a bone point to another bone point) and a frame or truss stiffness solve over the closed graph, so trusses, frames and lattice girders (rows 04, 09, 32) show axial member forces.

## reclaimed-trapezoidal-sheet-stack — structure solver (one rigid body)

Measured on `reclaimed-trapezoidal-sheet-stack`, pose `reinstalled-snow` at 1:50: the reinstalled sheet (4 purlin supports) and the remaining 29-sheet bundle (4 bearer contacts) are solved as one rigid body on equal-stiffness supports, so every support carries 2.19 kN; by hand the sheet alone (0.68 kN/m² × 1.035 m, three spans of 1.80 m) gives about 1.27 kN at an inner purlin and 0.5 kN at an end purlin, and the bundle's bearers carry the rest. The same happens to every reclaimed asset whose storage aids stay behind (`lifted`: bearers' weight goes to the hooks) or whose reinstalled part shares a pose with the remaining stack. Proposal: let `solveStructure` split the rig into bodies at prismatic or revolute joints whose subtree has its own active supports in the pose (or an explicit per-pose `bodies`/`detached` list), and solve each body's reactions separately. Worked around by stating the approximation in `structure.assumptions`.

## reclaimed-floorboard-stack — support solids for pose-dependent supports

Measurement: a reclaimed component changes its supports with the pose (stored: 6 bearer contacts; lifted: 2 crane-hook pins; reinstalled: 7 sleeper contacts of the strip). The structural solver activates every `structure.supports` anchor in every pose (`supportsOf` in `🏗️structure`), so listing the bearer contacts there makes them share the hook load in the lifted pose (equal-stiffness rigid body: about 6/8 of the pack weight would go to the bearers). The asset therefore keeps `structure.supports: []` and gives each pose its contacts, which the solver honours. Consequence: no `part: "support"` solids are possible (`volume-anchor` requires an anchor of `structure.supports`), although the brief asks for supports by type at the support anchors. Proposal: let `part: "support"` solids also name contact/support anchors that a named pose or clip lists in `contacts` (checked in that pose), or add per-pose `supports`. Work-around used: members rest on the ground/contact anchors; no shared code changed.

## reclaimed-roof-tile-pallet — structure solver: one rigid body for a lot that stays on the ground and a part that is reinstalled elsewhere

Measurement (pose `reinstalled`, 1:50 forces view, residual 0): the twelve rafter pins of the reinstalled double-lap patch carry 1.32 kN in sum (73 to 146 N each) and the nine pallet feet the rest, although the patch with its battens and snow weighs 0.39 kN; the single rigid-body equal-stiffness solve sends about 0.93 kN of the pallet's 2.6 kN into the rafters. Same root cause as the `reclaimed-steel-column` request above; here the storage aids cannot be made massless because the 138 tiles left on the pallet are part of the lot. Workaround: none, stated in `structure.assumptions`; the patch tiles hang under their battens in the rig so the batten members at least collect the patch weight. Proposal as above: solve each part of the rig that has its own active supports and no movable joint load path to the others as its own body.
