
## used-coffee-table — structure solver: separated parts stay one rigid body

`solveStructure` treats the whole asset as one instantaneously rigid body on equal springs. Storage aids that stay behind (here a 28.6 kg depot table) therefore share their reactions with the carried or set-up object: in the poses `carried` and `in-use` of `used-coffee-table` the four depot glides and the hands / leg ends of the coffee table (22.6 kg, 1.10 m ahead) take a reaction distribution linear in x instead of each body carrying its own weight (hand calculation for `in-use` with only the coffee-table weight: about 8 % leaks to the near depot glides, front and rear legs 0.17 W against 0.29 W per leg though the centre of mass is central). The stored pose (a resting stack) is exact. Proposal: solve each group of bones that is not connected by contact in the pose (rig subtrees whose contacts carry them separately) as its own rigid body, or let a pose declare `bodies: [[bones…], …]`. Work-around here: none possible without a product change; stated in `structure.assumptions`.

## used-coat-stand — structure solver: separated parts stay one rigid body (second case)

Same defect as `used-coffee-table`. In `used-coat-stand` pose `carried` the 1.2 kg moving blanket lies on the floor while two hands carry the 4.62 kg stand tilted 40 degrees; the grips (pin at 0.75 m, vertical roller at 1.20 m along the pole) report 61.0 N and 3.9 N, i.e. they carry the blanket's 11.8 N as well, and its weight 0.48 m behind the lower grip shifts the share between the hands (hand calculation for the stand alone: lower 29.7 N, upper 15.7 N). With a blanket lying aside in a home pose the same leak falsely stabilised the tipping check (11.8 N at 0.8 m behind the axis = 9.4 N m against 9.3 N m of the stand itself), so the blanket now stays under the feet as a drip mat in the home poses. The 2D plan of the tilted `carried` pose also distorts flat radial plan bands of curved rods (feet, hooks drawn as plan bands are carried with the depth of their elevation twin); only a per-bone 3D shape would draw them right. Proposal as for the coffee table (per-body solve or pose `bodies`).

## used-kitchen-base-cabinet — volumes-silhouette (tiled boundary distance)

At 1:200 (front view) a real 2 mm gap between the plinth panel (y 0.014) and the blanket (y 0.012), drawn identically in 2D and 3D, gave `p95M:front` = `hausdorffM:front` = 0.1641 m (limit 0.12 m) with IoU 0.9994. Dropping the 3D plinth gave 0.019 m; dropping any 2D shape changed nothing. 0.1641 m is exactly the top bin of the boundary histogram (21 cells of 7.8 mm), which `boundarySpread` assigns when one side has no boundary inside a tile; a sub-cell gap seems to leave a boundary in one raster and none in the other within a tile. Proposal: in `measureBanded`/`boundarySpread`, measure such cells against the coarse boundary instead of counting them at the top bin. Worked around by closing the gap at 1:200 (plinth from y 0.012, generalisation of a 2 mm gap that the scale cannot show). The same asset also shows the rigid-body leak of the two entries above: in `carried` the 3.2 kg blanket left on the floor is carried by the four grips (stated in `structure.assumptions`).

## used-oil-filled-radiator — volumes-thermal-coverage with `envelope: true`

A typified row declared `envelope: true` (validator item 13) is measured for `volumes-thermal-coverage` as if its gaps were closed: the 1:200 model of `used-oil-filled-radiator` (seven typified 30 mm fins over the true 0.358 m stack, 24.7 mm open gaps, above half the 30 mm feature) gives an outer surface of all regions of 0.990 m² against 2.688 m² (63 %, limit 30 %) with the flag, and passes thermal coverage (and cross-scale) without it. The gaps of a typified row of fins, louvres or slats are real air passages, so the outer area should be flooded through the solids as they are, and only `volumes-cross-scale` should use the hull. Proposal: let the envelope hull apply to `volumes-cross-scale` (and the volume band) only, never to the flood of `volumes-thermal-coverage`. Work-around here: no `envelope` flag (cross-scale passes without it).

## used-moving-boxes — volumes-thermal-coverage: a face resting on a closed neighbour of another region is not outer

C29 says faces covered by solids of other regions count as outer area. Measured on `used-moving-boxes` 1:50: region `carried-box` (a closed 0.614 x 0.344 x 0.354 m box, total outer surface 1.1007 m²) measures 0.898 m², i.e. its 0.211 m² bottom resting on the lid of the box below (region `stacked-boxes`) is not counted, because the covering lid itself faces only the closed cavity of its own box (C31 c: the covering solid must have air-reachable surface). Likewise the lid of the bottom pair under it. The rule therefore depends on whether the neighbour is hollow. Work-around: `areaM2` set to the measured definition (outer surface less the resting faces: 0.8895 and 1.5448 m², within factor 1.5), stated in the thermal assumptions. Proposal: count a face covered by a solid of another region as outer whenever that solid belongs to a region with air-reachable surface anywhere, not only at the covering solid.

## used-wc-pan-cistern — revolve `lateralScale` axis

README "Volume" says a revolved solid's `lateralScale` stretches along w (lateral); in the WC pan authoring it stretched the forward direction instead. The author compensated in `assets/used-wc-pan-cistern/author-*` and filed nothing. Proposal: confirm the axis in `🔨️modules/🧊️volume` against the README and fix whichever is wrong, then re-check every asset that uses `lateralScale`.
