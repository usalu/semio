# Planner slice latency: why a GIS slice read takes 15+ minutes, and the fix

Measured on 2026-11-26 15:30 to 16:10 on this Mac (16 GB, load 6 to 13, swap 6 to 7.6 GB, 15 agents). Scripts: `planner-slice-replay.ts`, `planner-memo-staleness.ts`, `planner-owner-cost.ts`, `planner-scope-overlap.ts`, `planner-registry-size.ts` (all in this folder). Logs: `🗑️generated/planner-before.log`, `planner-owner-cost.log`, `planner-after-tests*.log`.

## What the phase is

"checking the file inventory of the tree: scanning the owners (N completed owner checks) (M verified memo rows) (K owners admitted)" is `inventory()` in `build/🕸️graph/📥️load/🟦️.ts`: step `memo-check` (verify the remembered rows), then `scoped-discovery` = `closeBuildOwnerAdmissionV1` over the registry (4995 owners) with one `discoverBuildOwnerAdmissionFactsV1` per (owner, targets, input groups). It does not walk the tree: the catalog phase uses `admission.files`.

## Measured cause

1. The slice is already proportional to its closure, but the closure is large and legitimately observed. Replaying `closeBuildOwnerAdmissionV1` on real memos (2 s each, offline): scope `2f245baf` admits 616 owners and observes 612 of them (600 with input groups, requested by `dependencyInputs` of Cargo packages such as `norm-din4108-rs` with 560 native dependencies); scope `6e5f58d5` admits 443, observes 435. The ownership-only mode for `file` edges (planner-closure-fix.md) leaves almost nothing to skip, because the next edge kind (`input`, `dependency`) upgrades the owner. Live: 1252 to 1321 owners for the s-shell/puzzle closure (4995 in the registry), 1685 to 1692 owner checks (the surplus is settle rounds re-walking the closure, memo-trusted).
2. A cold owner discovery costs 0.44 s on average (22 ms median, 5.4 s for the first one that boots the analyses; 13 sampled owners, 8 s wall, `planner-owner-cost.log`), 287 s extrapolated for 645 owners, about 9 min for 1250 owners on an idle core. Under the observed load (swap, GC of a 3.9 to 5.6 GB heap with 14 to 20 M objects, 2 slice workers shared by all agents) this becomes the 15+ minutes seen. Warm, the same scope costs 11 s for 273,923 memo rows (`planner-memo-staleness.ts`), i.e. the cold to warm ratio is about 25:1.
3. Why scopes were cold every time: the admission format that keys every owner fact (in-process memo, `*.admission.json`, owner checkpoint packs, fleet exchange) was `sha256` over the BYTES of 8 files, two of which are the owner discovery module `inference/🔎️owner-discovery/🟨️.mjs` (50 KB, committed 7 times in 6 days, edited today 12:55 and 15:22 by other agents, with `[DEBUG]` instrumentation) and its `wasm-outputs` helper. Every edit of that file, even a debug line, moved the format and made every remembered owner fact of every scope unreadable (`format` mismatch is a miss, never a partial hit). The memos on disk show the format changing on 11-21, 11-22 (twice), 11-23 and 11-25; today's edit orphaned all 131 scope directories (2.2 GB). This is the "implementation-stamp change outside the requested closure invalidates owner records" case: no row of the owner is affected, only the stamp.
4. Other measured contributions (not changed):
   - Slice pool: `maximum: max(2, min(4, floor(totalMB/8192)))` = 2 workers on this Mac; both were held 29 min by other agents' cold loads, so my dry run spent 12+ min only in "waiting for a free slice worker (2 of 2 workers busy, 2 load(s) ahead)" (`planner-before.log`). The owner discovery farm needs at least 2 children inside the memory grant and is off here (no `[graph-owner-farm]` lines), so cold discovery is sequential.
   - Cross-scope reuse of owner facts is only partial: exact (owner, targets, inputs) suffixes shared between scopes are 98 of 451 and 110 of 275 (`planner-scope-overlap.ts`), because each scope requests different input group sets; superset reuse would not be exact, so it was not added.
   - Replicated tree I/O is not the bottleneck: hashing all 4568 file rows of a 645-owner memo takes 1.7 s; the 273k rows in 11 s including pseudo rows. The `[repo] still working ... source.served` lines are the wait for the graph service, not the scan.
   - Restart churn and the settle rounds are covered by planner-restart-churn.md (planner-quiet gate) and planner-closure-fix.md; the registry-follow, owner-isolation and admission fixes are in place and their suites pass.

## Fix

`build/🕸️graph/🔐️authority/🟦️.ts`:
- `graphAdmissionFormatUrlsV1` no longer lists `owner-discovery/🟨️.mjs` and `owner-discovery/🕸️wasm-outputs/🟦️.ts`; it keeps `🚪️admission/🟦️.ts`, the roster, the file-owner map and the three admission schemas.
- New `graphAdmissionStampsV1()` returns the declared stamp of every inferrer (`semio.graph-inferrer.<id>/<n>`, from the protocol in `inference/🔖️stamps/🧬️schema/🔣️.json`, which already carries `owner-discovery/1`, `source-analyses/3`, `script-inputs/5`, `target-policy/3`, ...), sorted.
- `graphAdmissionFormatHashV1(capture, stamps)` hashes the admission files plus those stamps. `loadedGraphAdmissionFormatV1` therefore moves when the admission semantics move (closure module, schemas) or when an inferrer stamp is bumped on purpose, and stays when a discovery module is edited without a bump.

Why this is sound here: the protocol already states that a record names "the protocol version of the code, never the bytes"; every remembered row still revalidates its own observations; a behaviour change of discovery is a stamp bump of its inferrer (the rule of the stamps protocol), after which every record is re-derived. An edit that is not a behaviour change (refactor, instrument, comment, other agents' work in the same file) no longer costs a cold re-discovery of every scope. Rule for authors: change owner discovery output, then bump `owner-discovery` in the stamps protocol.

Files changed:
- `🧰️framework/🛍️products/🦑️repo/🔨️modules/📚️library/🏗️build/🕸️graph/🔐️authority/🟦️.ts`
- `🧰️framework/🛍️products/🦑️repo/🔨️modules/📚️library/🏗️build/🕸️graph/📥️load/🧠️admission-memo/🧪️tests/🟦️.ts` (the format test now asserts: moves with each admission file and with every declared stamp, order-independent, keeps with every other graph module and with the bytes of both discovery modules; a memo of the same format still hits after a discovery-module edit and misses after an admission-module edit).

# Addendum: restarts from zero on a live tree (dev-s-react-prototype)

## What the browser-lane log shows (`🗑️generated/browser-s-shell.log`, host log `host-2bf1553db5e2.log`)

- The cycle "waiting for host capacity, waiting for the graph state lease, asking the fleet, checking the file inventory (0 distinct observations)" recurs at 108 s, 1462 s and 1760 s: that is a NEW slice worker, not a settle round. The line before the second reset is the guard `host-slice-owners-cut`: "the slice worker grew to 5862 MB, more than the 0 MB a plan may take on this machine, so the work is cut smaller". The pool (`🏡️resident`, `🧠️memory/✂️cut`) stops the worker and starts it again with half the projects and wave; only finished project records survive. The admission closure (the 15-minute `scoped-discovery`) lives in the worker heap and survived only through the owner checkpoint packs, which were flushed every 180 s or 32 records, so each kill lost up to 3 minutes of owner discovery plus everything not yet published, and each restart paid memo verification again.
- The "1 path(s) changed under the read, 0 owner(s) forgotten" text is the standing `note` of the last follow-up round (`graphDirtyTextV1`): a path nobody in the closure read forgets no owner. Those paths are not what restarted the worker. The settle and repair machinery already follows moved paths per owner: repair-rounds suite tests "an edit of a file nobody read changes nothing", "an edit of a file one owner's admission read observes that owner again and no other", "a changed member whose rows still hold is forgotten by name", "a worker above the heap threshold hands the read over at each pass boundary". They pass.
- Rows of other `BUILD_AUTHORITY_CHANGED` restarts in the resident logs name `taxonomy.json` and `📋️build-owners.json` (loaded at module load, contradicted by a later read); these two files change whenever an owner is added and are not covered by the loaded-implementation pin. Not changed (needs a design decision on which data files are pinned per process).

## Memory (measured)

- Discovery itself is light: 160 owners of a real scope in one process grew the heap from 47 to 63 MB (0.3 MB per owner, rows discarded), RSS 556 to 618 MB (`planner-owner-cost.ts`). Owner discovery is I/O bound on the replicated tree: 102 s user against 167 s system time for 160 owners (1.75 s per owner under load, against 0.44 s on a quiet host).
- The 5.6 to 5.9 GB of the slice worker therefore come from what the read retains, not from discovery: heap census at the kill is 14 to 20 M live objects (7.3 M strings, 4.1 M objects, 3.8 M arrays, 0.4 M WeakRefs) and 2 GB external, i.e. the rows of 1252 to 1321 owners (4,900 rows per owner, 330 k distinct), their facts and the pass memo. A closure that admits 1252 owners as `file` edges of `puzzle-plugin` (714) and `framework-os-node-graph-rs` (304) is the size driver; only owners that actually need inputs are observed, but their rows are all held until the seal.

## Change made for restart cost

- `build/🕸️graph/📥️load/💾️owner-checkpoint/🧬️schema/🔣️.json`: `flushMilliseconds` 180000 to 15000. A worker that is cut, killed by the warden or restarted loses at most 15 s of discovered owners instead of 3 minutes, and the restart recovers every flushed owner through its hold check instead of rediscovering it.
- Test (`owner-checkpoint/🧪️tests/🟦️.ts`, 8 pass): "a worker killed mid-discovery loses at most the declared flush interval of owners": asserts the declared limit is at most 15 s and that a save after the interval publishes the earlier unflushed owner to a reopened checkpoint.

## Not done

- No source change bounds the 6 GB retention (streaming rows to the row log until the seal, dropping rows of trusted owners). The row-table capture (`captureObservedSourceInputsV1`) already stores 4 bytes per distinct row; the remaining retention needs a heap profile of a live worker, which could not be taken while the pool is saturated.
- Resident service not restarted.

# Addendum 2: pinned data snapshot, I/O and memory profile

## (3) taxonomy.json / build-owners.json no longer restart the read (done, tested)

Cause: files a process reads while its modules load (owner registry, taxonomy, policies, schemas) become module-load authority rows. A peer edit of one of them mid-read made a later inference row contradict that authority, `conflictingInferenceErrorV1` answered `BUILD_AUTHORITY_CHANGED` ("implementation authority changed ... restart the owned build invocation: analysis:.../taxonomy.json, file:.../build-owners.json", seen in the resident logs) and the whole load started again in a new worker. Only registered implementation files were exempt (`finishOnLoadedImplementationV1`).

Fix in `build/👁️observation/🟨️.mjs`: when the module-load authority is sealed (`inferenceAuthorityDigestV1`, `inferenceAuthorityRowsV1`) the `file` and `analysis` rows of the process are kept once as its pinned data snapshot (`pinLoadedDataFactsV1`, first fact per kind/path/provider wins). `loadedImplementationRecordedFactV1` (used when a row is recorded) now records the pinned fact for such a path in a process that finishes on its loaded code, so a mid-read change does not contradict the sealed authority and ends nothing; the sealed result names the old digest, so the next read finds it stale and observes again (the next process pins the new bytes). Registry growth is still followed by the registry-follow path, which compares the parsed registry itself.

Test: `observation/🧪️tests/🔒️loaded-implementation/🟦️.ts` (8 pass): a data file read at load and edited afterwards records the loaded digest after `finishOnLoadedImplementationV1`, a file not read at load records its current digest, and the next process pins the new one. Also green: settle-conflict 3, stale-row 3, reuse 12, read-set 2, rows-at-end 1, seal-under-churn 3, repair-rounds 30, admission-memo 14, owner-checkpoint 8.

## (2) I/O profile (measured, not changed)

- A per-call wrapper around `node:fs` cannot be done in bun: named imports bind to the builtin, assigning to the default export object (`planner-fs-count.ts`) counted 1 call, and a `Bun.plugin` resolve hook (`planner-fs-preload.ts`) did not intercept the builtin. `dtruss` and `fs_usage` need root. No per-path syscall counts could be produced.
- What the process counters say for 13 real owners (4,915 rows each): user 3.3 s, system 3.8 s, 141,460 minor page faults, 120 major faults, 10,198 voluntary context switches (`planner-fs-run.ts`, `resourceUsage`). At about 11,000 faults per owner against 0.3 MB retained heap per owner, the system time is dominated by allocation churn and paging under the host's swap pressure (page faults and context switches), not by repeated stat/readdir of the same paths.
- The dedupe the coordinator asked for already exists: `fileFactHolds` is one `stat` per row and falls back to a hash only when the stamp moved (`fileShaMemoV1`, stamp-keyed, 200 k entries); directory facts have `directoryFactsV1.lookup(path, stamp)`; fields have `fieldDigests`. Rows restored from a memo or checkpoint carry `mtimeMs/ctimeMs/ino = 0`, so after a worker restart every file row of every owner is re-read and hashed once (4,568 file rows: 1.7 s in `planner-memo-staleness.ts`), which is cheap and is not a storm.

## (1) Memory profile (measured, not changed)

- An isolated slice load cannot be started outside the pool without the issued invocation environment (`receiveScriptProcessInvocation` in `slice-pool/👷️worker.ts`), so `--heap-prof` of a whole-closure load was not possible; the pool was also saturated (my `dev-s-react-prototype --dry-run` waited "2 of 2 workers busy, 2 loads ahead").
- Pieces measured in isolation (`planner-owner-cost.ts`, `planner-memo-heap.ts`): discovery retains 0.3 MB per owner; reading the 154 MB memo of a 616-owner scope costs 163 MB heap (273,923 rows) and 268 MB with the 645 owners' facts and row references. Extrapolated to the 1,252 to 1,321 owners of the dev-s-react-prototype closure that is about 0.6 GB for memo plus facts and 0.4 GB for discovery caches, i.e. about 1 GB of the 5.6 to 5.9 GB heap (3.2 to 3.4 GB managed, 2.0 to 2.2 GB external, 20 M objects). The remaining 4+ GB are not in these parts; the candidates are the per-project inference nodes and the compiler analyses of the 17.5 k-file TypeScript program (88 s each) retained until the seal, which only a heap snapshot of a live worker can separate. No memory change was made without that evidence.
- Restart cost is what was reduced instead: the 15 s checkpoint flush (addendum 1) bounds what each halving restart loses.

# Addendum 3: heap diagnostic in the worker, the dominant retainer, and its bound

## (3) `SEMIO_BUILD_GRAPH_POLICY=current`
`extractGraphPolicyV1` (`🕸️graph/🕰️generations/🔌️load`) reads it for any command and `current` means "serve the newest sealed generation without verification and without inference" for reads that go through generations (`repo show`, `graph`; the gate treats a `show` query under `current` as a sealed read that asks for no capacity: `sealedCurrentReadV1`). A selected plan (`repo run <target>`) does not read a generation: it reads a selected slice (`loadSlice`, owners of the closure only), so the variable does not turn it into a sealed reuse, and the slice read of the same scope can still take the memo path above. I would not make launchers use `current` for `run`: it skips verification, so a launcher could start on a graph that is stale against edits (here edited continuously). What a launcher can safely use is the memo: a second `repo run` of the same scope re-verifies the rows (seconds when idle, minutes under load) and only re-infers the owners whose rows moved; the cost to remove is the verification time under load, which the memory release and the string bounds help, and which a fleet-published sealed selected graph (`[fleet] Published the nx-graph of state ...`) already serves to other machines.
