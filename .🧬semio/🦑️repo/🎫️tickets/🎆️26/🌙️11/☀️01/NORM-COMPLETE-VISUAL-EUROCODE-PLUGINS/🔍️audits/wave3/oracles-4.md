## Disagreements and analysis
1. EN 1997 derived characteristic values (oracle was wrong, repaired). The Rust runs every check on `resolved_document` (derived c_u and phi' replace the declared values of the layer), the check-outcome oracle ran on the declared values. On `ground-showcase-pass|fail` the derived phi' of the sand (about 40 degrees from three independent samples) is far above the declared 32, so bearing, sliding and Bishop utilisations differed by a factor 1.3 to 3.3 between oracle and Rust in ten committed rows (`pass-sc-10/11/16/17/29/30`, `fail-sc-65`, and the three `phi` rows). I changed the oracle to follow the Rust semantics (specified in the Rust doc comment and the registry sample rule), replaced the seven rows by the new oracle values and dropped the three `phi` rows that now lie in the near-limit band (1.011, 0.954, 1.011). This invalidates the earlier belief that these rows had been checked against the Rust: if they were run, the Rust would have failed them; if not, they were wrong on arrival.
2. EN 1997: the ground-showcase "pass" document fails `characteristic.cu.layer-clay` (two independent soundings reach the clay, the rule needs three), so the "pass" showcase is not a pass document for this family. Not changed (adding a third CPT is a fixture decision for the showcase owner).
3. EN 1997: `t_95` of the Rust interpolates the Student table linearly in 1/nu between nu = 10, 20 and infinity, the oracle uses the exact `scipy.stats.t.ppf`; for 11 to 19 degrees of freedom the table gives up to about 0.3 percent more than the exact quantile. Rows built so far have n - 1 <= 10, so they agree to 1e-6; a layer with 12 to 20 independent samples needs a tolerance of 1e-2.
4. EN 1992 torsion, wall thickness: the Rust and the shared twin use t_ef >= 2 (c + phi_l/2), i.e. 2c + phi_l, ignoring the link diameter; 6.3.1(1) says twice the distance between the edge and the centre of the longitudinal bar, which includes the link (c + phi_w + phi_l/2). The two readings differ for slender sections where t_min governs (300 x 550 beam with 25 mm bars: 105 mm against 121 mm, about 15 percent in T_Rd,max). The fixtures (400 x 800) have A/u = 133 mm governing, so the rows do not expose it. The oracle follows the Rust reading (the compliance twin and the spec text give the same formula). Open design question; I did not change the Rust.
5. EN 1992 torsion, chord split and cot: links, longitudinal steel and the interaction use the cot theta of the shear-governing combination while T_Ed is the torsion-governing one (`uls_v` and `uls_t` taken independently), and `T_Rd,max` of the family `6.3.torsion` uses cot = 1 while the interaction uses the shear cot; both are Rust modelling choices that the oracle reproduces from the Rust doc comments (a more conservative reading would combine T and V of one combination). The tension-share of the outline is 0.5 for every rectangle.
6. EN 1992 strut-and-tie: the truss is a fixed three-node model (supports at 0.15 h, node at 0.1 h below the top) and the load 2 V_Ed is taken from the combination with the largest |M| (`governing(.., "uls")`), not the largest shear; the oracle follows this definition. The node factors k1, k2 and the cracked-strut factor are unverified registry rows (`unverifiedBasis` risk, see status risk).
7. EN 1995 T-90: the document `Case` has `k` as one slip modulus for all interfaces and the Rust also emits an `ei_ef_u` row with K_u = 2/3 K_ser when a shear is given; the oracle does not emit the ULS stiffness variant (info outcome only).
8. EN 1999 6.7.7: the Winkler strut model with the cantilever strip spring and `lambda_max = 1.0` is a mechanics model of the Rust ("registry row, mechanics threshold"); I could not identify the clause rule (6.7.7 longitudinal stiffeners is not an edge-stiffener rule in the standard as recalled), so the oracle repeats the model from first principles (textbook beam on elastic foundation, minimum buckling load 2 sqrt(K E I)); agreement proves the arithmetic, not the clause.
