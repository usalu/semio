## 1. Diaphragm classification (rigid or flexible)
- Module `🧬️schema/📇️masonry-catalogue/🥞️diaphragm/🦀️.rs` (in the catalogue crate, because the M-21 bracing case below it needs it; re-exported through `checks::masonry`).
- Model: the diaphragm of a plan direction is a shear beam of rigidity `G t * D` (declared `diaphragmStiffnessNPerM`, weakest declared value; D = plan depth in the load direction) loaded uniformly by the storey shear and carried by springs at the bracing wall lines. Wall spring: fixed-fixed, `k = 1 / (h^3/(E t l^3) + 1.2 h/(0.4 E t l))`, `E = K_E f_k` (`ndp.en1996.k-e`: EN 1000, DE 950). Solution: 201-node linear finite elements, one tridiagonal solve (`deflections`), springs interpolated between the two nodes they fall in.
- Displacement comparison (EN 1998-1 4.3.1(4)): ratio = largest diaphragm displacement over the rigid-assumption displacement `F/sum k`; rigid when ratio <= 1.10. The check reports the diaphragm deflection (max minus average wall drift), the average wall drift and the rigid drift in mm.
- Check `en1996.9.3.diaphragm.<x|y>` (document level, detailing part): utilisation = ratio / 1.10, `warn_above(1.0)`, remedy `at_least` on `walls[id=..].diaphragmStiffnessNPerM` (weakest wall) with the stiffness that makes the diaphragm rigid (`solve_threshold`). Nothing is emitted without a declared stiffness or with fewer than two bracing walls per direction.
- Load distribution follows the classification where the distribution already lives: the bracing case of M-21 (`bracing_case`, `bracing_distribution`). A flexible diaphragm marks the case `diaphragm = flexible` with the vector `trib` of tributary fractions (`Diaphragm::tributary_of`, half the distance to the neighbouring wall line, walls on one line split by stiffness); `bracing_distribution` then distributes `V` by tributary width without torsion, a rigid one keeps stiffness plus torsion. The scene diagram `bracing` states the rule in its description. M-95 text now points to the document check (no more "recorded only").
- 3D: scene module `🎬️scene/🥞️diaphragm/🦀️.rs`: plate of the diaphragm on the first bracing wall, strips shifted by the solved profile (magnified so the largest shift is 12 % of the span, factor in the accessible detail), undeformed outline as rods, utilisation colour from the check, annotation-free. Dependencies of the entity: all walls (`entity_dependencies`). Entity kind falls back to `wall` (no new insert palette row).
- Oracle: Python `diaphragm_rows` in `🔮️oracles/🚦️check-outcome/🐍️.py` solves the same shear beam with PyNite (axial bar of equal rigidity on spring supports; validated against the closed form `F/(2k) + F L/(8 S)`, exact to 1e-12). Rust has the same closed-form unit test. Fixture rows: `detail-rich-pass` 0.909406488 (rigid), `detail-rich-fail` 1242.90681 (flexible, kept in the fixture, not in the feature because the row is a warning, not a fail status).
- Mechanics solver: not used (masonry crates have no dependency on it and no shear panel element exists there); see request.
- Unverified: the 1.10 limit (recalled "10 %", registered as `ndp.en1996.m95.diaphragm-ratio`, script `🧰️ndp/wave2_en1996_v6.py`, codegen re-run), E = K_E f_k and G = 0.4 E reuse existing registry rows.
