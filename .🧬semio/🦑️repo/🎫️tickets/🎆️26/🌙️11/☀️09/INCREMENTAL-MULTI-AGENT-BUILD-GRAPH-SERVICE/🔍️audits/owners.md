## 1. Governor admission (done)

Files: `BUILD/🧠️memory/{🟨️.mjs,🟨️.d.mts,🧬️schema/🔣️.json,🧪️tests/{🟦️.ts,🐍️.py,🔣️.json,🧬️schema/🔣️.json}}`, `BUILD/🕸️graph/📥️inference/📦️project-nodes/🟨️.mjs`.

- `graphOwnerAdmissionV1` is the pure law: the first owner always runs; an owner of a kind without a learned peak runs alone; otherwise measured headroom (limit minus usage minus margin) minus the remaining estimates of the in-flight owners must cover the estimate of the new one. The estimate of an in-flight owner is the learned peak of its kind (else the guess), never below what it has taken; the headroom growth is split between the running owners in proportion to what each is still expected to take (an even split overcounted early owners and made the fixtures wrong).
- A sampler (every `pollMilliseconds`) charges growth while owners run, so growth that is freed before the owner ends is still seen. The peak of a kind is learned when an owner completes and persists through the `peaks` port (`createGraphOwnerPeaksFileV1`, `np/owner-peaks.json` in the inference data directory, atomic replace). `pressure()` tells the caller that the headroom no longer covers the largest peak.
- New message `learning` (EN and DE). New policy `limitEnvironment` (`SEMIO_GRAPH_MEMORY_LIMIT_MB`): a declared limit replaces the cgroup and charges the resident memory of this process (private harness, platforms without cgroups). Without it a host-wide reading charged other agents' memory to the owners (one owner "learned" 1.9 GB).
- Kinds: `ownerKindV1` in project-nodes (language tag, else `language:cargo` when a Cargo manifest is at the native root, else `project`); the first guess used the language tag only and learned a non-Rust peak for Rust owners.
- Tests (fixture `🧪️tests/🔣️.json`, independent Python model `🐍️.py`): admission rows, four queue scenarios (unlearned kind alone then five at once, persisted peaks admit at once, a guess below the truth cannot overcommit, kinds learn separately), persistence, pressure, the declared limit. 17 pass.

What was learned, in order of importance:

1. **The harness overstates a Rust owner.** The private copy has other inode and ctime than the recorded facts, so every `file` and `analysis` hold check falls through to reading and hashing the file instead of the stamp comparison (`sourceAnalysisFactHoldsV1`, `observationsHoldInRoundV1`). Hard links, which keep the stamps, are refused by the build ("Bounded physical Source revision required": a source file must have one link). The live Rust owners grew 250 to 280 MB in 120 s; my harness saw +2.4 GB in 10 s. The 4.3 to 5 GB guard hits of section 4 are therefore not a measurement of the live Rust owner. Everything measured before the first owner (load state, settler release, `lane` fix, prime recipes) is not affected, because it does not depend on those stamps beyond the verification the live run also does.
2. **Why a Rust owner observes tens of thousands of rows (answer to question 1).** `closureApiV1().resolve` merges `entry.rows` and the rows of every layer without a walk fact of EVERY entry the owner references into the owner's rows. The shared `tool-closure cargo-command` entry alone is the 44,870-module walk (three analysis rows per module: `build:module-imports`, `command-references`, `script-provider-ports`, one shared hash each) so every Rust owner copies about 45 k to 97 k shared rows. They are the same Cargo and tooling rows for every crate and belong behind ONE reference row per shared entry (its digest) with the rows verified once where the entry is verified; the owner record would name the entry instead of carrying its rows. That changes what a generation fragment records and how a fragment is vouched, so it is a design change of `resolve`, the settler and the row hold check, not a local edit. It was not started.
3. **Moving the prime into the capped child works for deriving and fails for reading.** A child of `heavy-questions` (`derive.scripts` of `openPlannerDeriverV1`, a writable binding of the closure store) derived all 21 shared entries within the 2,560 MB cap and stored them. A later process that LOADED them (the parent prime or a second child) held entries and layers and was stopped at the cap or at 4.3 GB: reading and verifying the 45 k-module entry costs more than deriving it, so the child only helps once the loaded entry is a reference (finding 2) and not a copy of tens of thousands of rows. The code was reverted for that reason.
4. Not done: compaction of `found.refresh` (needs lazy rows through the node store in `partRowsV1`, the settler's output shape, rowstore's area), per-owner byte bounds of the observation caches (`releaseInferenceMemoryV1` also clears the used-inference tracking of the running capture, so it cannot run between owners), the tightened flat-heap test (the test stays at 400 KB per owner), cached-owner timing and the end-to-end first 10 owners with peak RSS (the harness cannot represent the Rust owner, finding 1).
