m01 inferred 7 of 362 decided owners (98 percent taken). So workers do take owners the fleet has; they inferred only 0 to 32 percent. The hours are the per-owner latency of a look (6 to 64 s), with 4 looks at once and 600 owners: 600 x 36 s / 4 = 5400 s per pass.

**Where a look spends its time** (cumulative lookup clock of the process, `looks.phases`, `presence`, `fleet.gateTimings`):
1. `authority` (`projectAssemblyAuthorityV1`, the native closure walk that every native owner needs for its record key): 9593 s on m01 (about 13 to 26 s per owner), 7537 s on m03, 9849 s on m11. Root cause measured below: every hit of a memoized manifest closure re-validated every row of that closure because the walk ran outside a validation round.
2. `lookup.reusable` = the inventory presence check: 9614 s on m01, 9797 s on m08, 11227 s on m11. The presence coalescer takes one storage consumer per batch. On m01: 283 batches for 946 asks, `preVerify` 4,377,613 ms = 15.5 s per batch, of which `allocationJournal` 4,285,508 ms and `physicalGateVerify` 1,470,048 ms (mounted image, `👁️observation/📥️reuse`). The body of a batch (the actual file checks) is 15,310 ms in total, 54 ms per batch. A batch costs 15 s whatever its size, and with 4 looks at once a batch carries 3.3 asks.
3. Fleet fetch: on m12 `gateTimings`: metadata 538 calls, 5,001,796 ms (9.3 s each), blob 462 calls 4.5 s each, seed 413 calls 3.4 s each. The fleet cache server on the coordinator is slow or down. At 00:48 nothing listened on `192.168.178.70:6094` (connection refused in 2 ms; `fleet/cache.json` was republished at 00:45 with generation 9, i.e. the server restarts on each landing). `pool.json` of every machine says `lastError: the fleet cache did not answer`, `published 0 stored 0`, so no pool produces owners for anyone right now (including the `repo-mcp` owner that is `never` on a 16 GB machine).
4. `lookup.gate` (fleet decide/claim for records that are not reusable locally): 2.7 ks to 12.9 ks per worker; m08 spent 10,275 s in 22 looks (467 s per look) with zero started client calls: a stalled fleet.

**Why a second pass (`pass 2`, `ownerPassesV1`)?** `loadBuildGraphV1` runs the attempt inside `settleOwnerRegistryV1` (`B/🕸️graph/📥️load/🧭️registry-follow/🟦️.ts:63`): whenever the owner registry moved while owners were inferred (the checkout is a live replica, peers and the workers themselves keep writing files, `S/origins/g1-m01/segments` shows `put` rows for `Cargo.lock` and sources), the whole owner stage restarts and the ledger counter restarts at 0. In pass 2 all 600 owners are looked at again; the records pass 1 took are only `present` and cost a full look each (authority + presence + row hold). `never: ["repo-mcp"]` (one owner that a 16 GB machine can never infer, `waitHeavy`) keeps a stage unable to finish until a strong machine publishes it, which no pool does while the cache is down. m01 stuck at 249/250 for about 16,000 s: that is the heavy wait / one look blocked on the stalled fleet, not 16,000 s of work; the log only keeps the last 200 lines, so the exact owner is not recoverable.

**Do record keys differ between machines? No evidence of it.** The key text is `JSON.stringify([stamp, null, [...head, root, json, selectedTargets, selectedInputs]])`; the file stem is `sha256(root)[:24]`. None of its inputs is a machine path. New test `🗝️owner-key-text` pins both against a Python implementation and asserts no `/Users`, `/home`, drive or `/private` spelling in the text. Fleet hit rates above (273 fetched of 362 on m01) confirm that keys agree across machines. The implementation digest compare across machines is still impossible from receipts (no receipt records it; G2).

**Do claims serialize workers? No.** `fleet.gateTimings.claim`: 3 to 20 claims per worker, 64 s to 75 s in total; `peerWait` 0 on every worker. The serialization is the presence batch (one consumer at a time) and the 4-wide cut.

**Why only some owners are recomputed on several workers** (m11: 58 computed, 16 published; m12: 73 computed, 36 published, `refused 22`; m03: 45 computed, 18 published): `documentsOf` (`🪪️graph-state/📦️project-records/🟦️.ts:146-149`) counts `refused++` and publishes nothing when `neutralRecordV1` throws `UnportableV1`; 30 to 60 percent of the owners a worker infers are therefore never shared and the next worker infers them again. Which owners and why is NOT identified (the log of the refusal code is not kept). Written to `findings-E1.md` for the owner of graph-state.

## Round 3: owner records keyed by the owner's own inputs

Landing: `T/landings.md` "Wave 1122 E1 round 3" (locks `graph`, `graph-state`, `observation` taken before the first edit, released after the record).

### Design (schema-first: `📜️contract-reads/🧬️schema/🔣️.json`)
- **Key** = `[stamp, null, [registryDigest, commandRevision (native owners), assemblyAuthority (native or pipeline owners), "closure-references/4", root, json, selectedTargets|null, selectedInputs]]`. `projectAuthority` (the digest of ALL generator contracts) is gone from the key, from the pool wave globals, from the pool schema and from every fixture; `registryDigest` is the catalog-provider digest in both read kinds (before: the whole registry in a selected read). `ownerRecordHeadV1` builds the head in one place (parent, farm child, pool child).
- **Dependency list recorded while inferring**: `inferProjectBodyV1` runs the inference of an owner on a recording view of the contracts (`trackGeneratorContractsV1`, a Proxy per nested object): every primitive read (property path and value), every key listing and every `in` probe is recorded as a `global` observation row of the record (`{kind:"global", path: JSON ["generator-contracts", root, segments], fact: JSON string}`), once per observation scope, so a read made inside a memoized computation travels with that memo to every owner that replays it. The generated-output lookups (`scripts.generatedOutput`) use the contracts of the running scope (AsyncLocalStorage), so the shared script cache cannot hide a read.
- **Verification**: the observation module got the row kind `global` (`registerGlobalFactV1`, `observeGlobalFactV1`, `observationScopeV1`, kind added to the observation schema). The resolver of `📦️project-nodes` compares a recorded read with the contracts of the running read (or, in a process that holds none, the taxonomy file as it is, memoized by its size and mtime); a process without resolver answers "does not hold", so a record is inferred again and never wrongly adopted.
- Precision: an owner reads the filter fields (`ownership`, `ownerPath`, the output roots and their producer, `nativeConsumers`, the target names) of every contract and the rest only of the contracts that concern it. So an edit of a field the owner never read (description, check command, the output path or the target of an unrelated contract) leaves its record valid; an edit that makes a contract concern the owner, a new or a removed contract, re-keys (invalidates) it.

## Round 4

Landing: `T/landings.md` "Wave 1122 E1 round 4" (locks `graph`, `graph-state` before the first edit; `observation` was held by E14r3 and I made no edit there).

### (3) Header size and verification cost of the `global` rows: measured, no compaction needed
Measured on this host with the 36 real generator contracts (`scratchpad/E1/rows.mjs`, the real `generatorOutputOwners` filter plus the native-consumer and target-name scans, one recording view per owner, 39 owner roots):
- 267 rows per owner (183 identical for every owner, 84 owner-specific), 48.2 KB as raw JSON, **2.5 KB brotli** per owner on its own; the store shares identical rows in blocks, so the added storage is about the owner-specific 84 rows, about 1 KB per record. A stored record on this host takes 36 KB on average (39 MB for 1093 owners, `np/`), so the rows add about 3 percent.
- Recording: 0.95 ms per owner. Verification without any memo: 0.24 ms per owner, 1.2 s for 5000 owners; the row table verifies each distinct row once per round, so the real cost is lower. On a 16 GB worker (about 3 times slower per core) a full verification is a few seconds against read times of minutes: below one percent. Compaction (one row per contract) would save raw bytes only; the compressed and shared size is what is paid, so I did not change the row form.

### (1) `commandRevision`: nothing to record per owner, already constant in the real mode
Read `cargoTargets` and `nativeCommandInputs`: every native owner spreads the WHOLE command input list into the `inputs` of its `build`, `check` and `test` targets, so per-owner reads of it are the whole list for every native owner: there is no owner that uses less. In the mode that builds generations (`late`: whole read with snapshot and observer) `commandInputs` is a symbolic closure handle whose key is the digest of the RECIPE (`closureRecipeKeyV1`, "never the files it names"), and the files are carried by `closureRef` markers and `closure` rows that are verified per owner. So `commandRevision` is a constant there and an edit of a Cargo command input does not re-key anything; the dependency is already per owner and by row. In a selected read the list is undefined (`commandInputs ??= []`), so a selected read and a whole read of a native owner produce different records (different `inputs`); recording the list as a row under one shared key would make the two reads overwrite each other's records (first writer wins in the fleet cache, the other reader is stale for ever), which is worse than two keys. No code change; the head keeps `commandRevision` for native owners.

### (2) Selection part of the key: not changed, with the reason
`selectedTargets` is not an input that an owner reads at some point: `projectWithDefaults(..., admission ? selectedTargets ?? [] : undefined, selectedInputs)` FILTERS the declared targets before they are normalized (`admitted`), so the stored project holds only the selected targets and the selected inputs: the content of the record depends on the selection for every owner whose selection is narrower than what it declares. ## Round 5: the second lookup level (full-selection record first, narrower view derived): NOT built, blockers and a decision needed

Locks: I took `graph-state` and `observation` (E3 held `graph`), read the code, and released them without an edit. No file of this round was changed, no test added: I could not make the derivation exact and I will not land an approximation in the record path of the whole fleet without your decision. What I found, with the code:

### Why a view derived from the full record is not equal to what a selected read stores today
`inferProject` (`📦️project-nodes`) and `projectWithDefaults` (`🧱️project-assembly`) make the stored project of a selected read depend on the selection in five ways that are deterministic but not a filter of the full project:
1. **Targets**: `admitted = declared filtered by selectedTargets`; the unselected `json.targets` are merged back RAW after the inference (`result.projects[name].targets = {...inactive, ...targets}`), the unselected derived targets (Cargo `build/check/test`, component, print-document, generator, wasm) are absent. The full record holds the normalized form of all of them.
2. **Native decision by the selection**: in an admission read `native` is `ownerAdmissionCompilesV1(activeJson, selectedInputs, ...)` (a source-only selection does not compile), and `withNativePreparation`/`withReleasePipelinePreparation` are applied only to the admitted targets (`prepare(apply, predicate)`): the preparation (extra `dependsOn`, the `native-input-admission` target) is MERGED into the targets, so a view cannot remove it, and a view that keeps it pulls the Cargo closure owners into a slice that a source-only selection deliberately keeps out.
3. **Generated task dependencies** are applied to `json` BEFORE the key in an admission read (`applyBuildGeneratedTaskDependenciesV1`), so the key already differs.
4. **Named inputs**: `projectInputs(..., inputNames)` computes only the requested names; the shared command source groups are numbered `commandSources0..N` in the order the ADMITTED targets meet them, so the group names of a narrower selection differ from those of the full project and a derived view would have to renumber and re-reference them (`internCommandSources`, `mergeCommandSourceGroupsV1`).
5. **Closure recipes** (`closureRecipes`, `symbolicGroups`) are the ones the admitted targets refer to.
Items 1, 4 and 5 can be derived by a function (filter, merge raw inactive targets, renumber, prune recipes) with a Python oracle. Item 2 cannot: the preparation is merged into the full targets and cannot be separated afterwards, and item 3 changes the key input itself.

### What it would cost, and what a cheaper sound step gives
- Inferring the FULL record in a selected read (so that it can be published and shared) makes every cold single-target run normalize every target and hash every named input of every owner it touches; the admission was introduced to avoid exactly that. I could not measure the ratio (a real inference needs repo custody), so I cannot tell you how much slower a cold `repo run` gets; it is the price of "one record per owner".
- A read-only second level (adopt the full record when the selection does not narrow it: selected targets cover all declared ones, same native decision, no generated-dependency difference) is sound, but selected reads are narrow by design, so a selected read after a whole read would still infer: it does not satisfy your test.

### Decision needed (choose one)
- **A.** Redefine a selected read as a view of the full record: preparation is applied to all native targets (larger slices than today for source-only selections), generated dependencies are applied after the key, a derivation function (items 1, 4, 5) with a Python oracle replaces the admission filter inside `projectWithDefaults`, cold selected reads infer full owners. I can build it; it changes what a selected graph contains and costs cold single-target runs, so it needs your go and E14's agreement that slices may carry the larger nodes.
- **B.** Keep selected records separate (today), and let `repo run` and worker slices share records among themselves (same selection, same key: already true), whole reads share among themselves. This is the state after round 3; no change.
- **C.** Make whole reads publish per-target normalized parts so that a selected read assembles its project from parts: not possible for item 2 either.
I recommend B until the cold-run cost of A is measured on a real read; the measurement needs one whole read that is allowed to finish (E2/E8) and a repo-custody harness.
