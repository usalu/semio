## 2. Slow remote runs (86 of the brief; 227 in the window)
- Placement is pull-based and right: `planDispatchV1` (`🗓️scheduler/⚖️placement`) serves the queue FIFO, gives each action to the least loaded WAITING (polling) worker that is compatible and has headroom, at most one per waiting worker per round. A worker that is busy fetching has no free slot and does not poll, so a unit is not parked on a busy worker. Scheduler counters since 11:01: queue wait p50 **0.9 s**, p90 **20.7 s**, max 357 s (512 samples). The 65-80 s "away from it" are the tail of ONE burst: 12:44-12:47, ~50 units offered at once (44 light slots), `norm_en1999_io` 79.9 s (worker ...67118), `procedural_2d_editor_commands` 74.8 s (...89082), `iso16757_scene` 65.1 s (...26961). No scheduler defect found; I did not see a unit held while a compatible worker waited.
- Real lendable capacity is far below "advertised 10 slots x 11 workers": placement needs `running < concurrency` AND the machine's own headroom report (memory, pressure, free disk). Live `repo fleet status` 13:04: 6 of 13 workers "refusing, no headroom" (m01 slots, m06 pressure-warn, m08 memory, m10 slots, m11 slots, **m13 0.1 GiB free disk: 0 of 10 slots usable, one action needs 1.0 GiB**), loads 12-15 on 10 cores (they build their own units too). A stage-running worker lends at most 1 unit (W4). Realistic remote parallelism is ~3-4 per usable worker, roughly 25-35 in total, not 44. The requester's queue is bounded by `queueSaturatedMilliseconds` (122 s, then `queue-timeout`, local), which is the deliberate "waiting there is no worse than here" rule since local is 4 slots; I left it.
- Worker fetch: median 1.4-5.5 s on most workers, but `...25705` (7 runs, median **20 s**, max 70.8 s `en1995_io`), `...87472` (14 runs, median **16 s**, max 34 s) and `...43996` (3, median 12 s) keep fetching 20-35 s for closures of 550-900 MB that other workers serve from cache in 0-5 s (...26961: 19 runs, 12 of them <= 5 s). `...26961`'s 60 s (`din18599_scene`) and 25 s (`iso16757_scene`) are single cold blobs of a unit with a new rlib. Status has NO worker code digest field (worker rows: id, name, host, properties, concurrency, running, sandbox, lastSeenSeconds, draining, origin, confinement, network, headroom, offloadedIn, refused), so I cannot say "code older" per worker. Inference from the logs: the origin carries the worker pid; `25705` and `87472` first appear 12:41, i.e. after F5 (12:02 + 5 min quiet), yet show the pre-F5 symptom (re-download of the whole closure each action); the three that fetch fast (`26961`, `67118`, `2254`) show 0-2 s repeats. So either those two machines run code without the F5 prune guard (restart did not pick the new closure) or their blob store is slower or smaller than the closure (m04 reports 4.1 GB swap, 26 %). Needs a look on the machines themselves (worker `maximumBlobBytes` effective, free disk of `d/m/remote-execution`); no code defect in the requester. Suggested follow-up (not done, other files): publish the worker's code digest and blob-cache bytes in its registration row so status shows it.
- Mean cost driver: every remote unit ships 560-1330 MB (median ~700): the big derive rlibs of point 1 are inputs of nearly everything. 11 workers cold-pulling that is 10+ s each at LAN speed; hence fetch, and the queue behind fetching slots. The cure is point 1's second option or smaller derive output, not scheduling.

## 3. Wrappers without a rustc child (local wait)
Timeline of pid 3585 `norm_en1991_mutations` from `g.jsonl`: 12:40:33.3 start, remote-claim granted +0.3 s, **12:40:49.15 offload-declined not-portable (506 ms)**, slot-wait, rustc ran until 12:55:29 (895.7 s wrapper total). The 16 s between claim and decline are the gate: an unmeasured unit waits up to `unmeasuredWaitMilliseconds` 15 s for a local slot (`offloadGateV1`: `localSlot(15000)`) BEFORE planning discovers it is not portable; its ticket is dropped on timeout, `compile()` then takes a NEW ticket at the back.
Findings (live at 13:02, `s/`, `q/`, `ps`):
- **No slot leak, no priority inversion by slot holders.** 4 slots `s/0-3.lock`; each holder's slot age = its rustc child's age + ~3 s (27338 energy_model_initialization 536 s; 63211 90 s; 58707 185 s; 62855 157 s). Nobody holds a slot while waiting for a remote action: the gate takes `held` only in the `local-slot`/`unmeasured` branches that return "stay local", the light slots (`light-*.lock`, 44) are separate and are taken only after the gate says offload and released in `finally`.
- FIFO is real (ticket name = epoch ms, oldest live ticket first, stale reaped). 14 tickets queued behind 4 slots whose current units run 1.5-9 minutes: 44 local-only units / 4 slots x ~2-5 min = the 3.5-10 minute waits. It is capacity, not a stall; the 26 oversize units of point 1 and the 231 not-portable derive units (all of them local by construction) are what loads those 4 slots.
- Defect fixed: the arrival time was lost. A unit that waited in the gate (15 s) or tried the fleet (5-195 s) joined the queue behind everyone who arrived after it. Now `since` keeps the ticket at the arrival time (`script.ts` both acquisitions, `acquireSlotV1({since})`, never in the future); test: a waiter that returns with an older arrival is served before waiters that queued meanwhile.
- Fleet claims: the claim is taken at the start (`remote-claim granted`) and held through the slot wait. Server `claims`: 87 active, 2 waiting, oldest 1152 s (13:00); 41 active, 0 waiting, oldest 8 min (13:04). Only 2 foreign waiters existed, so claims did not cause the waits here. The claimant is alive (the wrapper), a dead claimant expires by lease; I did not change claims. Open idea: release or defer the claim of a unit that must queue behind 4 slots for minutes.
- Hold-off (O19 30 s after 507): `memo the content store is full (507)` records have 0 ms; not a wait. CAS6 retry: 2 records 18 s median. Not the cause.
- "charged 3.4/12 GiB, admitted-wait 0": the broker (memory session) never delayed these units; nothing in the memory admission is involved, so no note was written into their ticket.
- Not changed, deliberate: the gate's 15 s wait for an unmeasured unit (it is the way an unknown unit finds a free slot); with `since` it no longer costs queue position.
