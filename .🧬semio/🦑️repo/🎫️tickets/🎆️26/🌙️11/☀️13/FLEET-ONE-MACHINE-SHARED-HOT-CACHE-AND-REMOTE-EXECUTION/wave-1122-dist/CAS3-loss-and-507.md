## Fix (A): protection at the unlink choke point, enforced by the object itself
Both the oldest collector and the oldest reclaimer have always refused to unlink an object with `nlink > 1` (hard-linked into a workspace; `git log -S nlink>1` shows it in the first CAS commits). So a held object now gets a SECOND HARD LINK in `<cas>/v1/k/<holder>/<xx>/<rest>` (a "keeper"). Any process of any generation then sees a hard-linked object and leaves it alone, no version gate or permission needed, cross-platform (hard links exist on NTFS and APFS).
- Writer side (`unit-cache/💾️store`): `keepHostObjectsV1(root, entry)` after every entry write (`writeEntryV1`, `writeBuildRunV1`) and on every local hit (backfills old entries, `EEXIST` cheap).
- Collector side: `reconcileCasKeepersV1` at the start of every non-dry collection links every held digest and releases every keeper whose holder no longer names the object (or whose holder expired/vanished). The scan counts only FOREIGN links (`nlink - keeperCount`) as protection, so the capacity eviction of held entries still frees objects (`freeable`); reclaim of a digest that is not held drops its keepers right before the unlink (and does not treat them as foreign).
- Test (`🫂️holders/🧪️tests`): an independent "old reclaimer" (unlinks anything with `nlink<=1`, exactly the pre-CAS2 rule) leaves the two held objects and removes the 10 others; after the entry is gone `reconcileCasKeepersV1` releases 2 links and a normal reclaim then frees the objects and the keeper directory. Contract test in unit-cache store: keeper hard-linked to the same inode, idempotent.
- Residual window: a held object that has no keeper yet (entry written by a process on pre-CAS3 code, not hit since) is protected by holders only until some new-code process links it (any new-code hit/write of that entry, or the next new-code collection). Old-code collectors can still take such an object when it is older than the minimum age. The first new-code collection (sidecar after its self-restart) closes it for all 580 + 16 entries at once.

## (B) 507s
Reading `🚦️guard`: a refuse-over-cap store (the cache server's) judged a write over the cap, ran ONE automatic collection (`incremental`: only `incrementBytes` 2 GiB below the high watermark; planner stage 3), waited at most `admissionBudgetMs` 8 s (a scan of 107k objects with a ledger is longer, and a timed-out wait counted as "still over") and then threw `CasCapRefusalV1` -> HTTP 507. Protected were all objects younger than `minAgeMs` = 10 min (about 4.5-6 GB at 27-38 GB/h inflow, nearly the whole headroom) plus anything pinned/held/leased. The 19.29 GiB "by nobody" were mostly inside that 10-minute window or behind the single incremental step.
Changes (all in `🗄️cas`):
- `minAgeMs` 600000 -> 180000 (schema). After CAS1 the in-flight cases are covered by other mechanisms: offload inputs by `x-semio-lease: input` (30 min), outputs by the pin/holder set; the age only has to cover an upload by an OLD client (no lease header) until its action fetches it (scheduler queue wait p50/p90/max 0/2/8 s per scheduler log) and a put until its entry is written (seconds). Unreferenced, unleased blobs older than 3 min are plain candidates.
- Guard: a write over the cap on a refuse-over-cap store now runs a FULL collection (non-incremental: down to the low watermark = 75 %, ~7 GiB of headroom instead of 2) and, when it had to join a running incremental collection, a second full one; up to 3 attempts; refusal only if a full collection finished within the min age and stayed over (futile) or the budget ran out. The collection is single-flight and never cancelled by a timed-out writer. `admissionBudgetMs` 8000 -> 30000 (a 507 costs a local compile, a 30 s wait does not).
- Tests (`🫂️holders/🧪️tests`, real store + real guard + real collector, scratch home): (1) cap 20 MiB, 23 MiB present of which 3 held, 19 unreferenced aged 5 min (inside the old 10-min grace), 1 young: the write is admitted, held and young stay, at most 15 MiB remain; (2) every object younger than the min age and over the cap: typed refusal, nothing deleted. Existing CAS1 admission tests (relieve / keep / hang, ordinary store) pass unchanged (cas 49/0).
