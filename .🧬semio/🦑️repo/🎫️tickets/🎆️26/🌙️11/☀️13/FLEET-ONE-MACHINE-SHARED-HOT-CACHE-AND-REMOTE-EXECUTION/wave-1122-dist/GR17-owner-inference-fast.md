## Verdict
1. The dominant cost of a "warm" owner was not verification and not I/O: a fresh child judged every stored entry whose rows rest on a parsed file as STALE and walked it again, although nothing had moved. Cause: `analysis` rows name the parser that made them (`build:command-imports:v1:<owner>`); a process that has not yet called `prepareSourceAnalysesV1()` (every fresh pool child on its first look at a stored entry) finds the parser unregistered, so the row fails ("provider-unregistered … registered 3, same role none") and the whole `target-sources` entry of the owner (and every entry with a parsed file) is re-derived. Measured on the same store, no edit in between: second pass of the same owner `derived 7, stale 7, 36 to 47 s CPU` before, `derived 0, stale 0, 6 to 12 s CPU` after. Fix: `SI🟨️.mjs` `preparedAnalysesV1()` at the head of both entry verifications.
2. Landed item 1 (verified once per machine): a digest-keyed verification stamp. The first process that finds every row of a stored entry holding writes `s/<entry digest>.json.br` into the shared store: the stamps (size, both times, inode, mode) of the 4,800 to 9,500 distinct files and directories that decide its rows (`analysis`: its file; `lstat`/`exists`/`fileExists`/`directoryExists`/`stat`: the directory that holds the name, plus the link itself when it is a link; `readdir`: the directory; `resolve` of an absolute path: directory of the target) and the 1,600 to 2,000 rows it cannot witness (`field`, `global`, `registry`, `typescriptText`, realpaths), kept as `live`. Every later process compares the stamps (one stat per distinct path per round) and probes the live rows once per round; the layers are not read, listed or probed. Never stamped within the racy window (2 s) of the verification start, never over a plan port, `SEMIO_VERIFICATION_STAMPS=off` disables it.
3. Item 1 plus the fix, measured (same 6-owner method, fresh process per owner, load 30 to 45): second and later pass, entries unchanged: compiler-syntax-ecma-ts 1.1 s CPU / 1.7 s wall / 328 MB, draw-js 1.4 s CPU / 1.9 s wall / 359 MB, 0 files written, 0 derived (was 4 to 10 s CPU, 4 to 10 writes, 0.4 to 1 GB with entries stale). Three owners in one warm process: 1.7 s, 0.5 s, then a stale one. The warm target (under 1 CPU s, tens of MB) is reached for the second owner of a process (0.5 s CPU); the first owner of a process pays the process one-time cost (module load 0.84 s CPU in `bootstrap`, wasm index, first stamps) and the remainder is the live rows and the resolve rows.
4. Item 2 (packed layers) and item 3 (walk boundary at the declared core) were NOT done; reasons and the measurements that decide their value are in sections 4 and 5. Item 4 (profile) is section 3.

## 2. What changed (file:line at landing time)
- `SI🟨️.mjs` `preparedAnalysesV1` (before `entryStampHoldsV1`) called at the head of `closureEntryHoldsAsyncV1` and `closureEntryHoldsV1`.
- `O🟨️.mjs` `observationWitnessV1` / `observationWitnessHoldsV1` (after `observationRowFreshV1`); `SI💾️entries/🟨️.mjs` `loadStamp`/`saveStamp`, directory `s/` swept with the entries; `SI🟨️.mjs` `entryStampHoldsV1`, `keepEntryStampV1`, counters `stamped`, `stampsKept` in `closureEntryCountsV1`.
- `I🔎️owner-discovery/🟨️.mjs`: stat-stamped memo of the Wasm index file texts (`wasmFileTextV1`) and the `wasm-index` answer once per validation round (a stored record asks it once per `global` row: 233 rows each rebuilt the index over 4,869 owners, 1.0 to 1.7 s per process); `wasm-outputs/🟦️.ts` `within` by prefix.
- `O🧩️field/🟨️.mjs` `fieldExtractorIdentityV1`; limits in `I🧬️schema/🔣️.json` (EN+DE). The record identity moves once more (schema file of the inference authority); no compat code.
- Soundness of the stamp: a row kind is stamped only where its verdict is a function of the stamped stats (see the docstring of `observationWitnessV1`); everything else is `live`. Known assumption: a directory's modification and change time move when a name in it appears, goes or is replaced (true on APFS, ext4, NTFS); symbolic links are followed by the stamp stat so a retargeted link moves it. Holes accepted and documented: an extractor that calls a helper whose body is edited in another file changes that file's own `analysis` row (the core modules are rows of every entry), not the extractor source. Disable with `SEMIO_VERIFICATION_STAMPS=off`.
- A busy directory in a stamp invalidates it: `…/💻️os/🔨️modules/🔌️plugin/⏯️tool-run/🎞️tick/🎮️operations` changed 4 times in 20 minutes (a peer writes there) and each time every entry naming it was verified in full once and stamped anew. Cost bounded (one full verification per burst per machine); a per-row fallback (verify only the rows under the moved path) is the refinement.

## 3. Profile of 3 warm owners (item 4), 5.4 s of samples, process of draw-js, compiler-syntax-ecma-ts, canvas-react-renderer
Top terms (inclusive, share of samples): 1. Wasm compile index for the `global` rows (first look of a process: 1,642 manifests stat plus read, `within`/`relative` path arithmetic) 35 %; 2. `statSync` 31 % (the 4,800 to 9,500 stamp stats per round, 19 % `witnessTupleV1`, plus the manifest stats of term 1); 3. `sourceAnalysisFailureV1` 26 % (own `analysis` rows of the entries, replay of rows whose recorded file stamp is zero by construction); 4. `readFileSync` 18 % (manifests of term 1, cold in a fresh process); 5. `path.relative/resolve` 22 % (mostly term 1, before the prefix change; the change gave 0.94 s -> 0.55 s per index build in isolation). Not removable without a persisted cross-process memo of the Wasm index digest (witness over its 1,642 manifests, one stat each): that is the next item. Stop condition: a warm first owner of a process is 1.3 to 1.6 s CPU (above 1 s), later owners of the process 0.5 to 0.7 s; the remaining floor is the stamp stats (about 5 to 9 k syscalls on long emoji paths, 15 to 20 µs each) plus the per-process Wasm index, i.e. not irreducible but not worth more complexity before item 3.

## 4. Why items 2 and 3 are not landed (measured, not skipped lightly)
- Item 2 (packed layer files): after the parser fix a derive of an owner is dominated by the walk and by `target-sources` per owner, not by the number of layer files (profile of a derive: `walkImportsV1` 38 %, observe/analysis 26 %, layer index writes 5 %); the layer files matter on the import of 14 fleet entries (36,672 writes) and for `cad-js` (221,947 stats, 10,108 writes warm before the fix). It needs the store, the layer index (`x/`, one id list per layer: a second set of thousands of files), retention (`🧹️retention`), `bundle`/`adopt` and the fleet export to move together (about 1,000 changed lines plus tests); not attempted in this lane. The cheap part, the offer awaited 15 s on the critical path (`SI🟨️.mjs` `lane.offer` race), is also untouched.
- Item 3 (cut the walk at the declared core): the live evidence says the shared tool entries are not what goes stale between two passes of one owner (before the parser fix every stale entry of the repeat was an owner `target-sources` entry, 22 of 27; moved rows named owner `script.ts` files that had not moved: the unregistered parser). It changes lists and keys of every group and conflicts with the `blast-radius` and `tool-cover` laws (GR10 section 4); needs a policy decision first.

## 5. Bounded real read on the coordinator (TypeScript and project owners only)
`GR17/bounded-read.mjs`: one pool-child-like process, the real service (`open` + `run`) over 31 owners of an evenly spaced selection of 300 of the 316 non-Rust owners of the registry (Rust and cargo owners excluded: they are minutes each and a play release is building); shared writable store, fleet lane on, load 30 to 50. Not the 300 asked for: at this rate the cold read of 300 would have taken hours (one owner, `@semio-tech/cargo-workspaces`, took 462 s CPU and 927 s wall alone), so the process was stopped after 31 and the same 31 were read again.
| | owners | wall | CPU | CPU median per owner | peak RSS | store files |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| cold (empty store) | 31 | 2,145 s | 1,020 s (558 s without cargo-workspaces) | 8.5 s | 2.96 GB (after the outlier) | 32,113 |
| warm (same store, new process) | 31 | 317 s | 142.6 s (mean 4.6 s) | 1.74 s | 882 MB | +296, 0 derived, 185 of 196 loaded entries stamped |
Owners per minute per process: cold 0.9 (1.5 without the outlier), warm 5.9 under load 30 to 50 (about 13 on CPU alone); the warm heavy tail is real (cargo-workspaces 34 s, cad-js 18 s, ealparts 21.5 s, browser-actor-import 11.7 s), the median is 1.7 s.
Extrapolation for 5,231 owners on a 16 GB worker under a ~5 GB grant (5 warm children of about 0.9 GB): TypeScript and project owners (316): warm about 25 CPU minutes for one child; cold about 1.5 CPU hours for one child (median 8.5 s, mean 18 s, plus the outliers). Rust and cargo owners (about 2,600 of 5,231, minutes each cold, 92 to 186 s CPU for the two measured) were not measured warm and decide the total: at 100 s CPU each cold that is 72 CPU hours, over 5 children 14 hours cold; the warm figure is unknown and is the next measurement. Whole read, all warm, if Rust owners cost like the warm TypeScript mean (4.6 s): 6.7 CPU hours, over 5 children 80 minutes of CPU, 3 hours at the wall/CPU ratio of 2.2 measured under this host load. Treat all of this as an estimate, not a measurement of a whole read.
