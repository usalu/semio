## 1. What `mappingGrant` is and who holds it
- `mappingGrant` is the time of `withNativeStorageMappingReadV1` (SM) to get a SHARED lease on `native-storage-mapping:<sha256 root>` in the image's lease directory (an in-process owner slot first, max 32, then `acquireQueuedResourceLease` shared cohort). Every `store.verify()` (pre and post verify of a presence session, `reservation.recheck`) takes it. The reader side is already shared; it never conflicts with other readers (new test: 32 concurrent readers all inside together, peak 32, the last one waits only its own lease acquisition, about 17 ms each).
- The EXCLUSIVE writers (`acquireNativeStorageMappingWriterV1` / `...MaintenanceMappingWriterV1`, SM; callers: `💾️storage/🧹️cleanup/🎛️command` persistent child preparation, `🧱️fixed` funded children, `📤️publication/🌐️platform` protected maintenance) hold it for the whole preparation, retirement or maintenance of a NESTED child image under the mounted parent (attach, create, handoff): seconds to minutes, not milliseconds. On a worker those are the units, puller children and the remote-execution work volume.
- Data pattern in every worker's `slice-memory.json` (m01, m06, m07, m08, m10, m12): `preVerify` grant 0.03 to 2 s, `postVerify` grant 100 to 800 s, only ONE verification waiting at a time (`active 1`), the others instant. m06: 33 sessions, post grant 2,201 s in all. Two mechanisms:
  1. Queue preference. The queue gave a waiting exclusive ticket preference over later shared requests for `writerPreferenceMilliseconds` = 120,000 ms (`LP🔒️leases/🧭️order/🔣️policy.json`), so a reader asking after a writer arrived queued 120 s behind a writer that itself waited for long shared holders (children). The 100 to 170 s waits (m12 150 s, m10 170 s, m01 240 s = 120 s plus the next writer) match this. A real-process test reproduces it: with the old general preference a reader behind a waiting writer in the mapping namespace times out (`fairness` test run without the cycle row failed exactly that test).
  2. A genuinely HELD exclusive (granted kernel lease). Seen after the fix: m06 shows a 192 s shared wait with `holders` 0 in the process and the waiter at the head of the lease queue (no queue notice exists for position 1), m08 a 160 s one. No preference rule can shorten that: exclusivity must be short. Not fixed (see Open).
- Why a reader needs the grant at all: the mapping lease protects the namespace of the parent mount while descendant bindings are read, so a nested image is not attached or retired under a verifying reader. It protects against the exclusive work above, nothing else.

## 2. Landed
a. Bounded fairness for the mapping lease (schema first): `writerPreferenceByPrefix` rows may carry `cycle {standAsideMilliseconds, blockMilliseconds}` (`LP🔒️leases/🧭️order/🧬️schema/🔣️.json`, contract type, `LP🔒️leases/⚖️fairness/🟦️.ts` `WriterCycleV1`). A waiting writer lets readers pass for 4,000 ms, closes the queue for 1,000 ms and repeats from its arrival, for `native-storage-mapping:`. Readers wait at most one block (1 s) behind a waiting writer, and the writer gets a block window every 5 s, so continuous readers cannot starve it. Tests: pure law (every arrival time over 10 periods waits at most one block, a block recurs in every period, a writer behind a writer keeps order) and a real-process test (holder shared 12 s, waiting writer, readers served in under 1.5 s and 2.5 s instead of 120 s).
b. Coalescer: `createPresenceCoalescerV1` (`LB👁️observation/📥️reuse/🧺️coalesce/🟨️.mjs`) releases `draining` when the session's work (the batches) ends, not when the session returns from `postVerify`: the next ask opens session N+1 while N's tail verifies; at most `maximumTails` 3 tails at once (bounded), a failing tail no longer rejects callers who wait for another session. `presenceSessionV1` clears `state.held` by identity. Tests: next ask served while the tail waits (1 s race), bounded tails (third ask keeps queueing with `maximumTails` 1), 600 owners with a stub exclusive holder cycling 40 ms of every 80 ms: serial 1,029 ms, overlapped 591 ms. Verifying once per take pass was not done: with the overlap a tail costs no ask any time.
c. Visibility: `nativeStorageMappingQueueV1()` (SM): waiters (kind, step `slot` = in-process owner slot versus `grant` = lease, waited ms), in-process holders and exclusive hold time, owner slots, the lease queue head (holder kind, pid, held seconds, queue length; while a grant is waited for the lease registry names the kernel holder every 5 s, with its command), tallies of finished shared and exclusive waits. It is published as `presence.mappingQueue` in the slice report (`inferenceInventoryPresenceStatsV1`) and so in the workers' `slice-memory.json`; `repo fleet status` prints it per worker row (`readWorkerMappingV1`, schema `workers.mapping.*`, EN and DE), for example "mapping grant: 1 waiting for the lease grant, longest 192 s, head of the lease queue: exclusive holder, process N (cmd) for 75 s, 3 queued (finished: 9 shared grants waited 1 s, exclusive holds 0 s; report 4 s old)". Test: reader on a fixture slice report, freshness/size/torn/older-code cases, schema (facts and report), both languages without unreplaced placeholders.
d. Stall exemption (`readyGrantWaitV1`, process-tree): now keyed on the per-wait time of `mappingQueue.waiting` (the old cumulative `mappingGrant.milliseconds` could exceed the limit with finished sessions) and bounded to `grantWaitMaximumMilliseconds` 300,000 (was 1,800,000). A report without the queue (older worker code) never holds the stage. Test updated.

## Open
- Split the exclusive mapping work (attach/create/handoff of a nested child image) so no step holds the exclusive lease for more than a few hundred ms, or let the writer take a drained turn (the cycle already gives it one every 5 s, but a granted exclusive blocks readers for its whole hold). The persistent child preparation in `🧹️cleanup/🎛️command/🟦️.ts` (`openPersistentNativeImageBodyV1`, one 30 kB line) holds `parent.mapping` from `acquireNativeStorageMappingWriterV1` until `releaseMapping()` after `handoffPersistentPreparationV1`; restructuring that is a lane of its own. Now that the holder is named in `mappingQueue.head`, its first measured duration decides how.
- Reentrant shared requests of the same process bypass a waiting writer by design (`reentrantShared`), so a worker process with continuous overlapping readers can still starve a writer of another process; the cycle only covers strangers.
