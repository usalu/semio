## 1. Which rule removed the head: what the records prove and what they cannot

The head of the Ready `workspace` selection (scope `2f245baf...`, key `672eb18b...`, slot `4c2b3c4e...`, darwin-arm64) is ONE key; every republish overwrites that key.

What the records say (read-only):
- The server wrote NO eviction record. Its only counters are totals in `/status` (`evicted`, `blobs.evictedBytes`); `.retention/sweeps.jsonl` records the staleness judge only. So "which rule removed it at 21:2x and at 22:3x" cannot be read from any file: the key is gone and nothing says why. That absence is itself a defect, fixed below (section 3, journal).
- Refused / not stored is excluded for the published heads: lookups answered 1.6 s after the 20:30 publish (GR14) and the 21:54 head was looked up (W3). A refused or 507 write never produces a head that later disappears. The 256 MiB admission-document limit (`limit-document`) only prevented heads before 20:30 (GR14, fixed there).
- Staleness retirement removed nothing, ever: `.retention/sweeps.jsonl` (632 sweeps 13:31 to 23:06, all `mode:"dry-run"`) sums `retired` to 0. The judge cannot be the remover while it runs in dry-run.
- The age rule (30 days) cannot apply to a head that is hours old.
- Therefore the remover is the size path: `planRemoteCacheBlobBoundV1` (blob bound 13.9 GiB, tiers: stale/unreached 0, undeclared 1, declared 2, least recently used first) or, behind it, the host CAS collector reclaiming a head blob that fell out of the pin set, which makes the NEXT lookup delete the entry (`readResult` with `actionResults:"complete"` removes an action result whose blob left the store, `🖥️server/🟦️.ts:123-141`). Both are recency races and both produce exactly "no file in `main/ac`".

Measured, live (23:05 to 23:10): the store has 4,275 entries, 13.26 GiB of referenced blobs (bound 13.9 GiB, so the plan runs on every write burst); 4,233 of the 4,275 entries were accessed within the last 15 minutes and ALL of the referenced bytes belong to entries touched in the last 15 minutes. The sweeps show the store between 2.3k and 6.5k entries all day, dropping 600 to 1,300 entries per few minutes (21:02 -1,143, 21:11 -1,113, 22:24 -961, 22:34 -883), 242,512 at 13:31 before the bound existed. Unit outputs are republished (storedAt refreshed) continuously, so the window in which an entry that nobody touches survives is about 11 to 15 minutes of ingest (about 50 GiB per hour). A head is read once per worker start, so between two looks it is the least recently used entry of the store: observed lifetimes 71 minutes (20:30 to 21:41) and 40 to 50 minutes (21:54 to 22:34/22:44) are exactly that window plus the looks that refreshed it.
- Heads published before W3's manifest (the 20:30 head) were undeclared, tier 1: the first victims of every pass. The 21:54 and 22:50 heads declared `stage-output`, tier 2, in the SAME tier as the 3,419 `rust-unit` and 79 `build-script-run` entries and ordered among them only by recency, so tier 2 does not protect either (W3's assumption "declared, live" was right about the tier and wrong about what the tier buys).
- Pin side: `pinLive` pins the newest entries by `storedAt` until the pin budget is full; a head stored 40 minutes ago is outside of it, its blobs (874 KB, 4.4 MB, 4.7 MB, 166 B: the head is about 10 MB, not hundreds of MB) become reclaimable by the host collector, and the next lookup deletes the entry.
- Not provable per event: which of the two (size plan vs. blob reclaim then lookup delete) took each of the two heads. After the fix both are closed for the class, and the journal makes the next case readable.

Now (23:07): the head is present (stored 22:52, `.meta` declaration kind `stage-output`, mtime refreshed by the 22:56 lookup), its four blobs are in the live pin file `remote-cache-live-63016` (20,027 digests). It is protected by nothing but recency.

## 2. Design (schema first)

A distinct class `graph`, declared by the entry itself, not guessed by the server:
- Declaration `kind` enum gains `graph` (`B🪢️slots/🧬️schema/🔣️.json`, `🪢️slots/🟦️.ts`). The staleness judge treats it as a live root that is never stale and never reached (`B🌐️remote-cache/🏷️retention/🍂️staleness/🟦️.ts`, Python oracle and one new shared fixture scenario).
- Server policy `graph {sharePercent 10, supersedeGraceSeconds 1800}` (`B🔣️policy.json`, `B🏃️execution/🧬️schema/🌐️remote-cache.json`; wired through the sidecar, `serve`, hosted). Status `blobs.graph {protectedEntries, protectedBytes, reservedBytes, evictedEntries, supersededBytes}`.
- Plan (`B🌐️remote-cache/🧹️collector/🟦️.ts` `planRemoteCacheBlobBoundV1(rows,sizeOf,bound,graph?)`, pure): the newest entry of every slot is current; current entries, most recently used first, are protected while their blobs (each once) fit 10 % of the bound (the entry that crosses the reserve stays whole), so unit output pressure NEVER takes them; an entry a newer entry of its slot replaced is kept for the supersede grace after its successor, afterwards it is the first to leave (tier 0); graph entries beyond the reserve compete as declared entries. A lookup refreshes recency (`read` marks the entry used; unchanged and now tested: the looked-up head stays protected, the other competes).
- Store (`B🌐️remote-cache/💾️store/🟦️.ts`): the first plan treats graph entries as claimed (no age/budget removal), the blob plan gets the class, `pinLive` pins every protected graph entry's blobs outside of the pin budget, and the blobs of an entry that is replaced in place by a newer one stay pinned for the supersede grace (workers mid-take). A new journal `.retention/evictions.jsonl` (rotating at 5 MiB) records one line per collection (entries, bytes, reasons, graph entries, remaining, referenced bytes, bound) and one line per evicted graph entry (key, reason, tier, bytes, accessed, storedAt, slot), so the next loss is provable.
- Publishers: every entry of the graph exchange (heads, owner/project records, all graph-state kinds) now carries the manifest `stage.json` (`graphRetentionManifestV1`, `B🪪️graph-state/🛰️exchange/🟦️.ts`), excluded from the documents on read; the selected-exchange head uses the same manifest. A machine that takes a head published by older code redeclares it in place by compare-and-swap, without reading (`redeclareSelectedHeadV1`, bounded 3 s, never throws), and a held head without the manifest of this code is offered again instead of "current". So the live 22:52 head (kind `stage-output`) becomes `graph` the first time any machine takes it after the restart.
- Loud failures: a publish that cannot be stored now says why in the stage log (`blob: <refusal text>`, `head: <refusal text>` instead of `blob`/`head`); the server side was already loud (`507` + `retry-after`); the head is never silently half-stored because the head result is written last by compare-and-swap.
