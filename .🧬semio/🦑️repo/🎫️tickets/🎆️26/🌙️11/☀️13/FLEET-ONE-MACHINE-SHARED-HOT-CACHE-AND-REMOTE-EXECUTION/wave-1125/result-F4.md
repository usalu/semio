## Round 4: inode sweep order and time-box (landed, see `T/landings.md` "Wave 1125 F4 round 4")
- Root cause of the round-3 stall: one `repo cache gc` served every ledger row once. `cargo-incremental` (priority 10) used its whole 600 s budget and was killed; the later ledger rows, `dependency-generations` among them, then returned immediately without work.
- Fix, schema-first: the writer registry gets a unique `inodeRank` (dependency-generations 1, cargo-incremental 2, tool-generations 3, nx-native-data 4, closure-layers 5); the inode sweep (`scarce:"inodes"`) orders by it; every ledger row spawns `repo cache gc --area=<own area>` once, so each collector has its own 600 s budget and none starves another. Volume facts in failure logs now include free and total inodes. Tests: `🧪️tests` 23/23 (incl. an independent Python order model), `📥️need` 7/7, `📊️volume-facts` 4/4, `🖼️image-slack` 15/15, seven ready suites green.
- m04 live (17:05-17:21): new code at 17:08. The sweep now cycles through its collectors in about 2 minutes instead of sitting on one for 10 minutes (17:20 `cargo-incremental` finished in seconds, freed 6.6 MiB). Free inodes did not move: 123,043 at 17:05, 122,462 at 17:20 (falling slowly, replica applies). Need is the derived 428,796 (shortfall about 306,000). The stage has not started; F3's EACCES fix is not yet exercised there.
- m04 filesystem, from the evidence in `S/origins/g1-m04`: the store holds only bytes (475,758 MB total, 105 GB free), never the inode table. So the total number of inodes is not known; the new volume-facts line will print it in the next failure log. Collectable caches hold almost nothing (cargo 6.6 MiB; tool, closure and compile caches 0-1 MiB), and the dependency-generation collector retired nothing visible. So what holds the inodes is not a cache of ours. A 475 GB ext4 normally has about 30 million inodes, so 122k free means something else holds about 30 million, not two generations of 430k; the inode table is not "too small for our generations", it is nearly exhausted by something else.
- USER (on m04, read-only): `df -i /home/deck/semio/.cache` and `sudo du --inodes -x -d3 /home/deck/semio 2>/dev/null | sort -n | tail -20`. If the volume's inode table is small (a small bytes-per-inode ratio was not chosen at mkfs), the clean fix is a dedicated volume for the store with a high inode count, for example `sudo mkfs.ext4 -N 40000000 /dev/<spare-partition>` mounted at `/home/deck/semio/.cache`.
