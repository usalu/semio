# Warden Memory Observation

At the observed fleet sample the host had 16,384 MB physical memory, 2,325 MB available, 8,637 MB compressed and swap-out of 103 MB/s. The warden was enforcing danger.

`decideHostWardenV1` in `🧰️framework/🔨️modules/🏃️process/🏗️build/🌡️host-capacity/🛡️warden/🟦️.ts` classifies compressed memory at 50% as danger, but immediate danger cancellation requires `severe`: available under 5% (819 MB), headroom under 12.5% (2,048 MB), or deep swap and available under 10% (1,638 MB). The observed available memory and reported headroom do not meet those severe thresholds. With active swap-out the warden suspends subjects and counts the RSS of suspended subjects toward its deficit, even though suspending a process does not release its resident memory. It waits 120 seconds before cancelling overdue noncritical subjects; critical subjects are resumed rather than cancelled when the shortage is not severe, and one subject remains running. This explains a delayed response to this sample, but does not establish what caused the original allocation spike.

The decision now treats danger-depth compression with active swap-out as severe, retaining the existing pause deadline before cancelling pausable subjects and using at least the pressure deficit as its reclamation target. The last eligible subject may be paused under this condition; excluded or non-cancellable subjects remain protected. A small JSON fixture reproducing the sample is checked against the independent Python oracle and tests the quiet, lower-compression and pause-deadline boundaries. `repo test-file` passed all eight assertions. The existing oracle fixture check passed. No real host pressure test was run.
