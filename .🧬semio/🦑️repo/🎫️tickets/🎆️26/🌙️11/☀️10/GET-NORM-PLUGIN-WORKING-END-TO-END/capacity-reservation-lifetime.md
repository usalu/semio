# Capacity reservation lifetime

The live macOS ledger showed a 3,870 MiB graph-plan reservation for W14E, a 2,663 MiB Cargo grant, and a 512 MiB quiet run-many grant across two agent sessions. With the graph slice showing 127 MiB resident, Cargo showing 1,105 MiB, and run-many showing 76 MiB, the evaluator deducted 1,558 MiB of unrealized memory. That was exactly the Cargo difference. A later local snapshot reproduced the same behavior with 128 MiB for W14E and 1,332 MiB for Cargo. The same graph grant rose to 3,052 MiB observed at age 408 seconds, after Cargo had been reaped. This is approximately 2,924 MiB of growth after the earlier 128 MiB observation.

Status and admission both call the same capacity evaluator. This was an accounting decision, independent of the agent session. The previous rules let a graph-plan reservation decay after the default 60-second ramp and reduced unusually idle ramping reservations even earlier. They intentionally count a quiet forecast at its measured demand until the executing stage requests an upgrade. The live run-many grant was such a forecast. Explicitly sized capacity commands also lost their reservation after the same ramp.

Graph-plan and explicit `repo capacity run --memory` now keep their admitted memory charge for the full held lifetime. Admission subtracts only the unrealized difference from available memory, and the committed budget includes the larger of the reservation and observed charge. This counts the already-resident footprint once. A hard grant keeps its booking while the owner waits. The explicit command requires an active, sufficiently sized, durable inherited grant before it starts. The read uses the holder's process-start identity when validating custody. Forecast aging, quiet upgrades and loans from ramping forecast holders continue to use their existing rules. Fresh graph-plan writers persist the reservation mode, and readers identify graph-plan by operation, including active rows written before the update.

A language-neutral fixture assembled the observed-scale graph and quiet rows from separate sessions with a modeled 6,200 MiB host sample. The old evaluator charged zero unrealized MiB and admitted the Cargo contender. The new evaluator and independent SQLite query both deducted 3,743 MiB and made Cargo wait. The same test covers the 6,144 MiB explicitly sized workflow after 80 seconds, an expired ordinary estimate, and a quiet forecast. The framework's decision, lending and settlement suites passed 42 tests with 5,331 assertions. Two targeted gate tests passed, covering normal loan behavior and a durable owner waiting past its ramp. Three targeted CLI tests passed, including rejection of sufficient-sized inherited forecast grants. The repo-specific graph-plan producer suite passed all 13 tests and 70 assertions. Its isolated worker held 3,870 MiB with 126 MiB observed, blocked a 2,663 MiB contender past the ramp, and released the contender after the worker exited.

A real local smoke command admitted a harmless 128 MiB, one-core reservation. The child read its live ledger row through `repo capacity status --json` and confirmed `retainReservation: true`, the exact size and the core count. The earlier live graph and Cargo grants had already retired when the corrected CLI was queried. This establishes fresh local command adoption and graph behavior in isolation. It does not establish adoption by another already-running resident owner or fleet node. No graph, Cargo, browser or manual service restart was started for this work.
