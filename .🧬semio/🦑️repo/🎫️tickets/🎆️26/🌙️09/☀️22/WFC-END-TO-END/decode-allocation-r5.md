# Decode allocation transition

The current original allocation port accepts quote, admission, post and cancellation events. A quote must precede the owned effect and the original request, actual marker and callback progress must survive unwind in the owning frame. A post-effect notification cannot be translated into a quote without changing the contract.

`NativeDecodeControl::charge` now rejects forwarded allocation and remains a local-budget operation. The receiving error retains the full native allocation outcome as well as independent construction and receipt failures; its controlled retirement includes that outcome. The original prepaid failure-node laws passed 15 of 15 in the narrow failure owner gate 17727. This is not a decoder, IO, artifact or WFC verdict.

Four captured compiler sites concern `record_constructed_allocation`, `receive_allocated`, `receive_retained_work`, and the old root charge. The charge is migrated; the others require the caller to retain allocation progress while moving the actual producer inside the quoted operation. `receive_retained_work` appears on 62 source lines across decoder owned receivers, derived receivers, ordered, shared UTF-8, paged, and named-list owners. `NativeSharedReceiver` and `NativeIntrinsicSharedField` currently have no such progress slot. The migration also needs to preserve the original construction receipt and each actual child work count, including post-refusal and panic.

A new portable native decoder failure-event fixture and tests cover prepaid failure allocation quote, post, cancellation, refusal and actual zero birth; they have not yet run because upstream native decoder compilation remains blocked. The fixture and schema were independently checked with Ajv. Existing WFC artifact and separate bridge binary gates remain outstanding; the narrow retirement pass must not be substituted for them.

The decode allocation primitive is being changed concurrently by the allocation owner. Its progress is tied to the original callback identity, and unresolved callback progress cannot be retired as completed. No bare event wrapping or local unwind-only progress slot should be used to bypass the ownership transition.

The shared-birth portable fixture now covers accepted, original quote refusal, and original post refusal. Independent Ajv validation passed and rejected reversed quote/post ordering. A focused normal-ingress Rust run, session 11267, exited 1 at decoder compilation: three captured old post-effect event calls and a provisional new helper type inference error. The latter was fixed afterward. No test body ran.

The provisional helper implementations were removed after review found that an unwind during construction receipt acceptance could discard the child turn or an independent native allocation failure, and shared birth could lose its internally acquired construction grant. The authored new tests intentionally remain red until a frame owns the original grant, receipt callback phase, child turn and work, allocation actual marker and allocation callback progress through same-recipient resolution. All old post-effect callers remain to be moved; nested children that borrow the same control need a reentrant-safe scope.

A caller-owned generic `NativeReceivingEffect<E>` is now authored in the construction owner. It retains construction grant and unique recipient identity, allocation callback progress and actual, full child result/work, allocation outcome, and receipt callback state and refusal. Grant, allocation and receipt callbacks have explicit same-owner recovery boundaries; interrupted child work requires the caller to supply its real retained turn before settlement. A typed failure wrapper keeps `E` separate from all receiving failures, and supports paid retirement. Nonempty frames refuse a retirement quote until callback resolution and result/work transfer.

Construction now uses a caller-owned non-copyable `NativeConstructionRecipientIssuer` and exclusive short loan of the actual turn backend, following the approved original-source delegation model. The same issuer survives callback recovery; scoped decoder controls reborrow its identity and backend together. This is issuer-based authority, not immutable callback-object identity. Same-address replacement by a wrong issuer has an adversarial test. Existing construction call sites remain to migrate. The allocation port has a separate issuer in concurrent work.
