# Slice worker growth in the take pass: the look lanes ignored the cut

## 1. Is the live worker on the new code?
Yes. There is no resident graph service in this path: the slice pool runs in the `bun --eval` door process of the run (parent of the worker), and every cut step forks a fresh worker that loads the source at spawn. The worker environment carries `SEMIO_GRAPH_PLAN_CEILING_MB=6553`, `BUN_JSC_forceRAMSize=536870912`, `MIMALLOC_PURGE_DELAY=0`; its footprint log shows `gate installed ... grant=6553 MB`. The only old process is the replica supervisor (`shared-worktree/replica/supervisor`, unrelated). Nothing needs a restart; a changed `🟨️.mjs` is picked up by the next worker.

## 2. What grows (measurements)
- Growth is not between owners and not retention. Worker 95863/92393: `taken 0 of 4774`, `inflight 24`, managed heap 389 MB then 3.7 GB (12.4 M objects, 7 M strings) within 40 s, and after a full collection still 3067 MB heap + 2695 MB external. The `owners` report source says phase `taken`, i.e. the take pass (`resolveOwners`), not the inference loop.
- The take pass runs `takePassV1` per lane with the declared `concurrency` 12, two lanes (local, remote) = 24 looks at once. A look walks the script closure of its owner and, for a present record, runs `resolveClosureRefsV1`; the slow lines (`owner-inference mit-bestand-*`, `resolve-closure-refs`) sit exactly at the spikes. The cut ladder (`SEMIO_GRAPH_CUT_CONCURRENCY`) is only read by `projectConcurrencyV1` (the inference loop). So steps 2 to 5 of the cut halved the loop and the wave but left 24 looks, which is why growth persisted "with smaller waves".
- Worker 2061 (steps relaxed to concurrency 4, 8 looks): managed heap flat at 600 to 1100 MB over 12 070 looks (782 MB heap, 3.98 M objects after 8 minutes), footprint 1.4 GB (peak 2.8 GB). So there is no per-worker retention left in `releaseOwnerStateV1`: the previous agent's release holds.
- RSS versus footprint: `vmmap` of 2061 shows footprint 1.4 GB, RSS 4.2 to 5 GB (mimalloc regions resident but clean or freed-not-purged). The pool and gate judge by `footprint`, correctly; RSS overstates. Not the cause, no change.
- Open: one worker started after the fix (98732, 2 looks in flight) still reached 2.3 GB managed with 9.9 M objects at its first looks (`mit-bestand-praesentation-projektetage`, `mit-bestand-bericht`, `resolve-closure-refs`): a single present-record look of a many-target owner can be 1 GB or more. That is not fixed here. I added a `[DEBUG]` look probe to name the owner and removed it again because no new worker started during the session. The slice report interval (30 s) and snapshot window (heap 500 to 1000 MB) are too coarse to catch a 10 s spike.

## 6. Second fix: looks are admitted by the footprint gate
Nothing in the graph path called `gate.admit`; the inference loop only holds through the governor (`hold` while `headroomBytes()<0`), and the look path had neither a governor nor a hold. `takeAdmittedV1(attempt, gate, signal)` (take module) runs `gate().admit(signal)` before each look: below the soft share it returns at once, from it the process collects and shrinks, from the hard share (85 % of the plan ceiling) it waits in declared steps for the footprint to fall (bounded by the gate's `waitBoundMilliseconds` and cooldown). Used for the pass and the poll in `project-nodes/🟨️.mjs` with `footprintGateV1` (the slice ceiling gate of the worker; none installed, no wait).
Test: `take/🧪️tests/🟦️.ts` "a look starts only after the footprint gate admitted it ..." (admit blocks, the look must not start; no gate: no wait; aborted admission ends the look; a pass with a counting gate admits every owner once, outcomes unchanged). The independent check of the concurrency cap remains the Python model (`lanes`). The gate decisions themselves are covered by the existing footprint suite and its SQLite oracle.
Results: take suite 7 pass, 0 fail; `bun build --no-bundle` of `project-nodes/🟨️.mjs` was started in the background (the first build check passed before this edit).

## 7. Third round: one look in isolation, and the release that made the bursts
Isolated profile (`owner-look-probe.mjs`, a copy of the owner probe with an authority phase; the owner has only a `Cargo.toml`, so the job is synthetic with no targets): `projectAssemblyAuthorityV1` 3 ms and +0 MB, `resolveClosureRefsV1` 8 ms and +0 MB, the cold inference body 9.2 s and +223 MB (module code of the process, 1.1 M objects). So neither the cargo authority (`nativeDependencyRoots` is memoized per directory and spelled once) nor the closure-reference resolution of a stored record materializes 0.5 to 1 GB by itself; the isolated probe cannot reproduce the burst (honest limit: no stored record of the real worker was replayed).

What does explain the burst: the release after a look. `releaseAfterTakeLookV1` called `releaseOwnerStateV1`, which drops the module graph, reach sets, closure walks, source reads and tool groups every 32nd look. A look at a stored record mostly reads and leaves nothing (heap flat at 619 to 683 MB over 3000 looks in the probe log), so the release only made the next cohort of looks rebuild the shared walk state, which the code comment of `OWNERS_PER_RELEASE_V1` already names ("releasing it ... made each crate cost five times its own work"). In the probe log the jump (heap 683 to 1156 MB, external 360 to 908 MB, resident 1.9 to 5.7 GB in about 3 s, eight looks of the Rust cluster in flight) starts exactly 25 looks after a multiple of 32 (3040 = 95 x 32). This is consistent with, but not proven by, the release-then-rebuild cycle; I could not spawn a new worker to log the release events.

Fix (schema first): the release after a look follows growth. New declared limit `lookReleaseGrowthBytes` (128 MiB, en and de descriptions in `inference/schema`); `lookReleaseDueV1(lowest, managed, {shrink})` is true when the managed bytes (heap in use plus external) grew that much beyond their lowest reading since the last release, or while the host is short (`shrink`); `releaseAfterTakeLookV1` tracks the lowest reading and calls `releaseOwnerStateV1(..., {now:true})` (new option that skips the interval counter). Looks that accumulate (about 16 MB each when they resolve references) still release about every 8 looks, so the earlier "10 GB after 650 owners" protection stays; the inference loop keeps its count of 32 owners.
Changed files (under `🕸️graph/📥️inference/`): `🧬️schema/🔣️.json`, `🧭️script-inputs/🟨️.mjs`, `🧭️script-inputs/🟨️.d.mts`; tests `🧪️tests/🪫️owner-release/🟦️.ts`, `🐍️.py`, `🔣️.json` (new test: the independent Python model of the lowest-reading rule agrees with `lookReleaseDueV1` over a 15-reading sequence incl. shrink, and 96 flat looks leave the cache untouched while a shrink look clears it; the declared-limit test now includes the new key). `owner-release` 4 pass, 0 fail; `bun build --no-bundle` of `script-inputs/🟨️.mjs` ok. ## 11. activate-4 restart loop: exit 75 was a graph-queue yield, not code churn (fixed live)
Checked while the loop ran (service log `host-84875f6d99e4.log`, environment of service pid 53817): the workers did not die of module moves. Each lived about 15 to 22 s, logged "graph: waiting for the graph build of a peer (20 s in the queue ...)" and "a peer builds the graph; this run waits as its front door alone, without a loader", then ended with the declared restart code 75. The cause: the service inherited `SEMIO_BUILD_GRAPH_YIELD_TICKET=$TMPDIR/semio-graph-yield-53784.json` (the ticket path the front door of the command that started the service offered), so `graphQueueYieldsV1` (`graphs/generations/load`, after `yieldAfterMilliseconds` 15 s) let every slice worker yield: it wrote a ticket nobody reads, exited 75, and the pool took 75 for "the code changed under the worker" and restarted it every 30 s, forever (the round counter in the environment never advances because no front door re-executes it). A deterministic misclassification, not churn.
Fix, without touching a service-identity file: `slice-pool/👷️worker.ts` (a file only a worker loads; not part of the identity since round 9) deletes the ticket variable named by the yield policy before it does anything else, so a worker waits for the peer build in place (as before the yield existed) and keeps reporting. Verified live: the next worker (pid 67545) was still running after 81 s, past the 15 s yield threshold; `repo service host status` shows no reload pending for service 53817.
Not fixed (needs identity files, so left for a later restart): the service launcher should not pass the ticket variable to the service at all (`CLIENT_BOUND_ENVIRONMENT_V1` in `composition/host/🟨️.mjs`), and the pool should not count a worker that logged a yield as a code-churn restart. Add both next time the service may restart. No test for the worker deletion yet (a worker process cannot be exercised without the whole graph); the yield rule itself is covered by the existing `generations/yield` tests.

## 13. Review of every teardown that can SIGKILL a service (lead: draining service 31307 exited at 19:44:48, 53817 died then)
Paths read and what each can reach:
- Launcher `repository:command` (`runOwnedCommand`, `owned-execution`): spawns the service as a detached child (own process group); `terminate` runs `terminateOwnedProcessTree` (the child's descendants, plus every process group led by an owned process) only for its own timeout, deadline, abort, output limit or ceiling. A service ended by an outside SIGKILL shows up there as `OwnedCommandFailure` with signal SIGKILL and no stop reason, which is exactly the line seen ("repository:command was killed by SIGKILL"): the launcher did NOT kill it. The service's policy has `maximumElapsedMilliseconds` 0 (no timeout).
- Service teardown (`composition/host`, drain, `reload`): writes an exit row and calls `service.stop`; no group or tree kill (the cgroup helpers are Linux only). `replaceHostServiceV1` only SIGTERMs its own replacement child.
- Retirement (`retireHostServiceV1`): SIGTERM of the pid a status names; never SIGKILL, never a group.
- `consent`, `delegate`, `watch` kill only their own probe or child.
- Slice pool exit and load end (`sweepAtExit`, `reaped(load)`): the one path that kills by token and by DESCENDANTS: victims are the pids a worker reported, every process whose `ps -Eww` line carries the load token (children inherit it), and everything below them. A graph service started by a command that a worker ran (a nested `repo` command that needs a plan and finds no service starts one) inherits that token, sits in the worker's tree and is therefore a victim when its parent service's load is reaped or its service exits (`killNow` on exit). activate-5's sampler shows the real layout: launcher 85120 (ppid 1, own group), service 85145 (own group), worker 85271 (own group) with its children in the worker's group; nothing proves yet that 53817 was started under a worker (its launcher's parent 53768 is gone), so this is the only code path found that can kill a foreign service, not a confirmed cause.
Fix (no identity file; `slice-pool/🧹️reap/🟨️.mjs` is not in `serviceRevisionFiles`): rows carry `service` (the command line holds `service host run`, from `ps` on macOS, `/proc/<pid>/cmdline` on Linux); `victims` never includes a service launcher that is neither this process nor one of its ancestors, nor anything below it. The pool's own service and its workers are reaped as before. Test `resident/tests/🧹️reap-service`: 3 pass (a service started under a worker survives both by pid and by token match, the worker's other children do not; the pool's own service chain still reaps its workers; the host's process table marks no ordinary process).
Activation: the running service (85145) loaded the old reaper at its start, so the fix is live only for the next service started (identity unchanged, so none is started unless the current one dies or is stopped).
Open: if a service dies again, the sampler in `service-tree-samples.txt` shows whether the launcher, service and worker vanish together (then a parent's tree or group kill) or only the service (then a targeted signal).

## 17. Warm take at 9.4 GB: the farm children, not the worker; gate and pool read the same measure

## 19. Per-child footprint: measurement, root cause (a farm child never released its owner state), footprint cap with alone retry

Same eight-owner sequence minus the last two with the release: retained 264, 351, 374, 397, 657, 688 MB (against 288, 502, 552, 589, 955, 959), peak footprint of six owners 2002 MB. The release helps by about 30 %; a single owner like stdio-plugin still adds about 400 MB on its own, so the cap below is what bounds the rest.
Fix 2 (`project-farm/🏊️pool`, schema first, declared in `🧬️schema/🔣️.json`, texts en and de): the pool reads the footprint of every child that runs a job every `footprintMilliseconds` (1000), and a child above `childFootprintCapMegabytes` (2500) is stopped (SIGTERM, SIGKILL after `terminateGraceMilliseconds` 2000, only its own child) and its job goes to the head of the queue to run alone: no job beside it, no worker started beside it. Say lines `capped`, `cappedAlone`, `overrun`; stats `capped`, `overruns`, `peakFootprintMegabytes`; the reply carries `footprintMegabytes`. Numbers: a parent of 1300 MB, one sibling at the measured 1719 MB and a capped child stay under the 5570 MB hard share of the 6553 MB ceiling (2500), the alone cap is what the hard share leaves beside the parent (4200).
