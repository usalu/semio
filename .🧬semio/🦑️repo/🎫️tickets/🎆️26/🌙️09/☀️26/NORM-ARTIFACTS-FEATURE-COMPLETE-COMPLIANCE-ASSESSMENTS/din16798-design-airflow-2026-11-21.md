# DIN EN 16798-1 design airflow

The report, reference catalogue and category diagrams use the selected design method, annex, design occupancy and pollution class. The calculations use complete mixing. The method 1 design airflow for a low-polluting German category II office of 50.6 m² with six people is 278.712 m³/h. The published BAFA example gives 279 m³/h. [DIN EN 16798-1:2022-03 preview, NA.5.1](https://www.normsplash.com/Samples/DIN/366818232/DIN-EN-16798-1-2022-de.pdf) · [BAFA worked example](https://www.bafa.de/SharedDocs/Downloads/DE/Energie/qpeb_uebungsfragen_gebaeudetechnik_nwg.pdf?__blob=publicationFile&v=6)

The method 2 design flow divides the reference generation of 20 L CO₂ per person and hour by the concentration difference, using consistent units. Table B.9 gives 550, 800, 1,350 and 1,350 ppm above outdoor for categories I through IV. Table NA.9 gives 350, 550, 900 and 1,350 ppm. The German footnote refers workplaces to ASR A3.6, whose risk assessment uses measured indoor concentration and expected use. [DIN EN 16798-1:2022-03 with NA.5.1.3 and Annex B](https://de.scribd.com/document/1071290161/) · [BAuA ASR A3.6](https://www.baua.de/DE/Angebote/Regelwerk/ASR/pdf/ASR-A3-6.pdf?__blob=publicationFile)

EN office method 3 uses the larger flow calculated from the published per-person and per-area criteria. The EN living and sleeping room supply criteria in Table B.11 columns 2 and 3 have category I–III values. The residential CO₂ exercise uses the living-room column of Table B.12. Whole-dwelling flow, wet-room extract rates, bedroom CO₂, openings and non-occupancy flow require additional project inputs. NA.5.1.4 excludes method 3 and NA.5.2 uses a dwelling-unit floor area threshold of 250 m². Projects up to that area use DIN 1946-6 and larger units use the non-residential criteria. The project dimension is the dwelling unit.
