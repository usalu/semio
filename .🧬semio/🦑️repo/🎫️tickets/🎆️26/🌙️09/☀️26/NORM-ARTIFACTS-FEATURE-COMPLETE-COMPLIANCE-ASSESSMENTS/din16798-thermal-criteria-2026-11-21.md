# DIN EN 16798-1 thermal design criteria

The live seasonal temperature checks compare winter against a heating minimum and summer against a cooling maximum. Tables B.2 and NA.3 describe reference design temperatures for the specified use, typical clothing insulation and activity. EN living rooms have winter minimums of 18 °C and 16 °C in categories III and IV. Comparable seated offices have 19 °C and 18 °C. The narrower table for other dwelling rooms needs an explicit room profile. German applicability depends on the dwelling-unit area. [DIN official preview, Table NA.3](https://www.normsplash.com/Samples/DIN/366818232/DIN-EN-16798-1-2022-de.pdf) · [DIN publication, EN Table B.2 and Figure B.1](https://de.scribd.com/document/1071290161/)

The free-running EN check and diagram use Figure B.1. Category I is −3/+2 K from the central comfort temperature, category II −4/+3 K and category III −5/+4 K. The running mean must exceed 10 °C and stay below 30 °C. This summer and shoulder-season method presumes primarily sedentary occupants who can adapt their clothing and control windows in a building without active cooling. Below the lower limit the remedy raises the operative temperature. The German NA.4.1 requires hourly temperatures and evidence of occupied periods. A running mean cannot supply that assessment. ## German hourly temperature assessment

NA.4.1.2 derives hourly comfort temperature from the hourly outdoor mean. The centre is 22 °C at and below 16 °C, 26 °C at and above 32 °C, and linear between them. The expected operative band extends 2 K on either side. The method assumes occupants can adjust their clothing, typical clothing insulation in the 0.3–1.0 clo range and low activity. NA.4.1.1 requires a documented reference point in the occupied zone and gives 0.60 m as the reference height for a stratified space. [DIN EN 16798-1:2022-03, NA.4.1.1–2](https://www.normsplash.com/Samples/DIN/366818232/DIN-EN-16798-1-2022-de.pdf)

NA.4.1.3 calls for separate cold and warm totals during occupied time. The documented annual allowance in each direction is 1% of occupied hours multiplied by 2 K. The worked example has 3,650 occupied hours and an allowance of 73 K·h. The paragraph also limits departures beyond the band to a maximum of 2 K. The assessment retains duration, degree-hours and the largest departure individually. A short 3 K excursion may satisfy the annual degree-hour budget while exceeding the excursion limit. The source uses strict wording in one sentence and inclusive wording in the bullet list at the 1% duration boundary. The arithmetic vectors preserve the exact boundary quantities so an annex verdict can resolve their treatment explicitly.
