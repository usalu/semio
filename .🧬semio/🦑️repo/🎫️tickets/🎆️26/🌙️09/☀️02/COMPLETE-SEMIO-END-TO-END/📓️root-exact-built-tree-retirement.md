## Staged Ownership Design

The new contract-owned `BuiltTreeRetirement` retains one current boxed node, one typed cursor, and a fixed boxed stack of 384 child-page iterators. It closes all nine nonstructural fields through existing typed retirement: key, component, layout, style, activity, disabled, accessibility, bindings and menu. Compile-time depth guards cover every field. Each completed field resets the cursor; child handoff resets it defensively too.

Rejected pages are staged before ordinary pages so the LIFO walk closes ordinary children first. The bound counts unique retained pages, not observation depth or full parent nodes. The 384-page chain intentionally exceeds the observer's depth64; a parent with both lists verifies their order. Each page transfers existing boxed children without cloning or allocating a new child.

An internal iterator method transfers a child without locking. Final page release uses the exact key/epoch/reservation under `try_lock`; contention leaves cursor, backing and handback unchanged. Only an explicit Complete result permits dropping the iterator. No global child or UI-value retirement queue is advanced by this owner.

The strict owner requires callers to retain it until terminal closure. Early Drop is a contract violation, not a safe abandon/cancellation API. Its manually retained aggregate prevents a nested nonterminal UiValue destructor from double-panicking during that violation, but does not claim recovery. The fixture helper must catch projection errors/panics, close this owner fully, and only then return/resume. A later safe-abandon design needs a separate whole-owner handback and must not be inferred from these tests.
