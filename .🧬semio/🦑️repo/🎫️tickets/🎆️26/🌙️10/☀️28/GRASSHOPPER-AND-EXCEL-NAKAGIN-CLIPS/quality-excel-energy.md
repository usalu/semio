# Quality review: excel / energy (`Beat4_Energy`)

Reviewer: quality director (read-only). Inputs read: `quality-brief.md`, `plan.md` row, `scene-excel-energy.md`, `verify-excel-energy.md`, all 23 EN keyframes in clause order (hook, sheet, dims, areas, exposed, uvalues, ua, ht, climate, mistake, fix, volume, total, load, annual, energy, compare, shape, trace, whatif, frozen, repaired, recap), DE frames for mistake, annual, uvalues, shape. Comparison: the energy series heating clips (`⚡️energy/🔥️heating/🎬️clip`) and ventilation/cooling clip folders.

## Verdict

The physics chain is sound and the Excel mechanics (formula bar, reference frames, what-if, frozen total) are taught well and correctly; numbers are consistent across frames (30.92 W/K, 3.12 W/K, 681 W, 1,296 kWh/a, 182,901 kWh/a, 173,115 kWh/a after the window change). The video is a good "calculation walkthrough" but not yet "fullblown visual": from clause 9 on, half of the clauses draw a number or a label where the energy series draws a physical picture, and the building story (Nakagin, Tokyo, why this matters) is thin at the start and the end. Not yet at the house bar.

Compared with the energy series: its heating clips teach with a house section, wall layers, heat-flow arrows that scale, and a running comparison of loss paths. This clip matches that only in `ua` and `load` (arrows). Everything after `annual` is spreadsheet-centric.

## Ranked improvements (max 10)

1. **Hook and recap do not frame an answer (clauses 1 and 23). Effort M.** Hook shows a tower with a "?" and a question; recap is a static glossary with a small tower. Why: no payoff, so the learner does not feel the arc closed. How: hook = tower with one capsule pulled out, a thermometer (Tokyo, 0 °C outside / 20 °C inside) and the "?" ; recap = same capsule, now with the result chip "1,296 kWh/a, 148 kWh/(m²·a)" and the tower tinted by per-type totals ending in "182,901 kWh/a"; the glossary can go to a left strip that builds in line by line while each symbol lights on the capsule (U·A arrows, H_V blue arrow, hatch for area).
2. **Degree days are a bare number (clause 9 climate, 15 annual). Effort M.** `1,586 K·d` is the hardest concept for a beginner and appears only as a row and a one-line method note in grey. How: draw the twelve Tokyo monthly means (from `climate()`) as bars against the 18 °C heating-limit line; the shortfall area is hatched and sums to 1,586 K·d; in `annual` the same hatched area slides into the formula as the HDD factor. Why: it turns "degree days" into an area the student can see and ties Climate!B5 to a picture.
3. **Volume is not drawn (clause 12). Effort S.** B10 is framed in the sheet but the capsule is just the arrows; the label "air 3.12 W/K" overlaps the window and a second arrow sits on top of the 3.09 label. How: show the interior volume as a translucent box (2.3 x 3.8 x 2.1, dimension lines), blue air arrows through the window along the length, label placed outside the capsule; show "n = 0.5 per hour" as a small counter (one capsule volume of air exchanged every two hours).
4. **Annual demand is shown as units, not as meaning (clause 15). Effort M.** The right side is a tiny hatched rectangle 8.7 m² and a yellow unit chain. How: draw the floor plan of the capsule at scale (hatched 8.7 m²), a bar "1,296 kWh/a" built by stacking H_T x HDD x 24 / 1000 as three coloured blocks (W/K, K·d, h/d) that multiply into the bar; then divide the bar into 8.7 pieces for kWh/(m²·a). Also the overlay formula hides cell B14 while the value 1,296 kWh/a is exactly what is being taught: show the result before covering it.
5. **Why Types A, B, D give identical demand is unexplained (clauses 16 and 17). Effort S.** Energy sheet shows 1,296 / 1,296 / 1,340 / 1,296 and compare says "only C needs a little more", while the four colours imply four different capsules. A student will ask. How: one caption beat and a mini-drawing: the four types share the shell and differ only in fittings/window (state which, from `🏢️nakagin`), type C's larger window drawn bigger with its U·A delta. The Type D sheet explanation was not re-checked in verify; confirm against the model before changing.
6. **Typical mistake 1 sits on the Climate sheet, not where the learner works (clauses 10 and 11). Effort S.** The student changes b on Climate!B6 and watches arrows; they never see that the Type A formula for the back wall (`=D9*E9*attached_factor`) is what is wrong. How: split screen with Type A F9 formula visible (named reference highlighted) and the Climate cell; the wrong-version mistake that really happens is using `1` (an exposed factor) or hard-typing 0.6, so show F9 with a typed constant `=D9*E9*1` and the arrow turning thick. Keep the arrow growth (3.75 vs 1.88 W/K) - it already works.
7. **Arrow lengths are not proportional to U·A (clauses 7, 8, 10, 14). Effort S.** 6.00 and 5.00 W/K arrows look alike; the 3.09 window arrow is short because it points into the capsule. The caption promises "longer and thicker". How: scale length and width linearly to U·A on a stated scale (key: arrow of 1 W/K), point the window and front arrows outward along their normals, and fade arrows of exposed vs core-attached surfaces differently so the back wall's halving is visible next to the area (6.3 m² big, 1.88 W/K small).
8. **Building-scale payoff is mostly numbers (clauses 16, 19, 20, 21). Effort M.** After the what-if the tower is not shown; `trace`, `whatif`, `frozen`, `repaired` all keep the small single capsule on the right and the large tracing arrows crossing cell text ("38" of the Type A count is overdrawn). How: whatif = tower on the right with all 140 capsules recoloured by per-type demand and a counter 182,901 -> 173,115 kWh/a (-9 %), with the window of each capsule flashing from red to green; frozen = the same tower with one stale number tag and the delta (9,786 kWh/a) drawn as a red gap bar next to the true bar. This is also the stronger answer to "so what" for an architecture student.
9. **Shape clause (18) asserts but does not show (clause `shape`). Effort M.** The comparison of 148 vs 100 kWh/(m²·a) uses two small capsules and two number pairs, with "5.3 vs 3.0 m²/m²" unexplained. How: show a stack of three capsules (heated neighbours above and below) with the shared faces greyed out, and animate the exposed area ratio as unfolded faces (net) beside the floor area square. Shorten the caption to one idea: "Shared roof and floor halve the shell per floor area."
10. **Pace and density in `ht`, `climate`, `trace` (clauses 8, 9, 19). Effort S.** `ht` shows two formulas, a grey note line 8 px from the edge and the arrows at once; `climate` is a window plus six names plus two grey notes; `trace` has a full chain panel with five rows and arrows over cells. How: reveal in order (SUM first, then SUMPRODUCT only as a second pass), put the HDD method note in the new degree-day chart (item 2), and reduce the chain panel to Window U -> H_T -> demand -> total with the dependents counted live.

Open data items (not scene): twelve monthly means not re-verified against JMA; status-bar sums still unformatted (`Sum: 30.92126861` in the source; frames show rounded values only in the new Excel window, verify list notes older ones); trace arrow over the Type A count; `repaired` stage is nearly empty (covered by item 8).

## Three things that work very well (keep them)

1. **Reference frames and arrow colours tie cells to the building** (`areas`, `ua`): the colour of the frame around D3:D9 matches the face tint on the capsule, and U·A arrows carry the same numbers as column F. This is the exact "tool and Nakagin at the same time" the brief asks for.
2. **The live what-if (`whatif`)**: one input cell changes, and cells, arrows, chain panel, bar chart and formula bar update from the engine together (2.33 -> 1.10 W/(m²·K), 182,901 -> 173,115 kWh/a); followed by the frozen-constant mistake and its repair on the same stage. It is the best Excel-specific teaching in the video.
3. **Narration and German**: one idea per clause, active and concrete, two-line captions, and the German reads natural ("Beheizte Nachbarn würden den Bedarf deutlich senken", "Ein typischer Fehler: Die angebundene Rückwand zählt wie eine freie Wand"); decimal commas, German sheet names (Typ A, Klima, Energie) and UI labels are correct in all frames checked.
