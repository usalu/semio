## Calibration of the two audits

- Audit 2 is too harsh on 6 beats (it scored "STILL WEAK" where the code does a real part-matched morph and the frame shows it: back, mixed, pointer faces, total, threed capsule to value, slide shatter onto faces). It also read window fade-swaps in `index.md` (`FadeIn x7` in threed-04) as "values are faded in", when that step is the window swap and the morphs are the `Swarm` entries of the neighbouring animations.
- Audit 2 is right on the genuine gaps: the live formula bar never retexts, no seven tethers, span has no tab drag, deleted shows no deleted tab, fill2d morphs only the first cell, recap chips are anonymous blobs.
- The done file is accurate about what exists, but "11 morph animations in 10 clauses" (round 1) plus round 2/3 means roughly 20 morph animations in 24 clauses. The claim "most are built" holds for existence; it does not hold for how well they read.

## (1) Is the video genuinely morph-driven now?

Partially. It has moved from fade-swaps to a morph vocabulary and the chain from capsule to dimension to cell to area is real in pointer, back, mixed and the type chain in threed. But three things keep it from "perfectly":

1. The abstract/concrete pair is mostly a chip (a generic pill with a number) rather than the actual object. A face flies as an outline and arrives as a pill; the pill is then abandoned (`_drop`) instead of staying as the cell's content or sticking to the face. Identity is a flash, not a lasting binding. The strongest morphs (pointer, back, mixed) have a visible origin staying (ghost, lit tower) or a destination that persists (dimension label on the capsule).
2. The central teaching events of this topic (formula travels, absolute stays, reference slides, sheet is text, range spans, sheet deleted) happen in the formula bar or as a still window, while the morphs run in a side card or are anonymous chips. The explaining action must be the morph: the formula bar token itself retexts, the reference frame itself slides, the tab itself is dragged or shattered.
3. About 7 s (fill2d), 5 s (absolute), 6 s (mixed2 table fill), 3.5 s (recap) are state sweeps (ValueTracker) on live redraws. A tracker moving is a cut sequence, not a morph.

At full resolution many 0.6 tokens will be readable (the 640x360 frame is 3x smaller than the final), so size is a lesser issue than composition (windows, card and capsules compete for the same 8x5 stage).

## (3) General lessons for all nine videos (follow these)

1. **One idea, one actor, one morph.** Decide the object whose change is the lesson (a formula token, a tab, a reference frame, a capsule) and animate that object, never a duplicate in a side card. Cards are captions that follow the actor.
2. **Persist the bound pair.** A morph ends with a durable partner: the dimension label stays on the capsule, the area chip stays in D3, the value stays on the face. If the target is deleted a second later (`_drop`) the identity transfer was a flash. Keep a ghost or a lasting mark at the origin (faint dimension, outline frame) so the viewer sees both ends.
3. **Move the number, not the state.** If narration says "its value appears and the surface lights up", the number must leave the cell and the surface tint must arrive with it (A->C). Do not set tints from state and move chips in parallel; sequence: chip in flight, then tint at landing (`_when`).
4. **Never replace a morph with a tracker sweep.** `ValueTracker` + `live()` redraws are acceptable for a continuous quantity (width 2.5 -> 3.0 m), never for 5-7 s of cell-after-cell counting. Loop over the discrete items with short gather/split cycles (0.5-0.8 s each).
5. **Show the actual event.** Deletion = the deleted thing shatters. Move = the thing is dragged. Slide = the frame moves and leaves ghosts. Lock = a padlock closes on the locked part. If the idea is an event, fades and crossfades are wrong.
6. **Sequence structure before morph.** Do window swaps and card changes first (0.4-0.6 s), then run the morph on a clean stage; do not play a crossfade of two windows simultaneously with the morph (sheet, absolute, deleted lose the chip).
7. **Keep the morph legible.** Token text >= 0.6 height for numbers; carry the object's colour (type colour, blue width, red length, tint) all the way; keep flights off labels (route with `path_arc` above/below text, or in the margin); one flying group per clause; <= 7 flying objects.
8. **Check mid frames at the final reading size.** 640x360 frames hide text; capture the key morph at 1280x720 and read once; the audits should judge code intent too, not frames alone, and must not mistake the window swap (`FadeIn x7`) for a missing morph.
9. **Both directions in every video:** at least one C->A (face/capsule becomes value/cell) and one A->C (cell value becomes dimension, tint, lit capsule), chained so one object is followed (capsule -> dimension -> cell -> area -> tint).
10. **Match the narration.** Check each clause: the visual event happens while the key word is spoken (hook "changes", pointer "points to", sliding "off", delete "quietly shrinks"); morph timing aligns, not just present.
11. **Add the missing back-link.** Every abstract result that returns to the building (count -> lit capsules, sum -> tower glow, name -> climate cell) gets a visible reverse morph at least once; otherwise the viewer only ever goes building -> table.
12. **Toolkit hygiene.** Use `pairs=` for faces/dimension (keep real part shapes), `mode="parts"` default; use `gather/split` with `ghost` when many <-> one; don't use anonymous `path` tokens (labelled with the concept word) for recap-like scenes, use copies of the real mobjects.
