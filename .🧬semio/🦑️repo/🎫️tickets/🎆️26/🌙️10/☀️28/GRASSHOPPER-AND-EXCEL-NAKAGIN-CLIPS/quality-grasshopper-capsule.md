# Quality Review: grasshopper capsule (`Beat2_Capsule`)

Reviewed: scene NARRATION (23 clauses en/de), `scene-` and `verify-` notes, all 23 current English keyframes in clause order (the German `four` and `wrap` frames are newer than the verify note, so the verify blockers `cut`/`four` German and `wrap` counting are already addressed: wrap now has the numbered strip, `cut` and `four` text reads correctly in the scene file). Judgement is of the whole video, not pixels.

## Verdict

Strong, coherent video. The arc (hook, parametric idea, sliders, plane, box, mistake and fix, bolts, limits, types, Orient, List Item, Wrap, recap) is sound and the viewport/canvas pairing works. What holds it below "top quality" is a few places where the screen is static or abstract, two places where the picture is not yet what the narration claims, and a tangle of wires that a first-time student cannot follow.

## Ranked improvements

1. **`four` (types): make the four types actually differ on screen (M).** Frame shows only type C selected (Ø 1.60) while the caption promises offset and diameter change; types A and B have identical dimensions in the table and the four mini capsules differ only by colour. Play A, B, C, D through the sliders in sequence (one clause, four 2-second steps), highlight the table row and the one changed cell in orange, show the big capsule changing its window (Ø 1.30 to 1.60; offset 0 to -0.45 and a 5 degree turn for D), and add a small "same definition, four value sets" label. Learning gain: this is the payoff of "parametric"; right now it is a table.
2. **`frontplane` and `lift`: untangle the wiring (M).** Both frames still show crossing wires into A/B, Pt and Pl Origin, and the "2" constants sit inside wire paths, so the student cannot trace "half the length". Lay the helper nodes out as a clean left-to-right row (slider, A/B with the "2" panel directly above, Pt, Pl Origin), and pulse the active wire colour in the slider colour (length green, height blue, offset teal) as each is explained. Add a highlight of which viewport dimension each wire carries. Learning gain: this is the hardest-to-read pair of clauses for a novice.
3. **`parametric`: draw only what is wired (S).** The frame shows the finished rounded capsule with a window although only Width is wired into Center Box (B, Y, Z, plane empty). Show a plain box that grows only in width, with the three column headers Parameter, Dependency, Geometry keeping their colours, then let the later clauses add detail. Also say "Dependency" consistently (header is singular, narration plural is fine). Learning gain: the concept slide must not show more than the concept.
4. **`hook`: fill the canvas and make the lit capsule visible (S).** The left half is an almost empty sheet with a small panel, and the lit capsule is tiny in the tower. Zoom the viewport on one facade, lift the lit capsule out and enlarge it, and draw the single "Definition" as one component chain in outline on the canvas (slider box wire), with the 140 / 4 / 1 numbers as three counters. Learning gain: hooks the Nakagin and previews the whole video in one picture.
5. **`orient`: show the transformation, not only the target (M).** The Base A / Target B panels sit on top of the Orient wires (the Capsule wire vanishes behind a panel) and the capsule is already at B when the clause begins. Move the plane panels below the component, show the capsule at A, then slide it along the dashed arrow with a live translation vector `{-12.47, 2.75, 5.50}` and keep wires visible. Learning gain: Orient is the key idea of the second half; the move must be seen.
6. **`rotate`: arc plus all four angles (S).** Only -90 degrees is shown. Step the Angle slider through the facade angles (0, 90, 180, 270 from the model) with the arc and capsule following, and keep the rotated-plane panel next to the slider. Draw the core axis as a vertical dashed line through the core. Learning gain: confirms the capsule follows the plane on all four facades, which is exactly how the tower is assembled.
7. **`bolts`: relate the points to the capsule and the core (S).** Four orange rings on a bare capsule and a panel; the narration says "fix the capsule to the core" but no core is drawn. Show the capsule from the back with a small wall behind it and the four bolts as short rods with their coordinates tagged by index 0-3 (x 0.90, z 0.50 and 2.00 as dimension lines from the back-face corner). Learning gain: gives the points a meaning (position on the wall) instead of numbers.
8. **`wrap`: show the wrap-around as motion (S).** The strip of eight capsules and indices works, but nothing moves. Animate the index 4 jumping back to A with a looping arrow over the type list and light the planes 4 to 7 one by one while their capsules appear on the back faces of the core (currently ghost outlines). Learning gain: "modulo" becomes visible; the first-time student asks why 4 gives A.
9. **`types` and `domains`: draw the difference and the limit (S).** `types` is a table; add a type colour on every row and a small side-by-side silhouette so the window and angle differences read at a glance. `domains` shows the dashed envelope but the limit clause (`limit`) leaves a visible gap; move the width dimension to touch the envelope and flash it once when the slider hits 3.50 m. German: replace "Grenzen Lehrannahmen" with "die Grenzen sind Annahmen des Lehrmodells, zum Beispiel eine Transporthülle" (smoother).
10. **Recap and mistake clause polish (S).** `recap` repeats the final tower but does not point at anything; light the matching part of the picture while each term is spoken (slider, wire, capsule, plane axes, strip). `wrongplane` chip "Circle on the base plane" covers the capsule top and the "2" constant sits under the Offset stub; move both. Add the red-component idea (a slider outside its domain) for one beat in `limit`, as promised by the storyboard misconceptions.

## Three things that work very well (keep)

1. **Colour binding of parameters.** Each slider has a bar colour that is the same as its dimension line in the viewport and its row in the live parameter card (`sliders`), and plane axes always use Rhino red/green/blue. This is exactly the "value drawn by its meaning" bar of the house style.
2. **The mistake and its fix** (`wrongplane` to `frontplane` to `cut`): the circle visibly lies on the floor with an orange remark bubble, then the front-face plane carries it to the right place and the window is cut. Clear, concrete, and memorable.
3. **`outside` to `wrap`**: the engine's own warning ("Index 4 is outside the list of 4 items"), the `null` results, the "!" markers on planes 4 to 7 and the countable numbered strip of eight capsules that turns into a complete set after Wrap. Real Grasshopper behaviour taught in the viewer's own frame.

## Summary

- Overall: strong arc and visuals; German reads naturally in the checked clauses.
- Biggest gaps: `four` does not show the types changing, and the `lift`/`frontplane` wiring is untraceable.
- Static or abstract screens: `hook` (empty canvas), `parametric` (shows more than wired), `types` (table), `bolts` (no core).
- Orient and Rotate need visible motion and all four angles to carry the second half.
- Correctness: names, units and numbers match the model; no wrong values found.
