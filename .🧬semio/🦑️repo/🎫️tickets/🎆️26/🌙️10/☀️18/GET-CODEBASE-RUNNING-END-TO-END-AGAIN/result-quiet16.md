## Why quiet forecasts never held
1. **The door grew its own quiet grant at once.** `repo run` admits at the door (`withBuildCapacityV1` in `runBuildCliV1`), then re-enters `withBuildCapacityV1` in `runOwnedBuildInvocationV1` with the grant in `process.env`; the gate's inherited branch called `requestHostCapacityUpgradeV1` for every quiet grant before running the body. Every gate log shows it (`forecast quiet …` twice, then `reservation grown from 2048 MB to 3684 MB after 0s`: g41-dash3, g42-dash2, g42-hot2, g43-hot1..4, g42p/g43p/g43q edits …). A 0.5 GB no-op therefore reserved its class's full 3.4-5.1 GB, and on a real 16 GB laptop that growth is what waits (sim h16: 43-81 s).
2. **Every run was learned busy.** Because every quiet grant grew, the holder learned `executed: true` for every run: the live store (`~/Library/Caches/semio/host-capacity/estimates.json`, also the 14:50 snapshot) has 16 `~busy` samples per class and NO `~quiet` class. So (a) the history rule (≥ 2 quiet runs, ≥ 80 % decayed quiet share, newest quiet) could never hold, only the 30-minute follow-up rule, and (b) the quiet size never learned: it stayed at the 2048 MB reference although no-ops hold 0.3-0.55 GB. Even with (1) fixed, the 16 mislabeled busy samples (3-day half-life) keep the 80 % share out of reach for days: after any 30-minute pause the door asks the full request again.
3. **Executors could not ask from an inheriting process.** `markBuildCapacityPhaseV1("rebuild")` / `upgradeBuildCapacityBeforeRebuildV1` returned before asking when `SEMIO_HOST_CAPACITY_PHASE_MARKS=off`, which the inherited branch sets for its body (hidden by (1)). (Correction 18:30: the Bazel action birth IS an executor point, see the audit9 section; only the width after a partial growth was unbounded.)
4. **RSS vs footprint: already footprint, nothing to change.** `observeTreeMegabytesV1` defaults to the physical footprint (macOS `footprint -f bytes --noCategories -p …` over the `ps` tree, Linux `smaps_rollup` Pss+SwapPss, Windows private working set); the store says `"measure":"footprint"` and the learned peaks equal footprints (dash no-op 500-511 MB, product.py RSS 1147 MB). Measured here under load 25: `footprint` over a 3-4 GB tree 85-167 ms; `top -l 1 -stats pid,ppid,mem` 7.6 s (unusable per heartbeat). The 2.5-3x overstatement lives only in product.py's RSS column (buildmem's `product-fp.py` reports footprint); reservations were 2-2.5x the footprint because of (1)+(2).
5. **Release.** A quiet grant counts in full only during its ramp, then by what its tree holds; with (1) gone a no-op is never grown, so there is nothing to give back.

## Risks / caveats
- A quiet run whose work stays within its quiet reservation books 1 core while its tool runs all jobs (a forecast never changes what a run may use); the load soft stop and the 2 x cores ceiling still bound the host. Such a run is learned quiet, so the quiet size can drift up toward what small edits hold (bounded by `quietMaximumMegabytes` 4096 and the full request).
- Every real Bazel action waits at its birth for the growth and then for a width slot; the `outgrown` growth only covers work outside those executors (it does not pause). A cold refresh still pauses at the rebuild point as before. A partly grown run compiles narrower and longer instead of over-committing.
- `lastrun` leans quiet: the first run after a long pause that has to rebuild starts small and waits for its growth at the rebuild point (with its original arrival), instead of waiting at the door.
- Not product-verified (no official runs from lanes).
