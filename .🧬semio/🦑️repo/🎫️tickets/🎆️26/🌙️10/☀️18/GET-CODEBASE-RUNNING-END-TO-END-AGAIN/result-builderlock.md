## 2. Protocol (batch `multiwriter`)
1. **Slice custody** (new `🕰️generations/🔐️custody`, schema-first, EN/DE notices). A builder claims the owners it will write (the stale owners of its plan) as `generations/s/<token>.json` + an exclusive per-claim lease `graph-custody:<root>:<token>` (held from before the document exists until it is removed; a free lease = dead command, the next reader removes the document). Tickets under a short admission lease (`max(live tickets)+1`). A claim HOLDS when no live claim that is held or has a smaller ticket conflicts (`claimsConflictV1`: either structural or the owner sets intersect). Structural = cold generation, stale environment, added/removed project configurations (refresh's `pending.structural`). Hence disjoint slices hold at once, overlapping ones run in ticket order, a waiting claim is never overtaken by a later conflicting one (no starvation), deadlock-free (a claim only waits for held or earlier claims; held claims never wait). Waiters say why at once and every 20 s: `graph: 1 refresh(es) of the same projects go first (0 s; shared: a1, a2)` / `Graph: … gemeinsam: …`.
   Exact owner sets, NOT hash-sharded leases: two disjoint slices of a and b owners collide on S shards with p = 1 - e^(-ab/S); course (40) x print (58) -> ~100 % at S = 256, 43 % at S = 4096, i.e. sharding would serialise exactly the case this task is about.
2. **Widening**: the plan inside the custody may find more stale owners than were claimed (edits after queueing). `build()` calls `claim.extend(...)`: granted at once when no other HELD claim conflicts; otherwise `build` returns `needsBuild` with the union, `loadGenerationGraphV1` gives the custody back and queues again with the union (after `widenRounds` = 8: structural). So overlapping writers never build at the same time even when the overlap appears late.
3. **Merge on a lost swap** (stub rule symmetric since 0b) (new `🕰️generations/🔀️merge`): optimistic concurrency control with backward validation against everything published since the writer's base. THEIRS = owners whose manifest row differs base->head; OURS = owners the writer's manifest changed + project owners of its index patch. Refused (-> compute again from the head, as today) on: another identity/implementation or no head manifest; a stub change by a peer (presence, root, kind, targets, config, nativeRoot, tag: every computation reads all stubs); an environment move; OURS ∩ THEIRS; fact rows of an OUR owner changed base->head; both sides changed the global fragment or the membership differently; an unsupported derived fact in the merged index; 8 lost rounds. Otherwise: head owners with OURS replaced; head index + the writer's patch re-applied, where pseudo owners (`@env` and derived facts, whose names are digests of their answers) keep the UNION of both supports (more observations only make later verification stricter, never lose a dependency); global/membership/frontier from the side that changed them; verified stamp = (writer's covered owners minus THEIRS) ∪ head's covered owners at the older time (never claims coverage of what a peer rewrote); CAS again, re-validating cumulatively from the base against each newer head. No migration: manifests keep `built.kind: "delta"` with `base` = the head.
4. **Retirement** only by a holder whose claim can become structural because NO other live claim exists (`claim.alone()`); otherwise the next lone builder retires. Reason: with concurrent builders a fragment that aged out of the 4 retained generations can be re-referenced by a concurrent build (edit + revert = same fragment digest; `keep(ifAbsent)` does not rewrite it) and be deleted between its `keep` and the manifest write = a torn generation.
5. **Custody released before assembly**: `loadGenerationGraphV1` releases the claim as soon as `refresh` returns; assembly reads an immutable published generation (today the custody is also held through assembly + expansion).
6. Readers unchanged (pointer -> manifest snapshots, generation pins). `builderIdleV1` asks the custody (`claims.idle({builderFree})`), so a lone or disjoint command skips the prefetch as graphlat's idle rule intends.
7. `withBuildGraphStateV1` keeps its scope branch for SELECTION publications (`loadBuildGraphV1` passes a selection scope; headroom recovery takes those writers) - only the dead `scope===generationScopeV1` option is removed.
