## Intended Narrow Cleanup Owner

Before any creation/activation side effect, accept a finite cleanup reserve and retain the real directory/exclusive operation custody. Preserve the body control unchanged. Cleanup enrollment must be a private first-party capability, inaccessible from serialized evidence and unable to accept arbitrary commands, environment, paths, or new body work. It must not recreate `ScriptInvocation`, extract fresh authority from environment, or expose a newly minted `ResourceLeaseControl` to callers.

Record the actual created directory and backing identities before activation submission, and record the accepted activation command before awaiting its result. Always join that real child first. A failed/aborted attach result is not proof that activation never happened; reconcile complete bounded kernel/tool inventory against the accepted backing and exact mount/device identity. A local `mounted` boolean or receipt text alone is insufficient.

The preaccepted continuation may observe that identity, deactivate that identity, verify physical inactivity, and remove only accepted owned material. The platform port names neutral actions; macOS detach, Linux loop/mount identity, and Windows VHD/disk/partition mapping remain platform details. The cleanup reserve must be carved from accepted whole-operation authority before body execution, with an explicit absolute terminal bound; restarting a timeout after body expiration is not funding. Cleanup's own cancellation/deadline stops its owned child and records unresolved custody without renewing its budget. Actual guard release follows successful settlement or an explicit accepted unresolved-ownership transfer; a rejected cleanup cannot silently erase the owner.

Persistent publication is an explicit accepted handoff. Successful persistent reader close releases its held reader only. Failed fresh unpublished creation remains under its original exclusive owner until physical settlement or an explicit unresolved handoff. Preserve the primary body failure plus all cleanup/settlement failures. Do not swallow cleanup errors or recursively remove a path merely because a local mount flag is false.
