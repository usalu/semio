# Storage Pressure Headroom

Repeated compiler and immutable-toolkit activity exhausted physical storage despite guarded retirement of obsolete SDKs. The normal cache policy protects two days of recent units and cannot meet its budget during this development burst. A separate schema-owned pressure policy observes actual filesystem available bytes and protects an inactive working set while reclaiming bounded cold slices.

The final policy triggers below 8 GiB available, aims for 16 GiB, protects the last 24 hours of actual unit access/modification, and caps each operation at 32 GiB of logical deletion. Held units remain excluded. Continuations preserve the operation's original actual headroom observation and original control; they observe current headroom again rather than inventing a new trigger. Logical sizes are planning estimates. The executor checks actual filesystem available space after each deletion and stops at the physical target or the deletion cap.

The maintenance path still requires the original cache-prune lease, whole compiler-store exclusive custody, fresh compiler-process checks, OS-level Cargo profile locks, no-follow original lock-file identity, a fresh open-descriptor probe, and another current unit scan under those physical locks. Only profiles protected by the acquired physical locks are admitted. No source input or active compiler cache is authorized for deletion by a pressure estimate alone.

The neutral planner corpus covers below-threshold activation, threshold equality, bounded shortfall, already-held units, recent units, oversized units, original-trigger continuation, target completion, invalid policy, and invalid measurements. Ajv validates both the neutral and actual canonical policy, SQLite independently selects oldest eligible units, and strict TypeScript and both launch inventories are checked.
