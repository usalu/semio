## Meter Age And Finite Repeated Work

Exact authored policy: directory protocol meter intervalMilliseconds=2000 and intervalBytes=16777216; usage recentMilliseconds=5000, synchronousMaximumAgeMilliseconds=60000, concurrency=16 and batch=8. The meter refresh captures startedAt before awaiting measure and writes measuredAt=startedAt after completion. Therefore a walk lasting at least 2000 ms leaves this meter immediately stale for its next recheck. Usage cells also record their measurement start time, not completion. The meter explicitly calls usage.fresh, so changing only the usage recent-cache timestamp would not repair meter rescanning.

For a previously committed dependencies Store entered without an existing ambient Store, the mandatory pre-dispatch callback sequence is finite: (1) owner acquisition measure(recheck); (2) reservation post-acquisition storage.recheck; (3) reservation final storage.recheck after charge-receipt custody; (4) repositoryCacheStoreBodyV1 body-recheck. Each uses the same image meter. If every full walk lasts at least two seconds, all four start distinct sequential full dependency-tree walks before the tools dispatcher. If there is no durable owner, reserve/commit paths can add measurements; an existing held Store follows its distinct verify-before-callback path. This proves repeated whole-tree work in the source path, not the live job exact current position or measured duration. It accounts for a possible multiple of one slow scan without proving hours attributable to this cause.

It is not an internal stale-retry/livelock: recheck tests stale once, awaits at most one refresh, gates, and returns without retesting staleness. Reserve allows one additional refresh before refusal; commit always refreshes once. The walker processes a bounded number/depth of directory entries and drains accepted work; cancellation is checked between batches. Under finite filesystem-call completion, each measurement and the four-step sequence terminate. A changing tree can hit its finite bound and refuse, but these routines do not retry forever.

Safety constraints: admitted bytes accumulate between measurements, and refresh removes only the admitted amount captured before the walk, retaining additions made during it. Filesystem and physical ceilings remain checked independently. The usage cell singleflight is process-wide per exact root, not cross-process: no overlapping walks, with a running caller queued to one trailing walk that starts after its arrival. The test contract explicitly requires that no caller receives a scan begun before its own write; simultaneous commits cost at most two walks. Each waiter cancels independently, and the underlying scan is aborted only when its last subscriber leaves. A recent read may reuse its time-bounded observation; a commit still requests fresh.

Current neutral meter fixtures exercise cumulative writes, large writes, commit growth, elapsed clock staleness, reopen/recovered content, and independent SQLite capacity checks. Usage tests cover synchronous/async/Python/du agreement, entry bounds, cancellation/drain, singleflight/trailing run, recent cache and monotone recorded timestamps. Their delayed singleflight example is 20 ms; the simple freshness test measure does not advance its fake clock. None of the inspected cases asserts consecutive rechecks after a measurement taking longer than the two-second interval. This is a specific missing performance-contract vector, not a failed current safety test.

Completion-time caching would change freshness semantics: early entries in a long non-atomic walk could already be old when completion is stamped. ## Independent Review Amendment: Concurrent Accounting

The independent meter review found that the existing refresh baseline subtraction is not safe merely because usage walks serialize. Two refresh callbacks can both capture admitted=10; after the first completes it clears that baseline, an accepted reservation can add 5, and the later completion can subtract the original 10 again and erase the new 5. Those five reserved bytes need not yet exist on disk, so a completed scan cannot substitute for their admission charge. This is a concurrency-accounting counterexample requiring its own neutral proof, not evidence about the current live job position. Completion-time stamping alone is therefore insufficient for the proposal.

Required correction to the recommendation above: pair completed-observation reuse with retained-byte watermark/epoch accounting or proven meter-operation serialization, preserving post-arrival fresh scans and cancellation semantics. A monotone total-admitted counter with a successfully covered watermark can avoid subtracting one baseline twice; completed observation ordering must also prevent an older result from overwriting a newer observation or resurrecting stale accounting. Capturing the watermark before fresh is conservative if a queued scan starts later, but cannot lose reservations accepted after capture. These are design constraints for implementation/testing, not a newly issued authority or a complete proof of one algorithm.

Add a closed neutral overlap vector with two refresh requests sharing an original ten-byte admission baseline, a new five-byte reservation between completions, and SQLite expectation retainedBytes>=5 (totalAccepted minus only a successfully covered monotone baseline).
