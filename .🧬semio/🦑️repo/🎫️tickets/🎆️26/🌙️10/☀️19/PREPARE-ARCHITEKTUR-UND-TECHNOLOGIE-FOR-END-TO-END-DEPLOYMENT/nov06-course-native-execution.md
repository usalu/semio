## Compiler inventory investigation

`inspectPortableMsvcTreeV1` serially processes every entry. For each ordinary file it lstats, calls `readPhysicalCargoSourceV1`, hashes the returned bytes, and lstats again; it later lstats all observed files and re-enumerates every directory. The physical reader independently walks and lstats all ancestors from the volume root, then rechecks every ancestor plus pathname and descriptor before reading, after the read, after a complete byte replay, and after EOF, with further checks between chunks. This is repeated depth-times-file-count metadata work; no global quadratic scan was found. The duplicate byte reads and repeated identity/membership checks have distinct custody and race-detection purposes and cannot simply be removed.

A bounded candidate is to keep all existing checks but inspect independent files concurrently with a small fixed task limit and strict aggregate extent budget. Directory enumeration and both final validation passes remain intact; all file work must drain before final validation, including on failure or cancellation. This overlaps I/O without relying on cached identities or weakening custody. Its benefit is unmeasured; race, cancellation, memory-bound and independent-oracle coverage are required before adoption.

The approved candidate is now authored with a schema-declared four-job limit, 256 MiB per-file extent and 512 MiB aggregate reader allocation. Source inspection confirms that the physical reader retains the file buffer, a replay buffer capped at 65536 bytes and a one-byte EOF probe; its allocation weight includes all three. The scheduler stops admissions on failure, drains started tasks before returning and retains the original reader and final file/membership checks. The identity digest still sorts both collections. Neutral allocation, overlap, failure and cancellation vectors and an Ajv schema check were added before implementation.
