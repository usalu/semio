## Historical Aperture Continuity

Plate28 already contains the landscape aperture directly at the end of the upper exterior ramp and the table/ledge beneath it. Surrounding screen has a thin high horizontal band with vertical ribs: the aperture is the small lower opening in the rightmost framed bay, not the entire multi-bay screen. This is the same local topology visible in the modern CMN wider photograph. The modern close photograph resolves continuous sill material, vertical jambs and head material; the original plate28 table and surrounding frame confirm that the basic aperture arrangement is not solely a later restoration invention.

Consequently no restoration hypothesis is required to reconcile the existence of the aperture. Exact restoration history, original jamb spacing, finishes and every reconstructed section detail are not established by this comparison. ## Plate27 Barred Feature: Useful Positive Hypothesis

The completed upper photograph's451..470,y58..80 rectangle is cut into a substantially higher screen face behind the roof parapet. Enlarging the original pixel crop shows dark near-vertical lines and a dark diagonal triangular region inside it, plus a small horizontal ledge projecting below/left. There is no independently resolved glazed frame, door handle or stair-door threshold. Calling this a barred stair window solely from those lines is unjustified.

A concrete positive correspondence hypothesis is an exterior reverse view through the unglazed landscape aperture: the dark verticals/diagonal can be ramp handrails seen behind the opening, and the ledge can be the table/sill assembly. Plate28 establishes those real objects immediately in front of the aperture on the interior side. Their visibility through the opposite side is a stronger discriminant than rectangle shape alone. This remains a hypothesis because none of the supplied complete camera overlays registers all independent roof objects simultaneously. Do not promote it to a final identity or add a glazing/bar product.

The315..365,y63..77 low horizontal feature is a distinct low screen/frame silhouette; it is not automatically the landscape aperture. In the original crop it lacks a resolved bounded opening/table pair. The slender365..372 terminal is not morphologically decidable at its native7px width, consistent with R44's retraction.

## Actionable Registration Test

For each physically positive-depth candidate, project actual upper-ramp handrails, table/sill, aperture jambs/head and the full surrounding ribbed screen together. Test whether the handrail diagonal falls inside451..470 and whether the table projects to the visible bottom ledge. This can distinguish a reverse-aperture view from a stair opening without inventing fenestration. Use plate28 interior ramp/table topology as an independent second-view constraint. Do not fit only the aperture rectangle.

Also independently check CMN roof-plan XY orientation through unique stair/ramp adjacency and the actual garden side. R20 calibration through symmetric grid extents alone does not disambiguate a roof-only reflection/rotation. The archival structural register explicitly reverses original page X; the CMN roof mapping must be checked in its own page coordinates rather than presumed identical. This is an audit target, not an established source defect or permission to reflect the roof.

### What the Section Heights Actually Sample

Annex5 key plan shows two closely spaced longitudinal cuts through the ramp lanes. The upper section cuts solid screen; the lower crosses the landscape aperture. Each has crest+9.325 and inner cap edge+9.105. These are two local stations in the same front screen region; they do not independently measure every remote curved limb. It would overstate Annex5 to call its two leaders a direct numerical survey of all11 authored path segments.

There is broader primary reconstruction support for a common top band: Annex6 upper transverse section shows the ribbed wind screen in background with a single horizontal upper line labelled+9.325. Annex7 exploded structural axon (generated/archive-r8-page24.png) shows both curved ends and their connecting ribbed frame with visually consistent top-band level relative to the slab plane. This is evidence of the authors' reconstructed common-height family, with schematic-drawing precision.

CSIC article§5.2 describes every rib being tied by a concrete cap inclined inward and reaching the stair exit. The inward inclination is a slope across wall thickness; it does not describe a progressive longitudinal height drop along the screen. The text supplies no alternative lower-limb crest and no stepped-height schedule. This supports treating the cap as a connected construction family, while the exact common numeric9.325 height for every metre remains reconstructed rather than independently measured here.

Modern CMN close/wide photographs show continuous surrounding top-band/frame topology and no obvious step adjacent to the aperture. They cannot supply exact crest equality of unseen remote ends. Original plate27's left low315..367 silhouette is insufficient to establish a lower SC1 limb: depth, occlusion, a distinct lower object and correspondence are unresolved. The original exterior apparent-height contrast therefore does not justify lowering any native path segment. No source-supported varying-height fact was found in these inspected primary sources.
