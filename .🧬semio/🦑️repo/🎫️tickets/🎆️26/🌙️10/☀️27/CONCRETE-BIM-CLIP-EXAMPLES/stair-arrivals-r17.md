# Stair Arrivals and Continuous Handrails R17

## Physical Defects and Red Proof

The inherited guard covered all21 winding treads and fenced off the real right-side arrival landing. A new independent test required the actual landing/tread union to support an8cm foot strip and a0.8m body corridor to be free of guard/tube solids. It failed against ST-GF before the repair. The old fitted-detail arrival check used obsolete coordinates and did not establish this connection.

The inherited metal rail also comprised horizontal cap fragments separated vertically by each riser. It was not a continuous helical rail. The source retained its inferred35mm nominal diameter; this is not presented as a measured tube size.

## Published Geometry

Both upper stairs retain their registered centre(6.23842,9.263605), actual floor datums and source-assumed21-tread helical representation. The last two blocking guard sectors were removed. The remaining body begins at tread tops instead of duplicating6cm of tread volume. Short connected guard returns and landing flanks protect the arrival edges while retaining the real exit.

Each continuous helical metal rail comprises66 short inclined segments. Lower and upper rings of each segment have identical orientation and corresponding edges. Horizontal elliptical sections use minor radius0.0175m and major radius divided by the segment slope sine, so their physical perpendicular section corresponds to the nominal round rod.32-sided sections approximate the circle; this is a tessellated joined loft rather than an exact circular sweep. Adjacent rotated rings overlap in positive area at their common Z plane. The landing returns, horizontal cap pieces and vertical supports complete the rail connection. All geometry remains local to the owning stair's from-level.

## Independent Verification

GEOS confirms positive shared-cap area for every consecutive helical pair. NumPy polygon areas times segment rises agree with the model solid volumes, and the nominal circular-rod volume differs by less than0.7%, the stated32-sided-section approximation. The landing test considers convex projections of both bottom and top rings, not only the lower footprint, and passes for both ST-GF and ST-FR. The source remains an inferred geometric reconstruction of the stair rather than a measured fabrication model.

The source partial-model selectors were also repaired after suite renames/removals; the new foundations are included by their explicit architecture/structure domains. Stale material and type occurrence associations were removed. All three analytical links were recomputed after source publication and returned no consistency issues.
