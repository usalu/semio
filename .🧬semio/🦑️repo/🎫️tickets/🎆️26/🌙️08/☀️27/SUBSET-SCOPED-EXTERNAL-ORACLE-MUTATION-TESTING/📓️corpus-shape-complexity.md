Every fixture below was generated with `bun 🏭️generator/📜️script.ts generate --only <id>` (never a bare `generate`), then measured with `🔬️probes/📜️script.ts measure|topology|brep-validity|step-mesh-compare` against the committed `expected.step`. Every number in every recipe's `notes` field is one of these measured numbers, not an intended one — two recipes below only reached their final, shipped form because the first attempt's measurement contradicted the plan.

## Recipes attempted and abandoned/changed, with exact kernel behavior

1. **`fuse-torus-torus-interlock` at offset 14 (first attempt).** `fuse(torus, translatedTorus)` succeeded, `isValidSolid` was `true`, single solid. But `step-mesh-compare --input expected.step --input expected.step` failed with:
   ```
   ManifoldError: Not manifold
   ```
   thrown from `manifold-3d`'s `Manifold.ofMesh` after welding. The exact BRep is valid; its tessellation at the fixture's declared tolerance is not manifold. Rather than ship a fixture that fails its own mesh self-check, I swept nearby offsets (12, 14, 16, 18, and a pure-Z offset) directly against the kernel; only 14 failed. Shipped the recipe at offset 16 instead, with the offset-14 finding recorded in the fixture's own `notes` (it is real corpus knowledge — a future fixture author who reaches for offset 14 for two r=10/3 tori will hit this).

2. **`cut-helical-coil-groove` — original attempt returned `result: call(b, "cut", base, coil)` with `outcome: "applied"`.** `bun 🏭️generator/📜️script.ts generate --only cut-helical-coil-groove` failed:
   ```
   [generator] cut-helical-coil-groove FAILED — exportSTEP: {"kind":"IO","code":"STEP_EXPORT_CRASHED","message":"STEP export crashed the kernel (Out of bounds memory access (evaluating 'func(...args)')); the shape likely contains geometry the STEP writer cannot serialize","cause":{}}
   ```
   Isolated which of the three exports (operand-a, operand-b, result) was responsible: the coil TOOL alone exports fine (46398 bytes); only exporting the CUT RESULT crashes. Then tested whether the crash was recoverable in-process — it is not: after the crash, exporting the untouched base cylinder again, or exporting a brand-new, unrelated 5×5×5 box, both fail with the byte-identical `STEP_EXPORT_CRASHED` message. The crash poisons the whole WASM instance for the rest of the process, not just the one call. Because of that, the shipped `build()` never calls `exportSTEP` on the crashing shape at all (doing so, even just to "verify and fall back" the way `reject-ellipse-structured-error`/`reject-open-shell-fuse` do in the `failure` family, would poison the kernel before the generator's own operand exports run and take down the whole bundle). Kept the recipe with `outcome: "rejected"`, `result` falling back to the safely-exportable base cylinder, and the full finding — including the poisoning behavior — recorded in `notes`.

3. **`cut-spline-bounded-pocket` — first control-point layout.** `bsplineApprox([[0,0,0],[3,4,0],[7,-3,0],[10,2,0]])` closed with one line back to the origin produced a self-crossing boundary; the extruded tool measured `isValidSolid: false` (the subsequent cut still completed without error). Not shipped as-is — replaced the control points with a non-self-intersecting layout (`[0,0,0],[4,6,0],[8,3,0],[10,0,0]`) that measures `valid: true`, and recorded the discarded layout's exact failure in `notes` since it is itself informative (this kernel does not validate self-intersecting NURBS profile boundaries before extruding them).

No recipe was silently dropped. Every case attempted is either in the final 19 or documented above with its exact error.

## Single most interesting kernel behavior

`fuse-thickened-shell-into-block`: `thicken(getFaces(cylinder(15,30))[0], 2)` — thickening a cylinder's curved lateral face by 2mm into a closed tube-shell solid — returns a solid whose `measureVolume` is **−6031.86mm³**. The magnitude is exactly right (π×(17²−15²)×30 = 6031.86, the correct annulus volume), but the sign is negative, meaning the solid comes back with inverted face orientation. Fusing that inverted-orientation tube fully inside a 60×60×30 block does not add material: the reimported result measures `solids: 2`, total volume 101968.14mm³ — exactly the block's own 108000mm³ minus 6031.86mm³. `fuse()` treated the inverted-orientation operand as a subtractive tool, silently behaving like `cut()` and splitting the block into a disjoint inner core plus an outer shell-with-cavity. Declared outcome corrected to `disjoint` from the numbers, not the intent — a live re-run of the exact mistake this family's own header docstring already documents happening twice before (`cut-tangent-cylinder-exact`, `fuse-edge-touching-boxes`).

Runner-up: `cut-helical-coil-groove`'s STEP-export crash is not contained to the one call — it permanently poisons the WASM kernel instance, breaking every later export in the same process regardless of that shape's own validity. That is a corpus-generation hazard worth flagging beyond this one fixture: a future full (non-`--only`) regeneration run must never schedule a recipe like this ahead of others in the same process.
