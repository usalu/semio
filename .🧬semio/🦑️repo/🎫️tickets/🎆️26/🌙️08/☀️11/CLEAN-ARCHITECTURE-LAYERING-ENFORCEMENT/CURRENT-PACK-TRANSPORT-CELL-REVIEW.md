Native verification remains required for these unsafe access/destruction boundaries.

Caller accounting distinguishes cumulative committed charge from live physical bytes. Dropping an unused reservation restores its pre-reservation committed amount. Publishing the source preserves its admitted charge after final-owner Drop, even though its physical Vec allocation and concrete E are freed. The current provider corpus explicitly expects that cumulative behavior; the production documentation and native laws must state both witnesses so it cannot be mistaken for a live memory ledger.

The new source cell measures its own physical capacity, including E layout and the atomic header. It does not census opaque heap ownership already acquired by the external error. Those runtime proofs and whole-owner tests remain open.
