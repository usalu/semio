1. CATALOG AND AUTHORING QUALITY

Before bulk implementation, create a concrete asset manifest with stable IDs, categories, real dimensions, supported poses, clips, analytic definitions, viewpoints, export capabilities, and completion status.

Editable initial scope assumption: deliver at least 48 genuinely distinct, carefully authored assets:
- 12 humans with varied proportions, clothing, activities, mobility, and carried objects.
- 8 trees or other vegetation with distinct growth forms and branching.
- 6 animals.
- 8 furniture items or other objects.
- 6 vehicles or machines.
- 8 building components or structural assemblies.

If the repository already contains a larger agreed manifest, preserve that scope. Count meaningful differences in geometry, rig, function, and physical data; recolors, renamed duplicates, and trivial parameter changes do not establish archive breadth.

Use a typed, validated, versioned schema for identity, taxonomy, provenance, dimensions, geometry, materials, rig, contact/support anchors, poses, animations, thermal parameters, load cases, presentation, export support, and validation status. Author recognizable silhouettes and useful detail rather than generic geometric placeholders. Reuse engineering primitives where appropriate while retaining individually authored assets and parameters.

Every asset needs at least three meaningful poses/configurations/load states and two meaningful animation or state-transition clips. Rigid components must use physically appropriate transforms, moving subcomponents, loading transitions, or explicitly modeled deformation; do not invent anatomical joints for a static object. Validate all manifest entries. Do not count an asset as complete while any required view, pose, clip, model, or export is missing.

2. REAL DIMENSIONS AND DRAWING SCALES

Keep canonical geometry and physical parameters in declared real-world units, using SI internally. Implement 1:500, 1:200, 1:100, and 1:50 presets plus configurable additional scales.

Changing drawing scale affects paper-space size, line weights, level of detail, and annotations. It must not change the asset's real dimensions, mass, temperature, thermal output, or structural properties. Separate viewport zoom, real dimensions, and print scale.

Acceptance example: a 1.80 m person must occupy 3.6 mm at 1:500, 9 mm at 1:200, 18 mm at 1:100, and 36 mm at 1:50 in a dimensionally correct print export. Verify at 100% print size. CAD exports use explicitly declared model-space units.

3. SEVEN SYNCHRONIZED VIEWS

Implement four graphic styles and three analytic views:
1. Dark line.
2. Dark solid.
3. Light line.
4. Light solid.
5. Thermodynamic.
6. Structural forces/loads.
7. Structural moments.

All seven views must share the same geometry, dimensions, rig, pose, and animation time. Dark/light describe actual output styling and must work against suitable backgrounds and transparent exports. Provide deliberate line hierarchy, small-scale simplification, and legibility at each scale.

Analytic views use clear legends, explicit units and sign conventions, adjustable display ranges, and inspection of actual values. Use warm colors for compression and cool colors for tension, consistently with the thermal palette, while clearly identifying the different physical quantities. Include an accessible alternative to color-only interpretation.

4. RIGS, POSES, AND ANIMATION

Every asset has a reusable bone/transform hierarchy appropriate to its category:
- Humans and animals: anatomical articulation and contact anchors.
- Vegetation: trunk, branch, and foliage hierarchies.
- Machines and objects: relevant rigid assemblies, joints, moving parts, and attachments.
- Building components: rigid roots, structural nodes, connection/support anchors, and valid moving or deforming parts.

Implement deterministic pose evaluation, named poses, keyframes, interpolation, transitions, looping, playback rate, frame stepping, and arbitrary-time scrubbing. Preserve plausible proportions, joint limits, and contact behavior. Derive graphics and analytical overlays from the evaluated state; do not synchronize independently animated decorations by eye.

Each clip records duration, frame/time conventions, meaningful action phases, contact changes, and relevant thermal/load events. Prevent uncontrolled random jitter, disconnected extremities, and visibly sliding planted feet. Distinguish authoring geometry from generated display meshes/paths.

5. THERMODYNAMIC MODELING

The thermodynamic view is a solid representation with asset-specific surface fields and explicit environmental exchange.

For each asset, define thermal regions, material/emissivity, surface temperatures or the parameters from which they are calculated, exposed versus covered areas, ambient conditions, activity/operating state, and source strengths. Clearly distinguish surface temperature from emitted or net radiant heat flux; give each display its own units, legend, and model assumptions.

For humans and animals, account for clothing, exposed skin, activity, and relevant body regions under stated conditions. Do not assume every exposed part is always hotter or every clothed part is always cooler. For machinery, model relevant operating temperatures and exhaust sources. For vegetation, distinguish solar/ambient surface effects from transpiration.

Separate:
- Radiation.
- Convection and sensible heat transfer.
- Evaporation/transpiration and latent heat or water-vapor transfer.

Model breath and exhaust with source location, direction, temperature, flow assumptions, and temporal emission phases. Model tree transpiration as an evaporative/latent-heat process rather than a generic hot plume. Provide an explicit interpretation of curves, streamlines, and particles: whether they represent airflow, temperature, or vapor transport.

For every pose and animation, explicitly author thermal-region bindings, emitter attachments, time-dependent parameters, and event phases. Evaluate the fields throughout the timeline. Source positions and fields must follow the rig and respond to activity, wind, ambient conditions, and other modeled inputs. Reusing a shared solver is appropriate; attaching an identical decorative gradient or plume to every asset and frame is not.

Use physically defensible equations and reference cases. Document the distinction between measured parameters, literature-based assumptions, authored estimates, and illustrative approximations. Do not claim measured accuracy, CFD validation, or physiological accuracy that has not been established.

6. STRUCTURAL FORCES AND MOMENTS

Use the term "moment" for leverage multiplied by force. The requested structural quantity is torque/bending moment, not momentum.

Define segment masses, centers of mass, applied loads, contacts, supports, joint constraints, section properties, and material properties where needed. Derive reactions and internal loads from these inputs and the evaluated pose.

Keep these quantities distinct:
- Axial force and compression/tension.
- Contact force and contact pressure.
- Stress.
- Shear force.
- Bending moment or joint torque.

For a force F, calculate moment using the perpendicular lever arm, or the vector cross product when appropriate. Display N, Pa, and N·m consistently. Do not hardcode that feet always have the largest force or shoulders always have the greatest moment; calculate the actual pose, support sharing, carried load, and lever arms.

Provide synchronized free-body diagrams, applied-force and reaction arrows, centers of mass, support/contact markers, and applicable axial-force, shear-force, and bending-moment diagrams. A cantilever reference case must show the correct reaction and moment behavior.

For animations, recalculate as poses, loads, and contacts change. Where inertia is included, state the dynamic model. Where a quasi-static approximation is used, label it. Simplified human joint calculations must not be presented as clinically validated internal bone or muscle forces.

Validate force and moment balance in static reference cases and appropriate consistency conditions for dynamic cases. Ensure stress colors, internal-force diagrams, and numerical values agree with the same model.
