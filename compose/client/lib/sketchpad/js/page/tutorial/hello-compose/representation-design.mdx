This section shows how to **turn your brick molds** - the **Types** and **Variants** you've defined - into real, usable building blocks 🧱

As explained in _[Think in Compose]_, a Type is just a **blueprint**. It defines how a brick should look and behave — but it’s not a brick yet 📐

To actually build a design, you need to create **Pieces** — the bricks made from your molds, ready to place, connect, and assemble 🧩

---

## 🔖 Place Your Bricks (Representation Pieces – Pcs)

Now it’s time to **turn your molds into actual bricks** — called **Pieces** 🧩  
These are the elements you’ll place, move, and connect in your design 🎯

![📤 sketch drawing](/asset/image/🖼️sketch-drawing-piece.png)

When you create a Piece, you're telling Compose:  
_"Take this specific mold (Type + Variant) and make a real, usable brick from it."_ 🧱

---

Each **Piece** automatically includes everything from its mold:

- **Geometry (Representation)** — the shape defined by the Variant’s geometry file 🧱
- **Connectors** — the snap points and their directions from the Type ⚓
- **Semantic Data** — the Type name, Variant identity, and design role 🧠
- **Metadata** — any extra info like function, material, or tags 📄

In short: a Piece is a fully formed brick, based on the rules and shape you defined earlier 🔁

---

### 🆔 Name the Pieces

Each Piece needs a unique name — its ID — so compose can 🧭 track, 🔗 reference, and 🧩 connect it later on.
Think of it as giving your brick a personal label 🏷️

In this example, we name the Pieces based on the compose logo — using both **color** and **position** for clarity 🎨

- 🟨 `Yel` → yellow piece on the left
- 🔴 `Red-T` → red piece at the top
- 🔴 `Red-R` → red piece on the right
- 🔵 `Blu-T` → blue piece at the top
- 🔵 `Blu-B` → blue piece at the bottom

As always in Compose, naming is flexible 🔤  
But a clear and consistent system makes your design easier to understand, build, and share 🧠

> 💡 It’s like putting a sticker on each LEGO brick so you always know which one goes where 🧱

---

### 🧬 Link to the Mold

To representation a Piece, compose needs to know **which mold it’s made from** 🔗  
That means linking it to a specific **Type** and **Variant**:

- 🏷️ **Type name** → e.g. `'Profile'`
- 🔢 **Variant number** → e.g. `2`, `4`, or `5`

👉 Without this reference, the Piece has no identity — it won’t know what shape to take or how to connect ⚠️

---

1. 🆔 **Piece IDs** → Unique names for each brick (e.g. `Yel`, `Blu-B`)

Each row becomes a new **Piece**, named by its ID — like `Pce(Yel)` or `Pce(Red-R)` —

## 🔗 Connect the Bricks (Representation Connections - Con)

Now that you've placed your **Pieces**, it's time to tell compose how they connect 🔗  
Think of this like writing the **assembly instructions** for snapping bricks together 🧲

Each **Connection** says:  
👉 _"Connect this Connector on one Piece to a Connector on another Piece"_

<br />

![📤 sketch drawing](/asset/image/🖼️sketch-drawing-connections.png)

### 🧭 Set the Connection

To create a connection, you need to define four values:

1. 🆔 **Connected Piece ID (`CdPc`)** → the Piece you're connecting _from_
2. ⚓ **Connected Piece Connector ID (`CdPo`)** → the Connector you're connecting _from_
3. 🆔 **Connecting Piece ID (`CgPc`)** → the Piece you're connecting _to_
4. ⚓ **Connecting Piece Connector ID (`CgPo`)** → the Connector you're connecting _to_

---

Put simply:  
➡️ **Connect this Connector on this Piece → to that Connector on that Piece**

---

##### 🧩 Example: Connecting Two Pieces

🧱 Before snapping the full representation together, let’s start small — by connecting the **first two bricks**.  
We’ll connect Piece `Yel` to Piece `Red-T`, using their **west-facing Connectors**.

From the sketch, we can tell:

- Use the **`w` (west)** Connector of `Yel`
- Connect it to the **`w` (west)** Connector of `Red-T`

So the connection looks like this:
`Con(Yel:w → Red-T:w)`

![Connection Example](/asset/image/🖼️mode-connection-grasshopper.png)

This tells Compose:  
**Snap the west Connector of `Yel` to the west Connector of `Red-T`**

Once this first connection is in place, you can continue snapping all remaining bricks together using similar logic 🧩

   👉 **Snap the west Connector of `Yel`** to the **west Connector of `Red-T`**

2. `Con(Red-T:e → e:Red-R)`  
   👉 **Snap the east Connector of `Red-T`** to the **east Connector of `Red-R`**

3. `Con(Red-R:n → w:Blu-T)`

4. `Con(Red-R:n → w:Blu-B)`  
   👉 **Snap the north Connector of `Red-R`** to the **west Connector of `Blu-B`**

### 🎯 Adjust Snap _(Optional Step)_

When two Pieces are connected through Connectors, compose gives you control over **how the attached geometry is positioned** —  
without changing the Connector itself.

These adjustments are applied **relative to the orientation of the Connector**, based on how it was defined in the mold (Type).

📌 Use this when the geometry needs to be slightly offset — to float, shift, or align more precisely.

---

#### 🔁 Move (Translation)

You can move the geometry in three local directions:

- **Gap** ⬅️➡️ → Creates a space between two connected elements by pushing the Piece forward or backward along the Connector's axis
  Ideal for separating elements or creating breathing room between parts

- **Shift** ↔️ → Moves the Piece side to side, across the plane of the Connector  
  📐 Like nudging a LEGO brick left or right to line it up with studs underneath

- **Rise** ⬆️⬇️ → Lifts or lowers the Piece vertically, perpendicular to the face it connects to  
  🪜 Like lifting a brick slightly higher or lowering it onto the studs

  ![Connection Adjustment](/asset/image/🖼️translation-parameter.gif)

---

#### 🔄 Rotating (Orientation)

- **Rotate** 🔃 → Rotates the Piece flat, around the Connector's main vector
  🧭 Like spinning a round LEGO tile in place
- **Turn** 🔄 → Turns the Piece sideways, rotating it horizontally
  🌀 Like twisting a LEGO hinge side to side
- **Tilt** 🤸 → Tilts the Piece forward or backward, around a vertical axis
  🎢 Like tilting a ramp or slope brick up or down

  ![Connection Adjustment](/asset/image/🖼️Rotation-parameter.gif)

## 🏗️ Build the Design (Representation Design - Dsn)

Now that you've created your **Pieces** and defined their **Connections**, it's time to bring them all together into a final **Design** 🧰

### 🧩 What is a Design?

A **Design** in compose is your full representation — created by snapping together specific **Pieces** 🧱 using their **Connections** 🔗

It doesn’t just describe geometry 👁️ — it defines how each part fits, aligns, and works within the system 🧠  
You're building not just a shape, but a **smart, modular structure** 🧬

🧱 Think of it like a finished LEGO build:  
You’re not just stacking bricks — you’re creating a representation where every part has a **role** 🎭, a **position** 📍, and a **purpose** 🎯

---

### 🛠️ Assembling the Design

To build your Design in compose (e.g. in Grasshopper), you’ll use the `Representation Design` component.  
This is where you **bring together all the Pieces and Connections** to form your complete representation 🧩

You’ll provide three main inputs:

1. **Design Name** (`Dn`) 🏷️  
   A unique name for your representation — like `"Logo Example"` or `"Spaceship"`.

2. **Pieces** (`Pcs`) 🧱  
   A list of the Pieces you’ve created — each one linked to a Type, Variant, and unique ID.

3. **Connections** (`Con`) 🔗  
   A list of the snapping rules — telling compose how the Pieces are connected through their Connectors.

## ⚙️ Workflow Mechanics

Now that we've covered the core concepts of Compose, let's take a closer look at how the components actually work —  
and explore the key features of the compose workflow inside Grasshopper

### 🛠️ Grasshopper Workflow

compose doesn’t change **how** Grasshopper works — it changes **what** you’re working with.  
You’re no longer just wiring geometry — you’re wiring **design intent** 💡

- **Types** 🧱 – Brick molds with meaning, not just shapes
- **Pieces** 🧩 – Placed bricks that follow system logic
- **Connections** 🔗 – Rules that control how elements relate
- **Designs** 🧰 – Full assemblies of smart, connected pieces

This shift lets you create not just geometry — but structured, adaptable systems 🧠

---

compose components behave just like regular Grasshopper components 🧩

- 📋 You still work with **lists** and **data trees**
- 🔢 Items are matched by **index** or **branch**

Want to set a **gap** ⬅️➡️ or a **rotation** 🔁?  
Just provide a matching list of values — each one applies to the corresponding item in your list of **Connections** or **Pieces**.

---

---

### 🔄 Contruct, Deconstruct, Reconstruct

In Compose, **Representation components** like `Representation Type`, `Representation Piece`, `Representation Connection`, and `Representation Design` follow a shared logic:  
they’re not just builders — they’re also **apps** and **inspectors**.

Each component supports three fundamental actions:

---

##### 1. 🔨 Construct

Each Representation component can **create** a structured compose object from your inputs:

- `Representation Type` → outputs a `Ty` (Type)
- `Representation Piece` → outputs a `Pc` (Piece)
- `Representation Connection` → outputs a `Cn` (Connection)
- `Representation Design` → outputs a `Ds` (Design)

These are Compose’s core data types — they carry geometry, logic, metadata, and relationships.

---

##### 2. 🔍 Deconstruct

You can also use the same component to **inspect** or **extract** the inner structure of a compose object, and all its inputs will populate with the current values, like unpacking a brick to see how it was made.

---

##### 3. 🔁 Reconstruct / Modify

In Compose, you can update any representationed object — like a Type, Piece, Connection, or Design —  
by feeding its output back into the **first input** of the same Representation component.

- `Ty` → Type 🧱
- `Pc` → Piece 🧩
- `Cn` → Connection 🔗
- `Ds` → Design 🧰

When you do this, you can redefine any part of the object (like its name, geometry, plane, or metadata).  
Only the inputs you change will be updated — everything else stays exactly as it was

---

### 🧪 Example Use Case

<br />

#### 🧩 Modify a Piece

You can modify **any aspect** of a Piece — such as its **name**, **placement**, or other construction parameters by feeding its `Pc` output back into a new `Representation Piece` component 🧩

compose will only override the inputs you change — everything else stays exactly the same 🎯

> _E.g. In the "Hello Compose" logo example, rotating the first Piece flips all connected Pieces as well — because they’re linked logically_

#### 🔗 Adjust a Connection

You can adjust any **transformation or rotation parameters** of a Connection by  
plugging the `Cn` output back into a new `Representation Connection` component 🔁

compose will only update the values you change — all other settings will remain as they were 🔒  
This makes it easy to fine-tune how two Pieces are connected without rebuilding the whole logic 🔧

> _E.g. You can change the gap, shift, or rotation of an existing Connection, and all downstream geometry will update automatically._

#### 🧱 Update a Type

You can update a **Type** by feeding its `Ty` output back into the same `Representation Type` component 🔁  
This lets you modify its **Representation**, **Connectors**, or **metadata** — without changing anything else.

> _E.g. Swapping a simplified geometry file for a detailed one, or updating tags like material or category._

---

#### 🧰 Refactor a Design

To modify a full **Design**, feed its `Ds` output into a new `Representation Design` component 🔁  
You can update the **Design name**, reorganize the structure, or tweak how certain Pieces are connected.

> _E.g. Renaming a layout, replacing a few bricks, or adjusting a connection — without touching the rest._

---

#### 🖼️ What Happens Next?

To **see** your representation, plug everything into the `Preview Design` component 👀  
It displays all your Pieces in place — fully connected, correctly oriented, and ready to go 🧱

<Aside type="note">
In Compose, nothing is visualized automatically

This logic stays abstract until you pass it to the `Show Design` component —  
only then is everything **combined and visualized** as a complete representation

</Aside>
